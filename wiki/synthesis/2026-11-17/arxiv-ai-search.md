## LLMs, Agents & Language Modeling

### 1. RLiD: Reinforcement Learning in the Decision Plane for Language Agents and LLMs
- **Authors:** Ziyi Chen, Yan Zhang, Jianhui Wei, Jianing Yin, Daoan Zhang
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.02455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02455) (submitted 2 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Reinforcement learning (RL) has improved the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) across a wide range of tasks. However, token-level RL assigns an action's advantage to the entire generated response, including tokens specifying the parsed action, reasoning chains, and malformed content. Although the environment executes only the parsed action, variation in how the action is expressed in natural language can introduce additional variance into policy-gradient estimates. We propose RLiD (Reinforcement Learning in the Decision plane), a framework that directly optimizes the probabilities of executable action options. RLiD compiles observations, an optional compact memory, and a labeled action menu into the model input, and uses a large decision model (LDM) to select an action in a single forward pass. By eliminating autoregressive token generation and bypassing action parsing, RLiD avoids invalid action outputs and improves RL rollout efficiency. Our analysis shows that, within a fixed text policy, marginalizing over alternative realizations of the same action removes a zero-mean gradient component without changing the expected update. We further distill trained LDM policies into generative LLMs through supervision on their action trajectories. Experiments on ARC, Countdown, and Sokoban demonstrate improved performance or lower computational cost across several settings. On ALFWorld, the complete training and distillation pipeline reaches 99.3% success in 12.7 H100 GPU-hours, compared with 86.6% in 17.3 GPU-hours for token-level RL of the same LLM. With compiled memory, our strongest Qwen2.5-1.5B LDM and distilled generative LLM achieve 100% success on all 134 unseen and 140 seen ALFWorld evaluation games.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Moves RL from **token-level generation to the decision plane**: a large decision model (LDM) selects among a compiled menu of executable actions in a single forward pass, removing action parsing and invalid outputs.
  - Shows theoretically that **marginalizing over alternative linguistic realizations of the same action** removes a zero-mean gradient component without changing the expected update, reducing policy-gradient variance.
  - Distills LDM policies back into generative LLMs: reaches **99.3% ALFWorld success in 12.7 H100 GPU-hours** (vs. 86.6% in 17.3 for token-level RL) and **100% on all 274 evaluation games** with compiled memory.

### 2. Reasoning or Pattern Exploitation? Mechanistic Insights from RL-Trained Language Models
- **Authors:** Aishik Nagar, Cheston Tan
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2610.39182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39182) (submitted 29 Oct 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- **Abstract:** Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly described as acquiring "reasoning" skills after reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. However, whether these gains reflect genuine reasoning or more superficial adaptations remains underexplored. In this work, we probe LLMs trained in a finite and exhaustively analyzable logical domain, namely Tic-Tac-Toe, and trace how internal representations evolve across reinforcement learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Quantitatively, RL yields high accuracy and robustness across prompt variations, suggesting the emergence of game reasoning. Mechanistic interpretability, however, paints a different picture: features extracted with sparse autoencoders (SAEs) reveal that models primarily adapt to rely more heavily on information already explicit in the prompt, such as whose turn it is, game progression and board occupancy. In comparison, high-level concepts like board symmetries and strategic forks remain weakly represented, raising concerns that reasoning benchmarks overestimate model abilities. This suggests that RL-driven "reasoning" may conflate task-specific adaptations to input information with reasoning ability. We provide, for the first time: (i) a systematic interpretability pipeline tracing representation dynamics across RL training in small-scale LLMs; (ii) an extension of SAE-based feature discovery to hypothesis-driven testing in a finite logical domain; and (iii) an interpretability-based demonstration that RL post-training neither adds nor removes concept information but reorganizes which features dominate the representation, with causal interventions locating the decision downstream of it, in the late-layer state.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A **systematic SAE-based interpretability pipeline** tracing representation dynamics across GRPO training in a finite, exhaustively analyzable domain (Tic-Tac-Toe).
  - Extends **SAE feature discovery to hypothesis-driven testing** of high-level concepts (board symmetries, forks).
  - Shows RL post-training **reorganizes which features dominate** rather than adding/removing concept information; causal interventions locate the decision in the late-layer state — evidence that RL "reasoning" can be pattern exploitation.

### 3. FraudBench: A Legal Evaluation of AI Deception
- **Authors:** Kevin Wei, Sumaya Nur Adan, Stephan Llerena, Mark Gitau, Jakob Merane, Anita Srinivasan, Colette Le Brannan, Keethan Kleiner, Alessandro Tacconelli, Michael Tiu, Case Thomason, Kyle Emili, Natasza Gadowska, Alex Mark, Eric Ren, Umang Bhatt, Jatinder Singh, Jacy Reese Anthis, Doni Bloomfield, Noam Kolt
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page (accepted to NeurIPS E&D 2026)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.12167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12167) (submitted 9 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computers and Society (cs.CY); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- **Abstract:** We introduce FraudBench, an evaluation of large language models' (LLMs) propensities to recommend, assist, or directly take actions that would constitute fraud under U.S. law. Our evaluation contains a novel dataset of 159 scenarios based on real-world U.S. court cases and validated by legal experts. We present each scenario to LLMs via three tasks at varying levels of autonomy: advising users, drafting messages for users to send, and acting directly on behalf of users (477 tasks total). In each scenario, FraudBench re-enacts the facts of a court case to give LLMs an opportunity, but no explicit instruction, to either misrepresent or conceal a material fact in a high-stakes commercial setting--e.g., whether to describe company-funded product testing as "independent" to customers (misrepresentation) or whether to hide unfavorable regulatory investigations from clients (concealment). We evaluate 20 LLMs across 25 experimental conditions (totaling >300,000 generations) and include a preregistered human baseline. We score a response as fraudulent when it engages in, assists with, or recommends misrepresentation or concealment. Key findings include: (1) across LLMs, fraud rates ranged from near 0% to over 40%; (2) LLM fraud rates increased with autonomy, with overall rates of 3.0% when advising, 14.7% when drafting, and 21.4% when acting directly, without applying pressure; (3) LLMs sometimes engaged in fraud despite recognizing legal risks; (4) LLMs more often engaged in fraud by concealment than by misrepresentation; and (5) prompt-based interventions substantially affected fraud rates, with a prompt to obey the law reducing fraud rates, while prompts to maximize profits and act agentically increased fraud rates.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A **legally grounded benchmark** of 159 real U.S. court-case scenarios (477 tasks) testing misrepresentation/concealment across three levels of LLM autonomy.
  - Large-scale evaluation of **20 LLMs in 25 conditions (>300k generations)** with a preregistered human baseline; fraud rates rose with autonomy (3.0% advising → 14.7% drafting → 21.4% acting).
  - Finds **prompting materially shifts fraud rates** (obey-the-law reduces; profit-maximizing/agentic increases) and that models can commit fraud despite recognizing legal risk.

### 4. Agent-Guided Process Verification for Mathematical Self-Correction
- **Authors:** Rui Zou, Mengqi Wei, Xin Zhao, Ji-Rong Wen
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.19122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19122) (submitted 14 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Large language models have made substantial progress in mathematical reasoning, yet in complex multi-step reasoning, an error at any step may propagate along an incorrect path, and the resulting solution may omit necessary branches. Existing natural-language reflection methods often struggle to locate critical errors precisely, while outcome verification typically checks only whether the available candidate answers satisfy the problem conditions and can therefore overlook necessary branches. To address these limitations, we propose Agent-Guided Process Verification (AGPV) for mathematical self-correction. AGPV decomposes the current solution into an ordered sequence of key claims, verifies them individually while invoking a shared mathematical toolbox when needed, and stops upon detecting the first error. A summarization agent converts the failure evidence into a diagnosis that guides a correction agent to re-derive the subsequent solution from that point; AGPV then adapts the next verification iteration to the revised solution. AGPV also verifies solution completeness. We further prove that AGPV guarantees solution correctness and completeness when key claims provide sufficient coverage and local verification is reliable, provided that all key claims pass verification. The implementation compresses long working contexts and reuses successful tool results within each problem. We evaluate AGPV on five challenging mathematical reasoning datasets with four models from DeepSeek, Qwen, and GPT. AGPV achieves an average accuracy of 63.8%, outperforming the strongest among the representative baselines evaluated (50.3%) by 13.5 percentage points, with higher repair coverage than the programmatic-verification baselines.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **AGPV** verifies mathematical solutions step-by-step as ordered key claims, invoking a shared toolbox and stopping at the first error, then using a summarization agent's diagnosis to guide a correction agent.
  - Verifies **solution completeness**, not just correctness, with a **formal guarantee** under sufficient key-claim coverage and reliable local verification.
  - Achieves **63.8% average accuracy (+13.5 pp over strongest baseline)** across five datasets and four model families (DeepSeek, Qwen, GPT).

### 5. ShopMind: A Mental World Model for Personalized and Realistic E-commerce User Simulation
- **Authors:** Peixuan Hou, Bin Chen, Li He, Jian Xu, Xiuli Ma, Guojie Song
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.11897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11897) (submitted 9 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- **Abstract:** Large language models are widely used in e-commerce user simulation, providing a foundation for understanding users and predicting human decisions. However, existing simulators still struggle to achieve both personalization and behavioral realism. Most methods predict the next action directly from interaction histories and the current context, without accounting for the evolving mental world that underlies user behavior. Theory of Mind suggests that behavior is driven by mental states, including beliefs, uncertainties, and intentions. Users may interpret the same information in distinct ways, leading to divergent behaviors, while an individual's mental state evolves through interaction and shapes subsequent actions. Ignoring this process prevents simulators from faithfully capturing individual differences and realistic behavior patterns, and undermines its effectiveness in downstream applications. To address this, we propose ShopMind, a Mental World Model for e-commerce user simulation that captures how consumers interpret shopping information and how their mental states evolve, better reflecting individual users and real-world behavior patterns. We further use behavior-mental state pairs synthesized by ShopMind to train a Mental State Reconstruction (MSR) model, which infers consumers' latent mental states from observable behavior. Experiments show that ShopMind improves personalization by 18.0% and behavioral realism by 19.6% over the strongest alternatives. Moreover, MSR improves performance on consumption value measurement and CTR prediction, showing the utility of ShopMind for understanding and predicting real user behavior.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A **Mental World Model** for e-commerce user simulation based on Theory of Mind: simulates how users interpret shopping information and how beliefs/uncertainties/intentions evolve through interaction.
  - Introduces **Mental State Reconstruction (MSR)** trained on simulated behavior–mental-state pairs to infer latent mental states from observable behavior.
  - Reports **+18.0% personalization and +19.6% behavioral realism** over strongest alternatives, with downstream gains in consumption-value measurement and **CTR prediction**.

### 6. RedMall: Generative Recommendation for Large-Scale E-commerce
- **Authors:** Kunyang Zhou, Xin Jia, Yi Yang, Yongjun Yang, Manjie Xu, Honglei Zhang, Yan Zhu, Kai Zuo, Xu Tang, Zheng Wang
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page (e-commerce advertising/recommendation deployment; work in progress)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.08519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08519) (submitted 26 Oct 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- **Abstract:** Generative recommendation has shown considerable potential in industrial recommendation systems. However, the performance of existing generative methods is limited by several challenges. 1) Semantic IDs (SIDs) are treated as one-dimensional sequences, overlooking the hierarchical dependencies among different layers. 2) Next-token prediction used is unable to explicitly optimize session-wise recommendation. 3) Although reinforcement learning has been applied to align generated recommendations with user preferences, few existing methods explicitly optimize the ranking of positive items in session-wise recommendation. To address the above issues, we propose RedMall, a generative recommender designed for large-scale e-commerce. Firstly, RedMall constructs hierarchical SIDs through residual quantization over multimodal representations. Secondly, RedMall introduces a lightweight SID encoder to explicitly model layer-wise dependencies in SIDs. Moreover, RedMall incorporates sparse mixture-of-experts, dual gated attention, and multi-target prediction to scale model size, enhance user interest representation, and optimize session-wise recommendation. We further design an exposure-aware loss that treats exposed but unclicked items as negative feedback. To better align user preferences and list-wise ranking objectives, we combine Group Relative Policy Optimization with an auxiliary LambdaRank loss, using both real user feedback and item ranking to guide optimization. Experiments on four public datasets—Amazon Beauty, Amazon Toys, Amazon Food, and MovieLens-1M---show that RedMall outperforms the evaluated baselines across all reported Recall and NDCG metrics. Online A/B tests show improvements of 2.24% in advertiser value (ADVV) and 0.59% in click-through rate (CTR) for e-commerce advertising, as well as gains of 1.08% in GMV and 0.95% in daily active buyers (DAB) for e-commerce recommendations.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Hierarchical SIDs via residual quantization** over multimodal representations plus a lightweight SID encoder that explicitly models layer-wise dependencies (vs. treating SIDs as flat sequences).
  - Sparse MoE, dual-gated attention, and multi-target prediction for **session-wise optimization**, with an **exposure-aware loss** treating exposed-but-unclicked items as negatives.
  - Combines **GRPO with an auxiliary LambdaRank loss** for list-wise ranking alignment; online A/B: **+2.24% ADVV, +0.59% CTR** (ads) and **+1.08% GMV, +0.95% DAB** (recommendation).

### 7. CLEAR: Legible and Steerable Recommendation via Grounded Natural-Language Interests
- **Authors:** Qi Guo, Reza Mirghaderi, Iordanis Fostiropoulos, David Fan, Yuchen Liu, Shiliang Tang, et al. (54 authors)
- **Institution/Company:** Meta (stated in abstract; deployed in production)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.19110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19110) (submitted 14 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- **Abstract:** Modern recommenders keep learned preferences in representations people cannot inspect or change. We present CLEAR (Controllable and Legible End-to-end Architecture for Recommendation), an end-to-end system deployed at Meta and built around an editable natural-language interest layer. CLEAR makes a ranked subset of inferred interests readable and stores accepted additions and suppressions as authoritative preference state. Behavioral grounding maps each inferred interest back to supporting events in the user's own history. The resulting interest object is also a serving address: its label and profile-conditioned rewrites drive lexical retrieval, its supporting events seed behavioral retrieval, its projection queries an embedding index, and its stable identity supports ranking, attribution, and exposure control. A fresh user declaration may precede behavioral support, so it enters through the same serving identity and initially draws candidates from topical retrieval. Ranking makes those candidates competitive, while a delivery controller observes attributable exposure and replenishes underexposed interests. Direct user review found that users rated about 88.5% of displayed interests accurate. In an offline benchmark using a shared evaluation protocol, the Specialized 3B generator reached 0.3967 mean-user F1@10, compared with 0.3633 for Gemini 2.5 Pro and 0.1911 for the prior generator. For a newly declared interest, a user-randomized cumulative ablation measured same-day delivery losses of 8.7 percentage points for added-interest ranking terms, 11.1 for reserved top-slot insertion, and 13.7 for interest-conditioned candidate sourcing, conditional on the tested removal sequence. CLEAR components have operated in production for over one year; the system spans four surfaces on Instagram and Facebook, with an interest layer people can inspect, correct, and use to direct recommendations.
- **Key innovations:**
  - An **editable natural-language interest layer** deployed at Meta: users can inspect and correct inferred interests; accepted additions/suppressions become authoritative preference state, with **behavioral grounding** to supporting history events.
  - The interest object doubles as a **serving address** driving lexical, behavioral, and embedding retrieval plus ranking, attribution, and exposure control; a delivery controller replenishes underexposed interests.
  - Production system on **four Instagram/Facebook surfaces for over a year**; users rated ~88.5% of interests accurate, and a specialized 3B generator beat Gemini 2.5 Pro on mean-user F1@10 (0.3967 vs. 0.3633).

### 8. Learning to Rank Explanations for Recommendation with Language Models
- **Authors:** Ben Kabongo, Vincent Guigue
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.04775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04775) (submitted 3 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- **Abstract:** Recommender systems are ubiquitous on the Web, making understandable explanations essential for users. Although large language models (LLMs) increasingly generate natural-language justifications, evaluating their factual grounding and personalized relevance remains challenging. Statement-level ranking offers an alternative by selecting concise, review-derived explanations, enabling fine-grained and reproducible evaluation using established ranking metrics. However, existing statement ranking methods largely rely on collaborative representations, while semantic enhancements often fail to outperform simple user-history popularity baselines. Moreover, directly adapting language models for personalized statement ranking remains largely unexplored. In this work, we introduce LMStaR (Language Models for Statement Ranking), a fully text-based framework that formulates personalized explanation ranking as dense retrieval. A query encoder represents the user's interaction history and target-item metadata, while a statement encoder maps candidate explanations into a shared embedding space. Both encoders are jointly adapted through multi-positive contrastive learning to learn personalized relevance. Experiments on Amazon Beauty, Amazon Toys, and Yelp show that LMStaR achieves the highest NDCG@1 across all three domains, outperforming CLER by 1.06--8.25 points (+11.8--65.9%). It also leads top-five ranking on both Amazon datasets, although CLER remains stronger on Yelp. Task-specific adaptation improves all five evaluated backbones and transfers across domains without target-domain fine-tuning. Counterfactual analyses further reveal the importance of user-specific histories. These findings highlight the potential of task-adapted language models for personalized explanation ranking.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **LMStaR** frames personalized explanation ranking as **dense retrieval** with separate query (user history + item metadata) and statement encoders, adapted jointly via **multi-positive contrastive learning**.
  - Achieves highest **NDCG@1 on Amazon Beauty, Toys, and Yelp**, beating CLER by 1.06–8.25 points (+11.8–65.9%).
  - Task-specific adaptation improves all five backbones and **transfers across domains without target-domain fine-tuning**; counterfactual analyses confirm the value of user-specific histories.

### 9. Think Globally, Generate Personally: Multi-View Thinking with Adaptive Generation for Generative Recommendation
- **Authors:** Yuxuan Hu, Yingyi Zhang, Wenlin Zhang, Chao Zhang, Haotian Nan, Ziwei Liu, Jiancheng Dong, Tongzhou Wu, Beibei Kong, Lei Cheng, Chengxiang Zhuo, Zang Li, Wanyu Wang, Xiangyu Zhao
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.02463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02463) (submitted 2 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Semantic-ID-based (SID) generative recommendation (GR) represents each item as a sequence of discrete tokens and predicts the next item through autoregressive SID generation. However, existing methods face two challenges. First, insufficient shared context modeling for user sequences: full-history and recent behaviors provide overlapping yet complementary evidence, but modeling them within a single sequence may dilute current cues, whereas processing them through separate streams may fail to coordinate them. Second, insufficient personalized adaptation in progressive SID decisions: although decoder states evolve with generated prefixes, existing models typically use a shared output-side transformation across recommendation instances, without explicitly selecting a transformation suited to the current user need. To address these challenges, we propose Multi-View Thinking with Adaptive Generation (MV-Thinker), a framework that Thinks Globally and Generates Personally. First, Multi-View Temporal Thinking uses view-specific thinking queries and readers over overlapping full-history and recent-history views, coordinating both sources into complementary user-conditioned contexts before SID generation. Second, Hierarchical Residual Adaptation organizes lightweight adapters into nested residual paths before a shared vocabulary projection, adaptively transforming context-bearing decoder states for SID decisions. Extensive experiments on four public datasets demonstrate that MV-Thinker consistently outperforms strong discriminative and generative recommendation baselines across multiple ranking metrics. The implementation code and datasets are available on: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/MV-Thinker
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Multi-View Temporal Thinking**: view-specific thinking queries/readers coordinate overlapping full-history and recent-history views into complementary user-conditioned contexts.
  - **Hierarchical Residual Adaptation**: nested residual adapter paths before a shared vocabulary projection adaptively transform decoder states per recommendation instance.
  - Consistently beats strong discriminative and generative baselines on four public datasets across multiple ranking metrics.

### 10. RACE: Ranking-Uncertainty Aware Capacity-Adaptive Customization for Efficient Sequential Recommendation
- **Authors:** Tianqi Liu, Kaipu Sun, Kairui Fu, Wenkai Wang, Sihao Liu, Hui Zang, Ran Chen, Tao Wang, Shengyu Zhang
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page (accepted by WSDM 2027 Main Conference)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.18244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18244) (submitted 13 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Efficient sequential recommendation has emerged as a promising paradigm for providing real-time personalized service. To deploy models under resource-constrained environment, model compression becomes a necessity, yet existing methods typically apply uniform compression ratios, overlooking the heterogeneity in user intent complexity. This "one-size-fits-all" approach leads to a fundamental Capacity-Complexity Mismatch, where computational resources are either wasted on simple instances or insufficient for complex ones. To address this, we propose RACE (Ranking-uncertainty Aware Capacity-Adaptive Customization for Efficient Sequential Recommendation), a framework that dynamically adapts both model structures and input sequences with uncertainty-aware compression ratios. To ensure capacity-adaptive inference, RACE captures the model's intrinsic uncertainty of its predictions and transforms it into a proactive resource allocator. Specifically, we derive a list-wise ranking-uncertainty metric from the distributional stability of top-$K$ predictions, which serves as a measurable proxy for instance complexity. A lightweight predictor is then trained via self-supervision to anticipate this uncertainty from early-layer features. Guided by this signal, RACE performs instance-specific slimming in two dimensions: a hypernetwork-style channel sensitivity extractor identifies parameter redundancy, while importance-aware progressive sequence filtering mitigates data redundancy. Extensive experiments on four popular datasets demonstrate that RACE outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Diagnoses the **Capacity-Complexity Mismatch** from uniform compression and proposes **RACE** with uncertainty-aware, instance-specific compression ratios.
  - Derives a **list-wise ranking-uncertainty metric** from top-$K$ prediction stability, with a self-supervised early-layer predictor as a proactive resource allocator.
  - Slims two dimensions — **hypernetwork-style channel sensitivity** (parameters) and **importance-aware progressive sequence filtering** (data) — outperforming SOTA on accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability.

### 11. PRISM: Personalized Item Representations in User History
- **Authors:** Renzhi Wu, Zikun Cui, Junjie Yang, Li Sheng, Yongjun Xie, Jerry Fu, Nipun Mathur, Sri Reddy, Hong Li
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page (evaluated on "Surface 1," a billion-scale recommendation surface)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2610.39175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39175) (submitted 29 Oct 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Sequential recommenders typically construct history inputs from item identifiers or content features using an embedding map shared across users. The same item therefore yields the same initial embedding representation, even when users have different relationships to the item. We propose personalized item representations in user history, where each item's embedding expresses its relationship to the user. Personalization becomes a property of the history inputs themselves, supplying richer evidence for later sequence modeling. We instantiate this idea with PRISM, which reuses upstream ranking computation to generate personalized item embeddings. An autoencoder jointly compresses all layer states from a user tower, a user–item tower, and same-request candidate mixing into one embedding per scored pair for downstream replay. Offline evaluation on Surface 1, a major recommendation surface at billion scale, reports an average downstream normalized entropy (NE) change of $-0.15\%$, compared with $-0.07\%$ for VISTA at similar history lengths. We further identify an empirical sequence-length scaling law: over history lengths of 26--240 events, NE gain follows a fitted power law with exponent 0.468, increasing from 0.06\% to 0.17\%. Therefore, personalized item representations in user history provide a new scaling dimension for improving recommendation quality.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Makes **personalization a property of history inputs**: each item's embedding in the user history expresses its relationship to that user, rather than using a user-shared embedding map.
  - **PRISM** reuses upstream ranking computation — an autoencoder compresses user-tower, user–item-tower, and same-request candidate-mixing layer states into one embedding per scored pair.
  - Reports a **sequence-length NE scaling law** (power-law exponent 0.468 over 26–240 events), establishing personalized representations as a new scaling dimension.

## Generative Recommendation & Semantic IDs

### 12. UAGR: Utility-Aware Generative Recommendation
- **Authors:** Yang Hu, Duo Wang, Ning Li, Kang Li, Junda She, Chengcheng Guo, Kuo Cai, Bin Liu, Youhua Liu, Xiaoshuang Chen
- **Institution/Company:** Kuaishou (stated in abstract; fully deployed)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.02981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02981) (submitted 2 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- **Abstract:** Generative recommendation has been widely adopted in large-scale industrial recommendation systems, where multimodal item embeddings are quantized into offline semantic identifiers (SIDs) and items are retrieved by autoregressively generating their SIDs. However, conventional SID construction often underrepresents item utility reflected in post-exposure behaviors such as shares, long views, and video completion, making semantically similar items with different utility insufficiently distinguishable while struggling to accommodate rapidly changing utility signals. Moreover, generation likelihood may be misaligned with downstream utility, causing high-utility candidates to be prematurely pruned during beam search before post-generation reranking or training-time reward alignment can effectively compensate. To address these limitations, we propose UAGR, which aligns both SID construction and decoding with downstream multi-objective utility. Our Utility-Enriched Semantic Codes preserve the original semantic information while incorporating item-level utility without increasing SID length or vocabulary size, supporting incremental utility updates with no additional tokenization or sequence-decoding cost. Meanwhile, our Utility-Guided Decoding estimates candidate utility by combining predictions of multiple post-exposure outcomes and incorporates these estimates into beam scoring, reducing the risk of prematurely pruning high-utility paths while enabling flexible utility control without interfering with generative backbone training. Extensive offline experiments and online A/B tests under real-world traffic demonstrate the effectiveness and deployability of UAGR, which has been fully deployed on Kuaishou and increases video watch time by 0.206\% online.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Utility-Enriched Semantic Codes** inject post-exposure utility signals (shares, long views, completion) into SIDs without increasing SID length/vocabulary, with **incremental utility updates** at no extra decoding cost.
  - **Utility-Guided Decoding** folds multi-outcome utility estimates into beam scoring to prevent premature pruning of high-utility paths, with flexible control decoupled from backbone training.
  - Fully deployed on **Kuaishou**, with online A/B showing **+0.206% video watch time**.

### 13. HiGenTrans: Hierarchical LLM Knowledge Transfer for Generative Recommendation
- **Authors:** Zhuohang Jiang, Yuxin Chen, Haohao Qu, ZeFei luo, Yunlong Wang, Xu Yuan, Haojie Ding, Boheng Zhang, Fan Yang, Hongyang Wang, Jun Wang, Zhongbo Sun, Wenqi Fan, Li Qing, Wenwu Ou, Peng Jiang
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page (industrial setting; authors include W. Ou of Alibaba per public profiles)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.01129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01129) (submitted 31 Oct 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Generative recommendation promises to simplify conventional multi-stage recommender systems by generating item Semantic IDs (SIDs) autoregressively. However, two obstacles limit its effectiveness in industrial settings: interaction-only supervision underuses the semantic knowledge encoded in pretrained language models, while beam-search likelihood ranks token sequences rather than directly estimating item relevance. To address these limitations, we propose HiGenTrans, a three-level framework that introduces LLM knowledge at the input, representation, and decision levels. ### 34. When Cooperation Must Change: Semantic Strategy Unlearning in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract page

## Cross-cutting observations

- **Generative recommendation dominates IR/ads.** A large share of recent work reframes retrieval as autoregressive generation of semantic IDs, with innovation concentrated in (a) SID construction (hierarchical, tree-based, utility-aware, variable-length), (b) aligning decoding/beam search with downstream value (utility-guided, value-guided, KL-weighted multi-objective distillation), and (c) production serving (RecoGEM, CLEAR, GR4AD-X). Online A/B validation — revenue, eCPM, CTR, watch time, GMV — is now standard in these papers.
- **"Latent/efficient reasoning" migrates into recommendation.** Multiple works adapt on-policy distillation, self-distillation, and latent reasoning to GR (BRIDGE, KL-MOPD, SA-OPSD-adjacent work, HiGenTrans), aiming to transfer teacher knowledge at visited states without changing the generative backbone.
- **Interpretability and auditability cut across domains.** From SAE probes showing RL "reasoning" may be pattern exploitation (2610.39182), to MoTIF's auditable temporal experts and CLEAR's editable interest layer, there is a push toward models whose decisions users/operators can inspect and steer.
- **Agentic and game environments are scaling up.** Gamer, GTAGym, Script2Play, and Balatro reflect a trend toward long-horizon agents trained in generated/simulated environments with automatic rewards, while DUOS and CARVE advance the theory of general-sum and cooperative MARL (opponent shaping without opponent access; unlearning obsolete conventions).
- **RL is moving off the token level.** RLiD (decision-plane RL), DICE (decision-native recommendation), and Gamer (sparse-thinking + multi-turn RL) all reduce the variance/cost of token-level generation by acting directly in compact decision or action spaces.
