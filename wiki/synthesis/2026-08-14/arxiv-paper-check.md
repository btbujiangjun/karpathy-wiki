## 🔥 Highlights

### CTR, Recommendation, Advertising & IR

| Paper | Authors | Key Contribution |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| **MM-slotgate: Attribute-Conditioned Multimodal Slot Factorization for Controllable Fashion Retrieval** (2608.12570) | Najmeh Forouzandehmehr, Topojoy Biswas, Evren Korpeoglu, Kannan Achan (Amazon) | **Controllable attribute retrieval in fashion without attribute-labeled training — factorizes embeddings into named, gateable slots.** A multimodal slot encoder decomposes Fashion-CLIP text/image embeddings into four named attribute slots (category, color, pattern, demographic); each slot learns its own text-image gate, so visual attributes lean on image features while taxonomy-oriented ones lean on text. Retrieval score = combined slot-similarity + slot-logit signal. Evaluated on the H&M dataset — a direct industrial e-commerce/Rec datapoint: attribute-level controllability is achieved from jointly embedded representations rather than explicit label supervision. |
| **GEM: A Generative Embedding Model Bridging Reasoning and Retrieval** (2608.13200; cross-list cs.CL) | Zhili Shen, Craig Macdonald (University of Glasgow) | **One model does both — reasons over the query first, then emits an embedding token encoding the enriched context.** Unifies generative reasoning with embedding-based retrieval: the model reasons over the query before appending a special embedding token whose hidden state is trained for retrieval. Improves reasoning-intensive and instruction-following IR; beats its non-reasoning variant and matches much larger baselines; supports test-time compute scaling via prompting. Points at the near-term convergence of "retriever as reasoner" — the same weights that generate the answer also rank the candidates. |
| **TTT-Embed: Test-Time Optimization of Query Embeddings with Ranking Aware Reward Maximization** (2608.12569) | Tianyu Chen, Jiaxing Wu | **Distills ranking feedback into the embedding at test time — no weight access, no labels, no index change.** Optimizes a lightweight learned vector in the frozen model's output-embedding space using only scalar ranking scores (from a reranker or LLM judge) over the retriever's own candidate documents. A single `scope` parameter controls how rewards are reused (global/task/query), trading reusability against specificity under a fixed reward budget. A practical "train-the-query, not the retriever" recipe for domains where labeled retrieval data is scarce but a judge is available. |
| **When Should Multi-Round RAG Stop? Structured Stopping Judgments and Retrieval Reduction in Search-R1** (2608.13237) | Weimeng Luo | **First explicit stop-conditioning for reasoning-search agents — and treats "when to stop" as a sequential decision, not a classification.** Trains a Qwen3.5-2B judge on 3,009 states from 900 disjoint HotpotQA questions, adapting S2G-RAG's structured sufficiency-and-gap judgment to a frozen Search-R1 pipeline (judge checkpoint + stopping threshold chosen on grouped validation). Key insight: the *first* STOP on a trajectory determines policy, so stopping must be modeled as sequential selection. Result: **retrieval calls −77 (−3.70%)** vs Native Search-R1 with Official Exact Match down only **0.625 pp**. |
| **RAGSieve: Self-Referenced Local Contrast for Knowledge-Poison Detection in RAG** (2608.13010; cross-list cs.CL) | Xinlong Xu, Yoshua Y. Li | **Detects knowledge-poisoning attacks on RAG by contrasting documents against their *own* neighborhood — no external trustworthy corpus needed.** Two detectors: RAGSieve-Query (RSQ) scores the top-5 retrieved candidates against ranks 6–20 of the same retrieval to expose answer-anchor concentration and carrier transitions; RAGSieve-Graph (RSG) contrasts each document's semantically-similar-but-lexically-distinct neighbors against a corpus-local baseline to expose coordinated pre-query density. Across 3 QA datasets × 6 poisoning constructions: **95.2% AUROC**, **82.2% of poison caught** at only 5% clean-document removal. Self-referenced baselines make the defense portable to unseen corpora. |
| **EviReform: Evidence-Guided Query Reformulation for Multi-Hop Graph Retrieval** (2608.13006; cross-list cs.CL) | Xinlong Xu, Yoshua Y. Li | **Multi-hop retrieval that treats query reformulation and evidence aggregation as separate, propagation-aware steps.** From the same group as RAGSieve; decouples "revise the retrieval request" from "aggregate the evidence": retrieved source passages generate residual queries for the still-unresolved need, original and residual retrieval signals are normalized separately and combined, then propagated between propositions that share entities. On 2WikiMultiHopQA / HotpotQA / MuSiQue: exceeds the strongest baseline by up to **+5.59 Recall@5** and **+4.50 F1**. |
| **MASCOT: Model-Aware Submodular Coverage for Composite-Attribute Text-to-Image Retrieval** (2608.12532) | Aaryan Sharma, Vishak Prasad C, Virendra Singh, Ganesh Ramakrishnan | **Fixes a blind spot in diversity-promoting image retrieval — reframes multi-attribute diversity as resource allocation, not manifold repulsion.** Diagnoses that MS-DPP (max-min post-processing) degrades early-rank recall on tasks where diversity *decreases* over discrete metadata; MASCOT instead casts the composite-attribute problem as model-aware submodular coverage. A principled rethinking of "diverse top-K" for multimodal retrieval that avoids the recall/diversity trade-off trap of repulsion-based methods. |

### AI, Agents & Evaluation

| Paper | Authors | Key Contribution |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| **PAC-Bayes-regularized Meta-LoRA for Cross-Domain LLM Personalization** (2608.12389) | Xuefei Wang, Jun Han, Zixuan Wang, Qingkai Zeng, Xiao Wang, Ruijie Wang, Jianxin Li | **Personalization under sparse user evidence, made statistically honest.** Meta-learned LoRA initialization serves as both adaptation start and prior center; the update magnitude is scaled by support-set size and predictive uncertainty, limiting overfitting when evidence is scarce and avoiding negative transfer that plagues history-transfer methods. A PAC-Bayes view of "how much should I adapt my LoRA given how little I know about this user" — directly relevant to the wiki's LLM-personalization/rec thread. |
| **AQuA: Recursively Self-Improving Quantitative Trading Research Agents** (2608.12841) | Jiacheng Guo, Suozhi Huang, Yunlong Gao, Zihao Li, Jian Ge, Xu Kuang, Mengdi Wang | **Two closed-loop research agents that each self-improve without leaking unfixable artifacts into each other.** Separates symbolic factor discovery from trainable model development into two independent LM-driven loops in sealed sandboxes (data splits/features/labels/evaluator frozen); the agent may only act through constrained factor expressions or config diffs. This artifact-confinement design is a concrete methodology datapoint for the wiki's finance-alpha thread (cf. [wq101-alpha-daily](../../2026-08-13/wq101-alpha-daily.md) and AgonAlpha 08-13) — recursive self-improvement bounded so that bad factors can't corrupt the model loop. |
| **OmniScientist: An Omni-Modal Omni-Discipline AI Scientist** (2608.13558) | Bobo Li, Hao Fei, Tianjie Ju, Mong-Li Lee, Wynne Hsu | **A research agent that starts from raw heterogeneous evidence — not pre-digested text.** Perception layer ingests raw multi-modal materials, then three autonomous agents (ideation, experiment, writeup) run a deterministic pipeline. Distinctive: idea/rigour/claim checks are executed *in code* — novelty screening, statistical validity, execution provenance, numerical traceability — rather than asserted by the model. One of the most complete "AI scientist" architectures of the batch (see also VALG, ARAC below). |
| **Beyond Final Scores: A Systematic Evaluation of Agents for Long-Horizon AI R&D** (2608.13417) | Yiwei Li, + 12 authors (last three: Fei Sun, Xunliang Cai, Jingang Wang) | **Stops trusting final scores — characterizes agent behavior *inside* long-horizon runs.** 7 frontier models × 36 long-horizon R&D tasks; rule-based metrics measure Solution Framing, Execution, and Feedback Control during a run, plus controlled comparisons of experience reuse within and across tasks. Conclusion: current agents behave like **engineering optimizers rather than autonomous researchers** — a measurement-methodology result that directly contextualizes OmniScientist/VALG/ARAC claims. |
| **QuoteBench: How Matched Scores Can Hide Command-Path Failures** (2608.13547) | Shangao Li, Yao Zhang, Volker Tresp, Yuanyuan Yang | **Raw-generation scores are saturated and misleading — success collapses when the reply is actually executed.** 56 one-shot tasks from 14 incident-derived families, crossing generation contract with execution transport around one deliberately unescaped parser. Replaying the *same* reply through the added parser drops success **55.4–73.2 pp** across eight configs; disclosure recovers **30.4–60.7 pp** on six. Raw generation is near-ceiling at the frontier, but boundary adaptation cleanly separates models — matched "end-to-end" scores were hiding systematic command-path failures. |
| **Rules or Character? Scaling Laws for AI Safety Design** (2608.13345; *AIES 2026*) | Satoshi Takahashi, Nobuji Kouno, Masaaki Komatsu, Ryuji Hamamoto | **Formalizes the RLHF-vs-output-filter resource split as a scaling law.** Safety design = allocation α ∈ [0,1] between character shaping (training-time) and rule enforcement (inference-time), with scale-dependent filter degradation, common-mode failures, and character fragility (shaped behavior degrading under novel conditions). Closed-form expected harm under a multiplicative Pareto model + CVaR Monte Carlo. Across optimistic/moderate/pessimistic scenarios the optimal α* is interior or rules-only and shifts weakly toward character shaping as deployment scale grows (Δα* = +0.01…+0.21); the dominant parameter is the **baseline character fragility rate** (Δα* = 0.50 across its range), not tail severity, filter quality, or failure correlation. |
| **StateBridge: Training-free Hidden-state Alignment for Latent Communication in LLM Multi-Agent Systems** (2608.13317; *COLM 2026*) | Yanwen Peng, Delvin Ce Zhang, Xi Wang, Nikolaos Aletras | **Latent communication without training a projector — agents transmit aligned hidden states, not tokens.** Text's discrete bottleneck loses information; existing latent methods either inject working memory layer-by-layer or require trained projectors. StateBridge aligns the sender's final-layer hidden states to the receiver's input space via a **closed-form orthogonal transformation**, with lightweight norm calibration and vocabulary anchoring for compatibility with the pretrained input distribution; aligned states are prepended as a continuous prefix. Best/tied-best on **22 of 26 model-task pairs** across math reasoning, code generation, and QA with 4 models from 2 families. |
| **SkillShapley: Boundary-Adaptive Shapley Valuation for Skill Step Attribution in LLM Agents** (2608.13173) | Chang Liu, Yuqi Zhang, Yiman Zhong, Boyi Liu, Hengjun Wang, Shuyue Wei | **First step-level attribution for agent skills — which instruction in a skill actually carries the performance?** Models skill-step contribution as Shapley-value estimation, exploiting two empirical regularities: discretized benchmark rewards create sharp performance cliffs, and step interactions are mostly additive rather than synergistic. Two phases: identify informative coalitional regions, then adaptively sample new coalitions that yield reusable marginal evidence. On SkillsBench, efficiently and accurately isolates high- vs low-value skill steps — actionable guidance for skill authoring (prune, rewrite, reorder). |
| **VALG: An Agentic System for ML Theory Research** (2608.13060) | Dechen Zhang, Xuan Tang, Xinxiang Yin, Xingwu Chen, Jian Qian, Difan Zou | **Automating proof work, not just experiments — and being explicit about *what kind* of result it found.** Combines multi-level Verification, Adaptive formulation of Learning-theory problems, and Graph-structured proof development. Within each source-relative theorem branch it fixes the mathematical specification, checks theorem-level composition over a typed proof-dependency graph, and builds/reviews local proofs in dependency order; on failure it classifies the obstruction (derivation vs proof structure vs formulation) and routes accordingly, with formulation-level failures spawning explicitly-related variants/relaxations. On 9 subproblems from 5 COLT 2026 open problems: 2 internally-finalized theorem candidates matching their briefs, 7 restricted/special-case/conditional results — all kept mathematically distinct. Open source. |
| **DiG-bench: Discovery in Games** (2608.12593) | Ruairidh M. Battleday, Kai Sandbrink, Jimi Cullen-Drohan, Zihan Yan, Timothy Muller, Clare Maguire, Ales Kubicek, Fraser Greenlee-Scott, Sukrit Sumant, Tri Dao, Jürgen Schmidhuber, Michal Valko, Joshua Tenenbaum, Thomas L. Griffiths, Zeb Kurth-Nelson, James C.R. Whittington | **A benchmark for *discovery* — learning hidden rules by experimentation when the objective itself is unknown.** 70 independent games, each encoded as a short string with unique transformation rules that must be discovered through interaction; win conditions are also unknown. Seven difficulty tiers: the lowest is routinely solvable by multiple models, the highest challenges the best agentic harnesses. All 70 solved by at least one human on first attempt; **21 released publicly, 49 held private** for secure evaluation. A who's-who of author list (Schmidhuber, Tenenbaum, Griffiths, Tri Dao, Kurth-Nelson) targeting the "scientific discovery from interaction" gap in agentic benchmarks. |
| **Research Assistant: AstraZeneca's Agentic System for R&D** (2608.12395) | Piotr Grabowski, Mohamed Alameen, Jorge Bretones, Sabina Cardell, Miguel Carmona, Gavin Edwards, Ben Grainger, Sameh Hassan, Erik Jansson, Artur Kuziakhmetov, Albert Maristany, Hebatallah Mohamed, Andriy Nikolov, Sebastian Nilsson, Mark O'Donoghue, James Pacileo, Ashiq Sultan, Alex Voegele, Michael Ughetto | **A rare public technical note on an enterprise-scale R&D agent — chat interface over 6+ heterogeneous biomedical data classes.** AstraZeneca's internal system unifies evidence from scientific literature, knowledge graphs, chemistry, clinical trials, safety resources, expression data, and internal experimental systems; dual-mode (fast direct QA + multi-step complex research), responses grounded in retrieved evidence and linked back to source. Documented architecture, product design choices, and lessons from production deployment — a first-class enterprise-agent datapoint (cf. RecSys Factory 08-13) for the wiki's agentic-systems thread. |
| **ARAC: Benchmarking Auto-Research's Alignment and Completeness on End-to-End Research** (2608.12788) | Jiale Cui, Yueyao Yuan, Kaixi Zhong, Xiaogang Xu, Jiafei Wu, Zhe Liu | **Stops scoring final answers — scores whether the research *process* mimics rigorous human methodology.** ARAC-Bench: a Researcher-Mimicking evaluation framework with two components — the Academic Cognition Skills system (first to convert implicit reviewer expertise into stage-calibrated, quantifiable rubrics) and a three-stage diagnostic protocol decomposing research into Proposal / Experiment / Synthesis. Across 11 SOTA frameworks the best alignment score is only **67.9/100**; validation against Ph.D. candidate rankings shows **ρ = 0.8141**. Positions the rubric as both diagnostic tool and reward signal for training autonomous research systems. |

## 🔑 Key Trends (this batch)

1. **The "AI researcher" wave now has infrastructure: process-measuring benchmarks.** OmniScientist (code-executed idea/rigour checks), VALG (typed proof-dependency graphs over COLT open problems), and ARAC (stage-calibrated researcher-mimicking rubrics, ρ=0.81 vs PhD rankings) all attack the *process*; "Beyond Final Scores" supplies the cautionary measurement result — frontier agents currently optimize like engineering optimizers, not autonomous researchers. The evaluation layer is professionalizing faster than the agents.
2. **RAG security and retrieval efficiency matured into self-referenced, training-free solutions.** RAGSieve detects knowledge-poisoning with no external trustworthy corpus (95.2% AUROC at 5% removal); TTT-Embed adapts queries with ranking rewards and no weight/index access; the Search-R1 stopping judge cuts 3.70% of retrieval calls for −0.625 pp EM. The theme: *don't retrain the retriever — monitor it, gate it, and reformulate queries*.
3. **Latent/continuous communication is becoming train-free.** StateBridge transmits aligned hidden states between agents via a closed-form orthogonal transform (22/26 best/tied-best, COLM 2026) — after a week of text-bottleneck critiques, the fix is aligning pretrained spaces instead of learning new projectors.
4. **E-commerce/Rec keeps moving toward controllable, attribute-explicit representations.** MM-slotgate factorizes Fashion-CLIP embeddings into named, individually-gated attribute slots for controllable fashion retrieval — a clean industrial counterpart to this window's generative-recs fairness work in arxiv-ai-search (FSGR, DrIG), and PAC-Bayes Meta-LoRA gives personalization a principled uncertainty-scaled adaptation rule.
5. **Safety and alignment are being modeled as *resource allocations* with scale laws.** Rules-or-Character formalizes the RLHF-vs-filter split as an allocation α with the character-fragility rate dominating the optimum (Δα* = 0.50), and AQuA bounds recursive self-improvement by artifact-confinement in sealed sandboxes — both treat safety as an allocation/architecture problem rather than a training trick.
