## ① CTR, Ads & Auction Design (3)

### EvoSkillRec: Skill-Genome Evolution for Recommender Architecture Discovery
- **Authors**: Xiaopeng Li, Kuo Cai, Bo Chen, Wenlin Zhang, Mengyang Ma, Yingyi Zhang, Zichuan Fu, Yu Yang, Qidong Liu, Yiyu Wang, Ruiming Tang, **Wenwu Ou**, Jiang Wu, Zhanbo Xu, **Xiangyu Zhao**
- **arXiv**: [2609.34552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34552) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Recommender progress is framed as encoding **task-specific inductive biases through architecture** — and those biases are normally human-designed or searched in a fixed operator space. **EvoSkillRec** decomposes recommenders into atomic executable **skills** typed as a **skill genome** (input–output types + semantic annotations + implementation code), then evolves models across **two coupled spaces**: a *constrained skill-space* that mutates/recombines/specializes/reuses validated skills, and an *open-ended code-space* where LLM planners and synthesizers invent new skill modules from prior evolution traces. An **autoresearch controller** evaluates candidates, diagnoses failures, retrieves relevant skills, promotes validated innovations into the library, and **adaptively splits the proposal budget between the two spaces**. Validated on **CTR prediction**, multi-task learning, multi-domain learning, and — most relevant to production — **resource-constrained co-optimization of predictive quality and model FLOPs utilization in generative ranking models**.
- **Why it matters**: The window's only paper whose *primary* experimental target is CTR prediction, and the direct descendant of the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check|AgentX-Model]] thread (Wenwu Ou + Xiangyu Zhao author overlap → **Kuaishou lineage, author-inferred, tentative**). The architectural claim is sharp: LLM-driven code evolution **fails** when unconstrained (invalid architectures, underused design knowledge, no reuse of successful innovations) — the promotion-and-reuse loop is the contribution, not the LLM. The FLOPs-constrained co-optimization target is the honest production framing: the operative constraint in industrial ranking is accuracy *per unit of serving cost*, not accuracy.

### Privacy-Friendly Cohort Determination: Sealed, CSP-Independent In-Browser ML Inference of Professional Segments for Identity-Less Advertising
- **Authors**: Om Shankar Tiwari, Navnit Shukla, Guanyu Wang, Akshay Jain
- **arXiv**: [2609.36153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36153) — cs.CR
- **Key contribution**: B2B ad targeting keys on employer size / industry / function / seniority, historically obtained by **cross-site identity matching** — now degraded by Safari/Firefox third-party-cookie blocking, **Google's 2025 retirement of the Privacy Sandbox cohort APIs**, and reverse-IP firmographics decaying under remote work. **SIF (Sealed Inference Frame)** infers coarse professional cohorts **on-device** and emits only a **locally differentially private, taxonomy-coded label** into the OpenRTB bid stream, with no cross-site identifier. The web-platform property it makes precise: a navigated cross-origin iframe is the only way third-party code obtains a policy it controls — so inference runs in **WebAssembly even where the publisher's CSP forbids it**, and a nested worker served `default-src 'none'` gives the model no network; even a malicious model leaks **at most ~5 bits per site per week**. Labels pass through a memoised **k-ary randomised response** keyed to the publisher's first-party identifier (ε-local DP, defeats averaging, links no better than the identifier already sent); an **org-conditional k-anonymity rule** suppresses cells, more strictly on corporate networks than at home. Evidence base: a **crawl of CSP deployment across 7,969 top sites and 431 B2B publishers**, Heavy-Ad budgets, closed-form privacy–utility trade-offs, a re-identification simulation, and an assessment of *which attributes are predictable at all* — **company type and size are; seniority largely is not**. Closes with the load-bearing line: **on-device is a design property, not a consent exemption.**
- **Why it matters**: The most operationally concrete ads paper in the window, and it survives the "is this a real system or a GDPR essay" test by pre-declaring which target variables are hopeless. The **iframe-policy inference** is the clever part — it turns a compliance constraint into an execution environment. The negative finding (seniority not predictable) is the one to carry into any segmentation plan: the industry's default professional-attribute list overstates its own addressable space.

### When Privacy Moves ML-Mediated Decisions On Device: Information and Incentive Misalignment in Auctions
- **Authors**: Dipankar Sarkar
- **arXiv**: [2609.33312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33312) — cs.GT (cross-listed cs.DC, cs.LG)
- **Key contribution**: Moving ML-mediated decisions onto privacy-preserving clients **decentralises the economic decision along with the inference** — and shared budget constraints then depend on information that cannot be globally current, an **information-structure failure conventional pacing is not designed to solve**. Measured in an auction-logic-faithful on-device simulation (36 campaigns, 50 devices; accounting in dimensionless integer score units, no currency semantics claimed). Across 30 paired demand paths, **proportional Even pacing overspends 17.77% after one tick of staleness and 1,669.31% after 50 ticks** at 20× budget pressure — but the effect does not depend on severe pressure: at **2× pressure, 50-tick overspend remains 106.95%**. A visible-budget no-sale guard makes zero-lag compliance exact, yet **leaves 11.88% overspend at one tick** because other devices' debits remain invisible. Derives a finite-window expected excess-debit bound under conditional charge caps, positive paired slack in every bounded-value cell. **Second, independent misalignment**: when the ML/pacing score transformation may change payment units, **98.23% of rival auctions at one tick admit a profitable deviation**, isolated by an executable implementation-level counterexample. Honest boundary: critical-base-bid payment is **per-auction DSIC conditional on current multipliers**, does **not** establish dynamic truthfulness, and does not repair base-value ranking disagreement.
- **Why it matters**: The most mechanistically surprising result in the window for anyone running on-device bidding or pacing. The asymmetry is the point: **a local privacy-preserving agent can see its own budget perfectly and still be systematically wrong about the shared one** — staleness is not noise, it is a directional budget leak. The 1,669% figure is at a stress setting and should be read as a scaling demonstration, not a forecast; the **106.95% at 2× pressure** is the defensible headline.

## ② Recommender Systems: Architecture, Latency & Decision Quality (6)

### PEAR: Progressive Evidence-Based AutoResearch for Industrial Search Systems
- **Authors**: Yifan Wang, Shipeng Zhu, Fei Xiong, Yuqin Yang, Yonghui Huang, Kunyao Wu, Yue Wang, Weichao Meng, **Yu Gong**
- **arXiv**: [2609.35031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35031) — cs.IR (cross-listed cs.AI)
- **Key contribution**: AutoResearch (agents propose → evaluate → use results to guide exploration) meets industrial search and hits two problems the paper names precisely. **(1) The keep-if-better rule is wrong under non-stationary traffic** — transient gains get mistaken for persistent improvements, so search knowledge fails to accumulate. (2) Candidates are evaluable at **multiple fidelity levels** with different cost, objective alignment, and statistical reliability, and existing methods lack a unified basis for using evidence across levels. **PEAR** answers with: **Evidence-driven AutoResearch**, maintaining an *independent, hypothesis-guided research state per strategy task* inside a predefined objective and intervention scope, each evolving through **Plan-Execute-Evaluate-Update** with context-aware evidence interpretation and hypothesis revision; and a **Confidence-Gated Verifier Ladder** of four levels — **Offline Replay → Shadow-Traffic Evaluation → Rapid Online Evaluation → Decision-Grade Online Evaluation** — promoting a candidate only when a unified confidence gate certifies a **statistically significant positive effect**. This buys broad low-cost exploration while reserving expensive online experiments for promoted candidates. **Result in a real-world industrial search system: strategies optimized with PEAR increased Main Order/DAU by 2.7336% and 3.2957% over their respective baselines in two A/B experiments.**
- **Why it matters**: The single most transferable paper in the window, and the sharpest methodological correction to the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check|AgentX-Model]] entry. AgentX-Model reports *how many experiments cleared an AUC baseline* (560/636) — which, as PEAR shows, is exactly the statistic that non-stationary traffic corrupts. PEAR's two live A/B numbers are the industrial-rec evidence standard this wiki has been missing. The Verifier Ladder is also the correct answer to the ladder-of-fidelity problem in *any* deployed AutoResearch system.

### Beyond the Beam: Constructive Repair and Candidate Completion for Generative Recommendation
- **Authors**: Zijun Zhao, Peng Zhang, Gang Zhang, Yuanchi Ma, Hui He, Zhendong Niu
- **arXiv**: [2609.33745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33745) — cs.IR (51 pages)
- **Key contribution**: The window's most careful theoretical paper, and it takes the generative-rec catalog-expansion problem seriously rather than empirically. A valid identifier can fall **outside the beam** after catalog growth. **BB** first *characterizes* which failures identifier assignment can even repair: **output-invariance certificates** identify failures shared by all admissible assignments, and under a common effective prefix, **coupled support and ranking constraints give the exact feasible interval of new-item counts for target recovery** — i.e. a formal statement of when repair is possible at all. It then obtains **minimum-replacement repairs via an integral flow formulation**, selects a shared map, adapts the generator, and at inference combines **generative likelihood and collaborative evidence into one score** used simultaneously for ranking, candidate priority, and stopping; **retained prefix bounds guide candidate completion and certify global Top-K** when the stopping condition is met. Exhaustive finite-catalog evaluation confirms construction in every feasible case. **Across three Amazon Reviews categories × three seeds, the full T5 procedure improves mean Recall@10 by 15.5–46.3% and NDCG@10 by 15.2–44.4%** over the best evaluated generative baseline per dataset/metric; matched controls isolate shared construction and adaptation; combined scoring + completion improves NDCG@10 on all three datasets with both T5 and decoder-only LC-Rec.
- **Why it matters**: Fills the exact gap the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check|Evo-Rec]] entry left open — that paper added *reasoning* to semantic-ID recommendation but said nothing about what happens when the ID space grows underneath it. The **certified global Top-K** claim is the operationally load-bearing one: it is a stoppable, provably-correct retrieval loop rather than a fixed beam heuristic. 15–46% Recall@10 is a large swing for a structural fix with no new modeling.

### SPRINT: Single-Step Generative Recommendation via Average Probability Velocity
- **Authors**: Zhuo Cai, Shoujin Wang, Peilin Zhou, Min Xu, **Julian McAuley**, Fang Chen
- **arXiv**: [2609.34306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34306) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Both dominant Semantic-ID generative-rec paradigms **pay for generation token by token** — autoregressive decodes left-to-right, non-autoregressive decodes in parallel but still needs refinement rounds — and multiple forward passes per item is prohibitive under latency. **SPRINT** asks whether an item can be generated in **one forward pass**, via **average probability velocity**: treating SID generation as a flow of token-generation probabilities, characterizing it by its average velocity over the whole generation, and **proving that average velocity is fully determined by the average generation probability of each token**. So it parameterizes and learns all token probabilities in a single pass with a bidirectional Transformer. Because independently-generated positions lose token coherence, a **dual-level flow contrastive objective** restores it — contrasting the target SID against negatives at the **token level** (rank target-token generation probabilities above negative SIDs') and the **SID level** (score each SID's tokens as a whole item, capturing intra-item coherence). **Result: 8.39–10.04× speedup over the second-fastest AR/NAR method, with 7.77% average accuracy improvement over the second-best.**
- **Why it matters**: The largest *joint* latency-and-accuracy win in the window's rec cluster — and the usual pattern is a speedup that costs accuracy. The proof that velocity collapses to per-token average probability is what licenses the single-pass parameterization; the SID-level contrastive term is the price of losing autoregressive coherence, paid explicitly. Complements 2609.33745 (same SID family, opposite axis: *correctness under catalog growth* vs *latency per item*).

### No Attention, No Problem: Rethinking Session-based Recommendation with Pure Convolution
- **Authors**: Tao Huang, Wei Zhou
- **arXiv**: [2609.34802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34802) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Directly contests the field's default that session-based recommendation needs self-attention. **NextConvRec** is a pure-convolutional framework: a **Structural and Positional Convolutional Encoder** preprocesses the session by combining **learnable convolutional positional biases** with **session-level structural signals extracted through GCN layers**; the backbone expands the effective receptive field via **depthwise + pointwise convolutions** to model long-range preference with no attention at all. **On 4 benchmarks, ~+1.73% average over several SOTA baselines, with average inference time per session reduced 16.7%.**
- **Why it matters**: A rare rec result where the efficiency gain is free rather than traded. Same structural signature as the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check|CTC-Bench]] line — attention is a *default*, not a demonstrated necessity, and a baseline-shape audit (the 09-25 ternary-LM replication's 22.6% spread across shapes) says the same thing. The GCN-in-encoder move is the reusable idea: session structure is graph structure, and it is cheaper to inject before the sequence model than to learn inside attention.

### Beyond One Epoch: Uncertainty-Weighted Sensitivity Regularization for Recommendation Models
- **Authors**: Richard Lettich, Shagun Gupta
- **arXiv**: [2609.34083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34083) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: Names and diagnoses the **one-epoch phenomenon** — with sparse embeddings and a shared consumer, a second epoch lowers training loss while *sharply* degrading generalization. The explanation is a **violation of the prequential principle**: on epoch 1 an example's label has not affected the embedding rows used to score it; on later epochs those rows carry a **displacement induced by labels earlier updates**, creating an incentive for the shared consumer to exploit that displacement. Named **self-influence asymmetry**. Using an exact scalar model plus local influence analysis, the paper links the mismatch to **embedding uncertainty**, then verifies via embedding-consumer-update interventions in deep rec models, and proposes **UWSR**, which augments the loss to penalize the consumer for relying on uncertain embeddings. **Unlike existing remedies, UWSR preserves the learned embeddings**; across three benchmarks, four-epoch UWSR reduces test cross-entropy by **1.38–6.78%** and improves AUC by **0.0058–0.0231** relative to one-epoch training.
- **Why it matters**: A training-regime finding that is a *causal* account, not a heuristic, and the AUC delta is small enough to be honest — which is itself the point. Most rec papers train to convergence; this says the second epoch is a **measurement artifact of the training setup**, and gives the mechanism. Directly useful to anyone who has ever reported "we trained until the loss stopped dropping."

### Measuring and Mitigating Identity-Cue Preference Drift in LLM-based Recommender Systems
- **Authors**: Zhuoxiong Gan, Qiang Dong
- **arXiv**: [2609.34229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34229) — cs.IR (cross-listed cs.SI)
- **Key contribution**: In LLM recommenders, **identity cues embedded in prompts steer recommendations toward group-level patterns even when the underlying behavioral evidence is unchanged** — a drift in the recommender's output that is not a drift in the user. **PromptShift** is interpretable and **training-free**. *Drift* = divergence in **both item membership and ranking order** between a cued list and the reference list from the same user's history alone. *SliceShift* = how far the cued list gravitates, relative to that reference, toward items **more popular within the cued slice than among the global population**. New metric **DifHitRate** is a **difficulty-weighted hit rate** crediting only relevant items, weighted toward hits that are *less* popular within the cued slice and ranked higher. All components rest on an identity-slice-by-item table built from positive interactions, which also enables **adaptive post-hoc reranking** interpolating original LLM ranking with inverse slice-popularity at a personalized weight. **On two datasets × three LLMs: identity-cued prompts incur higher mean Drift than identity-free paraphrase controls — an effect beyond generic wording sensitivity — and SliceShift is positive in all six dataset-model settings; PromptShift cuts macro-mean SliceShift by 62.42%** while improving DifHitRate, HitRate and MRR, with a modest metric-dependent utility cost.
- **Why it matters**: The fairness-adjacent rec paper with the cleanest counterfactual design — the identity-free paraphrase control is what separates "identity cues" from "prompt wording sensitivity," and most papers in this space conflate them. The 6/6-positive SliceShift result means the effect is not a model quirk. Reranking-only mitigation matters operationally: no retraining, so it can ship as a post-hoc layer.

## ③ Benchmark Validity & Measurement Instruments (6)

### Pinned and Still Unstable: Within-Judge Verdict Variance and the Noise Floor of LLM-as-Judge Leaderboards
- **Authors**: Krishna Chytanya Ayyagari
- **arXiv**: [2609.33044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33044) — cs.CL (50 pages, single author)
- **Key contribution**: Tests the load-bearing operational assumption of LLM eval — that **pinning a judge to a fixed snapshot at temperature 0 yields reproducible verdicts** — and shows it fails as a property of *how LLM-as-Judge is operationalized on cloud serving infrastructure*, not of any model family. **Four frontier judges, all served via a single major enterprise cloud platform**, three standard benchmarks (Arena-Hard, AlpacaEval 2, MT-Bench): identical inputs to the same pinned temperature-zero judge give **different verdicts across re-runs — per-item flip rate ~5% on average but ~40% on the close-call items that decide leaderboard margins**, with a per-judge magnitude spanning a **40× range (0.13% to nearly 10%)**. Introduces per-item flip rate, a two-part stability profile (waver fraction + conditional intensity), and **adjacency separability**, and reports what the variance does to rankings: *for a single judge the aggregate ranking is stable* (0% top-K instability, 0% pooled winner flip) — **what degrades is precision**: under a paired hierarchical bootstrap, **roughly one-fifth to three-quarters of adjacent leaderboard positions are statistically indistinguishable**, attributed primarily to **finite prompt sampling rather than the judge**. Across judges, coarse ordering agrees but the middle diverges (**Kendall's τ as low as 0.42–0.64** on Arena-Hard); **of 13 published head-to-head ranking claims re-judged, 5 fail** under a defensible judge swap or re-run. Ships a minimal reporting protocol: several judge re-runs, published stability profiles and adjacency intervals, and ≥2 judges from different families.
- **Why it matters**: The sharpest correction to how the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check]] cluster should be read. That cluster concluded evaluation *verdicts* flip under protocol changes; this one localises the mechanism to **non-determinism that survives snapshot-pinning** — i.e. the reproducibility control the field already uses is insufficient. The discipline-relevant finding is the *asymmetry*: rank order is robust, **adjacent gaps are not**, so "model X beats model Y" claims are only sound at large margins. The 5-of-13 failure rate is a directly usable prior.

### Are You Sure You're Sure? Two Confounds in a Sycophancy Benchmark
- **Authors**: Atharv Gupta, Akshat Jindal, Lavanya Nigam, Aryan Sood
- **arXiv**: [2609.32867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32867) — cs.CL
- **Key contribution**: Audits **SycEval**, which reports that objections raised *before* the model answers (preemptive) cause more caving than those raised *after* (in-context), and attributes the gap to timing. Two features of its templates vary alongside the property each is meant to test. **First**: SycEval's objections escalate through four strength levels, and **at the two weakest only the preemptive template names a target answer** — so timing and naming vary together. The authors build the missing comparison on multiple-choice questions and SycEval's own free-form pipeline: **naming a target answer raises the follow rate by 14.1–49.5 pp**, and **once both templates name one, the timing comparison REVERSES on three of five model conditions — models caveat *less* under preemptive objections than in-context ones, opposite to SycEval.** **Second**: the output-format instruction benchmarks append for automatic grading also varies with objection placement; moving it from the pushback into the question **flips its effect in opposite directions across models on the same items (p = 0.0059)**; in the free-form pipeline, relocating the instruction **lowers caving by 5.1 pp on Llama-3.1-8B while an equivalence test confirms no effect on Qwen3-4B**. Both confounds live in the template, not the models — both are correctable; closes with three checks benchmark authors can apply before publishing.
- **Why it matters**: The strongest *causal-repair* audit in the window: it doesn't just report a confound, it **constructs the missing cell and shows the headline conclusion reverses**. The placement-instruction result (same items, opposite sign, p = 0.0059) is a general warning about grading instructions in prompt-pair benchmarks. Note the disciplined negative: Qwen3-4B shows **no** effect — the paper reports that as an equivalence test rather than hiding it.

### Opening LLM Judges: Recovering Preference Signals Beyond the Final Verdict
- **Authors**: Sourabrata Mukherjee, Sunayana Sitaram
- **arXiv**: [2609.32407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32407) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Asks the diagnostic question the window's other judge papers imply: **when a judge is wrong, is the information absent from the model, or present internally but not in the output?** Across **64 open-weight evaluators and 14 datasets**, with **causal interventions on 41 judges** (editing activations mid-run to see whether the verdict changes). On LLMBar — built so the **superficially better answer is the worse one** — **50 judges' verdicts agree with human labels only 0.456 of the time, even after averaging both answer orders.** Yet a small probe on the same judges' activations, **no weight updates**, reaches **0.846**, and **0.686** once surface features (length, position) are residualized out (0.507 with shuffled labels). The gap holds across eight benchmarks and model families, **but is not universal**: a score for how well surface features alone predict the human label, **computed before any probe is trained, predicts the size of the gain (Spearman ρ = 0.90)** — so there is a cheap diagnostic for *when* recovery is worth attempting. **On rubric tasks that score one answer at a time, leaving no surface cue to exploit, reading the internals gives no advantage.** Interventions show mid-network activation editing already changes the verdict before it can be read off, and locate the pathways carrying position and length bias. At the same label budget, the recovered signal lets a judge flag cases where it is likely wrong and yields better labels for preference learning.
- **Why it matters**: Pairs with 2609.33044 as the two halves of the judge problem: *that* paper says verdicts are noisier than assumed; *this* one says the noise is recoverable from activations, and — more valuable — **gives the precondition (surface-cue exploitability) that predicts whether recovery will work.** The ρ = 0.90 pre-probe diagnostic is the kind of cheap test that should precede any activation-probing project. The rubric-task null result is the scope limit: this is a pairwise-comparison phenomenon, not a general judge improvement.

### A Free Knob: Decoupling Calibration and Predictive Skill in Threshold-Based Evaluation
- **Authors**: Md Tanveer Hossain Munim, Bijoy Ahmed Saiem, Al-Amin Sany, Tanzima Hashem
- **arXiv**: [2609.33457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33457) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: Pool-and-threshold benchmarks for rare events **conflate spatial discrimination with amplitude calibration**: sharp observations push many blocks above threshold while **attenuated predictions from squared-error regression leave the same blocks below it**. The paper repurposes classical monotone calibration as a **symmetric audit** — a post-hoc transform fitted on held-out data and applied **separately to each system**. The transform **cannot reverse pixel ordering**, so any contrast it reproduces cannot establish improved spatial ranking. **On SEVIR, two released checkpoints of one architecture differ by −29.5% in extreme-threshold CSI before the control and +5.3% after it.** Across **450 pairwise contrasts among 6 systems**, the difference in pooled frequency-bias deviation correlates with how far the CSI contrast moves under the control (**r = +0.796**), and **51 contrasts reverse sign**. At CasCast's published extreme-event operating point the cascade-over-backbone CSI gap falls from **0.1601 to 0.0339 (78.8% reduction)**, remaining gap positive. Effect persists when the transform is fitted on a **window before the test period**, and calibration also **reveals advantages hidden by a better-calibrated baseline**. Grows as events get rarer; reproduces under patch-sum pooling in crowd counting; **semantic segmentation, where frequency bias is already near one, shows little average change.** Names the precondition honestly: the confound requires **both a fixed operating point and a training regime that leaves output miscalibrated there**. Recommends reporting pooled frequency bias + a symmetric held-out FreeKnob Audit alongside the score.
- **Why it matters**: The most *transferable* measurement paper in the window, because pooled-threshold scoring is everywhere — rare-event detection, crowd counting, any imbalanced pooled metric. The **−29.5% → +5.3% sign flip between two checkpoints of one architecture** is the single most alarming number here: the same model family, scored as designed, appears worse than better depending on calibration. The "transform cannot reverse ordering" argument is what makes the audit *valid* rather than a calibration-flattering trick, and the segmentation null result keeps it from being a universal-effect claim.

### The Selection Rule Decides the Winner: A Pre-Registered Audit of Open-Set Graph Anomaly Detection
- **Authors**: Farhan Shahriyar Hossain, Taufikur Rahman Fuad, Md Abrar Jahin, Md Rizwan Parvez
- **arXiv**: [2609.33370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33370) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: Three conventions are shared by published open-set graph anomaly detection results — the test score is read **at the best epoch on the test set**, baseline numbers are **copied from earlier papers**, and most anomalies are **minority classes relabeled as anomalous**. The authors re-run two recent methods (DEMO, NSReg) plus OUTPOST (their own first-order detector) under one protocol — identical seeds and splits, eight graphs, **ten seeds each** — scoring every run under both the **best-epoch rule and a deployable validation rule**, after **registering 40 predictions before the runs that test them**. Three findings hold: **(1) the rule changes the leader** — under best-epoch, OUTPOST and NSReg each lead three of seven graphs; under the validation rule **NSReg leads five**. **(2) The best-epoch bonus depends on how the benchmark was built: 0.045–0.080 AUC-ROC on the three small relabeled-class graphs but 0.002–0.014 on the three real fraud graphs** — i.e. the protocol artifact is an order of magnitude larger on synthetic than on real anomaly data. **(3) Pseudo-labeling in OUTPOST is worth 0.038–0.065 AUC-ROC on the same three graphs but gives no benefit on any real fraud graph.** Also shows a **0.002 tie band for hyperparameter selection lies below the paired standard error on all six graphs tested, even at ten seeds**. **Twelve of the 40 pre-registered predictions were falsified, and they report them.**
- **Why it matters**: The strongest *methodological transparency* paper in the window — publishing one's own falsified predictions is the practice the whole 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check]] cluster was asking for. The relabeled-vs-real gap (10×) is the portable insight: **convention-driven score inflation scales with how synthetic the benchmark is**, so synthetic-benchmark results in anomaly detection broadly deserve a discount.

### Rubric-Calibrated Preferences: Cross-Query Calibration of LLM Judgments via Item Response Theory
- **Authors**: Fabian David Schmidt, Donato Crisostomi, Carlos Lassance, Nils Reimers
- **arXiv**: [2609.35739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35739) — cs.IR (51 pages)
- **Key contribution**: Reranker evaluation runs on **nDCG with human relevance labels that are costly, sparse, noisy and discretely graded** — so as rerankers converge, nDCG increasingly fails to separate them. LLM judges could supply dense labels, but **relative judgments within a query separate close candidates yet share no scale across queries**, while **absolute grades share one scale but are too coarse**. **RCP** combines both: a **listwise Bradley-Terry tournament** orders each query's documents, and a **rubric of yes/no criteria of increasing stringency** provides the absolute standard; **Item Response Theory** then uses the shared criteria to put all queries' tournament scores on one scale, and **RCP-nDCG** replaces nDCG's discrete labels with the resulting calibrated relevance probabilities. Against blind grades from **46 external annotators**: correlation between a query's mean score and its mean human grade rises **0.538 → 0.795**; ranking a useful document above a non-useful one **AUC 0.910 (chance 0.5) vs 0.651 for the benchmark labels**. When annotator grades prefer one of two rerankers and exactly one metric agrees, **that metric is RCP-nDCG in 72.4% of 185 comparisons (chance ~53%)**. On TREC-DL, RCP-nDCG sides with NIST assessors' grades on **every** reranker pair those grades separate significantly; it resolves many of nDCG's ties and separates **1.9× as many reranker pairs on NanoBEIR**.
- **Why it matters**: The constructive counterpart to the window's five destructive audits — it does not say the existing labels are wrong, it supplies a better label source and validates it against 46 blind annotators. Directly usable for any rec/search team hitting reranker-convergence saturation. The 185-comparison agreement statistic is a much stronger validation design than typical "our metric correlates with humans" claims, and the **"every significant pair" TREC-DL result is the load-bearing one**.

## ④ Statistics, Experimentation & the Decision Layer (5)

### Learnable Randomization as Commitment Against Adaptive Optimizers
- **Authors**: Zihan Deng, Chuanzhi Xu, Xiaozhen Zhong, Haoyang Li, Junjie Huang
- **arXiv**: [2609.32772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32772) — cs.GT (cross-listed cs.AI)
- **Key contribution**: Opens with the mechanism in one paragraph: **a pricing page can walk the posted price up to the last amount a buyer still accepts, a recommender can hold back a better item for a barely acceptable promoted one, and a classifier can shift its boundary once applicants change their features.** The system predicts the response and then picks the menu serving its own objective, so the surplus above the user's cutoff is taken. Playing the single best action *publishes* that cutoff; noise on actions the user would never take throws away payoff and teaches the platform a worse menu is acceptable. The paper studies **unpredictable near-optimal policies (UNOP)**, which mix uniformly on near-best actions that remain individually rational — **the mixture is a commitment about the response.** Formal result on a finite price grid: when the best sure-demand price strictly out-earns the randomized band, a seller who already knows the curve posts below the band, **and the purchase that occurs is deterministic — knowing that curve is not the same as predicting the next draw.** The mixture can be learned and the optimizer can match its best response, while the user's payoff stays higher because the mixture changes which action is targeted. **In pricing and in policy-aware recommendation this leaves more surplus than greedy play when the platform optimizes against the curve and more than one action is acceptable.** The gain **disappears under quality ranking, a singleton near-optimal set, a wrong utility estimate, or a short-horizon explorer** — which is also where mixing should be turned off if the other side is trying to cooperate.
- **Why it matters**: The most *game-theoretically* interesting rec/pricing paper in the window, and it explains an everyday industrial practice that otherwise looks like unexplainable A/B noise: **deliberate randomization among near-best actions is a commitment device against an adaptive optimizer, and it is worth surplus precisely when the counterparty is optimizing against your response distribution.** The four falsification conditions are stated up front, which is why this is worth featuring over a hundred papers that report a 2% lift. Read alongside 2609.33312 (on-device pacing) as a matched pair: both ask what a *self-interacting* system does to its own measurement.

### Rerandomization under Interference
- **Authors**: Ziang Yuan, Xinran Li, Shuangning Li
- **arXiv**: [2609.33730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33730) — stat.ME
- **Key contribution**: Covariates improve precision in randomized experiments, but **under interference** — outcomes depending on other units' treatment assignments — standard covariate adjustment may fail to preserve the **no-harm property** (that adding covariates does not worsen estimator performance). Existing approaches need a **specified interference structure**, and their guarantees are **sensitive to misspecification**. This paper preserves no-harm while staying **largely agnostic to the interference structure**, focusing on EATE via the Hájek estimator, and studies **rerandomization under interference**: a **design-stage** procedure restricting the assignment space to allocations with sufficiently small covariate imbalance. Notably, the rerandomization covariates **may depend on the treatment assignment itself** — e.g. the proportion of treated neighbours, which "naturally arises in settings with interference." Proves rerandomization still improves precision asymptotically relative to unrestricted Bernoulli randomization under **mild** conditions on cross-unit dependence; with a conservative dependence graph, develops an **optimization-based conservative variance estimator**.
- **Why it matters**: The most practically correct paper in the window for anyone running marketplace or social A/B tests, where **interference (one unit's treatment changing another's outcome) is the default assumption, not the exception**. The design-stage/interference-aware covariate allowance is a genuinely new allowance: rerandomization on treated-neighbour fraction is a mechanism the field does not currently use, and it directly addresses the misspecification fragility of post-hoc adjustment.

### Bridge-Anchored Partial Identification of a Target Mean under Outcome-Model Drift
- **Authors**: Danhyang Lee, Shinyoung Jeon, Shu Yang
- **arXiv**: [2609.36132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36132) — stat.ME
- **Key contribution**: Transporting an outcome relationship from a source to a target population where the outcome is unobserved requires the **conditional outcome law to be stable**; when it is not (**outcome-model drift**), the target mean is **not point-identified** and existing transportability estimators are biased. In settings common to cross-cohort data integration where **bridge outcomes are recorded in both populations and can be expected to drift alongside the target outcome**, the bridge splits drift into a **detectable component, fixed by a bridge-matching equation**, and a **residual orthogonal to the bridge that no observed quantity restricts**; because the target is a mean, the residual acts through a **single scalar sensitivity parameter κ**. Proves the identified set has a **closed-form irreducible core** whose width is governed by the residual bound and by how much of the outcome the bridge leaves unexplained, and that its **center is first-order invariant to the residual** — for any working exponential family, with point identification arising only at the κ = 0 benchmark. Provides a **debiased, drift-augmented estimator**, semiparametrically efficient at anchored sensitivity, **doubly robust conditional on the bridge-identified drift**, yielding **rate-robust Imbens-Manski inference**. Key advantage: unlike bridge-blind sensitivity analysis, which must bound the **entire** drift channel, this bounds **only its bridge-orthogonal part**. Motivating instance: two survey cohorts whose **kindergarten mathematics outcome is available in only one**.
- **Why it matters**: The correct statistical posture for the wiki's recurring cross-population generalization problem, and its asymptotic property is the point: **the *center* of the honest answer is robust to the unidentifiable part; only the width moves.** That is a materially better deliverable than "we assume transportability holds." Applies directly to any rec/ads model validated on one traffic segment and deployed on another.

### OTROPE: Optimal Transport-based Robust Off-Policy Evaluation for Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Liner Xiang, Wenbo Zhang, Hengrui Cai
- **arXiv**: [2609.36264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36264) — cs.AI (cross-listed cs.CL, stat.ML) · code: `github.com/LinerXiang/OTROPE`
- **Key contribution**: Off-policy evaluation for LLMs is hard for three compounding reasons: **labels are scarce**, **behavior–target distribution shift is common**, and **response likelihoods are often unavailable for black-box LLMs**. **OTROPE** is a **likelihood-free** evaluation performing distributional correction **in a semantic space via optimal transport** to align labeled behavior-policy samples with unlabeled target-policy samples, combining corrected human-labeled residuals with proxy predictors — yielding a **doubly robust-style evaluation without behavior-policy modeling or density-ratio estimation**. Supplies the **theoretical characterization of why baseline evaluators fail under LLM distribution shift**, plus consistency and convergence rates when either the reweighted behavior distribution or the proxy predictor converges. On synthetic and real LLM eval tasks it consistently outperforms baselines, and notably **ensembles of weaker LLM evaluators can approach and sometimes surpass stronger evaluators**.
- **Why it matters**: The off-policy-evaluation problem for LLMs is exactly the problem of "can I tell if my new model is better without running it on live users" — the rec/ads auto-research version. Likelihood-free is the load-bearing constraint, since production LLM APIs frequently do not return logprobs. The weaker-ensemble-can-surpass-stronger result is the practically surprising part and the one to cite before building a single expensive evaluator.

### Anytime-Valid LLM Leaderboards via Benchmark-weighted and Block-Factorized e-Processes
- **Authors**: Hongfu Gao, Songxin Zhang, Zejian Xie, Bingyi Jing, Zhou Wang, Yiming Liu
- **arXiv**: [2609.32248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32248) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: Variability in evaluation outcomes across runs means mean-performance rankings can produce **unsupported claims of model superiority**, a risk compounded by leaderboard updates. **BB-EDGE** represents a leaderboard as a **directed graph whose edges certify pairwise mean-performance advantages**, with **anytime-valid family-wise error rate (FWER) control**. Per direction it constructs an **empirical-Bernstein e-process by factorizing evidence over protocol-defined blocks**, assigning **stakes proportional to block weights**, then applies **direct e-Holm across e-processes** to certify directional advantages as edges. Characterizes weight-proportional linear stakes under heterogeneous benchmark-average nulls and proves **anytime FWER control under arbitrary within-block and cross-pair dependence**. Extends to **anytime-valid Top-k certification and simultaneous rank intervals**; experiments on synthetic data and four real benchmarks maintain control while achieving high efficiency.
- **Why it matters**: The formal counterpart to 2609.33044. Where that paper measures the noise floor empirically and asks for re-runs, this one supplies the inference machinery to **certify edges with anytime validity** — you can stop as soon as the claim is provable. Together they define a defensible protocol: measure the floor, then certify against it. The block factorization over protocol-defined blocks is the piece that makes heterogeneous-benchmark weighting coherent, and Top-k certification matters more than pairwise edges for anyone who reports "top-3 models."

## ⑤ Agents: Safety, Memory & Measurement (4)

### AgentBoundary: Counterfactual Evaluation of Safety in Tool-Using LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Tianzhuo Yang, Zirui Mi, Yantao Huang, Guoxi Zhang, Jiawei Chen, Yaodong Yang, Jingwei Yi
- **arXiv**: [2609.33658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33658) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Conversational safety framing is **answer-or-refuse**; agentic framing is **decide-whether-to-act as permission-critical evidence emerges during execution** — and in that setting **apparent risk, action permissibility and task competence are easily confounded**, making agentic over-refusal hard to distinguish from ordinary task failure. **AgentBound** is the first four-way counterfactual generation-and-evaluation framework for tool-using agent safety: it transforms the same executable workflow by **independently varying apparent risk and action permissibility**, enabling controlled comparison of **risky-looking but authorized** vs **routine-looking but unauthorized** tasks — diagnosing over-refusal and unsafe compliance *while controlling for task competence*. Instantiated as a **human-validated 4,000-task suite** with trajectory-based and post-state-based judgments. **Across 17 model and harness configurations, high safety frequently coexists with poor authorized-task completion: GPT-5.5 blocks 99.5% of routine-looking unauthorized actions yet completes only 28.7% of risky-looking authorized tasks.** A lightweight runtime calibration module then improves authorized-task completion by **18.2%** on average across 10 configurations while *also* improving unsafe-action blocking by **5.4%**.
- **Why it matters**: The 28.7% figure is the most consequential number in the window for anyone deploying agents with tool permissions — a model that blocks 99.5% of unauthorized actions and completes 28.7% of authorized ones is not well-calibrated, it is *useless*, and the aggregate "safety score" hides it completely. The four-way factorial design is the reusable contribution: any agent-safety eval that varies risk and permission together cannot separate over-refusal from incompetence.

### Backdoor in the Loop: Compromising Agentic Search via Malicious Retrievers
- **Authors**: Beining Xu, Peichun Hua, Yunming Xiao
- **arXiv**: [2609.37468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37468) — cs.CR (cross-listed cs.AI, cs.IR, cs.LG)
- **Key contribution**: Agentic RAG **interleaves reasoning with repeated retrieval**, giving the retriever influence over both the evidence the agent observes *and* its subsequent search decisions. The paper studies **retriever backdoors that exploit this feedback loop and repurpose weak backdoor purification to conceal their presence**. Threat model is realistic: the attacker supplies **a compromised retriever checkpoint while leaving the search agent and deployment corpus unchanged** — with **no corpus write access**, the attacker can still **suppress useful evidence, persistently retrieve a selected existing document, or steer the agent toward prolonged search, inflating retrieval, context and latency cost.** Concealment uses a **controlled inject-and-remove cycle: deliberately inject a weaker backdoor, then unlearn it** — weakening detector-visible signatures and **fooling backdoor detectors with an illusion of purification** while preserving the malicious retrieval behavior. Conclusion: a weak defense becomes the attacker's concealment tool, **even when the underlying corpus remains trustworthy**.
- **Why it matters**: The window's sharpest inversion — *running your backdoor detector makes you less safe* — and it is directly actionable for search/RAG deployments, where retriever-checkpoint provenance is usually assumed clean. The latency-cost attack deserves attention on its own: in a latency-budgeted serving stack, steering an agent into longer search is a denial-of-service vector that leaves no corpus trace. Same group as 2609.37469 below, and Peichun Hua / Yunming Xiao also co-author 2609.32186 (backchain memory attacks) in this window.

### Relevance Is Not Sufficient Evidence: Detecting Evidence Gaps Before Generation in RAG
- **Authors**: Suting Chen, Peichun Hua, Yunming Xiao
- **arXiv**: [2609.37469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37469) — cs.CL (cross-listed cs.IR, cs.LG)
- **Key contribution**: Retrieved passages often **name the right entities without providing the facts needed to answer**. Even when instructed to abstain, **12 generators answer 40.0–99.3% of insufficient-evidence questions**. Training generators to abstain ties the decision to model weights, may reward answers **recalled from parametric knowledge**, and still requires a full generator call. The paper asks whether sufficiency can be judged **from the question and evidence alone, before any answer exists** — and first identifies the pitfalls in constructing insufficient-evidence tests: **removing relevant evidence or pairing evidence with unrelated questions can reveal labels through lexical overlap or evidence position.** Builds a **paired benchmark using substitution, deletion and question-swap constructions** that vary answer support while controlling selected surface features such as word use. **Sufficiency can be judged without generating an answer, but no single signal works across all datasets.** **RINSE** combines three signals: whether every part of the question is covered, whether any passage offers an answer, and whether a small LM reading the passages together judges them sufficient. **Across six datasets RINSE ranks sufficient above insufficient evidence at 0.837 (chance 0.5), exceeding the best of 10 prior methods (0.746) and a frontier model queried through an API (0.784). Its weakest dataset (0.684) beats any other method's weakest (0.676).** Runs locally pre-generation at **36.5 ms per question on a single GPU**.
- **Why it matters**: A cheap pre-generation abstention gate that beats a frontier-model API judge on its own benchmark — and the benchmark-design pitfall section is the more valuable half, since it explains why published insufficiency benchmarks disagree. The 40.0–99.3% non-abstention range *is* the finding: parametric recall masquerading as grounded answering is near-universal, and the fix does not have to live in the generator. Pairs with 2609.37468 as the same group's paired defensive/offensive pair.

### DISCERN: Can AI Agents Work Like Scientists and Guide Discovery?
- **Authors**: Nan Huang, Mario Tapia-Pacheco, Kun Zhou, Yiming Huang, Kevin José Barrientos Díaz, Tiffany Amariuta, Jingbo Shang
- **arXiv**: [2609.33357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33357) — cs.AI · data: `huggingface.co/datasets/discern-bench-anon/discern-benchmark`
- **Key contribution**: Existing benchmarks assess analytical task completion or hypothesis generation **separately**, rather than whether **reliable evidence supports valid and novel claims**. **DISCERN** (Data Integrity and Scientific Capability) is a controlled benchmark on real public datasets evaluating **three levels** of an automated research workflow: **(1) data integrity** under confounds and tool traps; **(2) analysis verification**; **(3) hypothesis generation and revision under adversarial review**, including counterfactual cases where evidence consistent with real data and documented phenomena **conflicts with established expectations**, motivating alternative explanations. **Across 203 tasks, eight life-science tracks, eight models: agents earn perfect scores in only 60.8% of Level 1, 34.2% of Level 2, and 0.6% of Level 3 evaluations.** Penalties attributed to **rejection of sound data**, failure to carry recognized limitations into conclusions, and wide variation in hypothesis production. **Cross-track rankings by token and code use are substantially more stable than rankings by evidence judgment** — i.e. consistency in computational effort exceeds consistency in evidence-based reasoning. Conclusion: current agents do not yet demonstrate reliable autonomous analysis or discovery.
- **Why it matters**: The **0.6%** Level-3 number is the honest headline and it is structural, not a model-ranking result: the difficulty compounds down the workflow, and aggregate scores hide it (the paper's own framing). The Level-1 penalty — **rejecting sound data** — is the most actionable failure mode, because a research agent that discards valid data under a confound looks *careful*. Relevant to this wiki's agent-for-research thread (EvoSkillRec, PEAR, search scaling) because those all assume the vetting step works.

### Rubric-Calibrated Preferences → (see ③) — the practical recommendation for reranker saturation
Covered in ③. The short version: if your rerankers have converged and nDCG cannot separate them, RCP-nDCG is validated against 46 blind annotators and separates 1.9× as many pairs on NanoBEIR.

### AX is the New AEO
- **Authors**: Ido Finder, Assaf Elovic, Gad Shalev, Liad Yosef
- **arXiv**: [2609.34951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34951) — cs.AI (cross-listed cs.IR; v2 2026-09-29)
- **Key contribution**: The standard advice — 2023's "seed the training data," then AEO's "scatter breadcrumbs across forum threads, listicles and off-site citations" — assumes being surfaced is enough. But **an agent opens the results and reads them before deciding**, and one buyer question sends it through several rounds of search and fetch. What decides the outcome at that drill-down step is whether the agent can **fetch and read the business's own site — agent experience (AX)**. The paper runs **37,927 agent journeys**, each a buyer question about a business, across **four independent harnesses** over **1,056 real businesses**, **matched on fame, prior model knowledge, and two AEO proxies**, then split on AX level. **Only 7–10% of the finished answer comes from the model's training knowledge**, whether or not the site is readable. **Agent-ready businesses have answers built from their own pages 78% of the time vs 56%, and are clearly recommended 1.9× more often**, while **every grounded answer about a not-agent-ready business costs the agent 64% more**. **Holding business, harness and question fixed, answers built from the site are 41% more accurate.** The dominant failure is **not fabrication but omission**: web-built answers are **3.7× more likely to contain none of the facts the buyer asked for**. Baselines differ sharply across the four harnesses — **clear-recommendation rates vary sevenfold from stack to stack** — yet the effect holds in every one.
- **Why it matters**: Two things. Substantively, it reframes the AEO field the wiki already tracks: the measurable lever moved from *being cited* to *being readable*, with a **3.7× omission** statistic that is more actionable than any ranking factor. Methodologically, the **sevenfold inter-harness spread in the baseline rate is the finding a CTR/ads practitioner should read first** — it bounds how portable any single-agent-visibility study is, including this one. The "only 7–10% from training knowledge" result also quietly retires the 2023 knowledge-seeding playbook.

### Learnable Randomization → (see ④) — the matching explanation for unexplained A/B variance
Covered in ④. Worth pairing with AX here: if your ranking/pacing layer already randomizes among near-best actions, an A/B harness measuring single-arm outcomes will see variance that is *policy*, not noise.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

1. **The direct CTR/ads drought ends, and it ends for a structural reason rather than a lucky draw.** Three papers (EvoSkillRec 2609.34552, SIF 2609.36153, on-device auctions 2609.33312) plus two on the rec-decision layer (PEAR 2609.35031, UNOP 2609.32772) sit in a window whose other 53 cs.IR papers are retrieval-mechanics. Read together they mark where the field's attention actually is: **the CTR model is no longer the object of study — the experimentation harness, the serving budget, the policy layer above the model, and the privacy constraints on identity are.** EvoSkillRec optimizes *quality per FLOP* for generative ranking, not quality. PEAR reorganizes how you *decide* to ship. UNOP says your existing randomization is doing strategic work. This is the same pivot the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check]] noted (research-operation automation over loss-function novelty), now with an industrial-search A/B result attached.
2. **The measurement cluster reaches its highest density in the wiki's history, and — for the first time — ships the remedies rather than only the findings.** Six papers (2609.33044, 2609.32867, 2609.32407, 2609.33457, 2609.33370, 2609.35739). The progression within one window is legible: *measure* the floor (noise floor of pinned temperature-zero judging, 0.13%–10% flip rates, 5-of-13 published claims failing) → *localise* it (activations carry the signal; ρ = 0.90 pre-probe diagnostic predicts recoverability) → *repair* it (RCP-nDCG validated against 46 blind annotators; anytime-valid e-process certification; three checks for benchmark authors; a reporting checklist). 2609.33370 goes furthest by **pre-registering 40 predictions and reporting the 12 it falsified**. Cumulative obligation on every leaderboard claim in this wiki: *adjacent positions are not distinguishable; snapshot-pinning is not reproducibility.*
3. **Self-interacting systems break standard measurement in three separate ways this window.** PEAR's keep-if-better rule fails under non-stationary traffic (transient gains accumulate as knowledge). Rerandomization-under-interference needs *design-stage* not post-hoc covariate adjustment (2609.33730). On-device auctions overspend **106.95% at 2× budget pressure** purely from one-tick staleness (2609.33312). UNOP then says the fix and the bug have the same shape: **deliberate randomization among near-best actions is simultaneously a commitment device and a source of harness variance.** Any A/B test over a system that adapts to its own measured traffic needs a design that acknowledges this.
4. **Two independent 2025-benchmark relabel audits, same discipline, different domains.** 2609.33370 (graph anomaly detection) shows best-epoch selection is worth **0.045–0.080 AUC-ROC on relabeled-synthetic graphs but 0.002–0.014 on real fraud graphs**, and that a 0.002 hyperparameter tie band sits *below the paired standard error* even at ten seeds. 2609.32867 (sycophancy) shows a graded template confounds its own variable and that the missing cell **reverses the headline on 3 of 5 model conditions**. Both converge on the same rule: **the artifact's size scales with how synthetic the benchmark is**, and the way to find it is to construct the cell the design left out.
5. **Latency wins in rec are no longer being paid for with accuracy.** SPRINT gets 8.39–10.04× speedup *and* +7.77% accuracy over the second-best; NextConvRec gets +1.73% accuracy *and* −16.7% inference time. Both reach this by attacking the mechanism rather than the model — one forward pass instead of refinement rounds; GCN-injected session structure instead of attention. Against the 09-25 [[arxiv-paper-check|CTC-Bench]] and baseline-shape lines, this is the third independent demonstration in two windows that **the field's architectural default is not a demonstrated necessity.**
6. **Agentic search/retrieval is the new attack surface, and its defenses are actively harmful.** A compromised *retriever checkpoint* with no corpus write access can suppress evidence, fixate a document, or inflate latency; and the proposed concealment — inject a weak backdoor, then unlearn it — **fools the detector with an illusion of purification** (2609.37468). Symmetrically, judge snapshot-pinning does not buy reproducibility (2609.33044), and freezing agent weights buys no stationarity (see runner-up 2609.34924). The window's shared lesson: **the control you are auditing is not the boundary the system actually crosses.** Counterpart on the defensive side: RINSE detects evidence insufficiency *before* generation at 36.5 ms/question, beating a frontier API judge — the guard does not have to live in the generator.
