## LLMs, Agents & Language Modeling

### 1. Sample-efficient Personalization of Large Language Model Alignment with Multidimensional User Preference Data
- **Authors:** Zhen-Yu Zhang, Jerry Huang, Gang Niu, Takashi Ishida, Masashi Sugiyama
- **Institution/Company:** RIKEN Center for Advanced Intelligence Project; Université de Montréal / Mila – Quebec AI Institute; The University of Tokyo (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.22210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22210) (submitted 17 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- **Abstract:** Preference alignment has become a central tool to align large language models (LLMs) with human values and desired behavior, and its online variant can further advance their capabilities beyond what static data alone offer by repeatedly generating and labeling new responses with human feedback. But costly, time-consuming annotations limit its scalability, making sample efficiency essential, and existing methods typically seek to improve it by expanding coverage of the response space or selecting pairs with the highest uncertainty for comparison. Although they are effective, these methods quantify uncertainty from the perspective of the LLM, which may differ from the uncertainty perceived by the user: a pair can appear highly uncertain to the LLM even when past comparisons from the user already imply a clear ordering. In this paper, we propose multidimensional preference-guided online direct preference optimization (MPG-DPO), which leverages low-cost multidimensional evaluations from automated verifiers on the user historical comparisons to construct a personalized preference cone guiding response selection for online preference alignment. Specifically, it first discards responses that are confidently dominated under the estimated user preference, then selects two responses that are competitive under the retained preference cone yet exhibit the strongest unresolved disagreement. Theoretical analysis shows that, with high probability, the fraction of queries spent on cone-dominated responses vanishes as learning progresses. Experiments across multiple LLM families show that MPG-DPO outperforms strong contenders under matched query budgets.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Builds a **personalized preference cone** from low-cost multidimensional verifier evaluations of a user's historical comparisons — user-perceived uncertainty, not just model uncertainty.
  - Queries only **non-dominated, maximally disputed** response pairs: discards cone-dominated responses, then selects competitive responses with the strongest unresolved disagreement.
  - Proves the fraction of queries wasted on dominated responses **vanishes with high probability** as learning progresses; beats strong contenders at matched query budgets.

### 2. TAILROLL: Tail-Aware Rollout Acceleration for RL-Based LLM Post-Training
- **Authors:** Niloofar Gholipour, Marcos Assuncao, Timothy Yu, Gursimran Singh, Rajkumar Buyya, Julien Gascon-Samson, Zhenan Fan, Yong Zhang
- **Institution/Company:** Not specified on arXiv abstract/HTML page
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.22206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22206) (submitted 17 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Distributed, Parallel, and Cluster Computing (cs.DC)
- **Abstract:** RL post-training of large language models (LLMs) is bottlenecked by rollout: heavy-tailed responses leave engines idle as each step waits for the longest response. Asynchronous training masks, but does not eliminate, idle time. We distinguish imbalance waste (uneven placement and decode contention) from straggler waste (waiting for the longest response). Tail length, not average load, often sets the step bound. We propose TAILROLL, an online scheduler integrated into an open-source RL framework. It combines stateless in-flight migration and sufficiency-gated termination. Migration moves decoding requests between engines at chunk boundaries and resumes them with one re-prefill under a freeness-based, cost-aware tail-balancing policy. Termination stops a response when its GRPO group has enough completed samples for advantage estimation, masking its partial output from the training loss. Across ten configurations, TAILROLL reduces rollout bubble ratio by 21% to 74% and cross-engine KV-cache imbalance by 44% to 79%. In end-to-end RL training, it cuts rollout time by 24% to 34%, full step time by 21% to 22% on long-CoT RL, and p95 request latency by 28% to 37%, while raising generation throughput by up to 33% with comparable or higher final reward.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Separates **imbalance waste from straggler waste** and targets tail length rather than average load as the step-time bottleneck in RL rollout.
  - **Stateless in-flight migration** at chunk boundaries with one re-prefill, driven by a freeness-based, cost-aware tail-balancing policy.
  - **Sufficiency-gated termination** stops a response once its GRPO group has enough samples for advantage estimation; cuts rollout time 24–34% and p95 latency 28–37% with up to 33% higher generation throughput.

### 3. Thinking Harder Doesn't Lead to Better Choices: Reasoning Effort Increases the Confidence but Not the Correctness of LLM Shopping Agents
- **Authors:** Adi Krishnamoorthy, X. Y. Han
- **Institution/Company:** University of Chicago (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.22033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22033) (submitted 17 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computation and Language (cs.CL); Computers and Society (cs.CY); Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC)
- **Abstract:** Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous shopping agents that process multimodal product listings (consisting of visual and text data) and recommend purchases on behalf of consumers. Naturally, one might assume that models with more thinking power would make higher-quality purchasing decisions. To test this belief, we ask two questions about such agents: does asking a model to think harder make it choose better products, and does the confidence it reports tell us the likelihood it may be wrong? We benchmark ten frontier models on three product-choice benchmarks built from WebShop, Amazon's SQID and Wayfair's WANDS platforms to see if LLMs with more thinking power actually make better-quality product purchase decisions. Across all three benchmarks, we found that increasing the thinking effort does not increase decision quality in a statistically meaningful way. The same change in thinking effort raises stated confidence at a statistically significant level, so extra thinking buys confidence rather than correctness. We also measure the calibration of these models (how well their reported confidence percentage matches their true correctness percentage) in these tasks and show that most models are not well-calibrated at every effort level. We also find that errors are strongly shared: when one model errs in a task, several others tend to err on that same task. Furthermore, on tasks where several models all err, a large number of them pick the same wrong product.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A controlled **ten-model, three-benchmark study** of LLM shopping agents showing more reasoning effort buys **confidence, not correctness** (no significant quality gain).
  - Finds **most models are poorly calibrated** at every thinking-effort level on product-choice tasks.
  - Documents **strongly correlated errors** — models fail on the same tasks and converge on the same wrong product, undermining naive ensemble assumptions.

### 4. EvoDisco: Strategy Discovery for Evolving Agent Memory
- **Authors:** Mingyang Liu, Qingcan Kang, Shixiong Kai, Kaichao Liang, Xiaojin Fu, Tao Zhong, Linqi Song
- **Institution/Company:** City University of Hong Kong; Huawei Technologies Ltd. (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.22102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22102) (submitted 17 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Large language model agents accumulate experience in memory, and how that memory evolves during interaction is as important as what it stores. Recent methods update memory online, yet both the operators that transform memory and the rules that select them remain fixed by design. We propose EvoDisco, which formulates agent memory evolution as evolutionary strategy discovery: the agent must discover which operator to apply, when to apply it, and how to instantiate it, rather than rewriting memory under a static operator schedule. A learnable policy conditions on the current memory state and recent task history, then selects and instantiates one of four semantic operators---Abstract, Refine, Bridge, and Reframe---which respectively compress related memories, decompose overloaded entries, connect weakly linked experiences, and reorganize retrieved reasoning chains. The policy is updated with a bi-level fitness. An inner signal scores each transformation using a retrieval-quality improvement regularized by memory complexity. An outer signal scores strategic efficacy over a window of past decisions, combining historical operator-utility regret, utility-prediction error, and selection diversity. Under a one-pass online plus frozen-transfer protocol on ALFWorld, WebShop, and LifelongAgentBench (OS and DB), EvoDisco improves over strong memory baselines including MemP and MemRL. The largest gains are on ALFWorld ($+8.8$ online / $+12.9$ frozen-transfer points over MemP; $+16.4$ / $+17.9$ over MemRL). Ablations attribute these gains to both fitness levels and the operator library.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Recasts memory evolution as **strategy discovery**: a learned policy chooses which of four semantic operators (Abstract, Refine, Bridge, Reframe) to apply, when, and how to instantiate it.
  - **Bi-level fitness**: an inner retrieval-quality-improvement signal regularized by memory complexity, plus an outer strategic-efficacy signal combining regret, utility-prediction error, and selection diversity.
  - Beats MemP/MemRL on ALFWorld, WebShop, and LifelongAgentBench, with **+8.8 online / +12.9 frozen-transfer points** over MemP on ALFWorld.

### 5. Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights
- **Authors:** Jiajun Fan, Neil He, Changyi Yang, Andy Mao, Xi Zhu, Chaoran Cheng, Kaiqu Liang, Shuaike Shen, Zetian Li, Ruihan Guo, Ge Liu
- **Institution/Company:** UIUC; CMU; Rutgers; Princeton; Stanford (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.21885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Language models are trained once, frozen and deployed: improving one again takes another training run, and a prompt, a scaffold or extra computation at inference is gone once the task ends. A frontier model has been shown to edit the weights of a much smaller one. We ask whether a model can rewrite its own weights to make itself better, and find that it can, by its own reasoning, with no gradients and no training; we call this self-rewriting. Given its weights, graded answers and the ability to run itself, a 27B model diagnoses its failure and edits the weights behind it. It surpasses itself: its own edit makes it 6.6 points more accurate on 490 problems it never saw, in a test fixed in advance, and another session's edit also makes it significantly better. It keeps getting better: it submits an edit only when its own tests support it, each edit replacing the last, and over its first eight such sessions its accuracy only rises. Unlike test-time scaling, the gain persists in its weights and adds no cost per answer; unlike training, it needs no pre- or post-training infrastructure, only an open model and hours on one GPU. It repairs a damaged copy of itself: it locates the corrupted matrix and rewrites it with values of its own, never reading the undamaged weights; found on text math problems alone, the repair lifts the copy on six image benchmarks it never saw from 7% to 70% accuracy, 83% of the undamaged model's. And the ability emerges with scale: across four sizes of one family, the 2B model makes no edit and the 4B and 9B models break themselves; only the 27B model works on itself as a human would, comparing its hidden states on answers that finish and on answers that run out of tokens, weakening the direction that separates them. Like a human, a model need not separate training from inference; improving a language model may come to take a conversation rather than a training run.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Demonstrates **self-rewriting**: a 27B model, given its weights and graded answers, diagnoses its own failures and edits its weights by reasoning — **no gradients, no training run**.
  - Gains **persist in the weights** at no per-answer cost; self-gated edits improve accuracy over eight consecutive sessions, and a text-only-learned repair lifts a damaged copy from 7% → 70% on six unseen image benchmarks.
  - Shows the ability **emerges with scale** (2B makes no edit; 4B/9B break themselves; only 27B works), suggesting improvement can take the form of a conversation rather than a training run.

### 6. Large Language Models as Nonlinear Dynamical Systems: The Three Clocks of Depth, Training, and Generation
- **Authors:** Maor Farid
- **Institution/Company:** Leo AI, Inc. (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.21983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21983) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Machine Learning (cs.LG); Computation and Language (cs.CL); Nonlinear Sciences — Chaotic Dynamics (nlin.CD)
- **Abstract:** Language models are dynamical systems. A transformer is iterated over its layers, over the steps of its training and over the tokens it generates, and on each of these three clocks it shows behavior that nonlinear dynamics has studied for a century: fixed points, bifurcations, synchronization, chaos and noise-driven escape from potential wells. The two communities rarely meet, so this behavior is often rediscovered under new names. We build a bridge between them. To describe, we condense about 200 papers into the Three-Clock Dictionary, which maps fifteen concepts of nonlinear dynamics onto each clock and marks every correspondence as proved, measured or conjectured, and we show each concept in a mechanical system and in a language model side by side. To explain, we derive reduced-order models: the softmax is a Boltzmann distribution, gradient descent an Euler step on a nonlinear spring, a loss spike a ball thrown out of a stiff valley, attention on the circle a Kuramoto model, and a repetition loop a well that deepens while it is occupied. We then test these explanations on open language models, on a laptop CPU. Escape from repetition loops in GPT-2 and Pythia follows the Arrhenius law with the logit gap as the barrier, and the barrier deepens and then saturates, so the temperature needed to leave a loop falls like $1/\ln N$ with the generation length $N$ and rises with model size. Coherent text lives in a narrow window of temperatures, which a small reasoning model shares at its recommended temperature. Training makes each Pythia layer more expansive, and a small transformer trains at a multi-mode edge of stability. The third step, solving problems of language models, is still ahead: controllers derived from the escape analysis remove loops but do not yet beat the best fixed temperature. We state twelve open problems where nonlinear dynamics could get there.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A **Three-Clock Dictionary** condensing ~200 papers, mapping fifteen nonlinear-dynamics concepts onto the depth, training, and generation clocks, each marked proved/measured/conjectured.
  - **Reduced-order models** — softmax as Boltzmann distribution, gradient descent as Euler step on a nonlinear spring, attention on the circle as a Kuramoto model, repetition loops as deepening wells.
  - Empirically shows loop escape follows the **Arrhenius law** (temperature needed falls as $1/\ln N$, rises with model size) and states twelve open problems linking nonlinear dynamics to LM control.

## Recommendation Systems

### 7. Cluster-Guided Decoding with Trust-Region Constraints for Personalized Recommendation
- **Authors:** Gezheng Wen, Chao Shen, Raviteja Anantha, Tan Shen Cliff Chung, Layne C. Price
- **Institution/Company:** Amazon (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.22163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22163) (submitted 17 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- **Abstract:** Most recommenders score each item in the catalog separately, and do not explicitly model which semantic region of the catalog a recommendation belongs to. Models expressive enough to offer that control are often too slow to run over a full catalog for online inference. In this paper, we present Cluster-Guided Generative Reconstruction (CG2R), which learns a hierarchy of semantic clusters and uses a cluster-level prior to steer item ranking for personalized recommendations. Unlike existing systems that only use clusters to pre-select candidates, CG2R adds the cluster prior directly to the item scoring rule, so that the learned cluster structure shapes the ranking itself. Because the prior is assembled from cluster centroids rather than per-item statistics, it remains defined even for cold-start users when no history is available. Across the three benchmark datasets evaluated, CG2R outperforms classical, neural, and LLM-based baselines in terms of Recall and nDCG, with its performance gains widening as the catalog grows sparser. In addition, CG2R serves recommendations in milliseconds, suggesting potential as low-latency first-stage retrieval.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Cluster-Guided Generative Reconstruction (CG2R)** injects a learned semantic-cluster hierarchy prior **directly into the item scoring rule**, not just candidate pre-selection.
  - The prior is built from **cluster centroids**, so it remains defined for **cold-start users** with no history; gains widen as catalogs grow sparser.
  - Beats classical, neural, and LLM-based baselines on Recall/nDCG across three benchmarks while serving in **milliseconds** as low-latency first-stage retrieval. (WSDM 2027.)

### 8. KVUse: Reusable-KV User Memory for Efficient LLM Recommenders
- **Authors:** Xin Guo, Qiang Zhang, Xiang Zhou, Arushi Rai, Siyu Lin, Yifan Wu, Xiang Shen, Lei Huang, Qunshu Zhang, Xiangjun Fan, Lizhu Zhang
- **Institution/Company:** Meta Recommendation Systems (MRS) (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.21920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21920) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- **Abstract:** Memory-augmented recommenders built on large language models (LLMs) maintain a natural-language profile of each user. These profiles are updated by a user profile update path that revises the profile as new interactions arrive and are consumed by downstream task paths like ranking. Because these user profiles are stored as text in the user memory, every ranking LLM call that consumes them must splice them in the prompt and calculate the KV cache of them during the prefill phase. Given these ranking LLM calls are prefill heavy (long prompt but short decoded output) and repeatedly called, these repeated prefill of user profile text dominates the serving. Prefix caching cannot remove it, because these user profiles are preceded by different text in different calls. We present KVUse, which stores the user profiles as reusable key-value (KV) caches instead of text. KVUse trains lightweight LoRA writers by self-distillation, so that the user profile update call produces the reusable KV of the user profile while decoding it, and the LLM can read these reusable KV at new positions and behind new context without recomputation. KVUse also has a customized KV scheduler that uploads the stored reusable KV of the next batch while the current batch is computed, hiding the KV transfer from host memory behind GPU computation. On four InstructRec datasets with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, KVUse shows no statistically significant difference in ranking quality from full prefill over continuous memory update rounds. Under saturated load, KVUse raises the throughput of the downstream scoring path by 4.0x-4.5x and of the profile update path by 1.4x-1.9x.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Stores **user profiles as reusable KV caches instead of text**, eliminating repeated prefill — which prefix caching cannot fix because profiles sit behind different context in different calls.
  - **Self-distilled lightweight LoRA writers** produce reusable KV while decoding the profile update; the LLM reads them at new positions behind new context with no recomputation.
  - A **custom KV scheduler** overlaps next-batch KV upload with current-batch GPU compute; yields **4.0–4.5× scoring throughput** and 1.4–1.9× profile-update throughput with no significant ranking-quality loss.

### 9. Take a Step Back: A Dual-Granularity Raw Feature Empowered Intermediate Fusion Graph Convolutional Network for Multimodal Recommendation (DIGCN)
- **Authors:** Jinfeng Xu, Zheyu Chen, Shuo Yang, Jinze Li, Wenhao Yuan, Ziyue Peng, Puzhen Wu, Shujie Li, Jian Chen, Zheng Lin, Edith C. H. Ngai
- **Institution/Company:** The University of British Columbia; The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; The University of Hong Kong; HKUST; University of Luxembourg (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.19974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19974) (submitted 15 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- **Abstract:** Although multimodal recommendations have shown the ability to learn user preferences and item properties by leveraging explicit multimodal information, they remain constrained by two critical limitations: low-quality representation learning and modality fusion. For the former, existing solutions primarily focus on multi-granularity representation learning; however, we argue that training such representations is difficult due to the inherent data sparsity problem in recommendation scenarios. For the latter, existing solutions often rely on contrastive learning to align representations of different modalities to facilitate modality fusion, yet alignment-only objectives may over-compress valuable modality-specific information. To this end, we suggest "Take a Step Back" and propose a Dual-granularity raw feature empowered Intermediate fusion Graph Convolutional Network (DIGCN) for multimodal recommendation. DIGCN leverages the multimodal understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to construct dual-granularity raw features directly. Furthermore, DIGCN introduces an intermediate fusion graph convolutional network equipped with dynamic cross-modality weight correction, designed to capture cross-modality signals and achieve effective modality fusion. Comprehensive experiments on four real-world datasets validate that DIGCN consistently outperforms a variety of strong baselines. Additional analyses confirm its superiority under varying levels of data sparsity and cold-start scenarios. Notably, DIGCN demonstrates high extensibility, capable of further benefiting from additional contrastive learning-based alignment tasks.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Uses **LLMs to construct dual-granularity raw features directly**, sidestepping hard-to-train multi-granularity representations under recommendation data sparsity.
  - An **intermediate fusion GCN with dynamic cross-modality weight correction** captures cross-modal signals without over-compressing modality-specific information (an alignment-only failure mode).
  - Outperforms strong baselines on four datasets, with robustness to **sparsity and cold start**, and composes with extra contrastive alignment tasks. (WSDM 2027.)

### 10. Edit Recommendation: Can LLMs Anticipate Edit Requests from Users, and When?
- **Authors:** Daisuke Kikuta
- **Institution/Company:** NTT, Inc. (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.21155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21155) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Computation and Language (cs.CL); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC)
- **Abstract:** After a large language model (LLM) agent generates an artifact, users often send edit requests for it. This paper studies whether an LLM can anticipate these requests before they are sent. Such anticipation is useful in practice because it reduces the effort of user review and feedback. We formalize this task as edit recommendation and build EditRecBench, a benchmark for evaluating the performance of LLMs on this task, from real-world conversation logs in WildChat-4.8M. Through benchmarking and failure analysis, we identify a practical challenge that some cases are inherently difficult to predict in real-world settings. To address this, we introduce decision model-based gating to filter out cases where predictions are unlikely to be correct before they are presented to the user. Our trained model outperforms existing decision models and raises pass@5 among shown suggestions by 12--25 points at a gating threshold.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Formalizes **edit recommendation** — predicting a user's edit request before it is sent — and releases **EditRecBench** built from 4.8M real WildChat conversation logs.
  - Identifies inherently unpredictable cases and introduces **decision-model-based gating** that suppresses low-confidence suggestions before showing them to the user.
  - The gated model beats existing decision models and lifts **pass@5 among shown suggestions by 12–25 points**.

### 11. Straight to the Product: What Online Retailers Miss When Shoppers Ask AI First
- **Authors:** Michael Iannelli, Alan Ai
- **Institution/Company:** Scrunch AI (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.20378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20378) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR); Computers and Society (cs.CY); Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC)
- **Abstract:** When people ask an AI assistant what to buy, what happens next at the store? We link the same people's assistant conversations to their later browsing at twenty large online retailers. People go to the product the assistant named: among shoppers who had not visited the store in the past week, visits to the named product within a day rise from 0.02 to 2.07 per 100 answers (+2.0 points [1.7, 2.4]), and on days with a product-page visit at the store, 44% show a product from their own answer, against 1.5% for a matched answer given to someone else. The result replicates in a separate five-month period. Few arrive through the assistant's link: 89% of visits carry no sign of it, and 54% follow a web search, the same share as their usual visits. When a search comes just before the first visit, it names the answer's product 28% of the time (1% in earlier weeks), as if the search box were an address bar. The chat names the product; the store logs a search. Once there, shoppers keep browsing. We propose product follow-through, the share of answers followed by a visit to the product they named, as a way to evaluate shopping assistants and agents by what people do next: it needs no referral data or provider logs and can be computed for each assistant.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Links assistant conversations to later browsing at **20 online retailers**, showing shoppers do visit the AI-named product (0.02 → 2.07 visits per 100 answers for lapsed visitors; 44% vs. 1.5% matched control).
  - Finds **89% of visits carry no referral trace** and 54% follow a web search that names the answer's product 28% of the time — the search box acts as an address bar.
  - Proposes **product follow-through** as a referral-log-free, per-assistant evaluation metric for shopping agents based on downstream user behavior.

### 12. EE-Motion: An EEG-based Web Architecture for Live Affective Music Recommendation
- **Authors:** Sabino Roccotelli, Potito Aghilar, Vito Walter Anelli, Alejandro Bellogín, Tommaso Colafiglio, Michelantonio Trizio, Tommaso Di Noia
- **Institution/Company:** Politecnico di Bari; Universidad Autónoma de Madrid; Wideverse (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.21032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21032) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Information Retrieval (cs.IR); Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC)
- **Abstract:** Music listening is intrinsically tied to emotion, which drifts continuously within a single session. Although modern browsers interface with wearable brain-computer interfaces, recommendation systems remain structured around discrete logs and lack the infrastructure to integrate continuous physiological signals. Furthermore, existing affect-aware models rely on static user profiles and offline training, failing to serve anonymous newcomers without model retraining. We present a web platform that resolves both challenges. Pairing with a consumer electroencephalogram (EEG) headband over Web Bluetooth, the system captures live EEG, mapping frontal alpha asymmetry and arousal index to normalized valence-arousal proxy coordinates on Russell's circumplex plane. Driven by these coordinates, an affective adapter unifies structurally diverse recommendation backbones under a shared live scoring mechanism. By parameterizing anonymous visitors through a few favorite tracks, the module recommends tracks conditioned on live proxy coordinates. This demonstrates that real-time EEG-derived control signals can steer recommendations without persistent user tables or model retraining.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A **web platform** pairing a consumer EEG headband over Web Bluetooth, mapping frontal alpha asymmetry/arousal to **valence–arousal coordinates** on Russell's circumplex plane in real time.
  - An **affective adapter** unifies structurally diverse recommendation backbones under one live scoring mechanism driven by those coordinates.
  - Serves **anonymous newcomers** via a few favorite tracks — live EEG steering without persistent user tables or model retraining.

## Generative Recommendation & Semantic IDs

### 13. Exploration of Reinforcement Learning in Generative Recommendation Models
- **Authors:** Pengfei Zheng, Wuchao Li, Shiyao Wang, Honghui Bao, Hongyi Xie, Jiaxin Deng, Xingmei Wang, Jinghao Zhang, Yufei Ye, Ruiming Tang, Guorui Zhou, Han Li, Kun Gai, Defu Lian
- **Institution/Company:** University of Science and Technology of China; Kuaishou (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.19742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19742) (submitted 15 Nov 2026)
- **Subjects:** Machine Learning (cs.LG); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- **Abstract:** Traditional cascaded recommender systems balance accuracy and efficiency over large item spaces, but misaligned stage-wise objectives hinder end-to-end optimization. Generative recommendation unifies these stages in an autoregressive framework, providing a natural interface for reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize the entire generation process. However, directly applying RL often leads to unstable optimization and degraded recommendation quality. We revisit Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and other ratio-based objectives through a unified logit-gradient view. We identify three issues: (i) clipped surrogates create dead-gradient regions, under-utilizing samples; (ii) negative-advantage updates can become effectively unbounded as importance ratios grow, allowing a small subset of negatives to dominate optimization; and (iii) ratio-based control lacks sensitivity to absolute policy confidence, so updates on high-confidence cases may fail to vanish. Motivated by this diagnosis, we propose Gradient-Bounded Policy Optimization (GBPO), a simple objective with stop-gradient normalization that is simultaneously (a) fully gradient-active, (b) sign-symmetrically bounded for both positive and negative updates, and (c) explicitly confidence-weighted under current policy probabilities. This ensures that updates gracefully vanish for $\pi\!\to\!1$ positives and $\pi\!\to\!0$ negatives. Across the evaluated settings, GBPO achieves competitive recommendation performance with more stable training than competing RL methods.
- **Key innovations:**
  - A **unified logit-gradient diagnosis** of GRPO/ratio-based RL for generative recommendation: dead-gradient regions from clipping, unbounded negative-advantage updates, and insensitivity to absolute policy confidence.

- **Key innovations:**
  - Diagnoses **partial feature collapse** in masked-data-modelling time-series encoders — supervising only in input space leaves redundant token dimensions unconstrained.

  - Scales monotonically **7.1M → 116M parameters** where its predecessor declines; matches or beats existing encoders on **143 uni/multivariate datasets**, with code and weights released.

### 28. A Temporal Algebraic Framework for Data Stream Evaluation Under Delayed Labels and Distribution Shifts
- **Authors:** Ronaldo C. Prati, Elaine Ribeiro de Faria
- **Institution/Company:** CMCC, Universidade Federal do ABC (UFABC); FACOM, Universidade Federal de Uberlândia (UFU), Brazil (from arXiv HTML author block)
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2611.21914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21914) (submitted 16 Nov 2026)

- **Key innovations:**
  - Extends an **algebraic confusion-matrix framework** to streams with O(m²) per-step updates (independent of stream length), keeping pooled measures as weighted combinations of per-class ones.
