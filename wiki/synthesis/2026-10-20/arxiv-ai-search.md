# arXiv AI Search 2026-10-20 — the Tuesday fresh block, three days wide

> **Tuesday run, three-day window.** Monday's [10-19 search](../2026-10-19/arxiv-ai-search.md) landed on Friday 10-16 only (the Monday block had not posted). This run picks up the whole **Sat–Mon 2026-10-17 → 2026-10-19** submission band: **18 paced Export-API queries** (15–18 s apart, all successful) returned **541 unique papers with full abstracts** back to 2026-07; **251 are new to the wiki**, of which **155 fall in the 10-17/18/19 band itself** (91 on 10-19, 47 on 10-18, 13 on 10-17).
>
> **Four threads run through the fresh block.**
>
> **① The LLM lane is a *step-credit* block.** The dominant methodological move is to stop supervising trajectories uniformly and start pricing individual steps. [`2610.25876`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25876) (IGPO) scores step importance by *parameter-level causal intervention* and rescales the RL advantage accordingly → **up to 62% token reduction** at better accuracy; [`2610.25693`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25693) (ToolOPD) shows on-policy distillation's uniform-credit assumption fails — outcome-reversal counterfactuals move credit across turns — and reweights the distillation loss by how much a hindsight skill changes the teacher's probability of each action; [`2610.25860`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25860) prices *rollout support width* (top-K 10 → 20 → full) as an RLVR control knob (+1.80±0.42 at matched 150M-token budget). The same move appears in papers already claimed today: APSD [`2610.25973`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25973) (interpolate/extrapolate against a self-teacher by correctness), the off-policy PG anatomy [`2610.25725`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25725), and ClueFlow [`2610.25980`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25980) (attribution chains decide which transitions get supervised).
>
> **② Recommendation/ads is nearly dry, and the ad-side money shows up as *auctions* and *the interface around the model*, not click models.** Exactly **one** new-to-wiki recommendation paper in the whole three-day band ([`2610.25096`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25096) RetailBridge — enterprise agentic RAG over heterogeneous retail data, whose finding is that **multi-source chaining**, not model scale, is the bottleneck). **Explicit CTR-prediction papers: 0 — the 8th consecutive empty October window** (newest remains 10-09 ECC / 10-12 WeGR territory; continues the streak recorded by the [10-16](../2026-10-16/arxiv-paper-check.md), [10-17](../2026-10-17/arxiv-paper-check.md), [10-19](../2026-10-19/arxiv-ai-search.md) and [10-20 paper-check](arxiv-paper-check.md) runs). What is *not* dry: [`2610.24323`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24323) (LinkedIn) computes **multi-slot first-price pacing equilibria** at production scale via Fermi-Dirac entropy smoothing — ad-market clearing math, not CTR modelling; [`2610.25461`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25461) reports a **14.8-million-visitor randomized field trial** of a sales agent fine-tuned on 4.6M commercial conversations (**revenue/visitor +10.0%** vs no agent, engaged conversion +21.6% vs a frontier-model agent, non-inferior to human associates at **1.8% of the cost**); and the GEO/shopper cluster (thread ③) treats the generative answer itself as the ad surface.
>
> **③ New cluster: Generative Engine Optimization and AI-shopper influence.** Three fresh papers treat LLM answers as a marketplace to be audited and optimized. [`2610.24081`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24081) (Univ. of Toronto) runs a controlled location audit — 300 questions × 19 declared locations + 5 network locations, **31,200 answers** — and finds **ChatGPT follows the declared location down to the city while ignoring the network; Gemini does the opposite**, so visibility in generative search is location-specific on two different channels. [`2610.23541`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23541) calls for a **common GEO evaluation standard** (a meta-taxonomy of *where* sources are edited, *how*, and *which signals* they target) because each benchmark's private edit-space conflates optimizer gains with edit freedom. The third, [`2610.25843`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25843) (A Cue's Hidden Price — prices seller cues in *money* rather than choice rate, 113,472 trials), is already claimed by the sibling [paper-check](arxiv-paper-check.md).
>
> **④ New cluster: System-1 decision models (Jev and its neighbours).** Four papers this window build on or measure a typed-decision "System-1" head that answers with calibrated probabilities over supplied options instead of generating text: [`2610.25768`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25768) evaluates **Jev as a *decision reader of truncated CoT*** — at a 1,500-token budget it recovers **+4.1 pp** overall and **+8.6 pp** on truncated questions across 7 reasoners × 5 benchmarks (35 pairs), at an offline-replay cost of 34.5% of a full run; [`2610.25456`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25456) (System X) adds a free-text reply option that **continues from the decision's own KV cache** when confidence is low (ECE 0.292 → 0.169, accuracy unchanged); [`2610.25553`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25553) (Narrow Decision Models) argues for *organizational* System-1 heads — one small model per company, Choice/Score/typed interfaces, open architecture; [`2610.23346`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23346) uses Jev-as-a-judge for Brazilian-Portuguese IR relevance (quadratic-weighted κ 0.52–0.82; ≥94.5% of annotations exact or ±1 level). Read with the papers already claimed today (Claim Laundering [`2610.25947`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25947): decision models act on *logged claims* over *observations* in 53–100% of cases), this is a fast-forming measurement+product cluster — the decision-model layer is being benchmarked, productized, and stress-tested in the same week.

#### A1. Selling in the Wild: Benchmark and Randomized Field Evidence for a Sales Agent Trained on Commercial Conversations
- **Authors**: Benjamin Tannenbaum
- **Institution**: Javo / AISO Boost Ltd. (Israel, stated in the HTML author block)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25461) · cs.IR, cs.CY · submitted 2026-10-18
- **Abstract in one line**: a sales agent fine-tuned on 4.6M consented, de-identified commercial conversations is evaluated in four studies — two public sales benchmarks against 10 frontier/open-weight models; a **randomized controlled trial on 100 live websites across 14 industries** (1 May – 30 Sep 2026, 14.8M visitors randomized to no agent / Javo / a frontier-model agent with identical interface, catalog tools and guardrails); a model-isolating arm comparison; and a comparison against each site's own human associates on 20 staffed sites.
- **Key numbers**: vs no agent, **revenue per visitor +10.0%** (95% CI +5.1…+14.9). Among engaged visitors, conversion **+21.6%** relative to the frontier-model agent (CI +16.9…+26.3) — i.e. the fine-tune, not the interface, carries the gain. Against human associates: engaged conversion **11.26% vs 11.20%** — non-inferior (margin −7%, one-sided p=0.004), **not** superior — at **1.8% of the cost per conversation**. Return rates, unsupported product claims and complaint rates stayed within pre-specified safety guardrails.
- **Why it matters for this wiki**: this is the "advertising/recommendation" lane's strongest industrial receipt of the week, and it is a *sales-surface* receipt rather than a ranking one. The frontier-model control arm is what makes it usable evidence — most vendor field reports lack it. (Abstract-only; single-source.)

#### A2. Toward Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Enterprise Retail Workflows over Heterogeneous Data Sources (RetailBridge + RetailRAG)
- **Authors**: Jeonghwan Choi, Jibin Hwang, Hayoon Park, Hyeonjae Cheon, Hwanjun Song
- **Institution**: not listed (no HTML author block rendered; code/data released under `github.com/DISL-Lab/RetailBridge`)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25096) · cs.AI, cs.IR · submitted 2026-10-18
- **Abstract in one line**: a **compositional** retail benchmark built from persona-driven simulations, whose questions are constructed by *linking entities and intermediate answers across heterogeneous sources* (structured product/order records + unstructured inquiries/reviews) so that agents must identify sources, carry evidence between them, and answer with grounding. RetailRAG — auto-schema briefing, source-routed plan chaining, diagnostic plan revision — beats existing agentic-RAG baselines on every axis.
- **Key finding**: across **ten LLM backbones**, **multi-source chaining is the dominant bottleneck** at every provider and model scale → "reliable retail agents require explicit cross-source orchestration rather than scaling alone."
- **Why it matters**: it is the only new-to-wiki recommendation paper in the entire 10-17…10-19 band, and its message pairs with the enterprise-rec thread in `[[ctr-scaling-landscape]]` neighbours: the rec system's *agent* layer is now the interesting surface, and the failure mode is orchestration, not ranking quality. (Abstract-only; code release stated, not verified.)

#### A3. Computing Multi-Slot First-Price Pacing Equilibria at Scale via Entropic Smoothing
- **Authors**: Yuchen Xie, Shenyinying Tu, Yuan Gao, Yi Zhang, Deepak Kumar
- **Institution**: LinkedIn Corporation, Mountain View CA (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.24323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24323) · math.OC, cs.GT · submitted 2026-10-16
- **Abstract in one line**: multi-slot ad auctions break existing pacing-equilibrium solvers because allocation is discontinuous whenever two campaigns' scores cross. For first-price pacing equilibrium (FPPE) with *K* homogeneous slots and unit-demand campaigns, the Eisenberg–Gale dual reduces (via a TopK identity) to a convex program over bids; regularizing each request's allocation with the binary **Fermi–Dirac entropy** yields a smooth, strictly convex dual whose gradient is exactly *spend minus budget* and whose evaluation splits into **independent one-dimensional threshold solves per request**.
- **Key results**: smoothed equilibrium converges to the hard one at an explicit **O(√T)** rate; a **duality gap certifies** how far any bid vector is from the hard equilibrium; multi-GPU solver tested on a **production ad workload**, used to compute an equilibrium *price anchor* (each campaign's hindsight equilibrium cost per key result).
- **Why it matters**: the October ad-lane story is that click-modelling has migrated into *market-clearing and interface* problems. This is the former, in production, from the largest ad-market employer in the wiki's coverage. (Abstract-only.)

#### A4. KiranaBench: Can a Language Model Run a Neighbourhood Shop?
- **Authors**: Bhuvan Arora, Ankita Karmakar, Chaitanya Jain, Dilesh Churiwal, Harshit Jain, Shivansh Manish Dubey, Manish Prasad, Raja Piyush, Kshitij Tayal, Dhruv Kumar, Jagat Sesh Challa, Raunak Manekar, Yash Sinha
- **Institution**: BITS Pilani, K. K. Birla Goa Campus × BITS Pilani, Pilani Campus × University of Minnesota, Twin Cities (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.22980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22980) · cs.CL, cs.MA · submitted 2026-10-16
- **Abstract in one line**: existing "LLM runs a business" benchmarks are English, Western, single-agent and omniscient. KiranaBench simulates an **Indian general store** with four LLM-agent roles (manager, cashier, helper, bookkeeper), customer/supplier dialogue in **English, Hindi or Hinglish**, and demand calibrated to India's national Household Consumption Expenditure Survey.
- **Key finding** (4 open-weight LLMs): **all fall far short of a fixed-rule shopkeeper baseline** — the best model earns about **one-eighth** of its profit, and two models finish **below an unmanaged shop**. Performance is consistently lower for Hindi and Hinglish than for English supplier communication.
- **Why it matters**: a rare negative-result benchmark for agentic business operation, and the multilingual gap is a second, independent axis (language *and* long-horizon coordination). Pairs with the market-evolution finding in C6. (Abstract-only.)

#### A5. Causal Value Attribution of Marketing Labels in LLM-Generated Ads: A Deconfounder Approach (D-VAE) — *condensed*
- **Authors**: Danhong You, Wei Jiang, Zhiqiang Zheng (author list as parsed from API metadata)
- **Institution**: not listed (no HTML block; the PDF is 74 pages and states "under review at the *Journal of Marketing Research*")
- **arXiv**: [2610.23025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23025) · cs.AI, cs.CY · submitted 2026-10-16
- **Sketch**: value in generative marketing shifts from the artifact to the **upstream expert labels** that steer generation; estimating their causal effect is confounded by stochastic LLM mediation, high-dimensional label interactions and multimodal confounders. **D-VAE** = multi-treatment deconfounder + causal representation learning, with PAC-based bounds on omitted-variable bias. On **29,177 foundation-makeup ad creatives** it beats SOTA benchmarks; a field experiment shows recommended label configurations raise engagement **+32.7%** and are **statistically indistinguishable from human experts**.
- **Note**: an *economic-valuation* framing of ads generation rather than a performance one — relevant to the "value moves upstream of the model" thread. (Abstract-only; venue claim self-reported.)

#### B1. Learning to Reason Efficiently with Interventional Step Credit (ISC → IGPO)
- **Authors**: Guanda Li, Xuefeng Bai, Yulong Chen, Zhiyong Wang, Kehai Chen, Min Zhang
- **Institution**: Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen × University of Aberdeen × University of Cambridge (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25876) · cs.CL, cs.LG · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: existing step-level signals (attention mass, step entropy) measure *how the model behaves at a step*, not *that step's contribution to the answer*. **Interventional step credit (ISC)** assesses importance through targeted **parameter-level causal interventions**; **IGPO** uses ISC to rescale the RL advantage, suppressing redundant steps while preserving load-bearing ones.
- **Key results**: across **two model scales and nine benchmarks**, IGPO achieves **up to 62% average token reduction** with better overall performance than the compared methods.
- **Why it matters**: the sharpest statement of this block's thesis (correlate → cause). It is the methodological twin of ToolOPD (B5) and of the already-claimed APSD/ClueFlow/off-policy-anatomy papers — see Trend 1.

#### B2. Adaptive Decoding Methods for Token-Efficient Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors**: Zhaonan Li*, Zelei Cheng*, Amritansh Mishra, Chia-Hsuan Lee, Mann Patel, Sambit Sahu, Ben Zhou, William M. Campbell (*joint)
- **Institution**: AI Foundations × Arizona State University (School of Computing and Augmented Intelligence) × Capital One (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25860) · cs.LG, cs.AI · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: in RLVR, *rollout decoding support* determines both which trajectories you train on and how many completion tokens you burn collecting them. The main schedule **widens candidate support from top-K=10 → K=20 → full** as the attempted-token budget grows.
- **Key results**: in a 4B code-RLVR setting the schedule beats the matched fixed-K=10 continuation in **all three runs** at an interpolated **150M-token validation budget**, mean gain **+1.80 ± 0.42** points, and is comparable to a separately trained fixed-K=20 (the strongest fixed setting in their sweep). Honest caveats in the abstract: the follow-up evidence is "noisy", mixed-reward-group diagnostics do not explain the gain, and a single-run test replay yields identical point estimates.
- **Why it matters**: sampling efficiency is the unglamorous half of the token-cost story; this treats *decoding config* as a training-time resource-allocation decision. (Abstract-only.)

#### B3. Statistical Learning Theory for Direct Preference Optimization
- **Authors**: Joel Wills, Yuanfan Li, Yiming Ying, Ding-Xuan Zhou
- **Institution**: The University of Sydney, School of Mathematics and Statistics (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25856) · cs.LG, stat.ML · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: DPO's logistic objective assumes Bradley–Terry. The paper separates **structural misspecification** from capacity limits and finite-sample error, and characterizes the structural term by the **triangle-curl of the preference log-odds**, which **vanishes exactly when preferences admit a Bradley–Terry representation**.
- **Key results**: excess logistic risk ≤ (score-class approximation) + (triangle-curl); a matching **lower bound** proportional to triangle-curl under boundedness; for ReLU score models (incl. clipped) **increasing capacity removes the approximation term but leaves the structural obstruction unchanged**; finite-sample bounds for clipped ERM that separate the three error sources; results placed in a surrogate-risk framework via **contextual preference accuracy (CPA)** as the policy property DPO's objective is consistent for.
- **Why it matters**: a rare *theorem-level* statement that DPO's ceiling is about preference geometry, not model size — directly relevant to the wiki's alignment/LC method pages. (Abstract-only; no full-text check of proofs.)

#### B4. Is it RL or Data? A Controlled Comparison of GRPO and Rejection-Sampling Fine-Tuning
- **Authors**: Lakshya Chaudhry (single author)
- **Institution**: Purdue University (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25812) · cs.LG · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: Bauer et al. (2026) found GRPO on **mixed-difficulty** prompts gets up to 5× the sample efficiency of easy-only in low-data RLVR. This paper asks whether that is an *RL* result or a *data* result: same procedural counting task, Qwen3-4B-Base + LoRA, GRPO vs RFT on identical prompts/verifier/token cap; **six primary arms × three seeds**; hypotheses and falsifiers **registered before training**; RFT given a **27-run hyperparameter sweep** while GRPO uses the published recipe unchanged.
- **Key results**: **GRPO outperforms RFT on every prompt set** — **+10.9 points** on held-out problems with curated prompts, **+8.4 / +9.2** in post-hoc matched-rollout comparisons on easy/mixed. Curating prompts does **not** help RFT (−2.7); mixed-difficulty data helps RFT (+3.7) while for GRPO the evidence is inconclusive. An **iterated-RFT** arm (resampling from the current policy) recovers **3.2 of the 10.9** gap (~30%). OOD, truncation prevents a conclusion.
- **Why it matters**: one of the few preregistered, swept-baseline, multi-seed dissections of the "GRPO needs mixed difficulty" claim; the iterated-RFT control suggests the remaining gap is genuinely algorithmic, not just data selection. Single author, synthetic task family — scope is limited and says so. (Abstract-only.)

#### B5. ToolOPD: Credit-Aware On-Policy Distillation for Tool-Use Agents
- **Authors**: Chu Zhao, Lei Tang, Yanming Wang, Jianzhe Zhao, Guibing Guo, Zhengzong Chen, Yuanyuan Zhao, Fei Huang
- **Institution**: Honor Device Co., Ltd × Northeastern University (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25693) · cs.AI · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: standard on-policy distillation assigns **uniform credit** along a trajectory. Credit-distribution analysis plus **outcome-reversal counterfactuals** show (a) credit concentrates on a **small number of critical actions**, and (b) reversing only the final outcome — trajectory held fixed — **substantially changes credit assigned to multiple turns**, so uniform credit both misses critical decisions and injects conflicting signal.
- **Method**: ToolOPD summarizes each completed trajectory+outcome into a **reusable hindsight skill**, measures how that skill changes the **teacher's probability of each student action token**, converts the change into **outcome-aware credit**, reweights the action-level distillation loss by it, and jointly optimizes with RL.
- **Key results**: across multiple long-horizon tool-interaction benchmarks, significantly outperforms RL and self-distillation baselines (magnitudes not given in the abstract).
- **Why it matters**: the same week, on the distillation side, as IGPO (B1) on the RL side — and its counterfactual methodology is the strongest evidence yet in this wiki that trajectory-level advantage in long tool chains is a *turn-local* quantity. (Abstract-only.)

#### B6. UtilityBench: Measuring Language Model Utility When Errors Have Different Costs
- **Authors**: Qifeng Lei, Zhiyong Yang, Zitai Wang, Shilong Bao, Linchao Zhang, Qianqian Xu, Qingming Huang
- **Institution**: not listed (no HTML block rendered; Qingming Huang is a Harbin-Institute-of-Technology lineage name — **inferred, do not treat as verified**)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25924) · cs.LG, cs.AI · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: mainstream eval protocols treat all errors as equal. **UtilityBench**: 15,000 instances with verifiable answers, error severity quantified by **Best-Worst Scaling**, utilities shaping answer-vs-abstain decisions; **UtilityAUC** = area under the utility curve induced by a model's response ordering.
- **Key results**: theory shows UtilityAUC separates models where standard accuracy and AURC cannot; across **20 SOTA models**, greater accuracy / more advanced architectures **do not reliably translate** into higher UtilityAUC — a gap between problem-solving ability and *responsible decision-making*.
- **Why it matters**: it is the cost-sensitive-eval twin of CXBench (paper-check #12, process-compliance eval) and of the paper-check's diatom-calibration bullet — the eval lane this week is about **what a wrong answer costs**, not how often models are right. (Abstract-only.)

#### B7. Truncated Reasoning Under a Budget: Evaluating System-1 Models as Decision-Makers
- **Authors**: Zhongyin Wang, Zhaoqian Xue, Junting Mu, Haozheng Liu, Ye Yuan, Haolun Wu, Lingyao Li

- **arXiv**: [2610.25768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25768) · cs.CL · submitted 2026-10-19

#### B8. MemDerive: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Agent Memory over Evolving Data
- **Authors**: Sen Gao, Yongan Li, Han Chen, Bingsheng He, Ming Wu, Jason Zeng, Michael Heinrich, Shixuan Sun
- **Institution**: National University of Singapore × Shanghai Jiao Tong University × Zero Gravity Labs (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.25373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25373) · cs.DB, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.IR · submitted 2026-10-18
- **Abstract in one line**: long-running agents' task state is usually a **derived state** — no record states it; it must be computed by rules from multiple records of an evolving history. Existing memory benchmarks score only the final answer, so a wrong answer cannot be traced to the state that was missed, and a silently outdated state goes undetected. MemDerive specifies each scenario **symbolically**, derives gold answers with **Datalog**, records the **gold derivation DAG** and supporting events per query, and renders histories in natural language with distractors.
- **Key numbers**: 10 scenarios, **4,692 events, 1,503 queries**, difficulty varied along access/evolution/composition. On derived state **every memory system falls behind simple BM25 retrieval**, although the same answer model reaches **96.01%** when given the supporting events. Most failures are **ungathered** (the system *holds* the missing intermediate state but does not retrieve it for the question that needs it); systems also rarely **propagate implicit changes**. Design principles: gather inputs along the derivation, store rules with their versions, maintain derived states through recorded dependencies.
- **Why it matters**: it converts "agent memory is bad" from a claim into a *diagnosis*, and the BM25-loses-to-nothing baseline is the kind of result this wiki's memory pages should record. Pairs with the memory one-liners below and with the on-file `JointCore` memory-selection paper. (Abstract-only.)

#### B9. StopLoss: Teaching Agents to Predict Failure and Stop Early
- **Authors**: Jiarong Liang, Xueguang Ma, Dongfu Jiang, Yuxuan Zhang, Ping Nie, Wenhu Chen

- **arXiv**: [2610.25525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25525) · cs.AI · submitted 2026-10-18

- **Why it matters**: the operational half of the "budget as a variable" thread — an *external* monitor is a deployable pattern that needs no change to the agent loop, and the paper is careful to report the failure-prediction signal, not just the dollars. (Abstract-only.)

#### C2. Cooperation Without Shared Rewards via Reciprocal Q-Augmentation
- **Authors**: Francesco Morri, Adel Abusitta, Quentin Cappart, Margarida Carvalho
- **Institution**: UCLouvain × Polytechnique Montréal (Mila) × Université de Montréal (Mila) (stated)

- **Key results**: when **all** agents follow RQA's cooperative policy, they learn to cooperate **faster and reach higher cooperation** than fully-cooperative (shared-reward) algorithms; and a **single** RQA agent that switches appropriately can **induce cooperation** in populations trained with standard RL that would otherwise defect. - **Why it matters**: a constructive answer to the "cooperation needs shared reward or shared state" assumption, with the rare single-agent-induces-population result. (Abstract-only.)

#### C3. Breaking the 1/3 Barrier for Approximate Nash Equilibria in Symmetric Bimatrix Games

ACTS runs **CTDE** with a dual attention: a **Role-conditioned Relational Attention** selects variable-cardinality partners from roles + evolving execution state, and a **Topological Attention Mixer** embeds the directed communication topology into **team value decomposition** for credit assignment. Across **9 benchmarks** (code, multi-step reasoning, knowledge, competitive math): up to **+6.19 pp** average accuracy with **>15% fewer tokens** than SOTA baselines.

#### C6. LLM Evolution in a Simple Simulated Market — *condensed*
- **Authors**: Charlie Pilgrim, Christopher Krupenye, Dora Biro, Richard P. Mann
- **Institution**: Johns Hopkins University × University of Leeds × University of Rochester (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.24033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24033) · cs.MA, cs.AI, cs.CL, q-bio.PE · submitted 2026-10-16
- **Sketch**: populations of **20 LLM agents** differ only in system prompt; bankrupt agents are replaced by mutated copies of successful prompts. A market that **rewards accuracy** selects high accuracy (**94%**) and high token use; a market that **charges per token** selects low accuracy (**10%**) and low token use — with only tokens incentivised, accuracy still collapses. Token use and accuracy are **correlated traits**, so selection on one moves the other and behaviour adapts along an **unpriced** dimension. A third neutral condition separates these effects from mutation-operator bias. Agents are never told the payment rule, get no feedback, and have no memory — "adaptation occurs through a collective optimisation process above the level of individual agents". Directly extends this wiki's market-selection material (pairs with the CATH/Prompt-market papers on file).

## 5. Trend Notes

1. **The step-credit block is real and cross-method.** Four independent papers this window make the same move — *uniform trajectory supervision is the bug* — on four different machinery: IGPO ([2610.25876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25876), parameter-level causal intervention → advantage rescaling, 62% token cut), ToolOPD ([2610.25693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25693), hindsight-skill likelihood shifts → distillation-loss reweighting, with outcome-reversal counterfactuals as evidence), Adaptive Decoding ([2610.25860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25860), support width as the resource being allocated), and — already claimed by siblings — APSD ([2610.25973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25973)), ClueFlow's attribution chains ([2610.25980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25980)), and the off-policy PG anatomy ([2610.25725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25725)). When RL, distillation, and decoding all converge on the same abstraction, the abstraction is probably the field's next default; the open question is which credit estimator wins (intervention vs likelihood-shift vs attribution graph).
2. **The rec/ads lane is empty where the wiki's name is and busy where the money is.** Zero explicit CTR-model papers for the **8th consecutive October window**, and only one new rec paper in three days (RetailBridge, whose headline is that *orchestration* is the bottleneck). Meanwhile the ad-side yield is two production-grade receipts of a different kind: LinkedIn's multi-slot FPPE solver ([2610.24323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24323)) and Javo's 14.8M-visitor RCT ([2610.25461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25461)). If this persists another two weeks, `[[ctr-scaling-landscape]]` should be reframed from "what's new in CTR" to "what replaced CTR".
3. **New cluster: GEO and the AI-shopper interface.** Three fresh papers (two full in §4.1, one claimed by the sibling paper-check) treat the generative answer as an ad surface: influence priced in *money* ([2610.25843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25843)), *location channels* differing per engine ([2610.24081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24081)), and a *common evaluation standard* ([2610.23541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23541)). The engineering implication from Toronto's audit is the sharpest: an audit run from one place measures one engine's channel and silently mismeasures the other's. Expect a GEO-audit page in this wiki soon; the cluster is dense enough to justify it.
4. **New cluster: System-1 / decision-native models.** Four papers build on or measure typed-decision heads with calibrated probabilities over supplied options (B7 + the B1-bis table): Jev-as-truncation-reader, KV-cache text continuation, organization-narrow variants, and a Brazilian-Portuguese IR validity check. Read against the sibling paper-check's Claim Laundering ([2610.25947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25947) — decision models act on logged claims over observations in 53–100% of cases, fixable with a layer-level veto), the pattern is: **the decision-model layer is being productized (System X), specialized (NDM), benchmarked (Truncated Reasoning), applied (IR judging) and attacked (Claim Laundering) within a single week**. Whether Jev itself is the durable artifact or merely the first well-instrumented one is the open question; nothing here verifies the vendor or the model's training data.
5. **Agent memory is being rebuilt as *derived state maintenance*, not storage.** MemDerive ([2610.25373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25373)) provides the diagnostic frame — gold derivation DAGs show the dominant failure is *ungathered* intermediate state, and BM25 beats every memory system on derived state — while the surrounding one-liners implement the response: bounded recurrent summaries (RSM), retrieval fed *into* learned memory (CoMem), revisable topic graphs (MemRevise), production compaction with honest time-cost accounting (ContextCompact 2.0), and the critical counterpoint that self-evolving harness rewriting is **not** continual learning ([2610.25858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858): plasticity costs stability, retrieval collides). If a `[[agent-memory]]` page exists by the next lint, this is its evidence base.
6. **Budget and energy have become first-class evaluation variables.** ATEST ([2610.25519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25519)) measures a **4.1× energy swing** for the same query under GPU load and budgets in *joules*; StopLoss ([2610.25525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25525)) buys **−4.5% / −31%** cost-per-retained-solve with an external failure monitor; UtilityBench ([2610.25924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25924)) prices *errors* rather than counting them; AsyncOPSD ([2610.25880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25880)) prices training throughput; and the sibling GLOBE ([2610.25970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25970)) cuts output tokens by ~half. The recurring methodological discipline worth copying: several of these report the *failure* of their own budget scheme (ContextCompact's vanishing gain under a 45-minute cap; ATEST's tail-latency trade-off; Adaptive Decoding's noisy follow-ups).
7. **Games: the win-rate paper recedes.** Of six fresh game/MAS papers, one is chess *style control* (CHSM8), one is approximation theory with a 2011 barrier broken (1/3 → 1−1/√2+δ, SODA 2027), one is a **reward-free** swarm that gets 64% of the way to the RL optimum with no reward signal, one is a general-sum cooperation mechanism that works with **no shared rewards**, one is communication-topology scheduling for LLM MAS, and one shows market selection on *price* collapses accuracy along an unpriced correlated trait. The through-line is **mechanism and selection pressure**, not agent strength — consistent with the 10-19 game-rl-daily's finding that the frontier questions moved to adaptability, consensus and verification.
8. **Affiliation provenance improved this run.** 29 of 33 `arxiv.org/html` fetches yielded rendered author blocks, so nearly every affiliation above is *stated* rather than inferred — a step up from the 10-15…10-19 runs, which had to rely on author-lineage inference or Semantic Scholar fallbacks that rate-limited. Five featured papers remain "not listed" (RetailBridge, D-VAE, UtilityBench, CHSM8, Narrow Decision Models); none were inferred.
