### 2611.33644v1 — "Physics-Informed Learning for Multiplayer Differential Games" (2026-11-25)
- **Authors**: Takuma Adams; Andrew C. Cullen; Tansu Alpcan
- **Affiliation**: **Takuma Adams** — Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, **The University of Melbourne**, Parkville, Australia; **and Defence Science and Technology Group, Australia**; **Andrew C. Cullen** — School of Computing and Information Systems, **The University of Melbourne**, Parkville, Australia; **Tansu Alpcan** — Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, **The University of Melbourne**, Parkville, Australia. Funding: Commonwealth Australian Government Research Training Program Scholarship.
- **Venue/conference**: **Accepted to IEEE Control Systems Letters (L-CSS) with presentation at CDC 2026**; arXiv preprint 2026-11-25; cs.GT / cs.LG / eess.SY / math.OC.
- **Abstract**: Computing per-player feedback strategies in N-player adversarial differential games is challenging as the associated coupled Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman system is high-dimensional and generally lacks classical solutions. We address this by approximating viscosity solutions using physics-informed neural networks within a policy iteration scheme with a vanishing-viscosity annealing schedule. Theoretically, we establish an explicit ε-Nash guarantee by bounding per-player regret via computable residuals and prove our method reaches this stopping criterion in finite time. For two-player zero-sum games, we further establish a computable bound on the approximation error of the value function. Numerical experiments on pursuit-evasion scenarios illustrate the tightness of these computed bounds and the framework's scalability to high-dimensional environments.
- **Key innovations**: **PINN-based policy iteration** for **N-player adversarial differential games**; **viscosity-solution** approximation + **vanishing-viscosity annealing**; **explicit ε-Nash guarantee** via computable **residuals** + **neural verification** (∂-CROWN); finite-time stopping; **computable value-function error bound** for two-player zero-sum; evaluated on **pursuit-evasion**, scaling to high-dimensional joint states; mesh-free alternative to curse-of-dimensionality grids. → also §6.

### 2611.33081v1 — "Breaking the Square-Root Barrier for Linear Swap Regret in Polyhedral Games" (2026-11-24)
- **Authors**: Vasilis Pollatos; Alberto Marchesi
- **Affiliation**: **Vasilis Pollatos** — Department of Mathematics, **National and Kapodistrian University of Athens**; **Archimedes, Athena Research Center, Greece**; **Alberto Marchesi** — **Politecnico di Milano**.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-24; cs.GT.
- **Abstract**: We study linear swap regret in self-play against all affine self-maps of each player's strategy polytope. Efficient adversarial bounds for this class scale as √T. On compact polytopes given by linear inequalities, our deterministic uncoupled dynamics achieve Õ(poly(D) T^(1/3)) cumulative regret, where D is the binary length of the game description. The bound is independent of the number of vertices. In perfect-recall n-player extensive-form games with at most M terminal sequences per player, we obtain O(n² M⁴ log T) regret against all linear deviations, extending the logarithmic trigger-regret bound of Anagnostides, Farina, and Sandholm [AFS23]. This class can strictly contain the trigger deviations [FP23] and consists exactly of the deviations implementable by untimed communication (UTC), in which a player interleaves moves with mediator queries along a separate feasible path [ZFS24]. The proof combines optimistic log-barrier learning on a compact matrix lift with stability of the Gordon fixed point, using treeplex geometry in extensive form.
- **Key innovations**: **linear swap regret** (affine self-maps on strategy polytopes); **breaks √T barrier** → **Õ(poly(D) T^(1/3))**; **O(n²M⁴ log T)** in perfect-recall extensive form; strictly extends **trigger deviations**; **UTC** (untimed communication) characterization; **optimistic log-barrier** + **Gordon fixed point** + **treeplex geometry**; deterministic uncoupled dynamics; **self-play** convergence insight. Theoretical; not a video-game policy paper. → §7.

### 2611.31149v1 — "Optimal Regret Lower Bounds in Matrix Games" (2026-11-23)
- **Authors**: Taira Tsuchiya
- **Affiliation**: **Taira Tsuchiya** — **The University of Tokyo; The University of Osaka; RIKEN**.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-23; cs.GT / cs.LG; 22 pages, 2 figures.
- **Abstract**: We study learning in two-player zero-sum games, also known as matrix games, in which two players with m and n actions repeatedly select mixed strategies for T rounds. The learning dynamics based on optimistic Hedge guarantee regrets of O(log(mn)), which improve to O(√(log m log n)) if each player knows the number of actions of the opponent, whereas matching regret lower bounds have been known only for specific learning dynamics. We prove that, for every deterministic learning dynamic, there exists a payoff matrix for which the maximum individual regret is Ω(√(log m log n)) for a wide range of m and n. Together with the upper bound O(√(log m log n)), this lower bound shows that the minimax regret of deterministic learning dynamics in matrix games is Θ(√(log m log n)) in this regime. For all m, n, and T, we also prove the lower bound Ω(min{T, log min{m, n}}). These lower bounds hold even if each player also observes the strategies and the feedback of the opponent. Our main technical contribution is an adaptive construction of the payoff matrix. While running the algorithms of the two players, we add in each round a rank-one matrix that keeps the feedback of the previous rounds unchanged and increases a lower bound on the sum of the regrets of the two players. As by-products, we obtain lower bounds on the rate at which the average strategies converge to a Nash equilibrium and on the query complexity of matrix-vector products that deterministic algorithms need to compute an approximate Nash equilibrium.
- **Key innovations**: **optimal regret lower bounds** for **matrix games**; **Θ(√(log m · log n))** matching **optimistic-Hedge** upper bound; holds for **every deterministic learning dynamic** even with opponent observation; **adaptive rank-one** payoff construction; **Ω(min{T, log min{m,n}})**; corollaries on **Nash-convergence rate** + **approximate-Nash query complexity**; bounds **self-play** equilibrium learning. Theoretical; not a video-game policy paper. → §7.
- **Links**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31149v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31149v1)

### 2611.28897v1 — "Last-Iterate Convergence of Payoff-Gradient Learning in Möbius Pricing Games" (2026-11-21)
- **Authors**: Martin Bichler; Philip Hierhager; Jan-Sebastian Höhener
- **Affiliation**: **Martin Bichler, Philip Hierhager, Jan-Sebastian Höhener** — Decision Sciences & Systems, School of Computation, Information and Technology, **Technical University of Munich, Germany**. Funding: **ERC** Horizon Europe (grant 101198689). Corresponding author m.bichler@tum.de.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-21; cs.GT / math.AP.
- **Abstract**: We prove global last-iterate convergence of payoff-gradient learning in every typewise Riemannian metric, which for scalar actions is the same as typewise mirror descent, with explicit rates, in a class of Bayesian bidding and pricing games that includes first-price auctions, unit-demand pay-as-bid auctions, rank-based generalized first-price auctions, and homogeneous-good price competition with inelastic demand. The result holds for symmetric self-play: all players use a common strategy and apply the same payoff-gradient update at each type. Convergence is from every continuous weakly increasing feasible initial strategy, including pooling, and does not rely on the monotonicity or Minty conditions that underlie existing guarantees in other games. Instead, the class is characterized by two structural properties: a direct payoff effect that is independent of the action, and a payoff difference at the allocation threshold that is affine in the action. These properties reduce the learning dynamics to a singular transport-damping equation for the common strategy, which, under the stated coefficient and metric assumptions, admits a unique viscosity solution. Comparison barriers make the uniform distance to the unique rest point a strict Lyapunov functional and yield convergence rates with optimal algebraic exponents. In allocation games, the rest point is the unique weakly increasing symmetric equilibrium under the stated tie-breaking conditions, and a positive lower slope bound converts strategy-error bounds into explicit approximate-equilibrium guarantees. A projected grid scheme with a slope floor makes the approximation constructive, with error bounds under deterministic perturbations and almost-sure convergence on a fixed grid under martingale gradient noise.
- **Key innovations**: **global last-iterate convergence** (not just average-iterate); **payoff-gradient / typewise mirror descent** in any typewise Riemannian metric; explicit rates; **Möbius pricing games** (first-price, pay-as-bid, rank-based GFPA, price competition); **symmetric self-play**; no monotonicity/Minty; singular **transport-damping** PDE; Lyapunov + **viscosity solution**; constructive grid scheme + noise; approximate-equilibrium guarantees. Theoretical. → §7.
- **Links**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28897v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28897v1)

### 2611.31979v1 — "Reading, Writing and Repairing Game-Theoretic Policies in Weight Space" (2026-11-24)
- **Authors**: Ancil Crayton
- **Affiliation**: **Ancil Crayton** — **Algoverse AI Research**.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-24; cs.CL / cs.AI; 7 pages, 7 tables.
- **Abstract**: Can a language model work in the weight space of a policy network for a repeated game? We put four questions to a mid-size open model, on recurrent networks of 1,218 weights with exactly checkable answers. Given a population of opponents described in English, write a policy that is optimal: its value is a finite computation over the agent's information state, so a submitted network has an exact gap from the optimum. Given a network's weights, report its action after each of fifteen opponent histories; recover the state machine it implements, judged on every opponent sequence to depth ten; or repair a network whose output was altered in one place and say which round first goes wrong. Repair is the exception: 4 of 18 repaired, 5 of 18 diagnosed, with a median patch covering one of the two entries needed.
- **Key innovations**: **LLM reading/writing neural policy weights** in a **repeated game**; exactly checkable scoring (exact optimality gap; per-action; state-machine recovery; fault localization + patch); four tasks — **Write**, **Identify**, **Read**, **Repair**; **symbolic state numbering invariance** (61/62 same program); results: recover 62/66, behavioral 34/36, optimum 20/20, **repair not solved** (4/18 repaired, 5/18 diagnosed, median patch 1/2 entries); small (1,218-weight) recurrent nets; no new trained game-agent framework. **Game AI Bot + Game Foundation Model** angle.

### 2611.26963v1 — "Causal Diffusion for Extreme Super-Resolution in Interactive Games" (2026-11-20)
- **Authors**: Christina Zhang; Jenna Kang; Colin Groth; Akshay Jindal; Stavros Diolatzis; Qi Sun
- **Affiliation**: **Christina Zhang, Jenna Kang, Colin Groth, Qi Sun** — **New York University**; **Akshay Jindal, Stavros Diolatzis** — **Intel Corporation**. Christina Zhang & Jenna Kang equal contribution. → §6.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-20; cs.CV.
- **Abstract**: Existing game super-resolution methods typically operate at moderate upscaling factors, struggling to recover fine visual details from extremely low-resolution inputs (e.g., 120p). At such resolutions, super-resolution shifts from reconstructing existing information toward synthesizing missing visual details. While video diffusion models offer powerful generative priors for this challenging task, their dependence on future frames limits their applicability to interactive games. We present CIDER, a causal diffusion framework for extreme game super-resolution that synthesizes high-frequency details from current and past low-resolution frames, guided by G-buffers (depth and motion vectors) to preserve scene structure and temporal consistency. - **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-21; cs.CL; 14 pages, 2 figures, 10 tables. Pre-registered protocol (v1–v5, amendments committed before games). Engine/harness/protocol: https://github.com/federicolucca/hexfolk (tag `bench-v1`). Companion to arXiv:2610.32955 and arXiv:2611.21393. No frontier model; paid pairings ≤50 games by budget.
- **Abstract**: Measure Before You Train argued that an agent's decisions should be measured against ground truth, under a protocol fixed in advance, before anyone trains or replaces the model behind them. We carry the method to a second, very different domain, a turn-based strategy game, and ask a deliberately modest question: how well do low-cost agents play under a pre-registered protocol? hexfolk is a minimal two-player 4X game written for this purpose, so no model has seen its rules or positions. A deterministic engine, one agent interface, a referee that never corrects an illegal or late move, and per-decision logs make every game replayable; questions and expected answers were committed before the games, on held-out seeds with mirrored seats and 95% Wilson intervals. Two small open language models (8B and 3B) lose every game to a few-line rule player. Answering in free text they are invalid on 98% of their decisions; constrained to legal moves they never play an invalid one, yet they lose to the uniformly random player (the 8B model wins 10.0 [4.3, 21.4]% of games), refuting our expectation that they would beat it; the result survives a control that shuffles the order of the options. - **Key innovations**: **hexfolk** minimal 2-player **4X strategy game**; **pre-registered** protocol + held-out seeds + mirrored seats + **95% Wilson intervals**; deterministic engine + replayable per-decision logs + referee; exact ground truth; **legality vs competence** separation; small open LLMs (8B/3B) + native decision models + paid agents; free **60 ms/move search** baseline beats paid models; honest negative results; **no frontier model**. → §5.

#### 2611.32691v1 — "Load Balancing Neurons: Firing-Rate Control Sustains Plasticity in Continual Learning" (2026-11-24)
- **Authors**: Jan Robine; Marc Höftmann; Stefan Harmeling
- **Affiliation**: **Jan Robine, Marc Höftmann, Stefan Harmeling** — Department of Computer Science, **TU Dortmund University**; **Lamarr Institute for Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence**.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-24; cs.LG / cs.NE.
- **Abstract**: Neural networks in continual learning often lose plasticity: some neurons become inactive, while others fire almost constantly. This limits adaptation to shifting data and wastes capacity. Prior work mitigates this by periodically reinitializing low-utility units, but such resets can destroy previously learned features and do not proactively prevent low utility. We study a simple diagnostic measure: the firing rate of ReLU units, defined as the fraction of positive pre-activations. Low rates identify dead units, while very high rates indicate linearized, always-on units. Based on this view, we introduce a lightweight load-balancing mechanism that adjusts per-neuron thresholds to keep firing rates within a target range. Across continual ImageNet and class-incremental CIFAR-100, firing-rate distributions explain differences in plasticity across a range of approaches, including well-known techniques such as L2 regularization and non-affine normalization, and load balancing matches or exceeds reset-based methods on both benchmarks. The same mechanism carries over to reinforcement learning, improving Rainbow DQN on Atari over a LayerNorm baseline.
- **Key innovations**: **firing-rate** plasticity diagnostic; **load-balancing** per-neuron threshold adjustment; beats reset-based methods; transfers to **RL: Rainbow DQN on Atari** (+over LayerNorm); not game-specific but a **game-RL-relevant** plasticity technique. (Not classic game RL; placed here.)

#### 2611.33193v1 — "Formalization, Transparency, and Collusion in Open-Source Game Theory" (2026-11-15)
- **Authors**: Colomban Duclaux; Riccardo Formenti; Pepijn Cobben; Terry Jingchen Zhang; Bernhard Schölkopf; Zhijing Jin
- **Affiliation**: **Colomban Duclaux, Riccardo Formenti, Pepijn Cobben** — **ETH Zürich**; **EuroSafeAI, Zürich**; **Terry Jingchen Zhang** — **University of Oxford** + **EuroSafeAI** + **Vector Institute, Toronto**; **Bernhard Schölkopf** — **ETH Zürich** + **Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems, Tübingen** + **ELLIS Institute, Tübingen**; **Zhijing Jin** — **EuroSafeAI** + **Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems** + **University of Toronto** + **Vector Institute**.
- **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-15; cs.GT / cs.AI / cs.MA / econ.TH.
- **Abstract**: Cooperation among AI agents will undermine alignment protocols when it takes the form of collusion. Under these protocols, agents often have some information about one another, and how much they have may determine whether they collude. Open-source game theory studies the extreme case, in which each program reads its opponent's source code and acts based on what it can prove about it. We develop formal foundations for this theory: we specify a concrete proof system, resolve an open problem concerning DupocBot and CupodBot through a transposition argument, and mechanize the framework in Lean 4; to our knowledge, this is the first mechanization of resource-bounded open-source game theory. We then extend the framework to the more realistic setting of partial transparency, where each program receives only partial information about its opponent. In Prisoner's Dilemma experiments, where mutual cooperation models collusion, greater transparency increases both the cooperation sustainable in equilibrium and the cooperation selected by replicator dynamics. This suggests that controlling how much agents can know about one another matters for alignment protocols.
- **Key innovations**: **open-source game theory** formalization; concrete **proof system**; resolve **DupocBot/CupodBot**; **Lean-4 mechanization** (first for resource-bounded open-source game theory); **partial transparency**; **transparency ↔ collusion/cooperation** (greater transparency ↑ equilibrium + replicator-dynamics cooperation); Prisoner's Dilemma; **multi-agent RL/game-theory + alignment**; no video game. → §1-style multi-agent.

#### 2611.19319v1 — "Hardening Agentic Web Populations Through Multi-Criterion Competitive Co-Evolution: Lessons from Wargame Strategy Optimization" (2026-11-14)
- **Authors**: Shashank Raj; Kalyanmoy Deb
- **Affiliation**: **Shashank Raj, Kalyanmoy Deb** — Department of Computer Science, **The University of Alabama**, Tuscaloosa, AL, USA.
- **Venue/conference**: **Accepted at the 1st NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Agentic Web: Decentralized, Continually-Adapting Agent Ecosystems** (non-archival; https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/loc/atlanta/174837); arXiv preprint 2026-11-14; cs.AI. Position paper.
- **Abstract**: Agents on the open web will face adversaries that keep adapting, so a defense that is only tested once will eventually be found and broken. We propose treating jailbreak attacks and safety guardrails as two populations that evolve against each other, rather than as a fixed attacker probing a fixed defense. This idea comes directly from wargame strategy optimization, where two-population competitive co-evolution with alternating evolution cycles and cross-play evaluation has already been shown to produce strategies that are robust to a moving opponent, and where a companion line of work has shown that the same machinery can measure how much competitive pressure a population must absorb. However, real-world applications can never rely on a single criterion as a key performance indicator, rather must use multiple conflicting criteria, such as, cost and efficiency. We describe how this mechanism transfers to language model safety, argue why the resulting multi-criteria, competitive and coevolutionary arms race is a genuinely networked, population-level problem rather than a two-agent toy setting, and lay out the open problems that stand between the wargame version of this idea and an agentic web version of it.
- **Key innovations**: **multi-criterion competitive co-evolution**; **two-population** alternating evolution + **cross-play**; **wargame** strategy optimization lineage; transfer to **LLM safety**; **population-based training**-adjacent; NeurIPS 2026 workshop; position paper; not a game-playing RL paper. → §6.

#### 2611.29014v1 — "Population-Based Post-Training for Large Language Models" (2026-11-21)
- **Authors**: Yixuan Even Xu; Aleksei Petrenko; Raja Giryes; Jackson Hamburger; Kevin Chen; Fei Fang; J. Zico Kolter; Philipp Krähenbühl
- **Affiliation**: **Yixuan Even Xu** — **Carnegie Mellon University** (work performed during internship at **Apple**); **Aleksei Petrenko, Raja Giryes, Jackson Hamburger, Kevin Chen, Philipp Krähenbühl** — **Apple**; **Fei Fang, J. - **Venue/conference**: arXiv preprint 2026-11-21; cs.CL / cs.AI / cs.LG; 31 pages, 10 figures, 10 tables.
- **Abstract**: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is one of the most popular post-training paradigms for large language models. RLVR typically trains a single model under hyperparameters chosen before training begins, while researchers tune hyperparameters across consecutive runs. We introduce Population-Based Post-Training (PBPT), which trains several models in parallel and lets the training process do the tuning through population-based training. PBPT periodically evaluates models on a validation set. High-performers are duplicated and their hyperparameters perturbed to explore new configurations. On reasoning benchmarks (Enigmata, Nemotron) and interactive tool-use environments (Gaia2, AppWorld), PBPT produces stronger agents than compute-matched groups of independent RLVR learners. Furthermore, PBPT is able to recover from poor initial hyperparameters, automatically discovering better settings over the course of training.
