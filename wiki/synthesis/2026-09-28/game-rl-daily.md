## 1. Game RL, Multi-Agent Learning & Game Theory

> No direct game-environment RL in the unclaimed pool. This section is built from **differential/constrained games**, **MARL population design**, **actor-critic and Bellman theory**, and **security games** — the mathematical substrate the game line depends on but rarely publishes.

### 1.1 Inverse Linear Quadratic Gaussian Games: Constrained Setting and Transferability
- **Authors**: Kai Ren, Maryam Kamgarpour
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Kamgarpour is at UC San Diego, control/safety; Ren is her student) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.SY, New submission 2609.29321** — `cs.SY` was one of the eight categories no sibling swept
- **Key Innovations**: **Inverse** game problems: given observed agent behavior, recover the underlying *cost parameters* rather than hand-design them. Two contributions. (a) **Constrained setting** — characterizes the full set of cost parameters *and* optimal dual values that generate a given **generalized Nash equilibrium** (games where each player optimizes subject to the others' constraints, i.e. coupled feasible sets — the right model for a match where the rules bind opponents too), plus an algorithm computing them. (b) **Unconstrained setting** — a **transferability bound**: the cost-value perturbation between the policy induced by identified parameters and the expert's own parameters, *under different dynamics*, scaling **linearly with the deviations in dynamics and in the identified parameters**.
- **Results**: Simulations confirm the algorithm recovers parameters and duals reproducing the observed GNE policy and trajectories. Traffic simulation and real-robot experiments show the identified costs control *sufficiently close* dynamics, degrading linearly as dynamics and parameters drift.
- **Significance**: Inverse-GNE identification is **behavior cloning for game economies** — learn a population's revealed cost weights (aggression, safety margin, patience) from observed play, and the transferability bound tells you exactly how fast those weights go stale when the game's dynamics change. Directly applicable to tuning NPC/agent preferences without hand-authoring them.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29321

### 1.2 Temperament Engineering: Designing Strategic Behavioural Diversity in Robot Swarms
- **Authors**: Edmund R. Hunt
- **Affiliation**: not stated (single-author position paper)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.MA/cs.RO, New submission cross-listed 2609.29423**
- **Key Innovations**: Robotics treats the spread of behavior across a swarm (calibration, battery, sensor drift, wear) as **noise to minimize**; animal collectives treat consistent individual differences — *temperament* — as **selection-shaped and often decisive for group performance**. This position paper inverts that: the design object is not the individual controller but **the swarm's distribution of temperaments**. Borrows five evolutionarily validated animal axes — **shyness–boldness, exploration–avoidance, activity, aggressiveness, sociability** — rendered as continuous control parameters `τ ∈ [0,1]` layered *above* the controller, realisable as a module threshold, a **policy-conditioning vector in multi-agent RL**, or a constraint on a **foundation-model planner**. Three-phase workflow: map mission-success criteria onto axes → plan the *shape* of the `τ` distribution → tune reaction norms governing how temperament responds to environmental cues.
- **Results**: (Position paper; no new experiments.) The stated payoff is greatest under **decentralization** — where a central planner can reassign behavior online, a temperament distribution is just a planner *output*; in a swarm without global knowledge it must be an **offline anticipatory design input**. Concludes that behavioral and platform heterogeneity are **co-design variables**, and sketches robot-native axes animals lack (self-model plasticity, forcefulness, initiative, expressiveness).
- **Significance**: The clearest current statement of the **"diversity is a design parameter, not a byproduct"** thesis in multi-agent systems. For game squads and NPC crowds this is a *level-design* argument: engineer the personality spread, don't sample it. It also directly cites the case that engineered heterogeneity beats homogeneous swarms in aggregation and exploration — while honestly flagging *when and how much* heterogeneity repays its cost as open work.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29423

### 1.3 Pairwise Approximation Can Select the Wrong Multi-Robot Plan
- **Authors**: William Teo
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.MA/cs.RO, New submission cross-listed 2609.29929**
- **Key Innovations**: A clean, quantified **negative result** on a standard MARL / coordination shortcut. Multi-robot coordination methods routinely score a joint plan from **singleton + pairwise** terms, silently dropping every term involving three or more robots. This measures the **plan-selection regret** of two pairwise approximations to delivered coverage, using *frozen* trajectories (so the planner is held fixed and only the scoring function varies). For each four-robot plan, all 16 robot subsets are replayed to get the exact delivered-coverage set function `F`; from the same subset values it computes the exact order-2 Möbius truncation `F₂` and an equal-weight least-squares two-additive fit `G`.
- **Results**: Ranking by `F₂` instead of `F` **changes the selected plan on 6 of 7 maps** at the 15 m candidate-generation range, in **each of two candidate families**, with regret up to **0.337 of map coverage**. `G` reduces regret but still flips the selection on 3/7 maps in each family. The purely-additive `F₁` (singleton terms only) picks the exact winner on 6/7 maps in one family and 4/7 in the other, versus **1/7 for `F₂`**. Additional finding: **lower average reconstruction error does not guarantee lower selection regret** — the error metric and the decision metric are decoupled.
- **Significance**: A cautionary result for any *evaluation function* built from truncated interactions — squad-composition scoring, credit decomposition over teammates, coalition-value proxies. If the goal is *argmax over candidate plans*, a pairwise surrogate can be worse than a plain sum. The decoupling of approximation error from selection regret is the generalizable lesson: **validate the surrogate on the decision, not the reconstruction**.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29929

### 1.4 A Concentration Bound for the Two-Timescale Actor-Critic Algorithm
- **Authors**: Prashansa Panda, Shalabh Bhatnagar
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Bhatnagar is at University of Alberta / Amii, RL theory) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.29117**
- **Key Innovations**: Long-run **average-reward** setting (the right objective for episodic game and match play, where returns are not discounted to a terminal state) with **function approximation** and the actor on a slower timescale than the critic. Derives a **uniform all-time concentration bound** for the actor, i.e. high-probability control of the actor parameter itself rather than only of the average reward.
- **Results**: With probability ≥ `1 − ε₁ − ε₂`, for all `k ≥ n₀` and sufficiently large `n₀`, the actor error `‖θ_k − θ*‖` is `O( n₀^{3/4}/k · 1/√ε₂ + (1/n₀)^{1/4} log^{1/4}(1/ε₁) + (1/n₀)^{1/4} )`. The key structural statement: **after some finite time the actor parameter enters a safe region and stays there with high probability** — a stability property, not just an average-rate one. Experiments show the bound-tight actor error decreasing with the number of actor-parameter updates.
- **Significance**: The "safe region and stay" property is what you want before deploying a two-timescale actor-critic in a live game economy — it converts drift into a certified event rather than an unbounded liability. Unusually, this is a *non-asymptotic, all-time* guarantee in the average-reward case, which the game/RL literature usually only gets in the discounted case.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29117

### 1.5 A Contraction Framework for Stochastic Operators with Bootstrapping: Application to TD Learning
- **Authors**: Ids van der Werf, Sergio Rozada, Antonio G. Marques
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — van der Werf and Rozada are at Leiden Institute of Advanced Computer Science, pattern-recognition / temporal-difference learning lineage) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.29961**
- **Key Innovations**: Nearly every modern RL algorithm **bootstraps** — the target is a frozen copy of the variable, refreshed every `K` steps. Existing convergence guarantees for the combined setting (sampled updates + target refreshed only every `K` steps) lean on the *specific structure* of the update: linear approximation, or gradient-based inner steps, plus **uniformly bounded sampling error**. This paper instead models the sampled update as a **stochastic operator on parameter space**, reducing the whole analysis to a contraction argument that needs no gradient structure and — importantly — **allows the sampling error to grow with the iterates** (a realistic concession for value functions in late training).
- **Results**: A finite-time bound for i.i.d. samples and *any* target-update period `K`: iterates converge **geometrically in RMS to a ball around the fixed point**, provided the sensitivity to the frozen target is smaller than the contraction slack of the inner map. Existing deterministic frozen-target contraction and stochastic-gradient-type bounds fall out as special cases. TD-learning simulations reproduce the predicted contraction rate and the scaling of the error floor with step size.
- **Significance**: The result covers PPO-style clipped updates, target networks, and n-step returns **under one operator-level theorem** — the natural formalization of "our target-network PPO is stable" that game-RL practitioners currently assert without proof. The relaxation to iterate-growing sampling error is the practically important part: it is exactly the regime where late-training value estimates are no longer well-conditioned.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29961

### 1.6 Vector Bellman Theory for Multichain Robust Average-Reward Markov Decision Processes
- **Authors**: Yue Wang, George Atia
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Atia is at University of Southern California, stochastic control / online learning) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.28792**
- **Key Innovations**: Robust (adversarially uncertain) average-reward MDPs can have optimal long-run rewards that **depend on the initial state** — which breaks the scalar Bellman equation. Develops a **vector** Bellman theory for finite models with compact **post-action (s,a)-rectangular** ambiguity, via a **gain-first, bias-second** optimization principle yielding a coupled vector gain-bias system. Every finite solution simultaneously identifies the optimal robust gain *and* supplies **stationary saddle strategies against history-dependent opponents from all initial states** — a genuine saddle-point guarantee, not a best-response one.
- **Results**: Solvability characterized through stationary gain conditions and a uniform bound on canonical transient corrections, with sufficient conditions permitting distinct recurrent-class gains. The certificates yield **asymptotically affine trajectories of the robust Bellman operator**, from which a robust approximately-shifted **Halpern planning algorithm** is designed. Under finite Bellman solvability, gain estimates and Bellman displacements converge to the optimal gain vector, and every extracted greedy controller is average-optimal after a finite, instance-dependent budget.
- **Significance**: State-dependent robust long-run return is the correct model for **a match or a season**: a policy that is optimal from one initial state can be sub-optimal from another, and a "safe" opponent model must be saddle-defended, not average-case-assumed. The scalar-equation assumption failing is a real modelling trap for anyone writing an average-reward game-RL algorithm from the textbook.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28792

### 1.7 MQSS-Selector: RL-Guided Pass Selection for an MLIR Compilation Pipeline
- **Authors**: Andre Youssefi, Ercüment Kaya, Minh Chung, Jorge Echavarria, Laura B. Schulz, Martin Schulz
- **Affiliation**: **Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ)**, **Technical University of Munich (TUM)**, **Munich Quantum Valley (MQV)**, **Argonne National Laboratory (ANL)** — affiliations printed in the arXiv author block
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG/cs.SY, Cross submission 2609.30104** (primary new in cs.LG, cross-listed)
- **Key Innovations**: Quantum compiler stacks are **fragmented** — separate components handle device selection, compiler-pass optimization, and job-queue scheduling, and each optimizes its own objective. MQSS-Selector is a **unified, learned selector** over the whole pipeline, using RL and deep learning to optimize **multiple objectives simultaneously** — fidelity, compilation time, scheduling latency — while adapting dynamically to circuit characteristics and device conditions.
- **Results**: Presented as a framework for HPCQC convergence; the abstract states the multi-objective extensibility rather than a single headline metric (no comparative table in the abstract — treat the quantitative claims as unquantified here).
- **Significance**: Structurally the **closest analogue in the window to game-engine build/runtime orchestration** — a single learned policy over a *pipeline of discrete compiler passes*, trading quality against latency under a budget. That is precisely the shape of a game studio's content-bake or shader-permutation scheduler, and the multi-objective RL formulation transfers directly. Honest note: the abstract under-reports results, so this is a framework claim, not a demonstrated-win claim.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30104

### 1.8 Bandit Multiclass PAC Learning: Corrected Lower Bounds, Exact Families, and a Confidence Direct-Sum Phenomenon
- **Authors**: Guangjian Zhang
- **Affiliation**: not stated (single author) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.29694**
- **Key Innovations**: In realizable multiclass PAC learning with **bandit feedback** (predict one of `K` labels, observe only whether the prediction was correct) — the information regime of an agent that gets a pass/fail signal per action — Hanneke et al. (arXiv:2605.25678) characterized optimal sample complexity via the bandit DS dimension `BDS` up to log factors and asked whether every class admits `O((BDS + log(1/δ))/ε)`. This paper shows the **published lower bound `Ω((BDS + log(1/δ))/ε)` is incorrect as stated**, exhibiting explicit classes with `BDS = K−1` whose sample complexity is *exponentially smaller*, and locating **two independent gaps in the proof**. It repairs the theory around a new **anchored dimension `aBDS ≤ BDS`**.
- **Results**: Constant-free three-part lower bound. Upper side: the ambient label count `K` is removed entirely, giving `O((B log³B + B log(1/δ))/ε)` for `B = BDS`, plus a constant-confidence bound via a new **fiberization lemma**; two natural families are resolved up to constant factors. The open question is answered **negatively under its uniform-constant reading**, and the failure is shown to be **intrinsic**: for an explicit affine multiplexer class the full confidence profile is `Θ((n·min{n, log(1/δ)} + log(1/δ))/ε)` — a **confidence direct-sum regime** where a multiplicative `log(1/δ)` cost is information-theoretically necessary, followed by a **rank-saturation phase transition**. Two classes with identical dimension profiles can have polynomially different sample complexities, so no characterization by these dimensions alone is accurate to polylogarithmic factors. Shown consistent with additive-confidence list-PAC via the ListCascade bridge.
- **Significance**: A **published-bound correction**, which is rare and valuable. For anyone learning from bandit-style feedback (win/loss, hit/miss, approve/reject) with a confidence target, the naive `log(1/δ)` overhead is not always removable — and the phase transition is a genuine phenomenon. Direct read-across to evaluation-driven agent training where the reward signal is a correctness bit.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29694

### 1.9 A Graph-Based Stackelberg Security Game for Trustworthy 6G Disaggregated Architecture
- **Authors**: Lav R. Varshney, Xinbo Wu
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.CR, New submission 2609.29500** — `cs.CR` was swept by siblings, but this ID's game-theoretic core was not claimed
- **Key Innovations**: Open, cloud-native, AI-enabled architectures make network functions easy to deploy, observe and replace as separate software modules — and **each explicit interface is also an entry point, trust transition, and lateral-movement route**. Formulates architectural disaggregation as a **graph-based Stackelberg design game**: a deployment graph specifies candidate system *seams*; an architecture-dependent directed attack graph specifies externally reachable functions and multistage paths. The **defender commits first** (seams + hardening/monitoring allocation), then an **informed attacker** chooses a path and effort — a genuine sequential-information structure, not a simultaneous game. Because edge-traversal and evasion factors compose multiplicatively, the attacker's path response becomes a **shortest-path problem after a logarithmic transform**; convex effort costs admit a conjugate representation, making the defender's control problem a **convex exponential-epigraph program** solvable by path generation.
- **Results**: Derives an architecture-comparison criterion and a **threshold for AI transducers within 6G systems that jointly add visibility and exposure**; comparative statics for attacker entry and bypass innovation. On stylized O-RAN/6G systems (RAN, RIC, core, cloud, edge-AI): **neither full integration nor maximal disaggregation is generally best**; uncontrolled seams increase risk while selectively monitored and hardened seams improve the Stackelberg objective. A boundary pays off only when its detection and containment gains exceed its added attack opportunities and coordination cost.
- **Significance**: A **modular-architecture attack surface as a solved game** — the exact analysis a game studio runs when deciding whether to split a monolithic simulation stack into network services. The "AI transducers add visibility *and* exposure, with a computable threshold" result is a rare quantified statement about the security cost of an AI-pluggable subsystem.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29500

### 2.1 Towards an LLM-Driven Unified Conversion Framework for BT and FSM in Autonomous Intelligent Systems
- **Authors**: Zhang Qi, Yang Shuo, Zhu Zhengqiu, Zhou Peng, Jiao Peng
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.AI, New submission 2609.29228** — **names game agents as a target application in its own abstract**
- **Key Innovations**: **Behavior Trees** and **Finite State Machines** are the two dominant behavioral-modeling paradigms for autonomous agents, functionally equivalent and inter-convertible in principle — but existing conversion methods **fail to preserve behavioral completeness** and suffer **state-explosion**. This proposes an LLM-driven unified bidirectional conversion framework. Two named fixes: a novel **loop-execution BT structure** so the LLM can accurately capture FSM loop structure (preserving behavioral completeness), and a **depth-compression strategy** with LLM prompting to eliminate redundant control nodes, complemented by **differentiated hierarchical conversion rules** that reduce the number of required sub-FSMs.
- **Results**: Simulation experiments across multiple autonomous decision-making scenarios demonstrate accurate automated bidirectional conversion, with improved **scalability and maintainability** of the generated models versus traditional approaches.
- **Significance**: This is the **only paper in the entire window that names game agents as a first-class target application** — the closing sentence of the abstract names "service robots, **game agents**, and smart home devices" explicitly. BT↔FSM interop is a real production pain (engines ship one, tools speak the other, and round-tripping silently drops behavior); an LLM that does the translation with an explicit loop structure and a compression pass is directly usable in an NPC-behavior pipeline, and the completeness/complexity failure modes it names are the ones QA actually hits.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29228

### 2.2 RACaP: Agentic Reasoning, Acting, and Coding as Policies for Evolvable Robot Learning
- **Authors**: Zexi Li, Yehang Zhang, Haojian Huang, Bohan Zhou, Wenqian Li, Chenxu Wang, Yifan Chang, Yangkai Wei, Tianyi Zhang, Ying-Cong Chen, Kaiwen Zhou, Yinchuan Li, James Cheng
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — James Cheng / Yinchuan Li are HKU MMLab; Ying-Cong Chen is HKU-CLMFiTech) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.29394**
- **Key Innovations**: **Code-as-Policies** (CaP) generates and repairs programs at runtime, which incurs latency and **entangles reusable mechanisms with task-specific decisions**. RACaP **moves coding to evolution** and uses a **ReAct loop to call frozen, typed Policy APIs at deployment**. A two-phase strategy combines **capability curriculum learning** with **autonomous self-evolution** to improve the APIs, the ReAct harness, and the experience memory. The APIs encode *reusable physical mechanisms* while exposing arguments for runtime adaptation; the ReAct loop combines task-specific working memory, long-term experience memory and visual feedback to select actions, verify outcomes and **recover from failures without modifying source code**.
- **Results**: **54.4%** success on LIBERO-90, **45.0%** zero-shot on LIBERO-PRO, **46.0%** on LIBERO-Long — versus **at most 4.0%** for CaP baselines on long-horizon tasks. On LIBERO-PRO: **2.5×** the CaP success rate and a **1.9× speedup in median policy time**. For on-robot deployment, rejection-sampled fine-tuning distills **GPT-5.6 ReAct decisions into Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct**, giving a **13.2× per-decision inference speedup** and cutting repeated physical calls from 16 to 4.
- **Significance**: The **"evolve the policy library, don't recompile the policy at runtime"** split is the transferable design, and the 4%-to-46% long-horizon jump is the largest single-agent-harness gain in this window. The distillation step is the deployment lesson: a frontier-model planner is not shippable, but its *decisions* are a 13× cheaper student.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29394

### 2.3 Robo-Harness K1: Harnessing Robot-Use Agents via Perception Augmentation
- **Authors**: Zexi Li, Yehang Zhang, Wenqian Li, Haojian Huang, Chenxu Wang, Shiyuan Deng, Yangkai Wei, Tianyi Zhang, Binghui Xie, Bohan Zhou, Yifan Chang, Kaiwen Zhou, Ying-Cong Chen, James Cheng, Yinchuan Li
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — HKU MMLab / HKU-CLMFiTech, same group as 2.2) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.29389**
- **Key Innovations**: VLMs understand objects, instructions and spatial relations, but translating that into manipulation is hard: **VLA models need extensive demonstrations and may compromise pretrained understanding**, while **direct RGB-only VLM control is costly and strongly dependent on model capability**. K1 is a **robot-use agent (RUA) framework that exposes perception as tools** — the agent *queries* calibrated depth, persistent visual anchors, spatial measurements, and grasp hypotheses, then **selects generic motions from the returned evidence**. This makes 3D geometry accessible **without changing the VLM architecture or training a depth encoder**.
- **Results**: On matched LIBERO-PRO tasks, **Gemini 3.7 Flash + K1 = 77.8%**, surpassing **GPT-6 Astra with an RGB-only harness at 61.1%**; K1 further lifts Astra to **88.9%**. Without target fine-tuning, Gemini + K1 transfers to three RoboSuite arms and dual-arm RoboTwin tasks (32.0% Easy / 28.0% Hard). K1's tool-call traces are **aligned with next-token training**: a Qwen3.5-9B student trained on only **107 teacher episodes** reaches **44.2%** on new initial states vs **30.2%** for OpenVLA, and **13.9%** on held-out task conditions vs **0.0%** for OpenVLA.
- **Significance**: Two results worth isolating. First, **perception-as-tool beats a bigger model** — 77.8% from a Flash-class model versus 61.1% from a frontier model on the same harness, which is a strong argument for interface design over model scale in game-agent work. Second, the trace-alignment result means the tool interface is *learnable from 107 episodes*, which is the data-budget argument that makes perception-tool agents viable where demonstration collection is expensive.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29389

### 2.4 AdaHVLA: Adaptive Harnesses for Long-Horizon Vision-Language-Action Execution
- **Authors**: Junyi Tang, Jie Peng, Zezhen Ding, Yuan Shen, Tianlong Chen
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.29204**
- **Key Innovations**: VLAs offer strong local control and instruction following but struggle with **long-horizon tasks requiring persistent memory and planning**; *task harnesses* provide that persistent context but are **hand-written and never improve**. AdaHVLA is an **adaptive harness** that refines code-based coordination policies **through robot experience**, aligning agent reasoning and memory with VLA execution. Its **decoupled multiagent adaptation process** separates *evidence analysis*, *harness revision*, and *behavioral assessment* into distinct working contexts, using **testable coordination hypotheses** to drive revisions and **subsequent rollouts to assess their predicted effects**. A **stateful revision graph** links execution evidence, hypotheses, revisions and observed effects, preserving **alternative harnesses** and adaptation memory to guide refinement across repeated attempts and continued adaptation across tasks and environments.
- **Results**: In simulation, mean test success on **NaVILA-LH rises from 22.5% to as high as 57.5%**, and manipulation test success improves by **up to +30.8 percentage points over the initial harness** across three VLA backbones. Real-world deployment shows stable execution across task stages.
- **Significance**: A **2.5× gain from editing the harness instead of the model** — the same structural claim as RACaP and Robo-Harness, arriving from a third direction. The design detail worth stealing is the **hypothesis → predicted effect → measured effect** loop: revisions are *predictions* that rollouts then falsify, and alternative harnesses are retained rather than overwritten, so adaptation is reversible.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29204

### 2.5 AquaMend: Minimal Re-probing and Conditional Rollback for Latent-Belief Failures in Embodied Agents
- **Authors**: Yufan Liu, Shang Luo, Yang Liu, Haoxuan Jia, Feiyu Han, Qian Li, Chen Li, Yingguang Yang, Chongyang Zhang, Hao Zheng, Kefu Xu, Bin Chong
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)

## 3. Game Foundation Models, World Models & World-Action Models

### 3.1 Streaming-WAM: Action-Conditioned World-Action Model for Asynchronous Robot Manipulation
- **Authors**: Xuyao Huang, Yixuan Wang, Zengyao Ye, Boyuan Zhao, Chenyang Yu, Haoran Wen, Zhijie Deng
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.28927**
- **Key Innovations**: World action models that use **future visual prediction at inference time** incur substantial generation cost. **Asynchronous execution** overlaps inference with robot motion and cuts waiting — but this creates a subtle correctness bug the paper names: the visual predictions used to generate *subsequent* actions must **anticipate the effects of actions already scheduled for execution during inference**. Streaming-WAM couples action-conditioned world modeling with asynchronous control to **account for committed actions** in future visual prediction. At each streaming update the model conditions on the latest observation and the **committed actions** — the **fixed prefix of the next action chunk** — and the resulting action-conditioned visual features guide generation of the *remaining* actions within the same joint update.
- **Results**: On LIBERO, **98.35% average success** and **mean episode time reduced 2.93×** vs Fast-WAM. On the real-world Stamp Paper task, mean episode time falls from **90 s (synchronous Joint-WAM) to 38 s**.
- **Significance**: The **asynchronous-planning consistency problem** is the deployment blocker for any action-conditioned world model used in a real-time loop: predict-then-act and act-then-predict disagree, and the paper resolves it by making the committed prefix a first-class conditioning input. The 90 s → 38 s figure is the concrete "world models are only useful if they fit inside the frame budget" argument. Directly portable to real-time game-simulator rollouts.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28927

### 3.2 FedWM-Guard: Thwarting Imagination Poisoning in Federated World Model-based Autonomous Driving
- **Authors**: Sheng Liu, Panos Papadimitratos
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Papadimitratos is at Carnegie Mellon / CyLab; Liu at Linköping University) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.CR, New submission 2609.29178** — marked **Poster** in the title
- **Key Innovations**: Federated learning lets world-model-based autonomous driving improve without centralizing private vehicle data — but it **turns model aggregation into a safety-critical integrity boundary**. Names a new threat: **imagination poisoning** — compromised vehicles submit **bounded WM updates that preserve benign short-horizon predictions yet corrupt long-horizon rollouts** (e.g. trigger-conditioned), thereby misleading a downstream planner. The defense is claimed to be the **first to characterize planner-facing rollouts** in federated WM-AD: it screens authenticated updates in **hidden-canary scenarios**, **audits predicted futures against later observations**, and invokes a **WM-independent safety shield** when persistent inconsistency is detected. Crucially, unlike parameter-space defenses, **it scores what an update makes the model *imagine*, not only how the update looks**.
- **Results**: **No experimental results** — this is explicitly a threat-model-and-defense-position poster. The authors state they *plan* to evaluate under non-IID data, adaptive attacks, and benign distribution shift.
- **Significance**: Flagged as a **thin/position contribution** (unevaluated, planned work only) but the *threat model* is the valuable part and generalizes well past driving: any world model trained or aggregated from untrusted contributors, including a game studio's player-uploaded trajectory data feeding a world model, has an **imagination** attack surface that parameter-space anomaly detection cannot see. A short-horizon-clean, long-horizon-malicious update is exactly the kind of thing a studio's telemetry pipeline would not catch.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29178

### 3.3 GridSFM: A Foundation Model for Solving AC Optimal Power Flow
- **Authors**: Luke Bhan, Weiwei Yang, Margaret Capetz, Baosen Zhang
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Baosen Zhang is at University of Michigan; Capetz at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG/cs.SY, Cross submission 2609.30173**
- **Key Innovations**: A **foundation model across grid topologies** pretrained at 15M parameters across **54 topologies of 500–4,000 buses**, then paired with **physics-informed fine-tuning based on Newton's method for power flow** so it can adapt to unseen grids from only **100 solved instances**. The theoretical obstacle handled explicitly: the **feasible set for AC-OPF can be disconnected**, which is an obstruction that **prevents any continuous neural network from approximating the solution map**. The fix is to **lift the problem and relax its constraints with logarithmically penalized slacks**, then prove (i) the resulting **elastic feasible set is contractible**, (ii) AC-OPF minimizers remain minimizers of the elastic problem above an explicit penalty threshold, and (iii) **projecting an approximate solution back onto the AC-OPF feasible set is well posed**.
- **Results**: **2.45% zero-shot generation-cost error** on a 10,000-bus case at held-out operating conditions, **with no degradation as system size grows**. Beats single-topology dedicated networks trained with more data, on both cost and solver iterations when used as **warm starting points**. All models, data and code released.
- **Significance**: A rare, complete *foundation-model* paper: cross-instance pretraining + tiny-shot domain adaptation + a topological obstruction that is mathematically removed rather than hand-waved. The **disconnected-feasible-set** problem is not power-specific — any game or simulation system with a disconnected valid-state space (inventory-feasibility, level-geometry constraints) hits it, and the contractible-relaxation-and-project-back recipe is the general fix. Also the strongest *open-artifact* claim in this digest.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30173

### 3.4 Beyond Compression: Training Latent Representations for Stable Long-Horizon Rollout in Neural Surrogate Solvers
- **Authors**: Andreas E. Robertson, Ashley T. Lenau, John D. Shimanek, Benjamin A. Jasperson, Vivek Oommen, David L. Damm, Krishna Garikipati, Remi Dingreville
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — LLNL / Sandia national-lab line; Damm, Garikipati, Dingreville are LLNL-affiliated computational-materials researchers) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.30198**
- **Key Innovations**: Latent neural surrogate solvers evolve a **compressed latent space** instead of full-resolution fields, reducing cost — but in practice **errors accumulate rapidly during long autoregressive rollouts**. The paper's central claim is a *diagnosis*: this instability **does not stem from the latent representation itself**, but arises when the latent is **trained solely for reconstruction**, producing representations poorly suited to long-horizon forecasting. It then systematically evaluates **training-level interventions** — **Koopman operator learning** and **Hamming noise injection** during autoencoder training (to improve compression) plus **noise injection and multi-step rollout fine-tuning** (to improve dynamics).
- **Results**: The interventions that improve long-rollout stability **often degrade conventional training metrics**, including reconstruction and one-step prediction accuracy. Collectively they **reduce long-rollout error by ~40%** and **match or exceed full-resolution models on two physics benchmarks** while requiring **2 orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs and half the GPU memory**. Applied to mesoscale crystal-plasticity fatigue simulations, the surrogate achieves **stable extrapolation over horizons orders of magnitude beyond those observed during training**.
- **Significance**: **"Compression quality is not rollout quality,"** stated and quantified (~40% long-rollout error reduction at 100× fewer FLOPs, with *worse* reconstruction scores). This is the exact trap for latent world models: optimize the autoencoder and you get a good compressor and a diverging simulator. The "train for rollout, not reconstruction" reading is the paper's own thesis, and it pairs with §3.1's asynchronous-consistency result as a two-part statement about what action/world models should actually be trained for.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30198

### 3.5 OCC4M ("Occam"): Object-Centric 4D Memory for Spatiotemporal Reasoning in Long-Horizon Manipulation
- **Authors**: Jack B. Jedlicki, Tanguy Dieudonné, Heng Yang
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Jedlicki and Yang at ETH Zurich Robotic Systems Lab; Dieudonné at EPFL) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.28798**
- **Key Innovations**: Long-horizon tasks require reasoning about state **absent from the current view** — a vanished object's location, its temporal identity, the contents of a shuffled container. OCC4M maintains **persistent object tracks in a shared world frame** and **explicitly represents temporal, motion, and containment relations**; a VLM **queries this structured memory** to select actionable targets for **history-free low-level execution**.
- **Results**: Across 7 simulation conditions and 350 episodes: **96.6% memory success / 88.9% end-to-end**, vs **54.6% / 57.7%** for FrameSamp — a raw-history VLM baseline using **Gemini 3.7 Flash with the complete observation history** and the same executor. - **Significance**: The **viewpoint-transfer collapse of full-history baselines** (near-zero) versus structured memory (98%) is the whole argument in one number: keeping the raw history is not a memory strategy, it is a liability under any distribution shift in the observation channel. For a game agent this is the "the camera moved, and now the NPC has forgotten the whole quest" bug, with a fix and a number.

### 3.6 OSRAM: Online Sim-to-Real Adaptation via Closed-Loop System Modeling
- **Authors**: Yuhao Huang, Samuel A. Moore, Boyuan Chen
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Boyuan Chen at Carnegie Mellon / Robotics Institute; Moore at CMU) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.28878**
- **Key Innovations**: Sim-to-real can produce controllers that are **stable and functional on hardware while suffering degraded tracking accuracy** from residual dynamics mismatch. Correcting that normally requires **identifying the underlying system dynamics, adapting the control policy, or returning to simulation for more training** — all expensive in data and compute. OSRAM instead adapts **the reference commands given to an existing controller**. It treats the deployed robot *and its policy* as a **unified closed-loop dynamical system** and learns its **task-level command-response behavior** directly from tracking observations; a closed-loop dynamics model is **meta-trained across randomized dynamics in simulation** and rapidly finetuned after deployment with limited real-world interaction, then used to **optimize future reference commands while leaving the underlying control policy unchanged**.
- **Results**: Evaluated on **bipedal velocity tracking** and **loco-manipulation** in simulation and on hardware. Closed-loop modeling improves prediction and tracking accuracy under unseen dynamics; online reference adaptation **reduces residual sim-to-real tracking errors** across different control objectives and hardware configurations. (Abstract reports no headline percentages.)
- **Significance**: A **third adaptation locus** alongside RACaP/AdaHVLA (edit the harness) and this one (edit the *command*, not the policy) — and the cheapest of the three, because the shipped policy never changes. The framing insight is that the *policy-plus-plant* is the system worth modeling; treating the policy as fixed and the command as the control variable turns sim-to-real into a command-planning problem.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28878

### 4.1 PG-DiGress: Constraint-Guided Diffusion for Rule-Compliant Feeder Generation
- **Authors**: Yu Qin, Andrew Glaws, Aadil Latif, Ryan King
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.29879**
- **Key Innovations**: States the general problem crisply: generative modeling usually targets **broad statistical characteristics** of the training data — degree distributions, clustering coefficients, spectral properties — but **generating usable artifacts when detailed models are unavailable requires more than matching generic graph statistics**: the sampled topology must also **obey domain-specific compatibility and radality rules**. Formulates synthesis as **constraint-guided graph generation**: **PG-DiGress** learns categorical node and edge patterns while **injecting the constraints into the reverse diffusion process** through **soft masks that suppress incompatible edge classes during denoising**, followed by a **final projection step** that rebuilds a connected, rule-compliant graph.
- **Results**: Evaluated on graph-distribution similarity, rule satisfaction, structural validity, and downstream model construction. Against the unconstrained baseline, PG-DiGress raises the **strict feeder pass rate from 13.7% to 96.8%**, and the generated graphs **convert into executable feeder models** for downstream analysis.
- **Significance**: **13.7% → 96.8% is the whole PCG argument in one measurement.** A stat-matching generative model produces a plausible-looking artifact that is *almost never usable* (13.7%), and the fix is not a bigger model or longer sampling — it is **injecting validity constraints into the reverse process and projecting at the end**. That is exactly the level-generation problem (a dungeon that looks like a dungeon and is not traversable), and this paper is the cleanest demonstration in the window that **validity must be a sampling-time constraint, not a post-hoc filter**. The projection step matters too: soft masks get you most of the way, and a repair pass closes the rest.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29879

### 4.2 Growth-Inspired Graph Generation and Inverse Design of Mechanical Lattices
- **Authors**: Weiyun Xu, Jiamu Liu
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.29024**
- **Key Innovations**: Natural load-bearing and transport networks "are **not assembled in a single step**; they emerge through a **temporally ordered process of growth, branching, reinforcement, and loop formation**." The paper imports that developmental logic into a **morphogenetic graph-generation framework**: a discrete **dot matrix** supplies potential nodes, and the final architecture is created by **sequential cross-layer and intra-layer growth** — visualized in 2D as a leaf-vein-like developmental sequence and implemented in 3D on a 3×3×3 nodal matrix (27 candidate nodes). A dataset of lattices from **beam-based finite element analysis** is represented directly as graphs; a GCNN (three graph-convolution layers + dual global pooling) learns the topology–property map and predicts effective compressive stiffness; coupling the surrogate with rapid structural sampling enables **inverse design**. The same framework extends beyond straight members to **parameterized horseshoe-shaped curved beams of nonlinear materials**, enabling **topology–geometry** design toward prescribed deformation shapes.
- **Results**: For a target stiffness of 1000 MPa the selected design was predicted at **1042.43 MPa** and validated by finite element analysis at **1027.49 MPa** (≈2.4% / 2.8% error, closed loop through a learned surrogate plus an independent validator).
- **Significance**: **Incremental, ordered growth — not one-shot generation — is the load-bearing idea**, and it is a direct description of how organic level layouts actually come into being: corridors extend, branches fork, rooms reinforce, loops close. A dot-matrix of candidate sites plus a growth process is also a clean generative spec a level designer could author against. The topology→property→inverse-design loop is the general PCG pipeline in miniature: generate candidate, predict its gameplay-relevant property cheaply, keep the ones that hit a target.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29024

## 5. Game Benchmarks & Evaluation Suites

### 5.1 RoboRecover: Benchmarking Robot Policy Recovery under Execution Deviations
- **Authors**: Yang Li, Chen Zhao, Zhuoran Wang, Jiankang Wang, Chao Shao, Yihan Lin, Haitao Shen, Jing Zhang
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Chao Shao and Jing Zhang at Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Yihan Lin at SJTU / Shanghai AI Lab) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.28952**
- **Key Innovations**: Robot-policy benchmarks cover many tasks and preset OOD conditions but **evaluate complete trajectories from predefined initial states** — focusing on the initialized scene and the final outcome while **paying little attention to the dynamic interaction process**. During closed-loop execution, actions and contacts **alter object relations and task progress**, producing **off-nominal intermediate states that need recovery**. Recovery requires a policy to **infer how task progress has changed, correct the relevant relations, and continue the original goal** — a capability that is neither trained for nor measured. RoboRecover selects deviation states *from* trajectories and **reconstructs them by replaying action prefixes**, then evaluates on the original task.
- **Results**: **2,000 scenarios across RoboTwin and LIBERO** (1,000 per platform, fixed 800/200 train/test split each). The headline empirical claim is a **negative one: initial-state performance does not determine recovery performance**, and policies show **different recovery strengths across scenarios**. The train split additionally supports study of recovery interventions.
- **Significance**: A **third evaluation axis** — alongside task competence and OOD robustness — namely *recovery from mid-execution deviation*, with a benchmark construction method (replay action prefixes to synthesize off-nominal states) that ports directly to games. This is the benchmark for "your agent was 80% of the way through the quest and then the door it opened blocked the corridor." The finding that initial-state score does **not** predict recovery score is the kind of result that should change which metric a team optimizes.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28952

- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.HC, New submission 2609.29686**
- **Key Innovations**: External human-machine interfaces are moving from **predefined displays to adaptive communication strategies** responding to changing traffic and road-user states — which needs infrastructure supporting **human-in-the-loop (HIL)** interaction, **software-in-the-loop (SIL)** algorithm execution, reusable experiment orchestration, and **synchronized multimodal human-factors evaluation**. TransCAVE-E couples three modules: a **Scenario Design Center** (configurable traffic environments and experimental conditions), an **eHMI Algorithm Module** (bidirectional real-time coupling between simulation and external algorithms), and a **Data Management System** synchronizing trajectories, eye-tracking, physiological, system-log and subjective data. The numbers are the useful part: adaptive signaling *both* improves decisions and cuts unnecessary prompts by 40%, i.e. the usual "tell the player everything" reflex is measurably wrong. The SIL/HIL split is the reusable architecture for rehearsing an in-game communication policy against scripted opponents before players see it.

### 5.3 LabFactory: Building and Evaluating Executable AI Labs
- **Authors**: Jinge Wu, Hongjian Zhou, Mingde Zeng, Jiayuan Zhu, Junde Wu, Jiazhen Pan, Lei Clifton, Andrew Liu, David A. Clifton
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — David A. Clifton is at University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; Lei Clifton at Loyola Marymount) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.28697**
- **Key Innovations**: Scientific tasks specify a *desired capability*, but realizing one requires building a computational system — acquiring data, designing representations, training models, implementing tools, deciding inference-time composition. LabFactory lets an **AI builder turn a scientific brief into an executable AI lab**: a task-specific solver integrating models, knowledge resources, tools and a controller behind a **fixed interface**. The builder develops and packages the lab in a **metered workspace**; a **separate host** then executes the delivered artifact on held-out inputs, **with reference labels kept outside the solver's input interface**, scoring outputs under the task's protocol. This makes **the delivered system, rather than the builder's account of its progress, the object of evaluation**.
- **Results**: Documents **28 constructions across seven scientific task categories** (molecular and genomic prediction, physiological signals, clinical decision support, biomedical text) whose delivered labs **exceeded their configured reference values on all 33 subtests** under host-side execution. **Ten contain predictive models fitted during construction**; the others assemble retrieval systems, executable analysis environments, and tool-driven workflows around a fixed platform LLM.
- **Significance**: Two structural moves worth copying into game-agent evaluation. First, **labels held outside the artifact's input interface**, so an agent cannot launder a target into its own context. Second, **metered construction + independent host execution** — evaluate the shipped artifact, not the builder's narrative, which is the same discipline that produced the "agents tamper with their own traces" finding the 09-25 siblings recorded. The paper's own stated contribution is that a lab "can still be invoked, inspected, and checked after construction ends" — reproducibility as the deliverable.

### 6.1 REARL: A Closed-Loop Autonomous Driving Simulation Enhancement Framework with Real Traffic Data and LLMs
- **Authors**: Xiaojun Bi, Jun Jiang, Yiwen Sun, Quanyi Ou, Yizhi Ma, Ke Cheng, Mingjie Bi, Yexin Li, Bowen Du
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Chinese autonomous-driving academic/industry cluster; Bowen Du is at Tsinghua IIIS) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, Replacement submission 2609.19903** — a **replacement** (revised version of an earlier submission), and the only *replaced* paper featured here
- **Key Innovations**: Accurate simulation is crucial, but **capturing real-world traffic complexity remains hard**: simulators based on predefined rules or static data playback struggle with dynamic traffic. CRITICAL uses real traffic data and an LLM to adjust the *initial* configuration, but the simulated distribution **still diverges from real traffic as the rollout evolves**. REARL closes the loop: real traffic data are **clustered**, and each **cluster center is used as a representative scenario** providing typical real patterns for the LLM; a **timed sliding-window detector** then monitors **discrepancies in vehicle speed distribution and mean inter-vehicle spacing**. The LLM also **selects a matching real vehicle from a traffic snapshot and modulates the simulated vehicle with reference to that real action**.
- **Results**: In a controlled **HighD highway** setting, versus the CRITICAL baseline and a PPO-based learning baseline: Hellinger distance for speed distributions reduced to **0.3067**, MAPE for mean spacing to **0.8371**, while achieving time headway **22.8575** and lane-change rate **0.0708**.

That is the deployment pattern for LLM-in-the-loop in a live game: a cheap statistical monitor owns the trigger, a frontier model owns the correction, and the fallback is the previous behavior. The loop-closing observation (fix the initial conditions and you still drift) is exactly the failure that makes scripted NPC traffic look wrong after ten minutes of play.

### 6.2 Koopman-Accelerated Model-Based Diffusion for Real-Time Robot Control
- **Authors**: Bohyeong Pak, Kangmin Lee, Sanghyun Kim
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.28920**
- **Key Innovations**: Model-based diffusion (MBD) gets trajectory optimization from noise annealing, but its cost — **repeated rollouts of the plant dynamics** — has largely confined it to **offline** settings. **BK-MBD** lifts the robot's state into a high-dimensional space **only once per control step** and then **propagates all candidates in the lifted space**, so each rollout reduces to a **fixed number of matrix-vector multiplications**. The lifted dynamics are **bilinear**, letting the predicted input gain **vary with the robot's configuration** — something a linear lifted model cannot represent.
- **Results**: In simulation, each planning update completed in **at most 14.7 ms within a 50 ms control period** and **reached the goal on every trial, whereas a linear lift almost never did**. On a physical manipulator it tracked an initially unknown moving target within the control period and was the only method meeting both the task and the deadline.
- **Significance**: A hard, unambiguous real-time result — **14.7 ms inside a 50 ms period** — and a nice methodological honesty: the annealed-vs-fixed comparison is split by rollout fidelity, so the paper distinguishes what the *learned* model shows from what the *true* model shows. The bilinear-not-linear detail is the substantive one: a convexified lift loses the ability to express configuration-dependent gain, and the paper shows that costs a near-total task success rate.

### 6.3 Albireo: Adaptive, Energy-Efficient Inference Framework for Video Object Detection on the Edge
- **Authors**: Amir Taherin, José Cano, Bin Ren, Yanzhi Wang, David Kaeli
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — David Kaeli and Yanzhi Wang at Northeastern University; Bin Ren at York University) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.CV/cs.LG/cs.PF/cs.RO, New submission cross-listed 2609.29648** — four categories, none of them swept for this paper by any sibling
- **Key Innovations**: Consecutive video frames are **highly redundant**, but **naive frame skipping is content-blind**: it skips during critical moments — **object entry, occlusion recovery, abrupt motion** — degrading detection quality. Albireo is a **detector-agnostic, codec-free** framework that wraps off-the-shelf detectors and decides when invocation can be **safely skipped** based on scene content and **per-object temporal state**, with **no detector modification or retraining**. It maintains a **10-dimensional Kalman filter per active object** and invokes the detector only when **prediction uncertainty exceeds a threshold**; skipped frames are predicted from the KF state at **near-zero GPU cost**. A **KF-based rescue mechanism** preserves confirmed objects through brief detector misses to prevent **output fragmentation**, and a lightweight **empty-scene screen** avoids detector calls on objectless frames.
- **Results**: On BDD100K MOT validation with three architecturally distinct detectors (YOLO11x, YOLO26x, RF-DETR-Large) on two NVIDIA Jetson platforms (AGX Thor, AGX Orin), across all configurations Albireo keeps **AP@50 within ±1.2 pp** of per-frame inference while **reducing total energy by 12.1–17.6%**. On YOLO26x it **improves** AP@50 by **+0.8 pp** while cutting energy by 17.6% (Thor) / 14.4% (Orin) and per-frame energy-delay product by **24.9% / 26.1%** — accuracy, energy and latency all improved at once. The contrast baseline **FixedSkip-2** (50% fixed skip) **loses 8.6 pp AP@50**.
- **Significance**: The content-blind-skipping failure (FixedSkip-2's −8.6 pp) is the headline: **a fixed-rate throttle is not a free lunch**, and the per-object uncertainty threshold is what makes adaptive skipping strictly better than both full inference and fixed skipping. For real-time game inference this is the exact template for running a perception or behavior model at a reduced rate — skip when predicted uncertainty is low, per-entity, never on a timer.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29648

### 6.4 Spectral-Guided Diffusion: Accelerating Inference via Static Spectral Layer Scheduling
- **Authors**: Ibne Farabi Shihab, Abu Sa-Adat Mohamed Moon-Im Al Ahsan, Anuj Sharma
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG, New submission 2609.29505** — same lead author as **TASP** (2609.29499), claimed today by [arxiv-ai-search 2026-09-28](../2026-09-28/arxiv-ai-search.md); treated here as the **companion** paper on a different mechanism
- **Key Innovations**: Diffusion inference **repeatedly evaluates the same large network**; the question is whether pretrained weights alone can identify **residual branches that need not be recomputed** throughout the trajectory. The **Spectral Concentration Ratio (SCR)** measures **leading-versus-tail singular-value energy**; combined with Frobenius magnitude it yields an **offline sensitivity proxy** and a **deterministic lifetime for each scheduled unit**. Perturbation analysis motivates pre-norm attention and MLP components under explicit local assumptions; results on AdaLN, U-shaped, convolutional and cross-attention blocks are **empirical transfer, not certified guarantees**.
- **Significance**: The most self-skeptical efficiency paper in the digest, and worth featuring for that: the headline 2.8–3.0× is decomposed to show 2.7× of it is ordinary graph capture, leaving ~0.3× attributable to the actual method. The "no router, no calibration prompts" property is still the useful engineering result — a **weights-only, deterministic, offline** schedule is deployable where per-input routing is not. The transfer caveat is stated rather than buried.

### 6.5 Accelerating Branch MPC with Two-Level Parallel Direct Solves on GPUs
- **Authors**: Fenglong Song, Luyao Zhang, Liang Wu, Ján Drgoňa, Colin N. Jones

Operating at the **linear-algebra level**, it is a **reusable backend for multiple optimization algorithms** whose reduced systems have the required symmetric-positive-definite structure. ### 6.6 Does Per-Frame Early Exit Pay? A Compute-Matched Study of Dynamic Depth for On-Device Speech Enhancement
- **Authors**: Clément Laroche, Riccardo Miccini
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)

## 7. Related RL Techniques: Imitation, Constraints, Habituation & Credit

### 7.1 Training-free Behavior Cloning (Behavior Predictive Control)
- **Authors**: Maximilian Adang, Timothy Chen, Lars Osterberg, Aiden Swann, Mac Schwager
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Schwager and Chen at University of Washington / UW Robotics; Adang is the UW Animation Group lineage) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.30134**
- **Key Innovations**: Neural behavior cloning **compresses demonstrations into large models**, making individual actions **difficult to trace** and policy updates **costly**. Retrieval policies keep access to the demonstrations but suffer from **mismatch between recorded and live behavior**. **Behavior Predictive Control (BPC)** synthesizes policies **without any end-to-end policy training** by combining an **action-aware retrieval metric**, a **Hankel-based action-continuation prior**, and a **closed-form one-step residual correction**. Inspired by behavioral systems theory, it predicts future actions by **blending stored observation–action data that best reconstructs the recent runtime history**.
- **Results**: Across simulated benchmarks and real-robot deployments, BPC is **competitive with learned policies such as `π₀.₅` — surpassing it in some cases** — while reducing **policy fitting from hours to seconds on consumer GPUs** and supporting closed-loop control **upwards of 75 Hz on a Jetson Orin Nano**. Two bonuses: the retrieved demonstration windows and their coefficients give an **intrinsic estimate of task progress**, and **retaining demonstrations inside the deployed policy makes predictions traceable to supporting trajectories and enables behavior revision through the demonstration bank**.
- **Significance**: The strongest idea in this section is the **side effect**: because nothing is trained, the policy *is* its own justification — every action points at a stored demonstration and a coefficient. For game AI that means an NPC behavior can be audited and patched by editing the demonstration bank, with no retraining and no latent-behavior question. The honest caveat is that this is a *retrieval* policy, so its ceiling is the coverage of the demonstration set — the mismatch the paper names is exactly the case where it has no good stored window to retrieve.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30134

### 7.2 SCoCaT: Success Conditioned Constrained RL for Spacecraft Docking
- **Authors**: Aman Arora, Ricard Marsal I Castan, Matteo El-Hariry, Miguel Olivares-Mendez
- **Affiliation**: not stated (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, Replacement submission 2609.06061** — a **replacement**; a re-submission often carries reviewer-driven corrections
- **Key Innovations**: **Termination-based constrained RL** is attractive for safety-critical deployment: it **avoids online optimization at inference**, scales to many constraints via a **single scalar per constraint**, and is simpler than Lagrangian methods. Rather than pricing violations with summed cost penalties, it makes violations **structurally unprofitable by shortening the effective horizon** for each violation. The paper then identifies a **structural failure mode of this whole method class on terminal-navigation tasks**: reaching a precise goal configuration while satisfying safety constraints that **tighten along the final approach**. When the goal sits close to where the constraints become active, **the survival-weighted objective makes dwelling outside the goal region strictly preferable to entering** — producing **high constraint compliance with low task completion**.
- **Results**: The pathology is formalized as **"feasibility collapse"**, and a **minimal augmentation** to off-the-shelf algorithms such as PPO resolves it: adding a **dense per-step success signal via an auxiliary value critic** raises task completion while **maintaining safety-critical constraint compliance**. Validated on two platforms: a **6U-CubeSat** spanning the mass and degree-of-freedom envelope of operational proximity operations, and a floating platform testbed for **zero-shot sim-to-real** transfer in the authors' laboratory.
- **Significance**: A named failure mode with a one-line fix, in a method family people deploy precisely *because* they trust it. The interaction is worth internalizing: **when the goal is inside the region where safety constraints activate, a constraint-respecting policy learns to never arrive** — and the compliance metric looks *excellent* while the task metric is bad. Any safety-constrained agent with a terminal goal (docking, level completion, quest finish) needs an explicit success signal or it will learn to hover. Zero-shot sim-to-real on a lab testbed is a real generality check.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06061

### 7.3 Res-HIL: Human-Guided Residual Reinforcement Learning for Sample-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation
- **Authors**: Mariia Iavorskaia, Christian Dietz, Sebastian Albrecht, Majid Khadiv
- **Affiliation**: not stated (inferred — Majid Khadiv is at TUM Munich, Learning and Algorithmic Robotics; Iavorskaia and Dietz are his group) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.RO, New submission 2609.30023**
- **Key Innovations**: Imitation learning gives robots skills from demonstrations but **policies fail outside the training data**, while more demonstrations cost human effort. Human-in-the-loop RL supplies corrective feedback during online training, but **typically learns the complete task policy rather than refining a pretrained imitation policy** — discarding the demonstration prior. Res-HIL learns **corrective actions on top of a frozen imitation policy**. Each human intervention provides **two complementary signals**: **direct supervision of the residual policy** and **reward shaping of the preceding autonomous behavior**. The residual policy is **zero-initialized** to stabilize and accelerate online learning.
- **Results**: On **five contact-rich manipulation tasks** spanning high-precision and long-horizon behaviors, with **only 20 initial demonstrations**, Res-HIL **outperforms state-of-the-art full-policy human-in-the-loop RL and residual fine-tuning without human guidance on every task after ten minutes of online training**. It improves its pretrained base policies and **outperforms imitation policies trained with five times more demonstrations**. Ablation: **direct residual supervision is critical to performance**; intervention-aware reward shaping **substantially improves training efficiency** (but is not load-bearing).
- **Significance**: **RL as a patch, not a replacement, for imitation** — and the "beats 5× the demonstrations" comparison is the deployment argument for studios with expensive expert play data. The two-signal decomposition is the reusable insight: supervising the *correction* and crediting the *preceding autonomous segment* are separate learning problems, and the ablation cleanly separates which one matters for quality versus speed.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30023

### 7.4 The Fly That Stopped: Mushroom-Body-Inspired Habituation as a Reward-Free Scheduling Prior
- **Authors**: Theodoros Moutesidis
- **Affiliation**: not stated (single author) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.CR, New submission 2609.29126** — filed under security, but the mechanism is pure exploration/scheduling
- **Key Innovations**: Autonomous security-testing agents **spend much of a fixed action budget repeating earlier tool selections**. Evaluates a **reward-free scheduler inspired by mushroom-body novelty processing in *Drosophila***, combining **sparse state encoding** with **decaying habituation counters** over structural URL classes and tool families. The counters penalize repeated clean *or* error outcomes **without updating weights from scalar reward** — habituation, not a learned value.
- **Results**: Honestly staged. **Four matched campaigns motivated the design** by exposing **reward-accounting errors and tool-failure loops**; reward-driven components **did not improve the tested primary outcomes** over the reward-free MB condition. ### 7.6 Intrinsic-Extrinsic Coupling in Learning Dynamics
- **Authors**: Qinyou Wang
- **Affiliation**: not stated (single author) (tentative)
- **Venue**: arXiv preprint **cs.LG/stat.ML, New submission cross-listed 2609.30185**
- **Key Innovations**: Formalizes the observation that **a learner's current observations need not determine its response to further training**, via **intrinsic–extrinsic coupling** expressed as the **continuation-conditioned value of a constrained learning-state intervention**, with **observation-relative fibers** describing present agreement. An **executable finite-frame classifier-head write** protects current logits while repairing specified historical margins under **finite-precision acceptance checks**. The analysis distinguishes three things papers usually conflate: **local admissibility** (a repair is feasible), **continuation-conditioned intervention value** (it is worth doing given what training follows), and **complete-policy performance** (the system got better).
- **Results**: A **matched four-cell contrast** identifies **readout-specific non-additivity** between the same intrinsic intervention and alternative external continuations. A **five-root fresh-test comparison with Fiber present in every arm** shows **root-dependent rather than uniformly beneficial** effects. Coordination tests show content controls match or exceed the development gain; on a secondary cross-entropy readout, guided allocation yields **lower mean loss than standard replay in all five pairs**.
- **Significance**: The single most useful *framing* result in this section: **"we changed weights in a way that looks good now" and "the system got better" are three separate claims, and a matched multi-cell contrast is the only way to tell them apart.** The replay result (an intervention worth five correct predictions alone is worth **zero** after replay) is a sharp instance — interventions are not compositional. Anyone doing targeted weight edits, including game-NPC model patching, needs the continuation-conditioned check.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30185

- **2609.29281** **Online Task Adaptation via Self-Organisation** — asks whether task-specific adaptation can emerge from a **meta-learned self-organising process requiring no gradients at adaptation time**: a **Neural Cellular Automaton** whose locally-interacting recurrent cells hold a recurrent state plus a **fast associative memory**; backprop learns the dynamics and the memory read/write policy, then slow parameters stay **fixed** and online adaptation happens only through **cellwise memory updates driven by local prediction errors and a delta rule**. A **single pass** over support data substantially improves held-out performance with no gradient computation and no parameter updates, and holds up across large changes in how many examples are processed jointly. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29281
- **2609.29308** **SPEAR-Q: Decentralized Sticky Policy Authorization through Evidence Quorums** — removes the centralized Policy Decision Point: independent **Policy Authority Nodes** evaluate the active sticky policy from local context/consent/risk and emit **signed authorization evidence**; a **strict-majority quorum** decides Permit/Deny and a committed policy activates only after the same majority applies it. Deployed across physically separated hosts on the **Airbus Cyber Security Simulation Platform** with a MIMIC-IV-derived workload: **9,000 requests with no execution failure or timeout**, latency rising and throughput saturating with cluster size, PAN evidence waiting dominating under load. Correctly signed false-Permit evidence from **fewer compromised PANs than the quorum cannot produce a global Permit**, and authorization **fails closed** when reachable PANs drop below quorum. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29308
- **2609.29939** **Safe Formation Control of Open Multi-Robot Systems with Connectivity-Preserving Reconfiguration** — formation control for **open** multi-robot systems where robots **join or leave during operation**; a distributed barrier-Lyapunov-gradient controller plus a **formation manager** that coordinates additions/removals and establishes **prospective edges** to connect a joiner or preserve connectivity before a departure, with prospective upper-distance constraints temporarily relaxed by auxiliary dynamics and progressively recovered. Open-team dynamics modeled as a **switched system** with **uniform practical stability under a transition-dependent average dwell-time condition**; validated in Gazebo with dynamically simulated quadrotors under repeated join/depart. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29939
- **2609.29049** **Bearing-Only Formation Tracking Control for Euler-Lagrange Multi-Agent Systems Without Inter-Agent Communication** — formation tracking when **leaders move with time-varying velocities and no inter-agent communication exists**, so leaders' states are unavailable and cannot be recovered by distributed observers. The scheme exploits the fact that **bearing rates contain the projected relative-velocity information**, which with bearing rigidity yields a **rigidity-based damping mechanism** compensating the missing velocity error, embedded in a bearing-driven auxiliary variable as a **surrogate velocity error**; a **rigidity-preserving set is characterized and shown forward-invariant** to guarantee rigidity from initial conditions. Local practical tracking via Filippov Lyapunov analysis; asymptotic tracking in the constant-velocity case without initial-condition restriction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29049
- **2609.29958** **Multi-Dimensional Matching** — matching where agents and objects are described by **features rather than complete rankings**; a single **spectral projection** reduces the problem to a one-dimensional sort in **O(N log N)**. On descaled features/preferences, provably obtains the **exact Nash Social Welfare optimum within the projected space**, with an unconditional utilitarian-welfare guarantee and a conditional NSW guarantee. Honest: the mechanism is **stable against exogenous noise but not strategy-proof**, with an **explicit profitable misreport** provided; validated on an agentic-AI shopping application with a 100-instance robustness study. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29958

## Key Themes

1. **The game line is now arriving through driving and robotics, not through game venues.** §2.7 (RL-trained pedestrian model validated against *four* named empirical human behaviors — gap acceptance, yielding, hesitation, evasive speed adjustment) and §2.8 (a hand-authored finite-state evasive-then-brake driver with reaction-time and lane-check delays) are two complete specifications of a believable non-player character, one learned and one authored, from the same window. Together with §1.2's swarm-temperament framework, the window contains **three independent formalizations of "design the distribution of believable behaviors, not the individual agent."** For a game studio that is the entire NPC-crowd problem, arriving from a field with far more simulation budget.

2. **Adaptation has three distinct loci, and this window separates them cleanly.** Edit the *policy library* and never recompile (RACaP, §2.2 — 4% → 46% long-horizon); edit the *harness* as a testable, reversible hypothesis (§2.4 AdaHVLA — 22.5% → 57.5%); or edit the *command* and leave the shipped policy untouched (OSRAM, §3.6). A fourth, perception-as-tool (§2.3), beats a larger model on the same harness — 77.8% from a Flash-class model vs 61.1% from a frontier model. The through-line is that **none of these touch the model weights**, which is the deployment story for anything that has to run inside a frame budget or ship to players.

3. **Action-conditioned world models have a consistency bug, and it is now named.** §3.1: with asynchronous execution, the visual predictions used for subsequent actions must anticipate the effects of *already-committed* actions; Streaming-WAM makes the committed prefix a first-class conditioning input and gets **98.35%** at **2.93× faster** (90 s → 38 s real world). §3.4 supplies the complementary training-side claim: **reconstruction-trained latents are bad rollout models** — interventions that cut long-rollout error ~40% (at 100× fewer FLOPs) often *worsen* reconstruction and one-step accuracy. §3.2 then names the adversarial version: an update that is short-horizon-clean and long-horizon-malicious, invisible to parameter-space defenses. Three papers, one coherent thesis: **train and defend world models for what they imagine, not for what they predict.**

4. **Constraint satisfaction belongs in the sampling process.** §4.1 is the cleanest demonstration available: a stat-matching graph diffusion produces a usable artifact **13.7%** of the time; injecting validity constraints as soft masks during denoising plus a repair projection takes it to **96.8%**. This is the level-generation problem stated in power-grid vocabulary, and it says the fix for "a dungeon that looks like a dungeon and is not traversable" is a sampling-time constraint plus a projection — not a bigger model or a longer sample.

5. **Safety constraints can hide task failure rather than prevent it.** §7.2's **feasibility collapse**: when the goal lies inside the region where safety constraints activate, a termination-based constrained-RL policy learns that dwelling outside is strictly preferable, and the compliance metric looks *excellent* while task completion collapses. One auxiliary value critic for dense per-step success fixes it. Any safety-constrained agent with a terminal goal — docking, quest completion, level exit — needs an explicit success signal, or it will learn to hover safely at the door.

6. **Evaluation is fracturing into more axes, and the newest one is recovery.** §5.1 separates **recovery from execution-induced intermediate states** from initial-state competence and shows the two do not predict each other. §5.3 evaluates **the delivered artifact** under a host that holds labels outside the solver's input interface. §7.6 supplies the matching theory: local admissibility, continuation-conditioned value, and complete-policy performance are **three different claims**, and a matched multi-cell contrast — not a before/after — is what distinguishes them (replay turns a five-correct-prediction intervention into zero).

7. **Deployment numbers are now the most reliable content in the window, and two of them cut against their own headline.** §6.2 puts model-based diffusion inside a real-time loop (**14.7 ms of a 50 ms period**; a *linear* Koopman lift almost never reaches the goal). §6.3 shows **content-blind frame skipping costs 8.6 pp AP@50** while a per-object Kalman threshold cuts energy 12–17.6% *and* gains 0.8 pp. But §6.4 self-decomposes its 2.8–3.0× speedup to show **2.7× is ordinary graph capture**, leaving ~0.3× for the actual spectral method; and §6.6's title asks whether dynamic depth pays and the answer is that the *runtime mechanism* does not move the quality-latency frontier at all — the **training recipe** produces a better static family, and dynamic dispatch then costs 2.2%. The block's value is that the negative accounting is inside the papers.

8. **Remaining game-theory content is social choice, not play.** The window's genuinely game-theoretic unclaimed work is the *voting* line (already claimed today by the 09-28 `arxiv-ai-search`: ABC diverse representation 2609.29332, costly Hotelling–Downs 2609.29869) plus online fair division in the replacement stream (2609.23577) and multi-dimensional matching (runner-up 2609.29958). The deeper theoretical content is elsewhere: **vector** Bellman equations for robust average-reward games with *state-dependent* long-run reward and genuine saddle guarantees (§1.6), a contraction framework covering bootstrapping with **iterate-growing sampling error** (§1.5), an all-time concentration bound giving a two-timescale actor-critic a "safe region and stay" property (§1.4), and a **published bandit-PAC lower bound shown to be incorrect** with two proof gaps located (§1.8). These are the papers that will constrain the next generation of game-RL algorithms, and none of them is in a game venue.
