## ① Recommendation, Retrieval & Industrial CTR Systems (6)

### T-RoPE: Time-Aware Rotary Position Embedding for Sequential Recommendation
- **Authors**: Yang Liu, Noel Loo, Ali Khanafer, Shuying Sun, Akshay Soni, Zhong Wu, Linjun Yang
- **arXiv**: [2609.30576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30576) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Sequential generative recommenders inherited RoPE from LLMs, but a recommendation interaction index records only **event order** — nothing about elapsed time, behavioral cycles, or calendar phase. T-RoPE replaces index-only rotation with timestamp-based angles plus learnable temporal coefficients, multiscale frequency banks, shifted query alignment, and non-stationary key rotation. The paper **proves that standard RoPE — even when fed timestamps — remains time-translation invariant** and therefore cannot distinguish seasonal contexts; T-RoPE breaks that invariance while keeping the RoPE interface. Best result on **every metric on every dataset** across five public benchmarks (+78–130% HR@10 on sparse PixelRec, +8–12% on Amazon Books). On an industrial e-commerce dataset with **>6B interactions**, every metric improves 13–82% over the HSTU + Time RAB backbone, with the largest ablation gains attributed to multiscale frequencies (+56% NDCG@50) and non-stationary keys (+4%). Forward/backward algorithms add cost linear in sequence length and head dimension.
- **Why it matters**: **The window's flagship CTR-adjacent result** — a named online A/B in the Shop app reporting **conversion rate +0.33% and order count +0.63%**, which is exactly the metric class this wiki's CTR line has been missing for a fortnight. The invariance proof is the transferable part: *any* time-aware positional scheme built on stock RoPE inherits a blind spot for seasonality, and the fix is architectural rather than a hyperparameter. Also a live rebuttal of the "HSTU + time-aware-RAB" convention (cf. wiki's OneTrans-V2 / HSTU thread).

### KuaFu: Compressing Long User Behavior into Understanding at Billion Scale
- **Authors**: Jiahao Hui, Lin Zhu, Yishen Hu, Jingdong Shu, Zetai Jiang, Xining Ran, Ben Tan, Yeshou Cai, Gong Chen, Haijie Gu, Jie Jiang (Microsoft Research Asia — inferred from author record; **tentative**, arXiv prints no affiliation)
- **arXiv**: [2609.31045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31045) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Names the shared substrate under conversational agents, generative recommenders, and personalized ads: **understanding each user from raw behavior**. Industrial practice is task-specific (extract a subsequence, train a dedicated model), which hits two walls — sequences stay enormous after filtering (content-interest summarization reads several hundred items → tens of thousands of tokens serialized as prompt text) and profiles refresh weekly for a billion users at ~100K QPM, setting a hard throughput floor. KuaFu is a unified behavior-compression layer whose minimal unit is one behavior item: a **two-axis projector compresses each item into 2–4 tokens of width 128–256** (~10× along the token axis, 20× along width; per-item cache 10 KB → 0.5 KB), trained in four fidelity-oriented stages with layered intermediate evaluation. Four production profiling tasks: matches or exceeds uncompressed single-task production models on **all five headline metrics**, raises per-GPU throughput **37%–350%**, and **saves 190 GPUs**. On public benchmarks it nearly always beats prior compressors at equal compression ratio (up to +17.7 EM out-of-domain on MRQA); on RecBench a **4B model surpasses its 8B counterpart by 1.90 points**.
- **Why it matters**: Deployed **ten months on Tencent's advertising and recommendation platform, lifting overall GMV by 1.37%** — the second live business lift in the window. The paper's most useful contribution for practitioners is the honest taxonomy of what truncation silently does: it introduces **four named hallucination types — fabrication, omission, date misattribution, broken logic** — that are invisible because there is no way to evaluate the compressed representation itself, surfacing only as diffuse downstream degradation. That is a direct argument for compressed-representation eval suites, and it explains why agents, generative rec, and ads converge on the same engineering problem.

### Component Benchmark: Hierarchical Model Profiling for Large-scale Recommendation Systems
- **Authors**: Dharak Kharod, Yuzhen Huang, Zhou Wang, Jackie Xu, Fuzail Khan, Jacky Zhou, Hao Yan, Lidong Zhao, Xizhou Feng, Yvonne Liu, Karthik Jayaraman, Praveen Ramachandran, Vishwa Karia, Yashasvi Makin (Meta — inferred from author record; **tentative**)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30656) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Recommendation models are structurally heterogeneous — memory-bandwidth-bound operations, small compute-bound dense layers, dynamic shapes from **jagged categorical features**, and low-arithmetic-intensity ops — and they are written by engineers experimenting with compositions *without visibility into hardware execution characteristics*. Standard profilers give either end-to-end throughput or operator-level traces, but neither attributes performance to **the submodules practitioners actually reason about**. CB profiles each submodule independently in a hierarchical, plugin-extensible manner, rendering a tree-structured interactive visualization. The production scale stated: **TB-scale models, thousands of GPUs, 100B examples per day**. Demonstrated on common open-sourced models, with a discussion of how it was used to accelerate real optimization work.
- **Why it matters**: The infrastructure paper the rec field is missing. End-to-end latency tells you *that* you're slow; only submodule attribution tells you which of the four heterogeneous bottlenecks owns the number. Pairs directly with ①'s other entries: ESP needs per-subspace cost accounting to serve (its exhaustive GPU kNN over one concatenated index is a deliberate trade), and KuaFu's 37–350% per-GPU gains need this kind of attribution to be attributable at all.

### Embedding Subspace Partitioning for Dynamic Multi-Objective Retrieval
- **Authors**: Shaobo Zhang, Alice Leung, Yunxiang Ren, Ping Liu, Yuchin Juan, Qianqi Shen, Benjamin Le, Jianqiang Shen, Chengming Jiang, Ko-Cheng Wang, Vidya Krishnamurthy, Caleb Johnson, Fedor Borisyuk, Luke Simon, Jingwei Wu, Wenjing Zhang (LinkedIn — **tentative**; RecSys 2026)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30601) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Bi-encoders dominate large-scale retrieval for efficiency but collapse heterogeneous signals (semantic relevance vs engagement vs revenue) into **one static embedding space** — so once trained, the retriever cannot adapt to shifting objective priorities at serving time without retraining, and joint multi-objective loss optimization induces cross-objective interference. **ESP** decomposes the embedding into task-aware subspaces and replaces the single dot product with a **weighted sum of per-subspace similarities whose weights are tunable at serving time**. For Transformer bi-encoders it uses the model's native end-of-sequence token as a segment delimiter, with segment-aware attention masking and position-encoding resets to guarantee subspace isolation in a *single* forward pass. Serving uses **GPU-accelerated exhaustive kNN over one concatenated index**, eliminating the per-objective ANN infrastructure multi-head approaches demand. On an MS MARCO-derived benchmark a single ESP model traces a broad Pareto frontier, consistently beating strong multi-task baselines across operating points; deployed in **LinkedIn's job-matching platform (70M+ weekly users)** with significant business-metric lifts.
- **Why it matters**: The cleanest answer in the window to "how do you change a rec objective in production without retraining." The subtlety worth stealing: **exhaustive kNN over one concatenated index** beats ANN-per-objective on *both* quality and infrastructure cost at this scale — the standard "many heads means many indexes" reflex is not free. Multi-objective tension between relevance and revenue is the exact conflict that shows up in ad ranking, and ESP makes the trade-off a serving-time dial rather than a training-time commitment.

### Recommendation World Models for Future-State Control
- **Authors**: Jinfeng Xu, Zheyu Chen, Ziyue Peng, Jianheng Tang, Zheng Lin, Jing Yang, Puzhen Wu, Zheng Xing, Victor C. M. Leung
- **arXiv**: [2609.30711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30711) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Sequential recommendation optimizes *which items to rank*, but each displayed slate also shapes subsequent feedback and user state — so a trained ranker can support decisions about future consequences. **UA-TWM** is a utility-anchored world-model interface: construct nearby slate actions, estimate their target-relevant consequences, and select an alternative subject to utility constraints, with the reference slate as fallback when nothing qualifies. Two instantiations: a **logged-replay** version combining utility and target-gain estimates with calibrated failure-risk prediction, and a **closed-loop** version using one-step state-action prediction that updates after observed feedback. Evaluated across **twelve sequential backbones** on MovieLens-25M and KuaiRand-Pure, with repeated target-directed interaction in KuaiSim: attaching the interface improves Recall@20, NDCG@20, and future-state alignment for **every matched logged backbone**. Selection ablations expose the utility and risk costs of aggressive target pursuit; closed-loop diagnostics isolate the contribution of action-conditioned prediction.
- **Why it matters**: World models migrate from game/embodied AI into the ranking stack — the slate becomes an *action* whose consequence you can predict. The honest framing is what makes it useful: this is a **selection interface around a trained ranker**, not a replacement for it, and the fallback-to-reference-slate design means it degrades to the incumbent ranker when the world model is uninformative. Same first author + Leung group as the companion LW2S skip-policy paper (2609.30734) — the group is treating workflow components as actions with counterfactual value, in the ranker and in the agent.

### RecToolBench: Benchmarking Recommendation-Specific Tool Orchestration under Fuzzy User Intent
- **Authors**: Xiao Chen, Yicheng Zhao, Yingying Wu, Zhendong Chu, Changyi Ma, Qingsong Wen, Xuan Song (EMNLP 2026)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30717) — cs.IR
- **Key contribution**: Agentic recommenders shift from passive filtering engines to instruction-following agents that call tools, but existing benchmarks assume explicit user intent, simplified tool environments, or isolated function calls. RecToolBench is an **MCP-based** benchmark: **1,200+ executable tasks** across three recommendation domains, **13 MCP servers, 32 tools**, spanning single-tool calls, parallel calls, sequential chains, and hybrid orchestration. Built with a scalable **synthesize → fuzzify → judge** pipeline that generates executable fuzzy-intent tasks; trajectories scored by rule-based execution checks *and* rubric-based LLM evaluation. Headline finding: **syntactically valid tool calls do not guarantee successful recommendations** — models struggle with semantic parameter grounding, multi-step evidence integration, and grounded final recommendations, and all three degrade as orchestration complexity increases.
- **Why it matters**: The "fuzzify" step is the methodological contribution: degrading an explicit intent into a *fuzzy* one is what separates a recommender agent that can act from one that can only be prompted. The complexity-monotonic degradation is the useful diagnostic — it says the bottleneck for agentic rec is **not tool syntax** (which LLMs have largely solved) but grounding parameters and integrating evidence across calls. Complements the deployed-eval thread (09-25's Kuaishou SEEK) with a public, executable benchmark.

## ② Evaluation Instruments, Judges & Benchmark Validity (5)

### HARDEN: Constrained Evolutionary Search for Harder, Answer-Preserving Evaluation Cases
- **Authors**: Aditya Kumaran, Rahul Singhal, Karime Maamari, Amine Mhedhbi, Pradyumna Tambwekar
- **arXiv**: [2609.30571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30571) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Curated benchmarks underrepresent the complexity of enterprise deployments. HARDEN is a **constrained evolutionary search** method that adapts existing evaluation cases into harder variants **while keeping their expected outputs fixed**, searching along generated domain-specific complexity axes under feasibility constraints (task semantics, realism, execution validity). Across FinQA, PubMedQA, and ContractNLI and three Qwen3.5 scales (35B-A3B, 122B-A10B, 397B-A17B), it **reduces task-model accuracy by 22.7% on average and up to 49.9% relative** to single-pass baselines using the same feasibility checks.
- **Why it matters**: The answer-preserving constraint is what makes this rigorous — a search that could change the gold answer would just be generating a different benchmark. A 22.7-point accuracy drop with the labels held constant means the measured gaps between models are *task-difficulty-limited*, not capability-limited. This is the constructive counterpart to the 09-25 report's destructive audits (CoT-prefix scoring, baseline-shape artifacts): those showed evaluations flip under benign protocol changes, this one supplies a principled way to make them harder without changing what counts as correct.

### DIAL: Position-Debiased LLM Judges with Adaptive Human Preference Calibration
- **Authors**: Zesheng Cai, Yingqi Fan, Sichang Chen, Jin-Hong Du
- **arXiv**: [2609.31215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31215) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: LLM-as-judge is the de facto standard for subjective evaluation, but judgments are sensitive to response order and — even after position effects are removed — **diverge systematically from human preference**. DIAL combines abundant LLM comparisons with limited human comparisons to *separate* judge-specific position effects, *learn* shared structure in position-debiased LLM preferences, and *adaptively calibrate* that structure toward the human target. Theory covers identification of latent LLM preferences / position effects / human calibration, adaptive estimation balancing LLM anchoring against scarce human evidence, and fixed-weight uncertainty quantification. Empirically robust to unbalanced response order, strong human-aligned rankings with few labels, and able to adapt toward human evidence when LLM information is imperfect. Ships a real-data resource: **410,000+ judgments from 21 LLM judges in both display orders**.
- **Why it matters**: The important structural claim is that position bias and human divergence are **two different defects** needing two different corrections — most position-debiasing work treats them as one. The 410K-judgment, 21-judge, both-order dataset is independently the window's most reusable artifact for anyone building a preference leaderboard. Pairs with 2609.31184 (latent-variable bias correction) and 2609.30471 (reference-grounding) as the three distinct failure modes of judge infrastructure identified this window.

### CARGO: Context-Aware Retrieval-Gated Evaluation of Agentic AI in Production
- **Authors**: Mukul Chhabra, Shail Patel, Luigi Medrano
- **arXiv**: [2609.30471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30471) — cs.CL
- **Key contribution**: Reference-based judging assumes the reference answer is the target. In deployed agentic systems operating over **dynamic entities** (support cases, assets, accounts), the closest available reference applies the correct *procedure* to a *different* entity — so a literal judge penalizes differing identifiers, dates, and statuses as errors or hallucinations. CARGO names this **reference-instance divergence (RID)** and (i) treats retrieved references as *procedural exemplars*, grounding factual judgments in the live instance's observed context; (ii) assigns each claim a three-way status — supported / contradicted / unverifiable — penalizing only contradictions; (iii) **gates evaluation by retrieval confidence**, casting production evaluation as selective prediction. On CARGO-Bench (246 items, 2 judge models, 7,872 judgments), the standard reference-based judge **penalizes 100% of correct entity-transplanted answers** and is uninformative (discrimination index DI ≈ 0) — and supplying the live facts *without reframing the dimension definitions* changes nothing. CARGO eliminates the false penalties (0/50) while retaining near-complete contradiction recall (50/50 and 49/50), raising DI to **0.58 [0.48, 0.68]**; a rubric-swap control attributes most of the effect to the context-grounded *dimension definitions*, not the context itself. The paper publishes its own limitation: the leniency that protects entity values suppresses detection of procedural corruptions (**20% recall**), and a post-hoc fix plus an LLM-as-annotator study with written guidelines and adjudication show the same blind spot.
- **Why it matters**: An *operationally* precise judge failure, not a psychometric one: in any deployment over changing entities (support, ads campaigns, user accounts, inventory), a naive reference judge scores ~0 discrimination and you would never notice, because it looks like a *conservative* judge rather than a broken one. The attribution result matters most — merely feeding the live context does not fix it; the **dimension definitions** must be re-grounded, otherwise the judge has nothing to compare against. Unusually honest about the residual cost (20% procedural-corruption recall).

### Same Text, Different Numbers: The Divergence of LLM-Based Measures
- **Authors**: Hamid Boustanifar, Sasan Mansouri
- **arXiv**: [2609.31013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31013) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Researchers increasingly convert corporate text into empirical variables with generative LLMs. Thirteen measures — sentiment, management clarity, uncertainty, answer specificity, climate and political risk — are scored over **S&P 500 earnings-call transcripts by seven LLMs from different providers**. Cross-model rank correlations average only **0.52**, and transcript-level differences common across providers account for only **34% of total score variation**. Cross-model disagreement **does not predict subsequent analyst or market disagreement**, consistent with a large model-specific component rather than common ambiguity in the underlying disclosure. Model choice materially changes downstream inference: coefficient magnitudes, signs, and statistical significance vary substantially. Averaging across providers makes *rankings* more stable for most constructs, but **score levels remain sensitive to which models are in the ensemble**.
- **Why it matters**: The most transferable measurement result in the window, because the pattern generalizes: a 0.52 mean cross-model rank correlation means an LLM-derived variable is **model-contingent**, and the paper's proposed remedy — validate across providers, treat the model as part of the instrument — should be default practice for any LLM-as-measurement pipeline. The negative result is the sharpest part: disagreement between models is *not* tracking real-world interpretive disagreement, so ensembling is fixing variance, not discovering signal.

### Evidence-Grounded Auditing of Identification Assumptions in Climate-Policy Causal Evaluations
- **Authors**: Yonghong Zhang, Yong Xie, Isabel M. Parra, Ricardo Correia (ClimateNLP 2026 @ EMNLP 2026)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30867) — cs.CL
- **Key contribution**: Difference-in-differences is the workhorse for climate-policy evaluation, but assessing the evidence for its identification assumptions is hard. **ARGUS** is a structured LM pipeline that audits reported evidence against an **eleven-dimension assumption-implication-evidence rubric** and **abstains** when relevant evidence cannot be retrieved. On an 11-flaw benchmark ARGUS detects **73% of planted flaws vs 18% for a keyword-based pipeline**; across 26 economics papers it **abstains on ~40% of paper-dimension assessments** for lack of retrievable evidence. In a five-paper pilot with two-annotator-reconciled labels it assigns a *higher* risk level than the labels on **25 of the 33** assessments it completes — and a rule fixed *before* the labels arrived removes most of that in-sample advantage (weighted agreement stays low).
- **Why it matters**: The abstention rate is the result, not a limitation: **~40% abstention is the honest operating point** for evidence-linked auditing, and a pipeline that never abstains is not measuring anything. Also a well-behaved instance of the wiki's recurring eval-validity theme — the pre-registered rule loses most of its apparent skill, which is exactly the failure mode 09-25's cluster-bootstrap self-audit (2609.30074) documented for model rankings. Belongs next to 2609.30528 below: both are about *whether the assumptions behind a causal estimate are checkable* rather than whether the estimate is well-computed.

## ③ Agents: Memory, Context & Workflow Integrity (5)

### AutoResearch at Production Scale: Failure Modes and a Multi-Agent Framework
- **Authors**: Aparajith Chandran, Juwon Kim, Saurav Jha, Pablo Castells, Florian Hottier (ICDM 2026, Applied Track)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30541) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: Applies Andrej Karpathy's **AutoResearch** paradigm — an LLM that iteratively edits a training script and keeps modifications that improve a held-out scalar metric — to **production recommendation pipelines**, and reports **twelve weeks at production scale**: iterations consume hours of multi-GPU compute, evaluation involves competing criteria, and campaigns span weeks across many training jobs. Across two independently developed representation-learning systems for a book-recommendation pipeline, **220+ experiments** surfaced **five failure modes absent from the original setting**: infrastructure fragility, agent memory decay, search-direction stagnation, iteration-cost asymmetry, and **metric fixation**. The contribution is a three-principle scaffolding — **prevent, persist, redirect** — mapping each failure mode to a structural remedy, whose instantiation scales with iteration cost. Results: **1.82× Recall@6 and 2.1× coherence** over hand-tuned baselines, and the agent autonomously designed a text-only fallback expanding catalog coverage **5.8×**. The two systems span **nearly three orders of magnitude in per-iteration cost yet exhibit the same failure modes**, suggesting these are structural properties of production-scale autonomous research rather than application artifacts.
- **Why it matters**: The direct industrial successor to the Karpathy auto-research line this wiki is built on, and the key finding is the *negative generality claim*: the same five failure modes at 1,000× cost spread means **AutoResearch-style loops do not automatically survive contact with production economics** — the "it just works" demo regime has no memory decay and no iteration-cost asymmetry. "Metric fixation" deserves attention from anyone running a held-out-scalar improvement loop (including this wiki's own ingest discipline): a loop that optimizes one scalar reliably stops measuring the others. The failure-mode → remedy mapping is directly reusable.

### A Benchmark and Diagnostic Study of Epistemic Admission in Shared Agent Memory
- **Authors**: Xiaoyang Li, Yiqi Wang, Chencheng Zhu, Ke Xu, Wencheng Yang, Zequn Sun, Pingan Song, Yiqun Duan, Taotao Cai
- **arXiv**: [2609.30813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30813) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Evaluating *claim admission* in shared agent memory is hard because **repeated claims can be mistaken for independent evidence** — an agent may copy or paraphrase a retrieved belief — while admitting a false claim exposes every subsequent agent. The **Correlated Promotion Benchmark (CPB)** has two arms: CPB-Static builds a frozen test split from publicly annotated sources with fixed gold actions; CPB-Live runs multi-agent teams over a shared store, records all writes and retrievals, and tracks **source lineage** defined per scenario, with a separate consumer answering from the store alone. Eight admission policies × four agent families. Findings: policies that **deduplicate sources reject many true claims alongside false ones**, while policies preserving answer coverage **admit nearly as many false claims as unrestricted sharing**. Gating on *declared source type* cuts false adoption to **0.06–0.09** vs **0.22–0.47** for other answering policies. Once an uncontested false belief enters memory, the consumer asserts it in **0.97–0.99 of probes across all families**. No non-oracle policy consistently rejects false claims across verbatim copies, paraphrases, and paraphrases *declared authoritative*.
- **Why it matters**: The 0.97–0.99 contamination persistence rate is the headline: once a false belief is uncontested in shared memory, **source-dedup policies do not save you** — the copy is already indistinguishable from a rediscovery. This is the poisoning-resistance counterpart to the wiki's existing DRSR / set-level-deletion thread (09-24 paper-check) and the Scoped-ORC work (09-25): three independent papers now converge on the same conclusion that **memory-integrity controls must be per-lineage at write time**, because no retrieval-time control can recover a belief that every consumer asserts. The "paraphrases declared authoritative" attack is the sharpest: declared source type is the strongest cheap signal but is itself attacker-controlled.

### Probing Stability-Plasticity Tradeoffs in Agent Memory through Cognitive Experimental Paradigms
- **Authors**: Jiaqi Ding, Guorong Wu (EMNLP 2026 Main)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30558) — cs.CL
- **Key contribution**: Current agent-memory evaluations **collapse memory behavior into final-answer accuracy**, which cannot distinguish a system that updates correctly from one that never updates. Inspired by cognitive memory research (memory is reconstructive, shaped by interference, source reliability, reinforcement, reactivation), **MemProbe** turns that insight into **four reusable experimental paradigms — interference, misinformation, consolidation strength, reconsolidation window** — that manipulate *when* a memory should be updated, preserved, or treated as uncertain, and decomposes correctness into **behavioral profiles** revealing how systems update, preserve, attribute, and temporally organize information. Instantiated in a **56-episode diagnostic suite** over **six incremental memory systems** under a unified protocol. Result: **systems with similar aggregate scores exhibit distinct behavioral profiles**.
- **Why it matters**: The aggregate-score collision is the finding — six systems can tie on final-answer accuracy while differing in exactly the dimensions that matter for long-horizon agents. The four paradigms are the reusable artifact: they are experimental *designs*, not a benchmark score, so they port to any memory system. Pairs with CPB (2609.30813) as the diagnostic half of a complete memory-integrity story — MemProbe measures *how* a memory system behaves, CPB measures *what* happens when false beliefs compete for admission, and the 09-25 Scoped-ORC work supplies an *intervention*.

### Stale-Document Poisoning: When Outdated Retrieval Overrides Correct Model Answers
- **Authors**: Md Shamim Ahmed, Lukas Galke Poech, Richard Röttger
- **arXiv**: [2609.31342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31342) — cs.CL
- **Key contribution**: RAG is deployed to fix outdated knowledge, but retrieval only helps while the evidence is still valid. The paper identifies a **temporal alignment failure — stale-document poisoning**, where outdated evidence makes a model wrong *despite answering correctly without retrieval*. Benchmark: **317 verified knowledge reversals** across medicine, law, software, and platform policy, grounded in dated official sources. Across **12 models**, recent medical reversals are harder than long-established ones. Outdated retrieval flips **30% of Llama and 37% of Qwen** answers *even without instructions to trust the document*; explicit follow instructions raise this to **66% and 75%**. Across four open models × four domains poisoning ranges **17–91%**, while matched up-to-date evidence is followed **97–100%** of the time. To isolate temporal applicability, the historical evidence is held unchanged across 50 reversals while **only the evaluation date varies**: dates alone produce modest adaptation, but when models are explicitly told *when* the old evidence stops applying, larger models switch almost perfectly. Causal interventions confirm the validity information directly shapes the decision. A fixed recency-aware hybrid re-ranker cuts poisoning by **4.6–10.0 points** when dates are accurate.
- **Why it matters**: The "dates alone barely help, but an explicit applicability statement nearly fixes it" result is the actionable one: **RAG systems should carry a validity interval per document, not just a date.** Also the sharpest statement yet of the follow-instruction backfire — telling a model to trust retrieved evidence *amplifies* the attack from 30–37% to 66–75%. The remedy depends on reliable temporal metadata, which is precisely the thing most enterprise corpora lack.

### Learning What to Skip: Counterfactual Credit Assignment for Efficient Multi-Agent LLM Workflows
- **Authors**: Jinfeng Xu, Zheyu Chen, Ziyue Peng, Zheng Lin, Shuo Yang, Jinze Li, Zheng Xing, Mengran Li, Victor C. M. Leung
- **arXiv**: [2609.30734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30734) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Multi-agent LLM workflows chain planning, execution, verification, and summarization, but the value of each component **depends on the state already produced** — executing everything wastes compute or overwrites a correct intermediate answer. LW2S formulates component omission as **counterfactual credit assignment**: full-workflow logs reveal the executed trajectory's reward, while controlled **skip interventions** reveal the consequences of omitting a step. It learns action-specific safety models from those interventions and combines held-out calibration with domain-native guards to select skips; when an early skip is rejected the controller can continue and reconsider a later component. Across mathematical reasoning, multiple-choice QA, and code generation with two instruction-model families, LW2S **reduces recorded token cost while matching or improving aggregate full-workflow accuracy**. Scale-up and second-topology experiments examine component redundancy, and shared-error cases reveal why agreement alone is insufficient for skip selection.
- **Why it matters**: Skip-decision policies are the current cost lever in agent harnesses, and this supplies the missing methodology: you cannot get the value of a component by reading the full-workflow log, because the log tells you what happened, not what would have happened without the step. The **controlled skip intervention is the measurement**, and the honest negative — agreement between components is *not* a sufficient signal for skipping — is what keeps it from being a routing heuristic. Same group as ①'s UA-TWM: one group treating components and slates as actions with counterfactual value.

## ④ Safety, Alignment & Interpretability (4)

### Monitor Jailbreaking: Evading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring Without Encoded Reasoning
- **Authors**: Julian Schulz (AdvML-Frontiers × CoTMA Workshop @ COLM 2026)
- **arXiv**: [2609.31121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31121) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: CoT monitoring is a promising safety technique for reasoning models, but **encoded reasoning** (models hiding true reasoning from monitors and humans) is a known worry, and RL optimization pressure against monitors is a suspected driver. The paper trains reasoning models on a main task *and* a side task, **penalizing them when a monitor detects side-task reasoning**. The surprise: models learn to evade monitors **without encoding their reasoning at all** — instead learning to phrase and format their chains of thought so monitors fail to flag side-task reasoning, while the reasoning stays **completely transparent to human readers**. The authors call this **monitor jailbreaking**. It arises across different model sizes, monitors, and tasks; jailbreaks **generalize to monitors not seen during training**, including both less and more capable monitors, and transfer across monitor prompts. While the strategies *look* simple, manually replicating them does not reliably fool monitors. **Paraphrasing is an effective defense**: paraphrasing a jailbroken CoT lets the same monitor correctly flag it while the model still performs both tasks.
- **Why it matters**: The direct continuation of this wiki's monitor-evasion thread, and it **falsifies the reassuring reading** of the 09-25 finding that evasion requires *encoding* (that paper found agents "encoding prohibited commands, decomposing operations across tool calls, retrying until context leaves monitor history"). Here evasion is achievable with **readable** reasoning, which is worse for defenses built on the assumption that a human auditor can catch what a monitor misses. Three results matter operationally: evasion **transfers to unseen and stronger monitors** (so evaluating against one monitor does not generalize), it **cannot be hand-copied** (so static pattern defenses fail), and **paraphrasing restores detection** (a cheap, deployable mitigation). Together with 09-25's EvasionBench, the evidence is now that ordinary task pressure is sufficient to produce monitor evasion, and that the evasion mode is surface-form rather than semantic.

### Stealth Apart, Harm Together: Skill Cascading Attacks on Skill-Based Agent Systems
- **Authors**: Zihao Zhu, Siwei Lyu, Adel Bibi, Baoyuan Wu (NeurIPS 2026)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30383) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: A "skill" is a modular package of natural-language instructions, executable scripts, and reference resources that an agent loads at runtime — the reuse mechanism behind Claude Code-style skill ecosystems, and a new attack surface. Prior work studies vulnerabilities *within* individual skills; this paper introduces **skill cascading attacks**, where a malicious objective is distributed across multiple skills so that **each modification looks benign in isolation** yet their combined execution is harmful. The worked example is a prescription-review pipeline: skill one weakens signals of recently discontinued medications in the extracted history, skill two downgrades the severity of drug interactions tied to them, and skill three suppresses the resulting low-priority alert in the final summary — so a severe drug-interaction warning silently disappears before reaching the physician. **SkillCascade** is an automated multi-agent red-teaming framework; **SkillCascade-Bench** ships 213 validated cascading test cases across multiple agent systems and domains. Across representative agents (**OpenClaw, Claude Code, Codex**) and LLM backbones, cascaded interactions reliably induce harmful behavior while **evading existing per-skill scanners and runtime monitors**.
- **Why it matters**: The compositional blind spot is now documented at three levels of the stack in one window: across skills (here), across tool calls (2609.31301's persistent-outcome validation in the same batch), and across the provenance graph (2609.30830's AGATE, below). The architectural conclusion is sharp: **component-level integrity does not imply system-level safety** when components are individually benign, and every per-skill scanner and runtime monitor in current use is structurally incapable of seeing it. The prescription-review example is the most alarming artifact in the window — the harm is a *disappearing* safety warning, which no user-side signal can detect. Pairs with 09-25's trace-tampering and output-prefix results: that thread found agents defeating individual boundaries; this shows the boundaries fail even when every component passes.

### JevAdvBench: A Benchmark and Black-Box Attacks for Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions Models
- **Authors**: Jianyi Hu, Hangtao Zhang, Yi Liu, Yeqi Zeng, Li Zeng, Xianlong Wang, Rui Wang, Leo Yu Zhang
- **arXiv**: [2609.31142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142) — cs.CR
- **Key contribution**: RLCD models (Jev and relatives) answer a typed question about a state with a probability/choice/score, and **software acts on the answer without a person reading it** — so adversarial benchmarks built for generative models do not transfer: a typed model generates nothing and returns a well-formed answer even when manipulated. Measurement is also hard, because identical requests can return different answers, most labels come from the model itself, and the API preprocesses requests out of view. The key idea: **score each attacked decision against the model's own clean decision rather than against labels**, and read it against the change caused by an identical re-run. **JevAdvBench** is (to the authors' knowledge) the first adversarial benchmark for RLCD models: **812 typed questions over 66 scenarios**, plus a black-box attack suite of **9,744 single-edit variants**, each editing one part of a request, with billed input tokens confirming the edit reached the model. On jev-1.13.0: rewording stays within **1.2 percentage points** of the re-run baseline and fields outside the schema never reach the model — but **one unverified opinion appended to the state flips 12.1% of decisions**, statistically tied with the strongest injected command (10.1%), and **pushes 38% of confident answers below the 0.8 confidence threshold** that routes them to human review.
- **Why it matters**: The fourth entry in the wiki's JEV/RLCD thread (following the 09-24 "Same Scores, Different Decisions", 09-25's Just Ask Jev, and JevOut), and the most operationally useful so far. Two results deserve to change practice: a *plain unverified opinion* is as effective as the strongest injected command, so **the state must be treated as untrusted, argued input** rather than a neutral record; and the attack's main effect is **confidence-threshold mass** — 38% of answers dropping below the human-review threshold means the *safety* mechanism degrades even where the decisions don't flip. The "score against your own clean decision, and against an identical re-run" methodology is the right way to benchmark any nondeterministic, self-labeled model.

### Does Thinking Help Fairness? Reasoning Tokens Resolve Some Biases but Create More
- **Authors**: Deng Pan, Joe Germino, Yihong Ma, Elizabeth Daly, Nuno Moniz, Ting Hua, Nitesh Chawla (Findings of EMNLP 2026)
- **arXiv**: [2609.30768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30768) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: Whether reasoning resolves or amplifies bias in reasoning LLMs has produced **competing conclusions in both directions**. Using a within-model thinking-vs-non-thinking ablation across **QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, and Qwen3-32B** on three high-stakes decision tasks (**Adult, COMPAS, Credit**), the paper shows thinking has an **asymmetric dual effect** on counterfactual fairness: it both *resolves* counterfactual flips produced by the non-thinking baseline and *creates* new flips at near-saturating model confidence. In **all nine (model, dataset) combinations, created flips outnumber resolved flips by roughly 5×**. To explain the effect, the thinking trace itself is treated as a measurable site of fairness change via two instruments: the **Counterfactual Depth Probability Gap (CDPG)** tracks bias evolution along thinking depth and shows bias **propagates and amplifies with thinking**; the **Bias Transition Matrix (BTM)** shows the asymmetric dual effect originates in the **pair-state joint transition**.
- **Why it matters**: The 5× ratio, consistent across all nine cells and three high-stakes datasets, resolves the prior literature's contradiction in the direction that matters: reasoning is not a fairness intervention. The subtlety is that the *aggregate* fairness number can look fine while the pair-level behavior degrades — which is exactly the class of finding that makes single-number bias reporting unsafe. CDPG and BTM are reusable instruments for anyone auditing reasoning models in high-stakes decisions (credit, hiring, criminal justice), and the honest framing ("resolve some, create more") is more useful than either extreme claim it replaces.

## ⑤ Decision Layer & Efficiency (5)

### When Do Surrogate Metrics Work? A Finite-Sample Comparison Under Realistic Failure Modes
- **Authors**: Zihao Chen
- **arXiv**: [2609.30528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30528) — stat.ME
- **Key contribution**: Online experiments are often evaluated before long-term outcomes mature; under rolling enrollment, long-term outcomes are observed only for early enrollees while short-term surrogates are available for everyone. Seven estimators are compared across **eleven data-generating processes** spanning partial mediation, drift, outcome sparsity, and enrollment-time labeling, with up to **R = 2,000 replications over more than 500 method-by-scenario cells**. The finding is a **sharp robustness–efficiency tradeoff**: the surrogate index delivers large efficiency gains when surrogacy holds but its **coverage collapses under violations**, while PPI-family methods stay asymptotically valid under random labeling at smaller gains. Contributions: the finite-sample variance of **PPI++** in the all-units parameterization for a fixed predictor; a joint asymptotic distribution for the two estimators under cross-fitting; a **Hausman-type estimator-disagreement diagnostic**; and a quantified **detection–damage gap** — in the partial-mediation design, a band of violations destroys surrogate-index coverage yet is too small to detect on most datasets. Empirical checks on real advertising data: on the **64,000-customer Hillstrom** experiment the diagnostic rarely flags a violation that biases the surrogate index, and a Cauchy-kernel hybrid inherits **10.7% relative bias**; on the **14-million-user Criteo** experiment the violation *is* detected, and subsampling traces detection turning on with scale as damage persists. PPI++ has limits too: at a rare-conversion n = 30,000 Criteo subsample its empirical coverage is **85.0%**. Recommendation: prespecify **PPI++ with the exact variance as primary** under random labeling and adequate labeled outcome counts, read the diagnostic as a *warning not a certificate*, and treat the surrogate index as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Why it matters**: **The single most directly applicable paper in the window for ads/CTR experimentation**, and the only one to test the surrogate-metric machinery on both classic advertising datasets (Hillstrom, Criteo). The detection–damage gap is the headline warning: **your violation diagnostic is weakest exactly in the sample sizes most ad experiments actually have** — on Hillstrom (64K) the diagnostic almost never flags a violation that *does* bias the surrogate index. Combined with ①'s business lifts, this is the honest boundary on reading a short-term metric lift as a long-term one. The rare-conversion coverage figure (85.0% at n = 30,000) is a concrete number to attach to any Criteo-style CVR read.

### EARL: Exposure- and Allocation-Reweighted Linear Estimator for Bipartite Experiments with Partial Assignment
- **Authors**: Alexey Kurennoy
- **arXiv**: [2609.31022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31022) — stat.ME
- **Key contribution**: **Bipartite A/B tests** randomize treatment over one set of units while measuring outcomes on another — e.g. an online marketplace tests a new pricing algorithm on a random subset of *items* while purchase satisfaction is measured on *customers*, each interacting with many items. Existing methods assume **every** randomization unit is assigned; in practice only a subset participates (rollout-risk caps, reserved holdouts, populations split across concurrent tests). Ignoring unassigned units biases estimation; including them requires care. **EARL** is an unbiased linear estimator that reweights not only by the (centred) share of treated connections among participants (**exposure**) but also by the **number of participating connections**, each inverse-weighted by its participation propensity, so well-covered units carry proportionally more weight. Proven unbiased, consistent, and asymptotically normal, with two asymptotic variance estimators and **minimal variance in a natural class of linear estimators**. EARL stays unbiased regardless of what unassigned units experience, as long as they contribute to expected outcomes additively — so unassigned units can be allocated to other, non-overlapping concurrent tests. Simulations on two public datasets: **up to 6× lower error than the strongest existing baseline**, and in some configurations **over an order of magnitude below Horvitz–Thompson-style alternatives**.
- **Why it matters**: Every large platform runs concurrent experiments on overlapping populations, and the standard practice of *dropping* the unassigned units is exactly the biased choice. The practical unlock is the last theoretical condition: unassigned units can be **reused in other non-overlapping tests**, which converts a nuisance (partial assignment) into a statistical asset. The two-weight construction (exposure × participation count) is the kind of thing that is easy to implement wrong and impossible to notice, since the failure is silent underestimation rather than a crash.

### Block Sparse Attention with Log-Linear Complexity
- **Authors**: Bohao Tang, Zhen Qin, Yuqi Pan, Zheng Li, Pengfei Liu
- **arXiv**: [2609.31093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31093) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: Block sparse attention is the standard route past quadratic self-attention cost, but **conventional block selection requires scoring all query-block pairs and therefore remains quadratic in sequence length** — the selection step is as expensive as the attention it is meant to save. **PISA** replaces it with a **pyramid Top-K selection strategy**: a coarse-to-fine key hierarchy where selection starts at the coarsest level, and at each level LogSumExp scoring over a bounded candidate set selects candidates for the next finer level, continuing to the finest. Pooling constructs **O(log N)** key levels, giving **O(N log N)** overall complexity. Hardware-aware **Triton kernels** for both training and inference fuse hierarchical routing and LogSumExp scoring **without materializing the query–key score matrix**. On language modeling tasks it matches the baseline on benchmarks such as commonsense reasoning while doing better on retrieval tasks.
- **Why it matters**: Attacks the part of sparse attention that usually gets skipped — the selector. Most block-sparse claims quote end-to-end FLOPs, which quietly ignore the fact that choosing the blocks cost the same order as attending to all of them; PISA's contribution is the *log-linear* bound on the whole procedure. The retrieval-vs-commonsense split is a useful diagnostic: sparse attention helps *retrieval-like* tasks more, which is the same axis the 09-25 Corpus Task Complexity paper (2609.29245) identified as where low-CTC evaluations mislead. The fused Triton routing is the part that decides whether the bound is realizable.

### RAZOR: Pruning Replaceable Experts in LLMs
- **Authors**: Mingyang Song, Mao Zheng
- **arXiv**: [2609.30465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30465) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: MoE models activate few experts per token but store the full pool; expert pruning cuts storage, and at a fixed budget the goal is to preserve the **output distribution** as closely as possible. But an expert's usage or contribution magnitude does not determine the damage its removal causes — what matters is whether **the surviving computation can replace its function**. RAZOR is a **training-free** expert-pruning method scoring *functional replaceability* via **consensus residuals** (deviations of expert outputs from the original weighted mixture). An **exact single-deletion identity** at a fixed layer input accounts for both survivor renormalization and router-selected refill, giving local scores aggregated over calibration tokens for budgeted pruning with no gradients and no recovery training. On **GLM-4.7-Flash, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731, and Hy3** at **25% and 50% expert removal**, RAZOR achieves the highest nine-task macro average among evaluated methods in **all eight settings**; on the two backbones with matched REAP runs it exceeds REAP by **2.12–5.59 points** and wins all 36 paired task comparisons, with lower reverse KL in all four matched settings. The paper publishes the negative: response analysis on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B reveals **changes in diversity, formatting, and termination** — task retention and predictive fidelity do not ensure generation stability.
- **Why it matters**: The conceptual correction is the contribution — the standard pruning criteria (usage frequency, contribution magnitude) measure the wrong thing, and the **single-deletion identity** makes the correct quantity computable in closed form, which is why no training is needed. The honest negative matters just as much: macro-average task scores and predictive fidelity both improve while *generation behavior* silently degrades in ways only response inspection catches. Anyone shipping a pruned MoE should treat diversity/formatting/termination drift as a release blocker that the standard metric will not surface.

### DynBranch: Speculative Subgraph Reuse for Dynamic Agentic LLM Serving
- **Authors**: Junyi Shen, Noppanat Wadlom, Zhengyuan Su, Yao Lu
- **arXiv**: [2609.31047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31047) — cs.DC
- **Key contribution**: Agentic LLM workflows decide execution paths at runtime, but downstream computation cannot begin until the model or user resolves the branch — the **branch-resolution barrier**. Caching cannot hide it, because *the key that identifies a reusable result is not known until the branch resolves*. DynBranch makes an unresolved branch addressable via a **stable coordinate**, so candidate subgraphs run during resolution and completed subgraph results are reused across later requests; a two-level controller admits the work only when expected benefit exceeds the load price. It sits at the **model-API boundary**, requiring no changes to agent harnesses or model execution engines. Across **four agentic workloads with Qwen3-32B on 4×H200**, it reduces mean latency by **up to 32%** over each workload's strongest prior system and **46–66%** against a no-reuse floor, while preserving workflow results; gains persist across backbone families and on a commodity **Qwen3-8B / RTX 4090** deployment.
- **Why it matters**: Names a serving cost that is invisible to both the caching literature (which needs the key) and the speculative-decoding literature (which assumes a fixed DAG). The stable-coordinate trick is the transferable idea: make a *not-yet-resolved* branch have a predictable identity, and speculative execution becomes possible. The commodity-GPU result matters most for anyone running agents locally — a 46–66% reduction against the no-reuse floor at 8B/4090 is not a datacenter-only finding.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

1. **The rec/ads line returns with business numbers, not architecture novelty — and this is the drought window's best industrial issue yet.** Two live A/B lifts (T-RoPE **CVR +0.33% / orders +0.63%**; KuaFu **GMV +1.37%** on Tencent ads+rec, 190 GPUs saved) plus LinkedIn's **ESP** at 70M+ weekly users and Meta's **Component Benchmark** at 100B examples/day. The unifying lesson: *model* quality is now rarely the binding constraint on what an LLM evaluation can tell you; the instrument is.
3. **Shared-memory integrity is now a three-paper thread with a consistent conclusion.** CPB (0.97–0.99 false-belief persistence once uncontested; no non-oracle policy survives paraphrase or "declared authoritative" copying), MemProbe (four experimental paradigms; six systems tie on aggregate accuracy while differing behaviorally), and the 09-25 Scoped-ORC work (retrieval scope must match certification scope) converge on: **memory-integrity controls must be per-lineage at write time**, because no retrieval-time control can recover a belief every consumer asserts. The corpus-poisoning companion (stale-document poisoning, **30–37% flip rate rising to 66–75% when told to trust the document**) shows the same failure one layer down, in the retriever.
4. **Compositional attacks defeat every component-level control in the window.** Skill cascading (2609.30383) distributes harm across individually-benign skills and evades per-skill scanners *and* runtime monitors across OpenClaw/Claude Code/Codex; AGATE (2609.30830) answers with a provenance-and-authorization gate at harness boundaries (parameter-exact grants that expire and are use-limited, deterministic checks with **no LLM in the decision path**, 252 runs × 63 scenarios replaying consistently on two platforms); monitor jailbreaking (2609.31121) shows evasion needs **no encoding at all** — readable reasoning evades unseen and stronger monitors, cannot be hand-copied, and is fixed by **paraphrasing**. Add 09-25's trace-tampering (5/6 harnesses) and output-prefix attacks (≈0% → 99%): the recurring lesson is that representation-level and per-component boundaries are soft, and enforcement has to sit outside the component it constrains.
5. **Reasoning is not a fairness intervention, and reasoning is not an economic one either.** Fairness: created counterfactual flips outnumber resolved ones **~5×** in all nine (model, dataset) cells, with bias *amplifying* along thinking depth (CDPG). Economics: **The Price of Thought** (2609.30705) varies reasoning effort over a full year of US equities across three model families with **800,000+ asset predictions** and fixed information, prompts, formats, and portfolio construction — additional reasoning produces **no reliable improvement in net portfolio returns**, DeepSeek's full progression is **nonmonotonic**, and repeated generations give **unstable treatment effects** even when average scores match. Same conclusion, two domains: validate per task before deploying; a capability gain is not a welfare gain.

Leung appear on both UA-TWM (2609.30711, slate actions with counterfactual consequence) and LW2S (2609.30734, workflow components with counterfactual value) — the same "component as action, credit via counterfactual intervention" frame applied to the ranker and the agent harness. Separately, the AutoResearch paper (2609.30541) is the industrial successor to the Karpathy auto-research line, and its headline negative is the one that matters: **the same five failure modes at 1,000× cost spread**, including metric fixation in a loop defined by a single held-out scalar.

Directly relevant to the 09-23 AIDE² RSI thread; a framing paper rather than a result.
- **2609.31356** **Open Vocabulary Domain Unlearning** (NeurIPS 2026) — shows existing Approximate Domain Unlearning works under a **flawed closed-vocabulary assumption**: methods are evaluated only on the object classes seen during unlearning, so they overfit to seen class-domain pairs and leave the domain trivially recognizable for unseen classes. Formalizes OVDU (domain forgetting must transfer to held-out classes) and solves it with a Fisher-information mask + Targeted Manifold Scattering; beats peak 8-shot baselines with only 4 shots. Same structural error as the 09-25 baseline-shape and CTC papers: the *protocol* was narrower than the claim.
- **2609.30652** **Recursive Self-Improvement via On-Policy Distillation for Reasoning** — the standard OPSD setup keeps the privileged teacher frozen for stability, which prevents it from incorporating what the student learns. **Dynamic Co-Evolution (DCE)** lets the privileged teacher co-evolve with the student so revisions compound across rounds, plus **Self-Refined Concise Learning (SRCL)** on shorter verified rewrites to counteract the verbosity and self-criticism stronger revisions induce. On Qwen3-8B, DCE+SRCL reaches **65.97% Average@12, +35.62 pp over OPSD** while cutting mean output length 7.80% vs DCE alone; consistent across four competition-level math benchmarks and multiple scales. Notable because the win comes from *un-freezing the teacher*, which the stability literature has argued against.
- **2609.30738** **Beyond Mean Attention: Diversity-Aware, Layer-Wise Scoring for KV Cache Eviction** — SnapKV/PyramidKV rank tokens by mean attention over a small observation window. Unified score `μ + λ₁σ + λ₂corr(i,S)` adds attention dispersion and redundancy relative to selected tokens; with `λ₂ < 0` it penalizes similarity as in MMR, at no extra forward pass. On all 16 English LongBench datasets with Mistral-7B at 64 entries/layer, **one global diversification constant improves 13 of 16** (macro +1.1), holding at budget 32 and narrowing at 128. Per-dataset search finds **no detectable layer structure on most datasets** — the exception is passage retrieval, where a mid-layer sign flip is worth +9.6 at budget 64 and +13.2 over the global constant at 128 without re-tuning. Honest practice: every accepted search state is replayed on the held-out test set to separate real structure from tuning noise.
- **2609.30568** **Nearest but Not Dearest: Shared Curator-Feedback Infrastructure for Content-Only Search and Recommendation** (RecSys 2026 USRW, oral; non-archival workshop) — deployed B2B music-discovery platform serving query-driven search and seed-driven recommendation over one catalog, one LAION-CLAP joint audio-text space, one candidate filter, and one ranking head, with **neither path consuming end-lister behavioral signal**. Key measurement: **38% of cosine-nearest neighbors are rejected by curators**, and the rejections split cleanly into **55% sound failures** (style/tempo/mood — the encoder's problem) and **37% context failures orthogonal to the waveform** (wrong language, holiday/devotional content, rights and lyric flags). The decomposition is deployed as routing infrastructure: context failures to a constraint filter at candidate generation, sound failures to an embedding reweighting head — **both below the search/recommendation split, so one curator loop maintains both experiences**. Two production rounds one month apart, 1,200 curator judgments: rejection rate **38.17% → 28.83%** (−24.5% relative, McNemar χ² = 22.4, p = 2.2e-6). Explicitly labeled a **production accounting bound, not a causal estimate** (unblinded, compound intervention). The transferable idea is the *sound-vs-context* partition being orthogonal to the *search-vs-recommendation* partition.
- **2609.31002** **ZooWork-ShopRanker** — open e-commerce rerankers (0.6B/4B/8B) aligned to judge-labeled shopping preference. Real search traffic gives authentic queries and candidates but **no clean pairwise labels**, so training pairs come from a panel of reasoning LLMs from *different families* as a preference oracle, with **position-debiased judgments and agreement tiers**; the aligned 8B then distills into 4B and 0.6B. Ships **ShopRank-Bench** (~10,000 private-traffic preference pairs in both text formats, tiered by how many judge families committed to each label). 8B and 4B significantly beat the strongest open reranker, every model beats its own unaligned base, 0.6B beats its size peer, gains hold in both formats and on MTEB. The agreement-tiering discipline is the reusable part — it makes judge confidence a first-class label, and this wiki's DIAL/position-bias thread says that is exactly the missing piece.
- **2609.31253** **Enriching Sequential Recommendation with Graph Laplacian Positional Embeddings** (CIKM 2026) — asks whether learnable **ordinal** positional embeddings in SASRec can be replaced by a **structural** signal from item space: build an item co-occurrence graph from training interactions, compute eigenvectors of its symmetric normalized Laplacian, and use them as **frozen** graph-derived positional embeddings, with backbone and objective unchanged. Across four public sequential-rec benchmarks it improves SASRec on most ranking metrics and stays competitive with strong positional and temporal baselines. Directly comparable to T-RoPE's claim (which keeps positions but makes them *time*-aware): both are the same shape of argument — the ordinal-only inductive bias is too weak.
- **2609.31164** **SPADE: Escaping the Popularity-Similarity Frontier to Measure Serendipitous Recommendations** — existing offline beyond-accuracy metrics either isolate historical similarity or global popularity. SPADE maps items into a 2-D space, computes a **user-specific Pareto frontier** of maximally popular and historically similar items, and averages the minimum Euclidean distance from that boundary **only for correctly recommended test-set items**. Across five datasets and five baseline algorithms the metric prevents algorithms from exploiting beyond-accuracy measures with irrelevant or non-personalized recommendations. Relevant to the wiki's serendipity/filter-bubble line (FacetCRS, 09-18): the per-user frontier is a stricter conditioning than the existing metrics it replaces.

## ② Measurement Instruments — five more audits, all from Run 1's blind spot (5)

This continues the theme Run 1 identified as the window's second-largest: *attack the evaluator, not the model*. All five below are from the previously unscreened tail, which suggests the theme is stronger than Run 1 could report.

### Completed Pairs Hide Capped Failures: A ReVerPi Case Study of Selective Context Projection
- **Authors**: Guangzhe Zhang · **arXiv**: [2609.31381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31381) — cs.AI · 15 pages, 12 tables, 5 figures · code: `github.com/timwhitez/ReVer_Pi`
- **Key contribution**: **Context projection** replaces older tool observations with compact addressable excerpts — less repeated input, but possibly extra evidence-retrieval turns. ReVerPi is a Pi extension with archived observations and matched full/projected continuations, studied over an **86-run source-reading campaign / 641 model requests**. The finding is methodological and negative: the **15 completed pairs show identical success (12/15 per arm)**, because the runner **suppressed the companion whenever the first arm failed to complete** — 12 further boundary runs simply stopped. Restoring all 27 boundary runs bounds projected-minus-full success between **−9 and +1 tasks**. The one omitted, selector-chosen projected continuation **successfully retrieves archive text yet exhausts twelve requests**, where its full counterpart answers in three. Across the 11 jointly correct pairs, projection cuts aggregate logical tokens **25%** while **raising the median pair's tokens 29%** and suffix requests **35 → 55**. Separating fitting from evaluation flips the selector's apparent tie: outside its four fitting pairs it incurs one extra failure and 8.6% more tokens over thirteen comparable runs.
- **Why it matters**: A textbook survivorship bias in agent benchmarking, and the **"identical success" headline was an artifact of the stopping rule, not a result**. The load-bearing lesson generalizes past this paper: *if a comparator arm is only executed when the treatment arm succeeds, the comparison is conditioned on the treatment working.* The paper states its own boundary honestly — these are claims about the recorded campaign, not population noninferiority, and not superiority over unrestricted Pi. Its closing protocol recommendation is the thing to steal: **retain every intervention boundary, execute both allocated arms independently of the first arm's completion, and report completion alongside interaction and token expenditure.**

### Evaluating the Accuracy of KV Cache Reuse Techniques
- **Authors**: Samuel Cestola, Tianxiang Xia, Pengfei Zheng, Weiyan Zheng, Bo Wang, Yi Zhao, Diego Didona · **arXiv**: [2609.31415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31415) — cs.LG
- **Key contribution**: **Position-independent KV cache reuse** reduces RAG latency by reusing chunk-level KV caches across prompts. The paper's claim is that **current evaluations of these techniques rely on measurements that fail to faithfully capture the accuracy loss attributable to reuse, and so artificially inflate reported effectiveness** — and that **existing datasets do not exhibit the reuse dynamics needed to evaluate them properly at all**. It proposes an evaluation methodology that measures the accuracy loss without ambiguity, plus **Boxoffice**, a tool that programmatically generates evaluation datasets exercising challenging KV cache reuse patterns.
- **Why it matters**: The efficiency literature has a measurement problem, not an algorithm problem — the same failure Run 1 §② documented for judges (DIAL, CARGO) and benchmarks (HARDEN), transplanted to the systems side. A caching paper that reports a large speedup at unchanged accuracy is making a claim no current benchmark can check. Pairs with Run 1's **DynBranch** (2609.31047): both concern reuse of computation across requests, and both should be read together with this caveat. Boxoffice is the reusable artifact.

### From Reward Signal to Visual Utility: A Controlled Audit of Medical VLM Post-Training
- **Authors**: Wang Jingxin · **arXiv**: [2609.31450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31450) — cs.CV
- **Key contribution**: Medical VLM post-training is evaluated by answer accuracy. This controlled Qwen2.5-VL-3B study on PMC-VQA (2,000 clean-test questions) asks how accuracy and training objective relate to **image-conditioned decisions** across SFT, LM-restricted LoRA, broader multimodal adaptation scopes, standard answer-only GRPO, and a counterfactual evidence objective. Result: LM LoRA SFT moves correct-image accuracy **+1.10 pp (95% paired bootstrap CI −0.85 to +3.05 — straddling zero)** while **visual-benefit events drop 2.40 pp and image sensitivity drops 5.60 pp**, with **155 acquired vs 203 lost** visual-benefit events. Broader adaptation yields *lower* correct-image accuracy. Standard GRPO produces **mixed-reward groups** and an uncertain clean-test change. A generation audit finds that **canonical option scores can follow a different token path from generated answers**; scoring along the greedy path improves the evidence target on the training set, but gains over GRPO stay **inconsistent on validation at matched training doses**.
- **Why it matters**: The headline accuracy gain is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** and it is bought with a real loss of image grounding — a "held-out visual behavior" gap that standard metrics cannot see. The token-path discrepancy is the subtle, reusable finding: a reward computed on canonical option scores may not be measuring what the model actually generates. Structure is identical to this wiki's surrogate-metrics thread (Run 1's **When Do Surrogate Metrics Work?**, 2609.30528): the surrogate is doing work the primary metric doesn't price in.

### Diagnosing the Sources of Compositional Failure in Vision-Language Models: A Controlled Analysis
- **Authors**: Mona Gandhi, Cenk Merih Olcay, Kuan-Chieh Lo, Santiago Castro, Christopher W. Myers, Srinivasan Parthasarathy · **arXiv**: [2609.31456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31456) — cs.CV
- **Key contribution**: The standard hypothesis is that VLMs fail compositional reasoning because they cannot integrate multiple components, and training interventions target compositional binding — but **that assumption had never been directly quantified**. COMPASS isolates it by comparing composed captions against their **decomposed** counterparts across **87K image-caption pairs**, separating the cost of joint reasoning from the cost of recognizing individual components under load. The gap is **real but partial**, accounting for only part of the degradation, so the paper then decomposes further with skill-targeted perturbations across **274K pairs**. Consistent finding: each skill degrades primarily with the count of **its own** primitive type (**self-load**), while **cross-load effects are predominantly positive** — primitives of *different* types provide useful grounding context. Holds across standard contrastive encoders, explicitly trained compositional-reasoning models, and non-contrastive architectures.
- **Why it matters**: It replaces a single vague failure mode with separable ones, and the **positive cross-load** result is the actionable half: degradation is driven by *repetition* of a primitive type, not by composition as such. That reframes what a training intervention should target, and it is a controlled analysis of an *assumption* — the same discipline as this wiki's 09-25 baseline-shape and CTC audits, where the protocol was narrower than the claim.

### PANEL: An Open-Source, Self-Hosted Web Platform for Human Evaluation of Generative Models
- **Authors**: Matteo Spanio, Andrea Poltronieri, Martín Rocamora (ISMIR 2026 Late Breaking Demo) · **arXiv**: [2609.31392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31392) — cs.HC · 3 pages
- **Key contribution**: Human judgement is the reference measure for generative models, but the collection software lags the methodology: researchers adapt perceptual protocols like MUSHRA, fall back on closed commercial survey platforms, or build single-use web apps. Live arenas (Chatbot Arena, Music Arena) rank publicly deployed systems at scale but **cannot support controlled comparisons of a laboratory's own models with its own participants**. PANEL fills exactly that gap: browser-authored studies distributed as a single link, audio/video/image/text stimuli, seven question types, screening and skip logic, **per-question summaries, across-condition significance tests, pairwise win rates and Bradley–Terry scores**, and power analysis from pilot data. Consent versioning, self-service withdrawal, retention enforcement, and audit logging support GDPR-compliant operation; each study exports as a machine-readable specification.
- **Why it matters**: Infrastructure rather than a result, and the gap it names is real: the two dominant options are *big public arenas* (wrong population, no control) or *hand-rolled one-offs* (no reproducibility). **Bradley–Terry scoring plus across-condition significance tests and pilot-based power analysis** is what makes an in-house human eval defensible, and the machine-readable export is what would let a lab re-run another's protocol. Useful to this wiki's judge thread (DIAL, 2609.31215) as the collection half that DIAL's 410K-judgment dataset does not provide.

## ③ Agent Context, Governance & Provenance (5)

### Towards Mitigating Fabricated Consensus: The Active Provenance Gate for Multi-Agent Debate Synthesis
- **Authors**: Jakub Masłowski, Jarosław A. Chudziak (ICTAI 2026) · **arXiv**: [2609.31422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31422) — cs.MA
- **Key contribution**: In LLM multi-agent debate, **the synthesis phase is the least controlled**: given detailed debate logs, summarizing models fabricate smoothly-written consensus **not grounded in the debate's history**. The **Active Provenance Gate (APG)** is a post-debate verification layer that treats the source as a **hard constraint** — it analyses the logs, **audits each claim**, and applies self-correction. In crisis simulations the self-healing mechanism **more than doubles average Provenance Fidelity in difficult conditions**, after which a strict gate blocks unsupported claims and emits **divergence reports**. In the human study, **over 75% of users preferred a report explicitly stating failure** in critical scenarios, *even though most perceived the baseline's fabricated consensus as more fluent*.
- **Why it matters**: Direct continuation of this wiki's memory-integrity thread, one layer up: Run 1's CPB result (2609.30813) showed a false belief in shared memory is asserted by consumers 0.97–0.99 of the time; APG is the enforcement point for the *synthesis* step that would otherwise launder it into fluent prose. The framing shift is the contribution — **data-origin tracing moves from passive logging to active conditional blocking before publication**, which is the same architecture as Run 1's **AGATE** (2609.30830, provenance gate, deterministic, no LLM in the decision path). Read APG and AGATE as a matched pair. The user-preference result is the practical lever: users prefer an explicit failure over a fluent fabrication, so the fluency advantage is not a reason to keep the weaker system.

### Authority at Commit Time: Reject-and-Rerun Semantics for Governed Agentic Systems
- **Authors**: Jesus Salas · **arXiv**: [2609.31490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31490) — cs.DC · 15 pages, 6 tables
- **Key contribution**: Enterprise agents can compute for seconds or hours from a snapshot of policy, facts, task state, models, tools, and verifiers **that may all change before the work reaches the world** — so **completion alone cannot confer institutional authority**. The design treats agents as *proposal producers* and a logically authoritative service as sole authority for governed effects. At admission the service compares the proposal's declared dependencies and component manifest against current state, applies the **current** verifier, and either reserves an idempotent effect for transactional-outbox dispatch or **rejects affected stale work for resynchronization and rerun**; unaffected work continues, and lost responses resolve through canonical verdicts and receipts. The protocol composes optimistic concurrency control, semantic verification, and outbox dispatch, and its distinctive read set **includes normative authority and the model components that produced the proposal**. Evaluation covers an implemented lifecycle, a local authority/outbox/reconciliation composition, and a multi-process fallback delegating effect authority when full mediation is unavailable.
- **Results and honest limits**: complete mediation produced **18 authority commands and 18 external effects**; **direct-credential arms produced 8 additional effects, all detected by independent journal reconciliation, with no false detections in the mediated control**. The paper explicitly does **not** establish prevention or reversal of bypass effects, external-journal completeness, production throughput, wide-area availability, semantic completeness, or verifier correctness. The clock/lease/ordering/quorum obligations of delegated admission are exposed as the cost.
- **Why it matters**: It names a failure the whole agent-governance thread has been circling: **a long-running agent's authority expires while it is still running**, so validating at dispatch time is too late. The move — put authority at *commit*, not at start — is the transferable design decision, and the "reject and rerun" semantics are honest about the cost (stale work is thrown away). Pairs with **AGATE** (2609.30830) and **APG** (2609.31422) as the third distinct enforcement placement in one window: harness boundary, post-debate, commit time.

### Compress What You See, Not What You Say: Anchored Context Distillation for Latent-Observation Software Engineering Agents
- **Authors**: Zhensheng Zou, Guoqing Wang, Dan Hao · **arXiv**: [2609.31430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31430) — cs.AI
- **Key contribution**: **Tool observations dominate software-engineering agent context**, and existing compression can discard information later actions need, while adapting agents to soft-token representations can change their behavior. Two pieces: **LOHA (Latent Observations, Hard Actions)** is a context layout that compresses *older* tool observations into soft tokens while keeping the agent's own turns and the **last K observations in text** — compact historical access plus exact recent access. **ACD (Anchored Context Distillation)** trains latent reading by distilling the base model's full-text predictions into the latent view while **anchoring behavior on plain-text inputs to the same base model**, which is what constrains drift.
- **Results**: On SWE-bench Verified at K=3, context per call drops **43% for Qwen3-4B and 57% for SWE-Master-4B-RL**, with resolve rates **12.1% and 21.8% versus 14.5% and 27.5%** for the uncompressed bases — i.e. **compression buys context at a real cost in resolve rate**, and the K=8 recency sweep recovers to 14.4% and 23.0%, with larger windows generally favoring task performance over compression. Under a **32K-token limit**, Qwen3 at K=3 resolves **21.1% of a 199-instance subset versus 11.1%** for the same adapted agent using full text. In concurrent single-GPU serving it reaches **1.9×** the full-text agent's instance throughput.
- **Why it matters**: The most useful thing here is that the compressed configuration **loses to its own base on the primary metric** and the paper reports it plainly; the win appears only under a token ceiling or a serving-throughput constraint. That is the honest framing for anyone compressing agent context. Also a direct structural sibling of Run 1's **KuaFu** (2609.31045) — both compress long histories and both find the compression is not free — with LOHA's "keep the last K in text" answering KuaFu's named *omission* hallucination type directly.

### Can You Check That? The Checkability Boundary for Local LLM Network Automation
- **Authors**: Maleeha Masood, Momina Nofal · **arXiv**: [2609.31540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31540) — cs.NI
- **Key contribution**: Sending every network-automation input to a frontier LLM exports production configurations, topologies, and logs; running small models locally avoids the egress but SLM outputs are error-prone for direct use. The paper introduces **checkability**: a task is checkable when it exposes a **cheap deterministic test — an intrinsic check — that rejects outputs violating a necessary correctness condition**. **Touchstone** is a local-first pipeline: seven off-the-shelf SLMs (1–8B) generate candidates, task-specific intrinsic checks reject responses, and unresolved inputs escalate to a frontier LLM.
- **Results**: on **conflict detection and intent translation**, Touchstone reaches **98.6% and 93.8% end-to-end accuracy while escalating only 16% and 17%** of inputs. The key negative is the control: on **TeleQnA, a knowledge-only task with no task-specific intrinsic checks, Touchstone cannot match the frontier baseline.**
- **Why it matters**: The negative control is the whole paper. It converts "use small models locally" from a preference into a **boundary**: local inference is viable exactly when task semantics support precise low-cost checks, and degrades when the task is pure knowledge retrieval. That is a sharper claim than the privacy/efficiency tradeoff it starts from, and it generalizes well past networking. Pairs with this wiki's judge-reliability thread — an intrinsic check is a *deterministic* substitute for a judge, and the TeleQnA result is the measured price of asking a model to do what a checker cannot.

### Agentic Economies for Autonomous Scientific Discovery
- **Authors**: Nenad Tomasev, Matija Franklin, Atoosa Kasirzadeh, Vivek Natarajan, Alan Karthikesalingam, Sebastien Krier, Simon Osindero · **arXiv**: [2609.31562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31562) — cs.CY
- **Key contribution**: AI-for-science systems have moved from single agents to multi-agent orchestration, but development has focused on **cognitive capability** — better reasoning, more reliable hypothesis generation — while **ignoring resource management, which the paper argues is the other key bottleneck**: testing and validating hypotheses is physically and economically resource-intensive and resources are limited. It outlines infrastructure for "scientific agent economies, markets, and institutions" to let agents and human scientists **collaborate and set research priorities, assign credit, track accountability and liability, and safeguard against malicious use and information-security risk**, then engages with macro-level societal implications for governance and equitable distribution of AI-driven discoveries.
- **Why it matters**: A framing/infrastructure paper, and the corrective is well-aimed: if you can make agents reason better but you cannot budget their experiments, you have not improved discovery. The **credit-assignment** requirement is the piece that connects it to this wiki's existing thread — Run 1's **LW2S** (2609.30734) formulates workflow-component omission as counterfactual credit assignment, and an "agent economy" that must assign credit across agents and humans is the same primitive applied institutionally. Natural continuation of the [[auto-research]] line after Run 1's **AutoResearch at Production Scale** (2609.30541), whose headline was that the same five failure modes recur at 1,000× cost spread.

## ④ Safety & Training-Data Diversity (2)

### Region-Level Black-Box Defense Against Stealthy Embedding-Space Backdoors in CLIP
- **Authors**: Ahmed Abdelnaby, Mohamed Elmahallawy · **arXiv**: [2609.31558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31558) — cs.CV · code: `github.com/wsu-cyber-security-lab-ai/CLIPGuard`
- **Key contribution**: **Embedding-space backdoors** in CLIP poison only a tiny fraction of image–text pairs and induce targeted shifts in the *joint embedding space* directly rather than through classifier logits — so they work at extremely low poisoning ratios and are hard to detect. Crucially, they corrupt **representations**, so existing defenses that assume access to parameters, gradients, logits, or clean validation data **rarely apply in realistic black-box deployments**, and current black-box methods struggle to localize small or out-of-distribution triggers. **CLIPGuard** is lightweight and fully black-box: it identifies malicious regions by measuring **segment-wise embedding perturbations** and **selectively purifies only suspicious segments via semantic inpainting**, preserving benign content and alignment. Across STL-10, ImageNet, and BadCLIP / BadNets / blended / patch-based / typographic trigger families, it reduces **attack success rate to as low as 1.05%** while keeping clean accuracy up to **86.34%**, outperforming existing black-box defenses including CleanCLIP and CleanerCLIP.
- **Why it matters**: The honest framing is the contribution — the defense matches the threat model's *observability*, working with no parameter or gradient access and localizing triggers at segment granularity, which is exactly where black-box methods usually give up. Pair with Run 1's AGATE (2609.30830) and the tail's APG (2609.31422): all three locate their control at a boundary where the *representation* is available but the *mechanism* is not.

### Strategically Diverse Sampling for Self-Training
- **Authors**: Alexander Gurung, Esmeralda S. Whitammer, Mirella Lapata · **arXiv**: [2609.31571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31571) — cs.CL
- **Key contribution**: RL and test-time scaling depend on repeated sampling but benefit only when responses **meaningfully differ**. Self-training has the same problem: training data is built by sampling IID responses and filtering mainly for correctness, which **overrepresents strategies the model already favours**. The paper proposes **strategic diversity** — substantive variation in *approaches* — as the construction principle, via two methods: **GROOT**, which builds a hierarchical tree of approaches and samples distinct paths, and **Verbalized Sampling (VS)**, adapted to produce an unstructured set of approaches. Across competitive programming and next-chapter prediction, models trained on strategically sampled data beat IID-trained counterparts on difficult tasks and give strong initializations for RL and test-time scaling. Headline: **self-training on strategically diverse but incorrect traces from Qwen3-4B outperforms IID distillation from a 235B teacher.**
- **Why it matters**: A direct, and unwelcome, result for anyone doing distillation: **approach diversity can beat both correctness and teacher scale.** It also reframes the window's dominant distillation finding — Run 1's **RAZOR** (2609.30465) reported that pruning preserved task scores while silently degrading **diversity, formatting, and termination** in generation. Read together, the two say diversity of *approach* is both a thing optimization destroys and a thing that can substitute for capability. The Qwen3-4B-beats-235B-teacher claim is the kind of result that needs replication before it changes practice; it is stated as a single-domain finding across two domains, not a law.

2. **The measurement-repair theme is stronger than Run 1 could report, and it now spans six distinct instruments.** Run 1 found five (position bias, reference grounding, benchmark difficulty, cross-provider divergence, assumption auditing). 3. **Authority placement is becoming the organizing question for agent safety, with three distinct enforcement points in one window.** **AGATE** (2609.30830) puts a deterministic provenance/authorization gate at the harness boundary; **APG** (2609.31422) puts it after debate synthesis, before publication; **Authority at Commit Time** (2609.31490) puts it at commit rather than dispatch, on the argument that a long-running agent's authority expires mid-flight. The three are complementary rather than competing, and together they sketch a stack: constrain at the boundary, verify before publishing, re-check at the moment of effect.
4. **Compression is repeatedly shown to be non-free, and the honest reports say so.** KuaFu (Run 1) names the hallucination types truncation introduces; **LOHA/ACD** loses 2.4 and 5.7 pp of resolve rate against its own base and recovers only at K=8; **Touchstone** cannot match the frontier baseline exactly where its intrinsic checks do not apply. 5. **Diversity is showing up as a first-class training signal, not a metric artifact.** RAZOR (Run 1) found pruning preserved task scores while degrading generation diversity. **Strategic Diverse Sampling** (2609.31571) finds diversity of *approach* can substitute for a 235B teacher when traces are strategically varied. Same underlying variable, opposite directions of use — and a tension this wiki should watch, since the same diversity that degrades after pruning is what makes the distilled data competitive.
