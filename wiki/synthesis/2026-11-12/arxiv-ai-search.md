## 1. LLM Post-Training, Agents, and Reasoning

### 1.1 AutoScaffold: AI-Guided Scaffolding for Reinforcement Learning of Language Models
- **Authors:** Zijian Zhang, Rizhen Hu, Jiangweizhi Peng, Shiyang Li, Sujay Bhatt, Sihan Zeng, Mingyi Hong
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14515) (cs.LG, cs.CL), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) produces rich training experience, yet outcome rewards reduce each attempt to a single number and leave most of this information unused. Training-time scaffolds, such as strategies or hints added to the prompt during training, offer a natural way to turn this information into guidance, but their design and adaptation are largely left to humans and tailored to specific domains. We introduce AutoScaffold, a unified AI-for-AI framework that turns a student's training experience into adaptive scaffolds, with minimal domain-specific information and no human intervention during training. A frozen teacher periodically examines this experience, diagnoses bottlenecks, and designs scaffolds in two forms: (i) strategies shared across a class of tasks and (ii) hints for individual hard tasks, adjusting the content and amount of support as the student learns. The scaffolds are removed at evaluation. We apply the same framework to ALFWorld, search-based QA, mathematical reasoning, and GPU kernel generation. When evaluated without scaffolds, students trained with AutoScaffold outperform those trained with the corresponding RL baselines in all four domains, with gains of 7.2 points in ALFWorld success and 4.0 points in average QA exact match. On GPU kernel generation, AutoScaffold increases the fraction of tasks with a correct kernel that runs faster than the PyTorch reference by up to 5.0 points. On math, our student surpasses QuestA's reported accuracy using approximately one-third as many student training steps. Analyses show that the teacher identifies a different failure in each task class and shifts its scaffolds from step-by-step workflows to targeted fixes as the student improves, and that its revised scaffolds help the student learn more than keeping the previous scaffold or using no scaffold.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **AI-for-AI adaptive scaffolding:** frozen teacher converts student RLVR experience into training-time guidance without human intervention.
  - **Two-level scaffold design:** shared class-level strategies + task-specific hints, both adapted over training.
  - **Scaffold removal at eval:** improvements transfer to unscaffolded inference, showing learned capability rather than prompt dependence.
  - **Cross-domain generality:** demonstrated on embodied tasks (ALFWorld), search QA, math reasoning, and GPU kernel generation with consistent gains.

### 1.2 A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets
- **Authors:** Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade, Sadhika Malladi, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Dylan J. Foster
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467) (cs.LG, stat.ML), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Policy optimization is central to language model post-training, yet principled guidance remains limited. We revisit its theoretical foundations to identify algorithms that learn near-optimal policies using total samples and gradient steps governed by the base model's response coverage, a measure of how readily it generates optimal responses. We call such algorithms coverage-preserving. This guarantee captures the goal of amplifying capabilities accessible through sampling from the base model. (1) Coverage collapse: REINFORCE, the basis for PPO and GRPO, can drive the probability of optimal responses to near zero, even as reward improves. This coverage collapse stalls optimization, and convergence can require exponentially many steps despite good initial coverage. The failure arises even with exact gradients and persists under KL regularization to the base model. (2) Reference resets: We show that repeatedly resetting the KL reference to the most recent policy mitigates coverage collapse. We formalize this intervention in Approximate Policy Mirror Descent (APMD), a meta-algorithm that approximately solves a sequence of KL-regularized RL subproblems. APMD enables stronger regularization, making each subproblem easier to solve, while reference resets allow convergence to global optimality. (3) Coverage-preserving algorithms: For softmax linear policies over pretrained features, we use APMD to establish the first global convergence guarantees showing that widely used families of post-training algorithms are coverage-preserving. These include stylized PPO and GRPO variants with explicit KL regularization and reference resets, and squared-loss algorithms that implement them implicitly, including those used to train the Kimi models.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Coverage-collapse characterization:** formalizes how REINFORCE/PPO/GRPO-style updates can suppress optimal responses despite reward gains.
  - **Reference-reset mechanism:** periodic KL-reference updates (via APMD) balance strong regularization with global convergence.
  - **Coverage-preserving guarantees:** connects practical post-training families (PPO/GRPO variants, squared-loss methods) to sample/step complexity governed by base-model coverage.

### 1.3 ARE-bench: Benchmarking Agents' Research Ability in Real-World Researcher Sessions and Workspaces
- **Authors:** Jiahang Lin, Wanying Ren, Wanghan Xu, Huayu Sha, Yang Shu, Zhonghang Lu, Chengjun Pan, Yi Dong, Shichun Liu, Yuyang Hu, Shihan Dou, Honglin Guo, Huijie Lv, Pujun Zheng, Zhe Sun, Yuankai Ying, Yiding Wang, Jiahao Chen, Zhixiong Yang, Dingbo Yuan, Yicheng Zou, Zhecan Wang, Guoxiu He, Xiaoyu Xing, Qi Zhang, Tao Gui, Xuanjing Huang
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14364) (cs.CL), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Research agents achieve increasingly strong benchmark scores, yet these scores do not necessarily translate into reliable delivery in ongoing research projects. Many research-agent benchmarks emphasize standalone tasks and relatively self-contained workflows in clean or curated workspaces. However, researchers ask agents to complete one step within an ongoing project, where requests depend on earlier session decisions and workspaces contain unfinished files, outdated versions, and failed attempts. We introduce ARE-bench to evaluate whether research agents advance ongoing projects while respecting prior decisions and existing workspace state, grounded in real session context and in-progress workspaces. ARE-bench includes 75 tasks built on agent sessions and full workspaces from recruited researchers' own projects, spanning seven research stages, 33 requirements, and 150 sub-requirements, with 1,173 binary checklist items in total. Each task is anchored at a real user request, preserves its requirements, and summarizes the earlier requests still in effect as background. The complete workspace is replayed to that boundary without cleanup, preserving artifacts from earlier turns. Each checklist is compiled from an intent-aligned rubric and credits any valid solution. Across seven frontier models, the strongest passes every checklist item on only 66.2% of tasks, and Design and Execution rank among the hardest stages for every model. Three models pass about 69% of tasks when only delivery contracts and general norms are checked, but just 28.4% to 40.0% once historical and material or workspace requirements are also checked. Evaluating delivery without these contextual requirements can therefore overestimate research agents and mask differences between them.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **In-progress research evaluation:** tasks anchored in real researcher sessions with messy, unrepaired workspaces (unfinished/outdated/failed artifacts).
  - **Context-sensitive rubric:** 1,173 binary checklist items covering delivery, historical decisions, and material/workspace constraints.
  - **Diagnostic finding:** large performance drop when contextual requirements are enforced, revealing overestimation by cleaner benchmarks.

### 1.4 Program-Aided Patchable Skill Evolution (PASE)
- **Authors:** Jiahao Wang, Yuqi Xiong, Qing Yang, Zhipeng Xu, Zhenghao Liu, Zulong Chen, Yubin Bao
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14019) (cs.CL, cs.AI), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Agent skills have emerged as an effective means of enabling LLM agents to solve complex, interactive tasks by encoding reusable knowledge without modifying model parameters. Recent approaches further improve skill adaptation by distilling reusable experience from agent execution trajectories and evolving skills based on downstream task feedback. However, existing methods primarily represent skills as natural language documents, intertwining procedural logic with descriptive knowledge and making skills difficult to maintain and evolve. To address this limitation, we propose PASE (Program-Aided Patchable Skill Evolution), a program-aided framework that iteratively evolves skills by associating them with executable programs. Unlike prior approaches that encode trajectory-derived experience primarily in natural language, PASE separates reusable procedural logic into editable programs, enabling targeted updates to executable components while reducing the need to repeatedly revise natural language skills. Extensive experiments across diverse models and benchmarks demonstrate that PASE consistently outperforms all baselines, by achieving over 2.8% improvements. Further analyses show that PASE exhibits greater evolutionary stability and generalisation capability, highlighting the critical role of programmatic knowledge in skill evolution.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Program-aided skill representation:** decouples procedural logic (executable programs) from descriptive natural-language skill docs.
  - **Patchable evolution:** enables targeted edits to executable components instead of wholesale NL rewrites.
  - **Improved stability/generalization:** reported >2.8% gains over baselines with more stable iterative skill evolution.

### 1.5 Synapse-Control: Making Agent Experience Actionable with Lightweight Runtime Control
- **Authors:** Zhiyuan Huang, Shengan Zheng, Chenghu Yang, Song Zhang, Lingyun Li, Chen Yu, Weiquan Huang, Linpeng Huang
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.13615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13615) (cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Large language model agents reuse execution experience to improve reliability on complex tool-based tasks. Systems such as Sentry call large models to generate failure diagnoses before retrieving advice, adding inference cost to experience reuse. We present Synapse-Control, an agent runtime whose lightweight control plane selects advice and determines when to send it to the task-executing model. Rules shortlist relevant advice, and a lightweight 1.7B classifier fine-tuned on teacher-labeled state-suggestion pairs scores those candidates instead of generating a diagnosis. Micro-contracts encode reusable interface checks, such as required arguments or valid options, without prescribing complete task programs. A Prompt Value Table records earlier guidance and feedback to limit repeated suggestions through cooldown and token-budget checks. Across 1,308 attempts per method in AppWorld and WebShop, Synapse-Control achieves 270 successes (29.8% more than Base), replaces 4,987 generative diagnosis calls, and reduces auxiliary control time relative to Sentry by 50.2% and 51.0%, respectively.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Lightweight experience reuse:** replaces expensive generative diagnosis with rule shortlisting + 1.7B classifier scoring.
  - **Micro-contracts:** reusable interface checks (args/options validity) for tool-use reliability.
  - **Prompt Value Table:** cooldown/token-budgeted guidance memory to avoid redundant advice; strong success/cost tradeoff on AppWorld and WebShop.

## 2. Recommendation, Sequential Modeling, and POI Prediction

### 2.1 SepLRM: A Foundation Model Paradigm for Recommendation
- **Authors:** Di Chen, Xijun Xiao, Qin Ren, Weijiang Lai, Zhijian Pan, Zhentao Pan, Pengfei Nie, Cheng Yuan, Zixuan Tan, Xuyang Liu, Tao Wang, Yunwen Huang, Hao Fang, Yichao Wang, Hao Zhang, Siyuan Wu, Hao Chen, Han Diao, Mingyu Yang, Yongkang Feng, Beihong Jin, Jinfeng Yang, Deping Xie, Peng Xu, Zheng Chai, Yuchao Zheng
- **Institution/Company:** Affiliations not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract; paper reports deployment in two large-scale industrial scenarios and online A/B tests at **ByteDance**
- **arXiv:** [2611.10356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10356) (cs.IR), submitted 7 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Large recommendation models (LRMs) improve recommendation performance by scaling behavior sequence length, model capacity, and training compute. However, the return on investment (ROI) of continued scaling deteriorates as marginal gains diminish and long-sequence encoding costs rise, especially when this computation is repeatedly executed by distinct models for diverse recommendation stages and business scenarios. To address this issue, we propose SepLRM, a recommendation foundation model (RFM) paradigm that identifies reusable sequence encoding and reorganizes multiple end-to-end (E2E) LRMs into one RFM and multiple independently optimized vertical models (VMs). The RFM adopts an encoder-decoder architecture and exports its final-layer encoder state as the shared key-value (KV) cache that is independent of downstream task-offered candidates. Unlike candidate-specific logits or aggregated user embeddings, the KV cache preserves fine-grained behavioral information. Separation at this native KV interface also allows each VM to construct candidate-conditioned queries while retaining the same query-KV interaction as its E2E counterpart, enabling high-fidelity capability transfer. To ensure both transfer effectiveness and system efficiency, we co-design cache compression, the training and serving workflows, and LG-Store, a distributed storage system for KV caches. Experiments on three large-scale datasets show that SepLRM achieves state-of-the-art offline performance with a transfer rate of approximately 100%. It also demonstrates strong transferability across model iterations, recommendation stages, and business scenarios. Deployment in two large-scale industrial scenarios substantially improves the ROI of sequence scaling and delivers consistent gains in online A/B tests at ByteDance.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **RFM/VM decoupling:** shared encoder (RFM) + task-specific vertical models to avoid redundant long-sequence encoding.
  - **Native KV-cache interface:** exports fine-grained behavior states (not logits/pooled embeddings) for high-fidelity candidate-conditioned transfer.
  - **Systems co-design:** cache compression, train/serve workflows, and LG-Store distributed KV storage; industrial deployment with online A/B gains at ByteDance.

### 2.2 SHARP: Semantic Hierarchical Agentic Reasoning for Next POI Prediction with Dynamic Memory
- **Authors:** Yuhang Zhan, Silin Zhou, Zhuo Chen, Haoxiang Lei, Yunren Feng, Lisi Chen, Shuo Shang
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14220) (cs.IR), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Next point-of-interest (POI) prediction supports personalized location services by anticipating users' next destinations. Recent methods leverage large language models (LLMs) for semantic reasoning and graphs for collective mobility modeling, improving POI prediction. However, three challenges still remain. Bridging LLMs' semantic reasoning and specific POI prediction remains challenging. User mobility also evolves over time, making it necessary to retain and update historical knowledge within limited LLM contexts. Meanwhile, global transition graphs smooth out time-specific category transition patterns. To address these challenges, we propose Semantic Hierarchical Agentic Reasoning for Next POI Prediction with Dynamic Memory (SHARP), an agentic hierarchical framework in which category-level reasoning guides specific POI prediction. A trajectory memory agent maintains a dynamic trajectory memory bank that preserves earlier mobility patterns and tracks behavioral changes with limited context growth. We construct multi-granularity time-aware category transition graphs with global and time-conditioned views to preserve temporal regularities obscured by global aggregation. A category predict agent combines these graphs with trajectory memory to predict likely next categories. SHARP learns semantic IDs (SIDs) through a residual-quantized variational autoencoder (RQ-VAE) to construct semantic POI representations, which are jointly optimized with a category-guided POI predictor end-to-end. On three real-world datasets, SHARP improves Acc@5 and Acc@10 by an average of 5.29 and 4.52 percentage points, respectively, over the strongest baseline.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Hierarchical agentic reasoning:** category-level LLM/agent reasoning narrows and guides fine-grained POI prediction.
  - **Dynamic trajectory memory:** maintains evolving user mobility patterns under limited context budgets.
  - **Time-aware multi-granularity graphs + semantic IDs:** preserves temporal transition regularities and learns RQ-VAE semantic POI codes jointly with predictor.

### 2.3 LUMIR: Decoupled Multimodal Representation Learning from Heterogeneous Data for Next-POI Recommendation
- **Authors:** Zehui Wang, Wolfram Höpken, Dietmar Jannach
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14004) (cs.IR, cs.LG, cs.MM), submitted 10 Nov 2026; accepted at DaQuaMRec Workshop (RecSys 2026)
- **Abstract:** As multimodal tourism-related information collected from heterogeneous data sources has become increasingly available, learning effective representations has attracted attention for improving recommendation performance. Existing approaches often tightly couple multimodal fusion with the architecture and optimization objective of a specific downstream model, which limits the generality and reusability of the learned representations. To address these limitations, we propose LUMIR, a decoupled multimodal representation learning framework based on contrastive learning. Multimodal information collected from heterogeneous data sources is first semantically unified into a shared textual representation space using large language models (LLMs). An interaction-aware fusion mechanism is then designed to model cross-modal interactions, while sequential signals are incorporated as a structural constraint to guide representation learning. To evaluate the learned representations, experiments are conducted on a multimodal point-of-interest (POI) dataset under the next-POI recommendation setting across multiple sequential recommendation backbones. The results show that LUMIR consistently outperforms existing multimodal fusion baselines in both accuracy-oriented and beyond-accuracy metrics, demonstrating that decoupling representation learning from downstream optimization provides an effective paradigm for learning reusable multimodal representations.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Decoupled multimodal pretraining:** separates representation learning from downstream recommender architecture/objective for reusability.
  - **LLM-based semantic unification:** maps heterogeneous modalities into a shared textual space before fusion.
  - **Interaction-aware fusion + sequential constraints:** contrastive learning guided by cross-modal interactions and sequential structure for next-POI tasks.

### 2.4 Exploiting Feature-invariant Scaling Laws for Efficient A/B Experimentation in Recommendation Systems
- **Authors:** Zhiliang Chen, Sammy Nammari, Jiahao Su, Xiaoqiang Luo
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.13539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13539) (cs.IR, cs.LG), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Researchers and engineers improve recommendation systems by performing A/B experiments with new model architectures, optimization methods, and data features. However, running full-scale experiments (e.g., full-model training with online data) for every feature change is prohibitively computationally intensive, while the result from a single small-scale experiment is often misleading. To address this challenge, we show that modern recommendation models exhibit feature-invariant scaling: the change of a model's performance follows a predictable scaling-law even when different feature changes are applied to the baseline model. We introduce an efficient method that exploits scaling-laws and uses statistical inference to predict (with confidence intervals) the full-scale effect of an A/B experiment in recommendation systems. This enables practitioners to efficiently filter out neutral or regressive feature changes with only small-scale experimentation. We validate our method on three distinct recommendation model families---Wukong, HSTU, and LLM-based recommendation models---across more than 40 architectural, optimization, and data changes. Our method accurately distinguishes beneficial feature changes from neutral and regressive ones while using only a fraction of the compute required for full-scale evaluation. It also identifies several changes that improve HSTU's state-of-the-art performance on MovieLens-20M.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Feature-invariant scaling laws:** empirical regularity where performance deltas scale predictably across feature/architecture/optimization changes.
  - **Compute-efficient A/B prediction:** small-scale experiments + statistical inference with confidence intervals to estimate full-scale impact.
  - **Broad validation:** >40 changes across Wukong, HSTU, and LLM-based rec models; useful for filtering neutral/regressive ideas cheaply.

## 3. Advertising and Ad/Retrieval Systems

### 3.1 TS-GEAR: Taobao Shangou GEnerative Ad Retrieval
- **Authors:** Guofeng Tong, Xu Zhang, Guangliang Yu, Haiqiang Wang, Guannan Cong, Xing Guo, Yang Zhou, Jingjing Duan, Shuang Li, Feng Shi, Hualei Liu
- **Institution/Company:** **Taobao Shangou / Alibaba ecosystem** (inferred from title, problem setting, and deployment context stated in abstract)
- **arXiv:** [2611.12180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12180) (cs.IR, cs.AI, cs.CL), submitted 9 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** On-demand delivery platforms such as DoorDash and Uber Eats impose a constraint absent in traditional e-commerce: merchants beyond the user's delivery range are invalid candidates rather than merely low-ranked. Generative retrieval has recently shown strong potential in large-scale retrieval systems. Adapting it to this setting, however, poses three challenges. First, existing semantic IDs encode geography only as a soft signal, so reachability can only rely on a non-differentiable post-generation hard truncation. Second, user preferences vary substantially across both regions and queries, so aggregating historical behaviors without conditioning on geography and the current query yields biased interest estimates. Third, the retrieved candidate set is evaluated by its collective value, yet autoregressive training scores each sequence independently, with no set-level signal to discourage redundant near-duplicates. To address the challenges, we propose TS-GEAR, a unified generative retrieval framework for on-demand delivery: (1) Geo-Semantic ID splits merchant identifiers into geographic prefixes and semantic suffixes, making reachability a prediction target rather than a post-hoc filter; (2) Query-Geo Target Attention aggregates historical behaviors using the real-time query and location as dual targets, while encoding each behavior's own location into its attention key; (3) GMPO (Group Marginal Policy Optimization) rewrites advantages using a deduplicated group reward and marginal advantage within GRPO, avoiding auxiliary diversity objectives. Deployed online with a lightweight 0.1B backbone, TS-GEAR serves over 150 million queries per day in the search advertising pipeline of Taobao Shangou, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art generative retrieval methods on both HitRate and geographic validity, and achieving 3.8% gains in ad impressions and 4.2% in ad revenue.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Geo-Semantic IDs:** geographic prefix + semantic suffix make delivery reachability a learned generation constraint.
  - **Query-Geo Target Attention:** dual conditioning on real-time query and location; behavior locations encoded in keys for less biased interest modeling.
  - **GMPO for set-level retrieval quality:** deduplicated group rewards/marginal advantages to reduce redundant candidates in autoregressive generative retrieval.
  - **Production impact:** 0.1B model serving 150M+ queries/day with reported gains in HitRate, geo-validity, impressions (+3.8%), and ad revenue (+4.2%).

### 3.2 CHO-Bench: How Do Context Hints Affect Ad Rankings in LLM Advertising?
- **Authors:** Shiqi Zhang, Yang Fei, Xiaokui Xiao
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.11955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11955) (cs.IR, cs.CL), submitted 9 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** LLM advertising selects advertisements based on user conversations, where user needs may extend beyond explicit product searches. ChatGPT Ads allows advertisers to provide natural-language context hints describing what they offer, who may find it useful, and when it may be useful, as additional information for ranking advertisements across diverse user conversations. However, how to effectively construct these hints remains unclear. We formulate Context Hint Optimization (CHO) as the problem of constructing context hints to improve an advertisement's ranking under a given budget. Drawing on established marketing frameworks, we develop seven context hint generation strategies covering What, Who, and When, along with a Unified strategy that combines all seven dimensions. We introduce CHO-Bench and conduct over 1.19 million paired evaluations across five datasets using embedding-based and LLM-based rankers. Our results show that context hints improve advertisement rankings under the evaluated offline rankers. While individual strategies achieve similar average gains and Unified offers no consistent advantage, strategy differences become more pronounced under specific generation-ranking configurations, with embedding-based and LLM-based rankers favoring different information dimensions. Increasing the hint budget further improves rankings with diminishing marginal gains. These findings establish an offline benchmark for studying ad-side ranking optimization in LLM advertising and highlight opportunities for model-specific hint selection and budget-aware generation and aggregation.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Context Hint Optimization (CHO) formulation:** ad-side natural-language hint construction as a budgeted ranking optimization problem.
  - **CHO-Bench at scale:** 1.19M paired evaluations across 5 datasets and both embedding-based/LLM-based rankers.
  - **Actionable findings:** hints help offline rankings; ranker type affects preferred hint dimensions; larger budgets yield diminishing returns.

## 4. Continual Learning / Games-Adjacent Evaluation

### 4.1 Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime
- **Authors:** Daniel Lenton
- **Institution/Company:** Not explicitly listed on arXiv abstract
- **arXiv:** [2611.14505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505) (cs.LG), submitted 10 Nov 2026
- **Abstract:** Agent harnesses have emerged as a promising mechanism for continual learning, alongside test-time parametric learning. However, the current benchmarks have several limitations: they generally focus on a single applied domain, serve each instance only once, and assume a single family of learner, so memory-based and weight-based systems are rarely compared on the same scale. We propose Continual-ARC, a lifelong stream built from the three ARC-AGI editions, whose tasks need no domain knowledge. The same tasks return after dormant gaps with fresh instances, while new tasks keep arriving. On each instance, the learner may pay for demonstrations, submit answers, or act inside the harness, with retries permitted on incorrect submissions, which are paid for. Each instance is scored based on the share of this feedback budget consumed. Since a learner may keep anything between instances, memory-based and weight-based systems are scored on the same scale. After the stream, the learner is frozen and tested on fresh instances of the tasks it met, which measures memorization, and on unseen tasks, which measures generalization. We run an agent harness with frozen weights, with and without persistence, on Qwen3.5-9B and GLM-5.3 Flash, comparing them with two prompting floors, a program-induction method and Qwen3.5-9B's weights trained to imitate GLM-5.3 Flash. Persistence in the harness generally does not improve memorization unless provided with explicit expert guidance. Most harness-based methods still learn less than a floor that simply caches each task's demonstrations, across all models we tested. No harness-based method gains on unseen tasks, whereas weight-based learning shows substantial gains. This opens the door to future weight-based continual learning that could teach models to drive harnesses in new domains.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Lifelong skill-acquisition benchmark:** recurring ARC tasks with dormant gaps, budgeted feedback, and unified scoring for memory- vs weight-based learners.
  - **Memorization vs generalization split:** post-stream evaluation on seen-task fresh instances and unseen tasks.
  - **Empirical insight:** persistence in agent harnesses underperforms simple demonstration caching in many cases; weight-based learning shows stronger generalization gains.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

- **Agent reliability and adaptation:** AutoScaffold, Synapse-Control, and PASE emphasize turning experience into reusable guidance/skills (scaffolds, runtime control, program-aided evolution) rather than relying solely on static prompts.
- **Context-grounded evaluation:** ARE-bench highlights that clean benchmarks can overstate agent capability when historical/session/workspace constraints matter.
- **Generative & foundation paradigms in rec/ads:** SepLRM and TS-GEAR push toward reusable sequence representations and generative retrieval with domain constraints (ROI/KV transfer; geo-validity and set-level objectives).
- **Sequential/POI modeling:** SHARP and LUMIR combine memory, temporal structure, and multimodal/semantic representations for next-destination prediction.
- **Theory-to-practice RL/post-training:** coverage collapse and reference resets provide principled guidance relevant to LLM alignment/RLVR pipelines.
- **Scaling/experimentation efficiency:** feature-invariant scaling laws offer a practical route to cheaper, more reliable recsys A/B decision-making.
