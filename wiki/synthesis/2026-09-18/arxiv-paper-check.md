## ① CTR & Conversion — the drought breaks (1)

### Dense Feature Representation over Sequence Modeling: A Solution to the KDD Cup 2026 UniRec Challenge
- **arXiv**: [2609.19787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19787) — cs.IR
- **Authors**: Yi Zhang, Weiliang Ji
- **Key contribution**: 10th-place solution to the **KDD Cup 2026 Tencent UniRec Challenge** — industrial **click-to-conversion (CVR) prediction** over **34.82M records** (the same challenge QueryFormer/STAR targeted in earlier windows). From the official **PCVRHyFormer** baseline, a documented **15-step single-variable chain** raises test AUC **0.813237 → 0.827816**, and the final submission reaches **0.828535**. The critical evidence is a **leave-one-out ablation from the full model**: removing the **dense-feature representation stack costs 0.0095 AUC** and the **orthogonalized optimizer costs 0.0028**, while **no sequence-modeling component** (merged single-stream backbone, polarity channel, auxiliary head, per-token FFN) **costs more than 0.0005** — on or inside the ±0.0004 seed band. The paper also reports a **generalization hazard**: the row-group train/validation split shares one time window, so validation AUC overstates the leaderboard by **~0.014**; anti-memorization and high-cardinality-ID changes even *invert sign* against validation, a divergence that survives a time-ordered re-split.
- **Relevance**: The direct CTR-track download this window — and a genuine tension flag for the wiki's CTR-scaling narrative (EST "unified single-sequence" scaling, UniCon context-centric units, HyFormer unified feature+sequence): **at this scale, dense repr + optimizer beat finer sequence modeling**. Also a methodological caution: shared-time-window splits can mislead by ~0.014 AUC and invert verdicts. Verdicts must come from the held-out leaderboard.
- *(tentative — single-source competition writeup; but gives full numbers and an explicit seed band)*

## ② Recommendation & User Understanding (4)

### From "Who Is This User?" to "What Does This Purchase Mean?": A Deployed Pipeline for Semantic User Profiling at Bank Scale
- **arXiv**: [2609.19928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19928) — cs.AI / cs.LG
- **Authors**: Ryota Mitsuhashi, Tetsuro Morimura, Hirotake Ito (Japan)
- **Key contribution**: Per-user LLM inference on transaction histories binds the inference budget linearly to user count — prohibitive at bank scale. Re-casts attribute inference from *per-user* to **per-transaction-pattern**. Three phases: **Resolve** (abstracts item names, optional web grounding), **Profile** (one LLM call per frequent pattern emitting predefined categorical labels, free-text attributes, per-attribute prevalence estimates), **Tag** (clusters free-text attributes into a queryable database). Because inference runs over patterns, budget grows with pattern count, not user count. On the public Open e-commerce corpus, the pipeline is **statistically indistinguishable from a per-user-LLM baseline in AUC**, and prevalence estimates carry discriminative signal between positive/negative users. Deployed at a **major Japanese bank profiling tens of millions of users** with ~**3 orders-of-magnitude fewer LLM inference targets** than a per-user pipeline.
- **Relevance**: "Meaning of the purchase pattern" as a first-class unit instead of "identity of the user" — a scale-honest recipe for any requirement-side semantic layer (banking, e-comm, ads targeting) where per-user LLM cost is the binding constraint. Practical cousin to [[Behavior2Value]]-style value measurement (today's arxiv-daily) but from the *system architecture* angle.

### EviRec: Continual Evidence Learning for Dual Cold-Start POI Recommendation
- **arXiv**: [2609.20313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20313) — cs.LG
- **Authors**: Rongchao Xu, Lin Jiang, Guang Wang
- **Key contribution**: POI recommendation assumes a fixed user population and POI catalog; a large-scale data-driven analysis of **10 U.S. cities** identifies substantial **POI churn, user turnover, category drift**, and decay in static POI memory. **EviRec** estimates how much historical evidence to trust **separately for each candidate POI**, scoring candidates from three views: **matching** (recent mobility profile), **transition-memory** (repeated mobility routines), and **lifecycle** (candidate maturity). Because a near-zero transition score could mean irrelevance *or* insufficient observation, EviRec qualifies evidence with each candidate's observation state and applies a **reliability gate** routing between transition-memory and lifecycle evidence. Evaluated on a full-year, five-city dataset (30,000+ users, 684,200 trajectories): consistently beats SOTA, gains concentrated on cold-start — **+20.4% NDCG@10 on Dual-New** cases over the strongest baseline, while largely preserving learned routines.
- **Relevance**: A clean "when evidence is missing, gate it" mechanism — the cold-start side of the CTR/rec deal is exactly the dual-new (new user × new item) regime EviRec benchmarks. Directly useful framing for production rec systems facing churn/drift.

### FacetCRS: Multi-Faceted Preference Learning for Pricking Filter Bubbles in Conversational Recommender System
- **arXiv**: [2609.20175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20175) — cs.IR / cs.AI / cs.HC
- **Authors**: Yongsen Zheng, Ziliang Chen, Jinghui Qin, Liang Lin
- **Key contribution**: Filter bubbles in recommender systems are usually studied in static settings, but real online systems **intensify them over time** via the user↔system feedback loop. **FacetCRS** treats filter bubbles as a dynamic problem to be *burst* inside a **conversational recommender system (CRS)**: it models user preferences into **multi-facets — entity-, word-, context-, and review-facet** — to capture diverse and dynamic preferences, adaptively learning representations of the various preference-facet levels plus diverse external knowledge, in an end-to-end CRS framework. SOTA on two public CRS benchmark datasets in mitigating filter bubbles and improving recommendation quality.
- **Relevance**: Answers the daily-reader question "does anything attack rec *diversity* health, not just accuracy?" — with a CRS (conversational) variant. Complements the diversity caps/co-purchase direction tracked in earlier windows.

### Reproducing Transparent and Scrutable Recommendations: Exploring Open-Weight Models via Natural-Language User Profiles
- **arXiv**: [2609.19831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19831) — cs.IR / cs.AI
- **Authors**: Noah Mamié, Laurin van den Bergh
- **Key contribution**: Reproducibility study of **User Profile Recommendation (UPR)** — injecting generated natural-language user profiles (from raw review text; Amazon Movies & TV, TripAdvisor) into LLM-based rec to enable direct user intervention/correction. The authors reproduce the core findings (profiles enable transparent rec with competitive reranking-protocol performance) and extend with systematic **context ablations, 5-seed stability, and nnsight-based mechanistic interpretability**. The sharp result: **perturbing the natural-language profiles changes predicted ratings uniformly across genres — no detectable genre-selective effect — and rankings stay unchanged even under direct activation steering.** They trace this to the **rating-regression objective rather than the profile interface**; ranking-objective models clearly exceed in this task.
- **Relevance**: A rare *negative* mechanistic verdict on "scrutable rec": transparency ≠ steerability when the head is regression. Exactly the kind of check the wiki's LLM-rec and judge/reproducibility threads want before betting on profile-based interventions.

## ③ IR, Search & Agentic Retrieval (3)

### Algebraic Retrieval: Composable Search for Agents
- **arXiv**: [2609.19482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19482) — cs.IR
- **Authors**: Damian Delmas
- **Key contribution**: Lets AI agents **compose search strategies at query time** — relevance criteria, eligibility constraints, and ranking preferences expressed together in a single mathematical query, with the query surface exposing available operations so an agent can combine them for the question at hand and **revise a program after inspecting results**. Builds on Programmatic Embedding Modulation (PEM), which exposes **vector and score arithmetic** during retrieval: contrastive scoring, candidate-pool reranking, and weighted ranking become composable queries, alongside executable SQL and PyTerrier counterparts. Evaluates **execution parity, not agent behavior**: on the public 11,429-document Vaswani fixture, each program's implementations select the same document set with score differences below **1e-6**; one tied pair orders differently across scoring paths.
- **Relevance**: The "search as a programmable object" direction for agentic IR — a small, clean building block (composable scoring algebra) rather than another retrieval model. Useful contrast to the heavy RAG stacks tracked this week.

### Self-Evolving Search Index
- **arXiv**: [2609.19656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19656) — cs.IR / cs.AI
- **Authors**: Sangam Lee, Wonjae Lee, Sunghwan Kim, Deogyong Kim, Jaehoon Kim, Daye Nam, SeongKu Kang, Dongha Lee
- **Key contribution**: Retrieval quality depends on how index keys expose each document's knowledge, but optimal representations **vary across retrieval environments**, and evolving an index has stayed human-driven (diagnose → refine strategy → reprocess). **SELF-INDEX** makes it autonomous: an **Optimizer diagnoses retrieval shortfalls, selectively revises the responsible index keys, and validates each revision before updating**; beyond reacting to observed demands, a **Query Simulator proactively explores additional retrieval demands**, letting the index evolve beyond the queries already available. Consistently improves retrieval across diverse corpora/retrievers, outperforming fixed index-optimization methods, with benefit transferring to **downstream search agents and agent-memory retrieval**.
- **Relevance**: Treats the *index* (not the retriever) as the learnable artifact — and closes the loop with validation, which is the anti-drift discipline the wiki keeps flagging. Directly relevant to RAG and agent-memory layers.

### Characterizing Web Search by Conversational LLM Agents
- **arXiv**: [2609.19244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19244) — cs.AI / cs.IR
- **Authors**: Mahsa Amani, Seungeon Lee, Abhisek Dash, Asmaa El Fraihi, Yunah Jang, Elisabeth Kirsten, Qinyuan Wu, Krishna P. Gummadi, Manish Gupta, Abhilasha Ravichander, Muhammad Bilal Zafar, Soumi Das (MPI-HIIS lineage)
- **Key contribution**: First end-to-end study of **agentic web search** across **four major conversational platforms (ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, DeepSeek)**, combining real-world interactions (in-vivo) with controlled experiments using the same platforms' models via API (in-vitro). Investigates agentic search-invocation decisions, query formulation strategies, domain preferences in returned results, and how results become grounded responses. Findings: **search decisions vary substantially across platforms/models while more frequent web-search invocation does NOT yield better response quality**; agents use different complex querying strategies; **platform-specific search engines return results from their preferred domains**; and although responses are largely grounded, **some claims rely on uncited search results** — an attribution/reliability concern.
- **Relevance**: The empirical census this era needs — "how does search actually happen inside the agent" — with a clean in-vivo/in-vitro design. The uncited-claims and domain-preference findings feed the wiki's sponsored-search/delegation and a-commerce audit threads.

## ④ Tabular & Classical-vs-LLM (2)

### Is It Still Worth Training a Classical Model in the Era of LLMs? A Crossover Benchmark on Tabular Data
- **arXiv**: [2609.20218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20218) — cs.LG / cs.AI
- **Authors**: Kaihua Ding
- **Key contribution**: LLMs can label a tabular row from plain English with no training (now shipping in Copilot-in-Excel / Claude-for-Excel), so when labels are expensive, is it better to **prompt a frozen LLM or collect data and train**? Answers with the **labeled-data crossover N\***: the training-set size at which a trained classical model's learning curve overtakes the LLM's flat, training-free error. Aggregating **126 independent student evaluations of small GPT models under 8 prompting configurations across 18 tabular datasets**, paired with power-law learning curves for six classical model families: **a trained classical model beats the small frozen LLM using no more labeled data than is already on hand in 86% of cases**, wins at the smallest tested subset in 40%, and the **observed median crossover is ~6% of the training set**. In-context few-shot does **not** behave like training (no power law vs shot count); an independent-implementer replication has CV 0.148; a controlled probe indicates dependence on **recognizable feature-name semantics** (making the crossover a *conservative* estimate). Verdict: **for a typical business table, collect a few hundred labels and train a gradient-boosted model.**
- **Relevance**: The tabular/classical-vs-LLM question the CTR-scaling and business-modeling threads keep asking — now with a concrete N\*. Consistent with the wiki's thesis that CTR/ad stacks stay numeric-native (EST deployments, TabPFN-3.5 in today's daily) rather than prompt-the-LLM.

### Null Importance: Disentangling Relevance for Interpretable Machine Learning
- **arXiv**: [2609.19511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19511) — stat.ML / cs.LG
- **Authors**: Garvesh Raskutti, Kris Sankaran, Jiaxin Ye
- **Key contribution**: "Importance" in feature-importance analysis conflates several genuinely different notions of relevance. Unified framework built on **null importance** — the population-level statement "a feature is irrelevant *under a specified notion of relevance*" — covering **marginal/conditional statistical relevance, predictive risk, functional invariance, and causal effects**, and showing how each answers a different scientific question. Applications where the distinction is consequential: **algorithmic fairness** (fairness criteria correspond to different nulls) and **genomic perturbation modeling** (different relevance notions lead to different conclusions about what a model learned). Provides sufficient conditions for nulls to coincide, counterexamples where they diverge, and a characterization of which null each method family targets — validated on simulations plus image/multiomics case studies.
- **Relevance**: The vocabulary to stop over-claiming "this feature matters" in tabular/CTR model analyses — exactly the discipline the CTR-scaling and feature-engineering threads need (e.g., the UniRec dense-vs-sequence attribution above is a model-level relative attribution; null importance is the population-level vocabulary under it).

## ⑤ Agent Harness Economics (1)

### How Do Agent Harnesses Create Value? Planning Information and Release Control in Stateful LLM Agents
- **arXiv**: [2609.20474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20474) — cs.AI
- **Authors**: Yukun Zhang, Kemu Xu, Yishen Chen
- **Key contribution**: Harnesses supply planning guidance, organize execution, and check completion — this paper isolates how. In two Retail experiments and an Airline pilot on **τ²-bench**, the primary comparison pairs **prewritten task-specific plans (Fixed) with shuffled policy text matched in word count (Sham)**, isolating the contribution of guidance *content*. Across **265 matched cells, Fixed improves oracle-verified success by +7.17pp** (90% task-clustered bootstrap CI 1.15–13.36), with gains concentrated in higher-complexity tasks. A **read-only terminal verifier rejects 61% of Retail oracle-invalid episodes while withholding 17% of correct ones, at under one cent of additional cost per episode**. Which component matters depends on the loss assigned to erroneous acceptance: at low liability planning dominates; at high liability the verifier's avoided false-passes dominate — and **a standalone verifier captures nearly all the false-pass benefit of the full planning+verification stack at a fraction of its cost.**
- **Relevance**: Puts a price on harness components (content + verification) — the "software 3.0 tooling as the product" thesis in measurable form, extending the harness-engineering thread (AutoTuneBench's honest measurement, Traverse debuggers, the two-gap framework). The 61%/17% verifier economics are directly actionable for production agent release gates.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

1. **The CVR drought breaks — with a contrarian message.** 2609.19787 is the first classic end-to-end conversion paper in the feed for ~6 windows, and its leave-one-out verdict is: dense-feature representation + optimizer engineering move CVR AUC at 34.82M-scale, while sequence-modeling refinements sit at the seed band (≤0.0005 vs 0.0095/0.0028). This is a measured counterweight to the unified-sequence-scaling narrative (EST/UniCon/HyFormer/TabPFN horizon) — flag for the CTR-scaling thread. It also re-issues the train/val time-window warning (~0.014 leaderboard overstatement; sign-inverting verdicts). *(single-source, tentative)*
2. **"Train the classical model" gets its number.** Independent of the UniRec ablation, the tabular crossover benchmark (2609.20218) puts N\* at a median ~6% of the training set and recommends a gradient-boosted model over a frozen small LLM — two independent sources this window reinforcing the numeric-native, model-training-centric view of tabular/CTR prediction.
3. **User understanding moves from identity to meaning and evidence.** Bank-scale profiling goes per-pattern instead of per-user (~3 orders of magnitude fewer inference targets, AUC-matched) (2609.19928); EviRec gates evidence per-candidate to fix cold-start (2609.20313); FacetCRS attacks filter bubbles as a dynamic feedback process (2609.20175). The rec periphery keeps migrating from CTR-model tuning to *what a user/preference/evidence means*.
4. **Scrutable doesn't imply steerable.** The UPR reproducibility study (2609.19831) shows natural-language profiles are transparent but produce uniform (genre-insensitive) rating shifts with unchanged rankings — a caution against over-trusting profile-mediated interventions; attributed to the rating-regression objective.
5. **Harness and index become first-class engineering artifacts.** SELF-INDEX evolves the index autonomously with validation (2609.19656); agent-harness value is decomposed into plan content (+7.17pp) and a <1¢/episode verifier (2609.20474); search itself becomes a composable program (2609.19482). The "middleware is the product" theme continues across the feed.
