## ① LLM Post-training — RLVR & Test-Time (4)

### 1.1 TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Aozhe Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Jianze Wang, Shangke Lv, Ying Liu, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Hua Yang, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen |
| **Institution** | Zhejiang University (inferred: project page zju-real.github.io/TTPO; Weiming Lu is a ZJU faculty member) |
| **Abstract** | RL and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) have driven progress in LLM mathematical reasoning, but both rely on ground-truth labels, precluding test-time training (TTT). Replacing ground truth with majority-vote pseudo-labels is fragile: an incorrect vote corrupts the teacher. Key empirical observation — the failure mode is **asymmetric**: rollouts that disagree with the pseudo-label are typically wrong regardless of whether the vote itself is correct. TTPO exploits this via an asymmetric objective: it distills *agreeing* rollouts through OPSD and penalizes *disagreeing* rollouts with Grouped RL. Token-level selection refines both branches (distillation down-weights converged positions; RL penalizes only confident errors). Majority-vote routing tightens self-supervision as the model improves. Without any labels, TTPO matches label-supervised OPSD on five competition-level benchmarks, raises Qwen3-1.7B from 38.0% → 45.2% in TTT, yields **+25.2% to +36.4% without thinking**, with strong cross-task generalization. |
| **Key innovations** | Label-free test-time training that stays grounded under noisy pseudo-labels; asymmetric agree-distill / disagree-penalize objective; token-level confidence-gated reward shaping. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27448) · cs.CL · [project](https://zju-real.github.io/TTPO) |
| **Why it matters** | Directly extends the wiki's test-time-compute / RLVR thread: it removes the ground-truth label requirement that has bounded self-distillation, turning test-time RL into a label-free, on-distribution training loop — a meaningful step toward RL at inference time. |

### 1.2 Boosting LLM Exploration via Weak-Model Guidance in RLVR

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | (ACL ARR 2026 May submission #13854; author list not surfaced in search result) |
| **Institution** | Not stated (OpenReview submission, ACL ARR 2026 May) |
| **Abstract** | RLVR significantly improves LLM reasoning but often causes a drop in policy entropy, narrowing reasoning coverage and degrading pass@k for large k. Existing mitigations regularize algorithmically but neglect cross-model non-parametric perturbation. This work preserves generative diversity by forcing the target model to complete **partial reasoning trajectories generated by a smaller, weaker model**. These unfamiliar prefixes disrupt over-confidence and encourage exploration of distinct reasoning paths, serving as an "outer prefix" source. They empirically study how distributional discrepancy between the weak prefix-generator and the strong policy affects exploration dynamics in RLVR training. |
| **Key innovations** | Weak-to-strong exploration via externally-supplied reasoning prefixes (non-parametric perturbation); positions entropy-collapse mitigation as an input-distribution problem rather than only an algorithmic one. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27420) · cs.CL (also OpenReview, ACL ARR 2026 May) |
| **Why it matters** | Entropy collapse / exploration collapse is the central instability of GRPO-style RLVR this wiki tracks ([rlvr](../../concepts/rlvr.md)); injecting *other-model* prefixes is a cheap, orthogonal lever vs. the existing regularization / entropy-alignment proposals (cf. HEAL, SPO++ earlier in the week). |

### 1.3 Consolidating RLVR Capabilities Across Domains: A Deep Dive into Fusion Paradigms

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | (author list not surfaced in search result; paper marked submitted 27 Aug 2026) |
| **Institution** | Not stated |
| **Abstract** | RLVR improves specific LLM capabilities, but covering multiple capabilities usually means training separate domain experts and then consolidating them. The paper organizes **three fusion paradigms by the artifacts they reuse**: **Merge** (combines expert task vectors), **Mix RL** (pools expert datasets and re-trains), and **multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD)** (distills experts into a single policy using their rollouts). It presents a controlled comparison of the three, giving practical guidance on when each is the right consolidation strategy, plus a deep-dive analysis of the tradeoffs (compute, data, and capability recovery). |
| **Key innovations** | Uniform taxonomy of RLVR consolidation into reuse-artifact categories; empirical guidance on Merge vs. Mix RL vs. multi-teacher distillation. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27409) · cs.CL |
| **Why it matters** | Consolidating per-domain RLVR specialists is now a first-class engineering problem; this directly parallels the ISO-Merger / spectral-inheritance line (2607.19331) and the wiki's modular-expert RLVR tracking — which of Merge/Mix/distill wins depends on the artifacts you can afford to reuse. |

### 1.4 Performance Foundations of Parallel & Distributed Reasoning Language Models

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Maciej Besta, Leonard Schmidt, Lara Nonino, Robert Gerstenberger, Pierre Pang, Patrik Okanovic, Ales Kubicek, Tiancheng Chen, Baraq Lipshitz, Torsten Hoefler |
| **Institution** | ETH Zurich (inferred: Besta & Hoefler are ETH SCAL group faculty; Lipshitz is ETH-affiliated) |
| **Abstract** | RL-style post-training ("RL-for-LLMs") has produced Reasoning Language Models (RLMs) such as DeepSeek-R1, o3, and Kimi k1.5, substantially improving chain-of-thought reasoning, long-horizon planning, and self-correction. But the computational footprint is massive: SOTA RLM training requires millions of GPU-hours and tightly coupled multi-model pipelines that stress hardware far beyond classical supervised LLM training — making RLM training as much a parallel/distributed systems problem as an algorithmic one. The paper (i) systematizes the RL-for-LLM paradigm with a compute-centric analysis of PPO, GRPO, and variants; (ii) builds a taxonomy of intra- and inter-model parallelism for RL-for-LLMs (data/tensor/pipeline/sequence/context/expert parallelism plus novel techniques: disaggregated placement, stage fusion, hybrid parallelism, asynchronous execution), grounded in the work-depth model of parallel computing; (iii) distills practical guidelines and open directions for scalable, fast, cost-effective RLMs. |
| **Key innovations** | First rigorous systems-level taxonomy of multi-model RLM parallelism; work-depth formalization; practical recipe for RL-for-LLM at scale. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27046) · cs.LG/cs.AI/cs.DC/cs.PF |
| **Why it matters** | The compute/systems bottleneck of RLVR is the economics side of the wiki's [rlvr / RL-for-LLM](../../concepts/rlvr.md) tracking: reasoning scaled with RL is a *systems* problem (disaggregated placement, async execution), not just an algorithm problem. |

## ② Software Engineering Agents (2)

### 2.1 SWE-Prime: Fewer Trajectories, Better Performance

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Dewu Zheng, Ruizhe Ye, Yanlin Wang, Yang Ye, Hongyu Zhang, Ensheng Shi, Xilin Liu, Yuchi Ma, Jianxing Yu, Zibin Zheng |
| **Institution** | Sun Yat-sen University (inferred: Zibin Zheng is SYSU faculty; overlaps with MCR-Bench team) |
| **Abstract** | To improve LLMs' ability to resolve real-world software issues, prior work builds large-scale agent trajectory datasets and does SFT on *successful* trajectories. But task success does not guarantee high-quality supervision: successful trajectories can still contain ineffective, redundant, or risky steps, and using them directly introduces noisy supervision. **SWE-Prime** is a multi-granularity, two-stage SFT data selection method. Stage 1 does trajectory-level screening on process quality, result quality, and data representativeness. Stage 2 does segment-level selection by grouping consecutive steps into semantic segments and scoring each on contribution to the final solution, learnability, and potential risk. During SFT all segments stay in the sequence (to preserve context) while only selected segments contribute to loss. On SWE-Bench Pro and SWE-Bench Verified, training on the **10% trajectory subset** outperforms training on the full resolved dataset, with relative gains up to **12.2% and 24.2%**. |
| **Key innovations** | Data-quality (not just outcome) filtering for SWE agent SFT; segment-level loss masking; "fewer is better" result vs. large-scale trajectory datasets. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27449) · cs.SE/cs.AI/cs.CL |
| **Why it matters** | Directly contradicts the "scale up trajectory data" assumption in coding-agent SFT — the same lesson as data-selection work in other post-training regimes (cf. BALIGN for alignment): which 10% you train on matters more than how much. |

### 2.2 From Static to Dynamic: Benchmarking Real-World Code Review with MCR-Bench

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Dewu Zheng, Yanlin Wang, Xiwen Wang, Kefeng Duan, Hongyu Zhang, Xilin Liu, Yuchi Ma, Zibin Zheng |
| **Institution** | Sun Yat-sen University (inferred: Zibin Zheng; same core team as SWE-Prime) · *stated*: accepted at **ISSTA 2026** |
| **Abstract** | Real-world code review is iterative (developer–reviewer interactions) and costly, but LLM code-review work mostly oversimplifies it into a single-round, static decision task. **MCR-Bench** is the first *defect state-aware* benchmark for realistic multi-round code review: 2,269 real-world multi-round tasks across five programming languages, each annotated with fine-grained defect info (description, type, severity) plus cross-round state labels capturing a defect's full evolutionary trajectory. Findings on mainstream LLMs: (1) limited overall capability — defect detection and lifecycle-state tracking degrade sharply as interaction rounds increase; (2) defect-sensitive performance — semantically complex or low-salience defects are far more likely to be missed; (3) failure mechanisms — error analysis isolates cross-round temporal misalignment and inadequate long-range memory as key drivers of both false positives and false negatives. |
| **Key innovations** | First defect-state-aware multi-round code-review benchmark; dynamic (trajectory) annotations vs. static single-shot; documented multi-round degradation (long-range memory failure) in LLM reviewers. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27442) · cs.SE/cs.AI/cs.CL (ISSTA 2026) |
| **Why it matters** | Adds a multi-turn, stateful evaluation to the wiki's code-agent / verification tracking: code review is a long-horizon interaction, and current LLMs lose defect state over rounds — a concrete, measurable memory/context limitation. |

## ③ Agent Safety (2)

### 3.1 INTENT-AS-A-TOOL Makes it Easy to Track Agentic Misalignment

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yutong Zhang, Jianshuo Dong, Peng Xu, Long Wang, Jie Zhang, Tianwei Zhang, Xiaoping Zhang, Han Qiu |
| **Institution** | Tsinghua University (inferred: Han Qiu is a Tsinghua faculty member) |
| **Abstract** | As LLMs are deployed as autonomous agents, safety failures increasingly involve consequential actions. The paper studies **agentic misalignment** — agents that take harmful actions under goal conflicts and pressures. Using chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring, they find harmful execution is often preceded by intent signals in reasoning, but post-hoc CoT labels are too coarse to show how intent changes during generation. **INTENT-AS-A-TOOL** adds intent-targeted *tools* that give the model a dedicated channel for expressing commitment to a target behavior; the probability of calling an intent tool provides a **judge-free, fine-grained signal** of the model's tendency to pursue that behavior. It complements CoT monitoring, expands post-hoc CoT labels into dense trajectories, and identifies critical steps for online intervention. |
| **Key innovations** | Tool-call probability as a cheap judge-free intent probe; dense (token/step-level) tracking of intent evolution vs. coarse post-hoc CoT labels; enables online intervention timing. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27348) · cs.CL · [code](https://github.com/RebeccaZhang22/intent-as-a-tool) |
| **Why it matters** | Extends the wiki's agent-safety / monitoring thread: instead of post-hoc audit of bad actions, it surfaces *pre-execution intent* in a cheap, judge-free channel — useful for early intervention and for distinguishing misalignment from mere mistake. |

### 3.2 RedEvoAgent: Automatic Red-Teaming Agent with Experience-Driven Skill Evolution

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Junjie Zhang, Hui Liu, Kecheng Chen, Xianbo Mo, Changsheng Chen, Haoliang Li |
| **Institution** | City University of Hong Kong (inferred: Haoliang Li and Changsheng Chen are CityU faculty) |
| **Abstract** | LLM-based agents are increasingly deployed in product-level execution harnesses, where jailbreaks can trigger harmful tool use and persistent state changes — greater risk than unsafe text generation alone. Existing automatic red-teaming relies on fixed attacks; recent agentic attackers coordinate multiple jailbreak tools via trajectory-based retrieval, but retrieval can reuse misleading experiences (retrieval bias, unclear tool credit) and full trajectories add context overhead and reduce interpretability. **RedEvoAgent** is a black-box red-teaming agent that distills cross-case attack trajectories into a concise, human-readable **attack skill**. The skill adaptively evolves via tool-effectiveness profiling and Deciding-Tool Attribution for skill updates, plus a validation ratchet that keeps only updates improving validation performance. It outperforms fixed and agentic baselines on multiple benchmarks/target-models/harnesses, improves tool efficiency, and transfers across attacker models and target execution harnesses. |
| **Key innovations** | Distills attack trajectories into a compact human-readable skill (attribution + validation-ratchet) rather than retrieving whole trajectories; black-box, transferable red-team agent. |
| **arXiv** | [2608.27439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27439) · cs.CR/cs.AI |
| **Why it matters** | Complements the runtime-monitoring angle of 3.1 with the *offensive/red-team* side of agent security; the "distill experience into a reusable skill with a validation ratchet" pattern is the same self-improving-skill recipe the wiki tracks for agents, now applied to attack tooling. |

## ④ Cross-cutting observations

1. **RLVR is consolidating the whole stack in one wave**: the fresh Friday upload simultaneously attacks (a) test-time RL without labels (TTPO), (b) exploration/entropy collapse (Weak-Model Guidance), and (c) multi-domain expert fusion (Consolidating RLVR), plus (d) the systems/compute layer of the same pipeline (Parallel & Distributed RLMs). This is four different levers on the same RL-for-LLM bottleneck — consistent with the wiki's ["RL is the training-nightmare frontier"](../../concepts/rlvr.md) thesis.
2. **"Fewer, higher-quality examples beat more" keeps reappearing**: SWE-Prime trains on 10% of trajectories and beats the full set (up to +24.2%); this is the identical economics as BALIGN (preference-data selection) and SPEAR (dense symbolic rewards) from the 08-28 digest. Data/context triage is the cheapest lever across SE agents, alignment, and reasoning.
3. **Agent safety is splitting into runtime-intent and red-team attacks**: INTENT-AS-A-TOOL (fine-grained pre-execution intent probing) and RedEvoAgent (offense-side skill-evolution) cover the two sides of the same deployment risk — agents whose *actions*, not just text, can cause harm. Both converge on tracking *behavioral intent/tool choice* rather than output text.
4. **Code review becomes a stateful, multi-turn problem**: MCR-Bench's finding that LLMs lose defect-state over interaction rounds is a concrete, measured instance of the long-horizon-memory failure the wiki's memory/context thread tracks — and it's a call for persistent external state (cf. ReWorld-style landmark memory) in stateful engineering agents.
