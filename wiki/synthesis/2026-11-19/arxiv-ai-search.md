### 1.1 ReflectScene: MLLM-Guided Closed-Loop Diffusion for 3D Indoor Scene Generation
- **arXiv:** [2611.23845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23845) [cs.CV; cs.AI] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Jiawei Shen, Jie Feng, Junpeng Zhang, Guanbin Li, Wanyi Chen, Weisheng Dong, Guangming Shi
- **Institution/Company:** Xidian University (Shen, Feng, Zhang, Dong, Shi); Sun Yat-sen University & Shenzhen Loop Area Institute (Li); Soochow University (Chen)
- **Abstract:** Text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation requires layouts satisfying geometric, functional, and semantic constraints. However, existing diffusion methods rely primarily on static textual conditions, limiting their ability to assess evolving scenes and translate semantic discrepancies into executable object-level corrections. MLLM-based post-hoc refinement provides semantic feedback but remains decoupled from denoising, potentially disrupting scene coherence. We propose ReflectScene, an MLLM-guided closed-loop diffusion framework. First, we introduce Hierarchical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding that aligns structured layouts with multi-view visual evidence, to interpret object-specific context and scene global semantics via a frozen multi-modal large language model (MLLM). Second, we propose MLLM-Guided Semantic Reward Evaluation to distill MLLM's diagnoses and assessments on possible candidate corrections into a lightweight Reward Predictor, enabling correction decision making with reduced computation burden. Finally, Decision-Conditioned Trajectory-Consistent Diffusion is proposed to integrate selected corrections through transient conditioning and persistent clean-estimate projection, correcting object layout in the generated scene while preserving non-target arrangements throughout denoising. Experiments on 3D-FRONT demonstrate improved functional consistency and semantic alignment while maintaining competitive generation quality and diversity.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Closed-loop MLLM-guided diffusion: instead of static text conditioning or post-hoc repair, the MLLM judges evolving denoising states and emits object-level corrections every 50 steps, which are executed as transient conditioning and persistent projection constraints on subsequent clean estimates.
  - Three-way decomposition: Hierarchical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding (multi-view alignment pretraining; SGR adapters; stop-gradient metric queries), MLLM-Guided Semantic Reward Evaluation distilled into a lightweight Reward Predictor (52.4× latency reduction vs the frozen teacher), and Decision-Conditioned Trajectory-Consistent Diffusion (target loss on perturbed attributes + preservation loss toward the baseline denoiser on the rest).
  - Metrics: improved FID on all 3 room types (108.70 vs 115.63 best baseline on Bedroom), best KID on Bedroom/Dining Room, best PSA and CLIP-Score overall; validated reasoning-strategy sensitivity (Qwen2.5-VL-7B beats 3B and 2B on FID/PSA).

### 1.2 NarrowSpec: Draft Hidden Width Need Not Match the Target in Speculative Decoding
- **arXiv:** [2611.23835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23835) [cs.CL; cs.LG; cs.PF] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Alagappan Valliappan, Ankit More, Dheevatsa Mudigere, Jatin Mitra
- **Institution/Company:** NVIDIA (all four)
- **Abstract:** Speculative decoding is widely used to speed up generation in large language models (LLMs), with a small draft model proposing tokens that one forward pass of the target model verifies. Draft models that read the target's hidden states, such as DFlash and EAGLE-3, typically inherit the target's hidden width. We show that the draft's whole residual stream can run at half or a quarter of that width while largely preserving acceptance. For DFlash, which borrows the target's vocabulary projection, the trained output adapter folds into it, giving one narrower projection with logits unchanged up to floating-point rounding. We test two drafter families, DFlash and EAGLE-3, on two targets, Qwen3-8B (NVFP4) and GPT-OSS-20B (MXFP4), in SGLang on a B200 and a DGX Spark (GB10). At batch size 1 and temperature 0, narrowing the draft to half or a quarter width speeds up end-to-end decoding by 1.06–1.12× on B200 and 1.13–1.44× on GB10. The speedup holds at larger batch sizes. From batch 32 to the largest batch each GPU fits, narrowing reaches up to 1.21× on both GPUs, and no narrow draft is slower than full width.
- **Key innovations:** First study that narrows the entire draft hidden stream (half or quarter of target width) rather than only the final projection layer; DFlash-specific fold of the trained output adapter into the narrower shared vocabulary projection (exact logit equivalence); large end-to-end wins (up to 1.44× at bs=1 on GB10, 1.21× at max batch) on Hopper/Blackwell and GB10.

### 1.3 Thinking Fast, Anywhere: One System-1 for the Decision Points of Any Agent Workflow
- **arXiv:** [2611.23751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23751) [cs.AI; cs.CL; cs.LG] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Rui Li, Liyang He, Zheng Zhang, Zhenya Huang, Qi Liu
- **Institution/Company:** University of Science and Technology of China (4); Nanyang Technological University (1)
- **Abstract:** Autonomous LLM agents incur substantial cost and latency by invoking a large model (System-2) at every step, even when many intermediate actions choose among a small set of known candidates. Existing dual-system agents offload such decisions to a lightweight System-1 model, but rely on workflow-specific hooks authored per environment. We present AnyS1, a framework that turns human-authored procedural skills, whose instructions naturally specify where choices arise and which options apply, into a shared, domain-general source of decision points. Offline, AnyS1 mines grounded enumerable decisions across over 1,000 public skills, gathers execution-grounded trajectory states inside isolated containers to construct Any-DP, and trains a single calibrated 4B pointer System-1 with label smoothing. At run time, compiled workflows resolve routine decisions in one forward pass and fall back to System-2 when confidence falls below a calibrated threshold. Across three unseen benchmarks and distinct System-2 model families, AnyS1 preserves task reliability while cutting episode cost by 75–91% and model-call time by 63–92% relative to an uncompiled System-2 agent, and maintains performance under zero-shot threshold transfer.
- **Key innovations:** Converts unstructured procedural skills into a universal set of decision points; Any-DP dataset of execution-grounded states (>1,000 skills); single calibrated 4B pointer model trained once (not per environment); pluggable System-2 fallback via calibrated threshold keeps robustness while eliminating most System-2 calls; substantial, reproducible cost reductions (63–92%).

### 1.4 Beyond Intelligibility: Fine-Grained Perceptual Assessment of Dysarthric Speech with Auditory Large Language Models
- **arXiv:** [2611.23768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23768) [eess.AS; cs.SD] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Haotian Wei, Can Long, Wen Xian Jim (equal contribution), Guangzhi Sun, Zengrui Jin, Chao Zhang
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata (author line shows only "member institutions").
- **Abstract:** Accurate assessment of dysarthric speech is vital for clinical diagnosis and rehabilitation, yet existing automated methods rely heavily on isolated, single-task models or generic speech quality metrics that fail to disentangle heterogeneous neuromotor deficits. This work presents the first systematic investigation into adapting auditory large language models (ALLMs) for multi-dimensional dysarthric speech assessment under a unified, prompt-conditioned framework. Four representative ALLMs are benchmarked across five clinical perceptual dimensions from the Speech Accessibility Project (SAP): naturalness, monopitch, intelligibility, imprecise consonants, and inappropriate silences. To bridge generic perceptual quality and clinical evaluation, a two-stage training paradigm is explored, leveraging out-of-domain pre-training followed by in-domain clinical adaptation. Experimental results demonstrate that leading ALLMs reliably capture fine-grained pathological variations, exhibiting distinct and complementary strengths across prosodic and articulatory attributes. These findings establish ALLMs as a scalable foundation for clinical speech evaluation and provide practical insights into model selection and multi-task transfer potential.
- **Key innovations:** First benchmarking of ALLMs for dysarthric speech assessment across 5 clinical perceptual dimensions; prompt-conditioned unified framework, replacing per-deficit task models; two-stage OOD→in-domain training; identifies complementary strengths of ALLMs for prosodic vs articulatory assessment.

### 2.1 CONCORD-Rec: Catalog-Conditioned Modality Utility for Semantic-ID Diffusion Recommendation
- **arXiv:** [2611.23710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23710) [cs.IR] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Haopeng Jin, Bingkang Shi, Tao Yu, Yifei Ma, Hao Liu, Hongzhu Yi, Jinwen Luo
- **Institution/Company:** Tencent (Jin, Luo); University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shi, Yu, Liu, Yi); Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications (Ma); corresponding authors Shi, Yi, Luo.
- **Abstract:** Multimodal generative recommendation with discrete semantic identifiers (SIDs) requires distinguishing historical modalities that merely alter token predictions from those that genuinely reduce catalog-level ambiguity. When audiovisual, visual, or textual streams are missing, degraded, or semantically conflicting, uniform fusion and unconstrained token gates can propagate misleading content across iterative denoising steps. We propose CONCORD-Rec, a reliability-conditioned discrete diffusion recommender supervised by counterfactual catalog-partition utility. Given a fixed tokenizer and a partially masked SID state, CONCORD-Rec evaluates out-of-fold teacher coalitions over the exact feasible catalog support and aggregates item posteriors across the target code partition at each unresolved position. Rather than distilling raw token probabilities directly, the resulting counterfactual loss differences supervise an availability-masked tokenwise gate that allocates bounded modality residuals around a behavioral denoiser while retaining an exact behavioral fallback slot. We establish theoretical fixed-support ranking stability bounds under gate perturbations and evaluate position-level and code-conditional allocation across audiovisual and image-text recommendation benchmarks under controlled missingness, signal degradation, and semantic conflict. Across 20 source-compatible protocol–dataset rows, CONCORD-Rec improves NDCG by 4%; under matched 30% conflict it improves NDCG by 14.63%. The full-vs-uniform-T=16 sweep improves quality by 2.00–3.58% while reducing p95 latency by up to 86.89%.
- **Key innovations:** Novel supervision object — catalog-partition loss difference between teacher coalitions (with vs without modality) on the feasible catalog support, instead of token probabilities; counterfactual utility targets from out-of-fold teachers; availability-masked gate with bounded residuals + exact base fallback; fixed-support ranking stability proof (Proposition 1); strong robustness (+14.63% NDCG under controlled conflict, p95 latency advantage of 4-call over 16-step uniform decoding).

### 2.2 Better Rankers, Worse Retrievers: The Hidden Cost of RL Post-Training in Generative Recommendation
- **arXiv:** [2611.22838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22838) [cs.IR; cs.AI] — 17 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Yuheng Chen, Fan Zhang, Yixuan Xu, Chao Yang, Yang Xiang, Can Yang
- **Institution/Company:** The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Hong Kong, China (6); Trip.com Group, Shanghai, China (2)
- **Abstract:** Generative recommenders decode the semantic ID of a user's next item with a language model, and post-training them with reinforcement learning (RL) after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has become standard practice. The gains are mostly reported on the first ten results, yet in deployment such models hand hundreds of candidates to a downstream ranker or reward model. We show that RL post-training has a hidden cost: it improves accuracy at the top of the list (hit@K for small K) but, on all five datasets we study, lowers the recall of the candidate list (hit@K for K in the hundreds). The model becomes a better ranker and a worse retriever. Controlled experiments trace the loss to the negative advantages that RL assigns to the candidates it samples, the rollout. The root cause is that only one next item is logged per user, so RL treats every other candidate as wrong. Yet these candidates are the model's most plausible alternatives, and often the next items of other users with similar histories. Penalising them pushes down exactly what similar users choose next, and these items drop out of those users' candidate lists. This diagnosis points directly to a remedy: out-of-rollout anchoring, a single additional loss term that preserves the SFT model's ordering of the items outside the rollout. It keeps the gain at the top and restores recall to the SFT level on all five datasets, and it is effective under other RL configurations as well. To our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of this recall loss: we measure it, identify its cause, and show that, once the cause is known, a simple remedy suffices.
- **Key innovations:** First systematic study of a real degradation in generative recommendation under RL post-training (better top-K, worse retrieval); root cause: negative advantages penalize out-of-rollout candidates, which are the most plausible alternatives for similar users; remedy "out-of-rollout anchoring" fully restores recall while keeping top-K gains, validated across 5 datasets and RL configs.

### 2.3 Keep it Simple! A Multiple-Choice Task can Benefit Generative Recommendation
- **arXiv:** [2611.19320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19320) [cs.IR] — 14 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Wei-Hsiang Huang, Jyun-Yu Jiang, Pu-Jen Cheng
- **Institution/Company:** National Taiwan University, Taipei, Taiwan (3); Coupang, California, USA (1).
- **Abstract:** Sequential recommendation has recently witnessed growing interest in generative retrieval. One of the most popular approaches is to autoregressively generate the semantic identifiers (SIDs) of items as recommendations. Existing efforts mainly rely on semantic alignment, sophisticated architectural designs, and extensive hyperparameter tuning. However, the conventional learning framework for generative models could have foundational limitations. Specifically, most generative models directly predict target items without explicitly learning negative compositions and relative ranking signals. This is not well aligned with the philosophy of Bayesian personalized ranking (BPR), which emphasizes the importance of relative rankings between positive and negative samples. In this paper, we show that adding a simple multiple-choice auxiliary task into the learning objective can effectively address this issue. With minimal adaptation, we introduce Task Identifiers and Candidate Constructions to synthesize training data for the multiple-choice auxiliary task. In addition, we provide theoretical analysis of the fundamental benefits of the multiple-choice auxiliary task from the perspectives of information theory and compositional generalization. Extensive experiments on four publicly available benchmark datasets show that our approach can improve TIGER, a conventional generative recommendation model, by 4.9% to 79% in NDCG@10.
- **Key innovations:** Shows that a conceptually simple multiple-choice auxiliary task (predicting the right SID among candidate SIDs) fixes the missing negative/relative-ranking supervision of BPR in generative retrieval; introduces Task Identifiers + Candidate Constructions to synthesize training data; provides theoretical grounding from information theory (sufficiency of positive-negative comparisons) and compositional generalization; +4.9–79% NDCG@10 over TIGER on 4 datasets.

### 2.4 Match Rate Is Not Match Quality
- **arXiv:** [2611.23851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23851) [econ.EM] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Tobias B. Konitzer, David M. Rothschild
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata.
- **Abstract:** Third-party identity-resolution vendors commonly emphasize match rate, the share of submitted records they can link to identifiers for advertising activation, as their core metric of quality. Yet a high match rate does not necessarily mean that those matched identifiers belong to the submitted records (i.e., the intended targets). In this paper we explore match quality as an alternative metric to match rate, utilizing a unique data set to evaluate match quality. We are able to compare two demographics, age and gender, from a submitted record (voter files) to the same demographics in self-reported surveys matched to mobile advertising identifiers linked together by two leading identity-resolution vendors. The data and matching was from 2019–20. There is substantial ambiguity in match quality of the returned records. First, both vendors' activation files contain multiple device identifiers per match. Second, match quality among audited pairs (Vendor A) and for explicitly labeled individual-level matches (Vendor B) is only 21.8% and 23.6%, respectively. Utilizing a mixture model that assumes that correct links always agree on these two demographics and incorrect links agree 10% of the time, these rates imply conditional identity-level precisions of approximately 15% and 13%, respectively. This paper utilizes identity-resolution products that are several years old, but the question we address remains fundamental: match rate alone cannot establish whether advertisers are reaching the people they intended. Evaluating identity resolution requires independent evidence of match quality alongside match rate.
- **Key innovations:** Empirical critique of match rate as an advertising quality metric; defines and quantifies "match quality" (agreement of demographics between submitted records and matched survey answers) and computes identity-level precision (~15% / ~13%) of the two leading identity-resolution vendors; argues that match rate alone cannot ensure reaching the intended people.

### 3.1 Halting in Recurrent Sequence Models
- **arXiv:** [2611.20377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20377) [cs.LG] — 16 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Simon Dräger (single author; institution not listed in arXiv metadata)
- **Abstract:** Recurrent sequence models, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and state space models (SSMs), are often trained to emit an end-of-sequence (EOS) token when their output sequence is complete. During training, EOS is treated as another token without a special loss term or incentive. Although this training paradigm is widely used, it is not understood how models decide to output EOS. We study a minimal version of the EOS halting problem in which networks are trained to emit a given input sequence an instructed number of times, followed by EOS. We train tanh RNNs and linear SSMs on this task, finding that the RNNs encode the number of tokens left to copy in a linear subspace, of which two principal components capture most of the variation. Hidden states with the same remaining count converge to a shared representation regardless of their instructed repeat count. By modulating the hidden state using an affine transformation at the boundary between copied sequences, we shift the RNN's state to the representation shared by states with one fewer copy remaining. The unchanged network emits EOS one copy earlier while reproducing the shortened target sequence in 94.4% of new sequences, revealing that this representation causally determines the stopping time. In the SSMs, the difference between trajectories with different repeat counts is separated across two-dimensional rotation planes whose contributions to the EOS comparison cancel during copying and align near the stopping time. Rotating the planes independently removes most of the intended stopping while preserving the copied content, which shows that the stopping time depends on the planes' relative phases. These results identify two candidate primitives of EOS halting in recurrent models: a shared encoding of the number of remaining tokens in tanh RNNs and phase alignment across fixed rotations in linear SSMs.
- **Key innovations:** First mechanistic study of how recurrent models learn to emit EOS: RNNs encode the "tokens remaining" count in a linear subspace (2 PCs capture most variation) and a shared representation drives early EOS emission (94.4% new-sequence reproduction); SSMs use phase-aligned 2D rotation planes whose relative phases control stopping time; provides candidate primitives for halt/termination behavior in RNN/SSM design.

### 3.2 Searching for Autoregressive Neural Architectures from Fundamental Operations
- **arXiv:** [2611.21111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21111) [cs.LG; cs.AI] — 16 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Thomas Woehrle, Linus Ericsson, Henry Gouk, Elliot J. Crowley
- **Institution/Company:** Affiliations listed on the arXiv page: University of Edinburgh (multiple authors) and University of Glasgow (Linus Ericsson). No other institutional details are published for this paper.
- **Abstract:** Expressive Neural Architecture Search (NAS) search spaces are desirable as they do not restrict the space of potential architectures to what is already established. However, few search spaces are expressive enough to represent a broad range of architecture families, and those that are have focused primarily on image classification problems. The task of autoregressive sequence modelling has become ubiquitous in modern machine learning, but no existing search space supports the construction of and search over sequence models from fundamental operations, instead imposing strong inductive biases on the structure of potential architectures. This work describes how the expressive einspace search space can be extended to yield a search space capable of representing autoregressive models using fundamental operations. We perform a series of experiments showing that the novel einspaceAR search space can find capable autoregressive sequence models from scratch--i.e., without imposing strong inductive biases. We believe that our einspaceAR search space will be a useful part of the NAS toolbox and that the techniques described in this work can serve as a blueprint for the integration of autoregressive modelling in other expressive search spaces.
- **Key innovations:** First NAS search space (einspaceAR) that directly searches over autoregressive sequence models from fundamental operations with no hand-built inductive bias; demonstrated capability on sequence tasks; extends the expressive einspace space.

### 4.1 Learning Martial Arts Motion Skills for Animating Opponents in VR Sparring Games
- **arXiv:** [2611.23825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23825) [cs.HC; cs.AI; cs.MM; cs.RO] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Jiqing Wen, Qixuan Shi, Robert LiKamWa, Pavan Turaga
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata.
- **Abstract:** Animating virtual opponents is an important yet challenging task in game development. In martial arts games, opponent motions must not only be physically plausible but also exhibit appropriate technique, timing, transitions, and target-directed intent. Traditional game animations often rely on handcrafted clips, state machine transitions, and procedural adjustments, which can be labor-intensive and may limit the behavioral diversity of non-player characters. This paper presents a martial arts motion skills learning framework for character animation in virtual sparring games. Inspired by Tang Soo Do, we collected a martial arts motion capture dataset containing isolated attacking and defending techniques, technique combinations and transitions, and contextual sparring variations. We first pretrained a reusable repertoire of martial arts motion skills from the captured data using reinforcement learning (RL) with adversarial style rewards, and then adapted these skills with task-specific rewards for target-directed striking and threat-aware evasion. We evaluated the motions generated by the learned policy through a user study, in which participants assessed their physical plausibility, smoothness, and martial arts form. To demonstrate their applicability to interactive gameplay, we developed a VR sparring prototype for Meta Quest headsets. The prototype combines policy-generated motion clips with lightweight state-based control to support interactive opponent behavior. Together, these findings support the feasibility of using a learned martial arts skill repertoire as a motion foundation for animating opponents in VR sparring games.
- **Key innovations:** RL-based acquisition of a reusable martial-arts skill repertoire (adversarial style rewards) + task-specific adaptation for target-directed striking/evasion; full pipeline validated with a user study (plausibility, smoothness, form) and deployed in a Meta Quest VR sparring prototype.

### 4.2 PCGRL for Complex Platformers: Synthesizing Playable and Diverse Levels via Inflated Envelopes and Forward Curricula
- **arXiv:** [2611.22250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22250) [cs.LG; cs.AI] — 17 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Di Wu (single author; institution not listed in arXiv metadata)
- **Abstract:** Procedural Content Generation via Reinforcement Learning (PCGRL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for automated game development. However, existing PCGRL frameworks still suffer from severe exploration bottlenecks and scaling limitations when faced with long-sequence generation tasks governed by strict rigid-body rules and non-linear kinematic boundaries (e.g., platformer constraints). Consequently, traditional approaches frequently fail to maintain reliable level solvability on large-scale canvases. To address these challenges, we present a physics-aware, curriculum-guided PCGRL framework. First, we construct an "Inflated Volume Envelope" mechanism, which provides a physics-constrained representation that incorporates continuous kinematic feasibility into the discrete state space of the MDP. Second, to alleviate the sparse reward challenge induced by extremely long decision sequences, we introduce intermediate state-transition feedback and a multi-stage forward extension curriculum learning framework, enabling informative value estimation and stable credit assignment over long-horizon trajectories. Finally, to resolve the issue where deterministic policies after convergence generate highly monotonous map paths with a lack of diversity, we activate high-entropy stochastic inference during the synthesis phase, regularized by a post-hoc parallel rejection filtering mechanism. Experimental results demonstrate that our framework significantly enhances structural diversity while maintaining high physical playability across multi-scale environments. Comparative evaluations across multi-scale canvases demonstrate that our framework maintains superior convergence stability and dimensional scalability, reliably synthesizing solvable levels where traditional PCGRL baselines degrade.
- **Key innovations:** "Inflated Volume Envelope" physics-constrained MDP representation encoding continuous kinematic feasibility; multi-stage forward curriculum for long-horizon credit assignment (addresses sparse-reward exploration collapse); entropy-regularized synthesis + parallel rejection filtering for level diversity while staying playable; scalable to multi-scale canvases where baselines degrade.

### 4.3 Certified Nash Equilibrium Learning for Nonzero-Sum Jump-Diffusion Games: Policy Iteration and Off-Policy Q-Learning
- **arXiv:** [2611.21272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21272) [math.OC] — 16 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Pengwei Wang, Na Li, Jiacheng Fan, Tielong Shen, Zuo Quan Xu (submitted by Na Li; institution not listed in arXiv metadata)
- **Abstract:** This paper establishes two model-free reinforcement learning methods for infinite-horizon N-player linear-quadratic nonzero-sum stochastic differential games whose drift, Brownian diffusion, and Poisson jump amplitudes depend on all players' controls. Control dependence in the diffusion and jump terms couples the players' simultaneous feedback updates. A verifiable certificate ensures that the partially model-free policy iteration map is contractive on an invariant closed ball centered at an admissible initial feedback profile. All feedback iterates are mean-square stabilizing, and the algorithm achieves local geometric convergence of feedback gains and value matrices to those associated with a closed-loop Nash equilibrium. The term certified refers to a verifiable convergence certificate that guarantees mean-square stability of all policy iterates and local geometric convergence to a closed-loop Nash equilibrium. To implement the policy updates from data, the off-policy Q-learning algorithm reuses the same behavior-policy data across iterations without requiring the system coefficients or jump intensity. Under quantitative persistence of excitation and a common initialization, exact population regression reproduces the policy-iteration sequence. The same local convergence guarantee therefore applies to off-policy Q-learning at the population level. Simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of the two proposed algorithms.
- **Key innovations:** First certified (provably contractive + mean-square stabilizing) policy-iteration map for nonzero-sum jump-diffusion LQSDE games with data-driven off-policy Q-learning variant; reproduces exact policy-iteration sequence from reused behavior data; local geometric convergence to closed-loop Nash equilibria.

### 4.4 PReMind: When Personal Memory Meets Visual Hazards
- **arXiv:** [2611.23808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23808) [cs.CV] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Yeongtak Oh, Donghyeok Lee, Joonhee Oh, Jaewon Jeong, Sungroh Yoon
- **Institution/Company:** Seoul National University — Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering; Interdisciplinary Program in AI (all five)
- **Abstract:** Personalized vision-language assistants use visual observations and personal memories to provide advice tailored to the user. When the observed scene contains a physical hazard, personal context, such as allergies, medical conditions, or mobility constraints, can change what help is appropriate. Responding appropriately therefore requires recognizing the visible hazard and applying the relevant person's information. We introduce PReMind-Bench, a diagnostic benchmark that evaluates this combination across diverse hazards and personal-memory conditions. It separately measures memory access, grounding, abstention, and hazard response, and assesses joint success on interdependency cases. Existing models often satisfy one requirement while failing the other, and training only for safety or personalization leaves a gap in joint success. We therefore propose PReMind, a two-phase reinforcement learning framework that combines on-demand and forced-call interactions (memory always retrieved) to jointly train grounded memory use and hazard response. At 4B, PReMind raises joint success from 12.9% to 28.6% over its base on the interdependency set and improves hazard adequacy and correct abstention on the main test. Joint-success gains persist under forced-call evaluation and under a second LLM judge across three Phase II seeds; averaged over these seeds, joint success also improves on unseen hazard groups. These findings highlight the need to evaluate and train personal assistants to connect visual evidence with the relevant person's context.
- **Key innovations:** PReMind-Bench: first diagnostic benchmark separating memory access, grounding, abstention, and hazard response, with joint-success metric on interdependency cases; PReMind 2-phase RL framework (on-demand vs forced-call memory retrieval) achieving 12.9%→28.6% joint success at 4B.
