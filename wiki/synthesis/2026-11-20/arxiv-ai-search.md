### 1.1 TAP: Tapping the Discriminative Potential of Frozen Representations for Class-Incremental Learning
- **arXiv:** [2611.25478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25478) [cs.CV] — 19 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Zihao Wang, Yu-Wei Zhan, Xiao-Qian Liu, Zhen-Duo Chen, Weitong Liu, Xin Luo, Xin-Shun Xu
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Pre-trained models provide transferable representations for class-incremental learning (CIL). However, sequentially adapting these representations introduces optimization costs and may disrupt old-class feature structure. This motivates us to explore whether the discriminative potential of these representations can be further exploited by continually accumulating class information while keeping the encoders frozen. To this end, we propose TAP, a new and novel framework for tapping the discriminative potential of frozen representations in class-incremental learning. It accumulates visual and semantic class information in the frozen CLIP embedding space with only lightweight optimization. TAP comprises three modules: class-conditional visual distribution modeling, class-adaptive semantic calibration, and visual-semantic posterior fusion. The first module retains class means and within-class scatters, combining class-specific and shared covariances to stabilize distribution estimation. The second module learns class-specific weights over text templates from current-task images, adapting text representations to downstream classes. The third module combines the visual and semantic posteriors through a weighted product of experts for prediction over all seen classes without retaining images from previous tasks. Across nine datasets under two incremental protocols, TAP achieves state-of-the-art performance, attaining the highest average incremental and final-stage accuracies among the compared methods. The code is provided as an ancillary file.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Frozen-embedding class accumulation: TAP keeps the CLIP encoder frozen while accumulating visual class information (class means and within-class scatters combining class-specific and shared covariances) plus semantic class information (class-adaptive weights over text templates learned from current-task images); no data from earlier tasks is retained.
  - Visual-semantic posterior fusion: predictions over all seen classes go through a weighted product of experts combining the frozen visual posterior with the calibrated semantic posterior.
  - SOTA class-incremental performance: top average incremental and final-stage accuracies on nine datasets under two incremental protocols.

### 1.2 SR-Agent: An Advanced Intelligent Agent for Seismic Data Restoration
- **arXiv:** [2611.25466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25466) [physics.geo-ph] — 19 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Songmin Wang, Xintong Dong, Zilong Wang, Xinyang Wang, Zhengyi Yuan, Wenshuo Yu
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Field seismic data often suffer from multiple degradations, including noise contamination, incomplete geometry, low-frequency loss, and reduced spatial resolution, requiring different restoration operations. Existing methods mainly address individual degradations, while sequential application of task-specific models often relies on human experience. Although all-in-one models integrate multiple restoration tasks, they face a trade-off between generalization and task-specific accuracy. To address this issue, we propose a SR-Agent (Seismic Restoration Agent) containing three sequential stages: classification, restoration, and evaluation. For the first two stages, we design an intelligent classifier based on ResNet to identify the degradation types presented in low-quality seismic data, and the classification results are used as task instructions to activate the corresponding restoration models in a pool, which contains dedicated networks for different restoration tasks. For the last stage, SR-Agent can dynamically schedule the activated models and explore an optimal restoration path, rather than relying on a predefined processing pipeline. Specifically, we first develop a no-reference restoration-performance evaluation measurement by combining four parameters: seismic trace coherence, energy distribution in F-K domain, spectral smoothness, and high-frequency energy ratio. Second, the evaluation measurement is applied to quantify the restoration performance of different candidate restoration paths. Finally, the path with the highest evaluation score is automatically selected and then applied to input seismic data. The results show that, when multiple degradation mechanisms coexist, SR-Agent can effectively plan an optimal restoration path, achieving accurate and intelligent restoration of degraded seismic data.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Three-stage agent pipeline: a ResNet-based degradation classifier activates dedicated restoration networks from a pool, then a dynamic path scheduler picks the best candidate via a no-reference metric (trace coherence, F-K domain energy distribution, spectral smoothness, high-frequency energy ratio) — no fixed pipeline.

### 1.3 Knowing What You Know: A Trait Theory of Reviewer Value in Human-AI Cognitive Systems
- **arXiv:** [2611.24932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24932) [cs.AI; cs.CE; cs.HC; cs.LG] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Priyanka Sinha, Sugam Pandey
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** When a capable model and a human reviewer cooperate on the same decision, which human still adds value? We argue this is a question about a single cognitive trait, metacognitive calibration: the alignment between a reviewer's behavioral confidence and the actual correctness of their judgments. We develop a trait theory of reviewer value to make it precise. The theory makes five claims: value is governed by calibration rather than by activity, speed, agreement, or tenure; calibration leaves observable traces in behavioral telemetry; those traces are non-diagnostic on their own, because the fast, low-revision signature of a calibrated expert is indistinguishable from that of a confident novice until it is gated by outcomes; human calibration and a model's self-knowledge are the same construct, so the joint system should defer to whichever agent is better calibrated on each item; and individual calibration is causally identifiable only above the level of the single judgment. We ground each claim in a six-month production window of an AI-assisted accounting system (541621 reviewer events, 483 reviewers), and find that a purely behavioral expertise profile inverts the ground truth, scoring an overconfident cohort (5.2 percent correction survival) higher than genuine experts (99.2 percent); that reviewer quality is flat across tenure, so the trait is not learned on the job; and that the same calibration logic that separates good from bad reviewers, applied to the model, concentrates 92.5 percent of human effort on genuine errors. We close with the boundary the theory must respect: under majority-vote aggregation, individual causal value is unidentifiable.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Metacognitive calibration as the trait: reviewer value is governed by the alignment of behavioral confidence with outcome correctness, not by activity, speed, agreement, or tenure.
  - Production-grounded findings: on 541,621 reviewer events (483 reviewers), purely behavioral profiles invert ground truth (overconfident cohort: 5.2% survival vs. genuine experts: 99.2%); calibration is flat across tenure; applying the calibration logic to the model concentrates 92.5% of human effort on genuine errors.
  - Boundary: individual causal value is unidentifiable under majority-vote aggregation.

- **Abstract:** Real-world harness engineering is driven by explicit and implicit user requirements, which guide development and validation. Together, these results show that requirement-centered harness evolution is a more general method for improving agent capabilities.

### 1.6 Auditing black-box clinical prediction models with a large language model through queries alone: a blinded test with planted flaws
- **arXiv:** [2611.24877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24877) [cs.LG; cs.AI] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Zhiyu Liu, Xinyu Yang
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Large language models (LLMs) are used to explain clinical prediction models, on the promise that they can say what a model computes, find its faults and even reveal new knowledge in it. We tested the three promises where the answers could be checked. From the International Stroke Trial we trained four kinds of model (logistic regression, explainable boosting machine, gradient-boosted trees, neural network) for two outcomes on patients randomised earlier, planted one flaw in each of 24 copies (an under-predicted subgroup, a reversed risk factor or an implausible step) and mixed them with the 8 clean models. Of 601 unexpected relationships it read out of the clean models, 11% held in patients randomised later (random pairs 6%; a regression scan's shorter list of its strongest candidates, 28%); after a check on the training data, 33% against 30%. With query access alone, an LLM can describe a black-box model faithfully and find nearly every planted flaw, as a purpose-written script also did; its reports of problems must be checked on the model and its discoveries on data.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Blind tool-mediated auditing: with query access alone, found the planted flaw in 69/72 runs (96%) on models whose internal details it never saw.
  - Falsifiable audit reports: 60% of 1,089 checkable reports failed a pre-committed check against the model's average behaviour (post hoc: most real but small steps); a faithful surrogate (median 128 terms, mean $R^2$ 0.976) for estimating the log-odds.
  - Verification protocol: check reports on the model itself and discoveries on data — 11% of unexpected relationships held on later patients vs 28% for the strongest candidates and 6% for random pairs.

### 1.7 Can Programs Replace LLM Judges? Executable Program Judges for Scalable and Efficient Relevance Labeling
- **arXiv:** [2611.24849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24849) [cs.IR; cs.CL] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Eugene Agichtein, Charles L. A. Clarke, Kaustubh Dhole, William Stern
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Large language model (LLM) judges have become a common way to create large-scale relevance judgments for retrieval evaluation. However, each judgment requires a potentially expensive call to a frontier model, and the judgments are hard to interpret and may not be reproducible. In this paper, we ask whether an LLM can write a readable program for each topic that replaces its per-document judgments. To address these issues, we propose ExecJudges, a method for generating fast, inspectable and scalable judges for automatic relevance labeling. Specifically, ExecJudges uses a frontier LLM to turn assessment guidelines into executable programs, which rely only on lexical matching, standard NLP libraries like parsers, and pool statistics. Our results suggest that ExecJudges offers a reproducible, inspectable, efficient and cost-effective alternative to LLM judges for labeling deep result pools for system evaluation or ranking improvements.

### 2.1 LQ-Rec: Late Quantization for Sequential Recommendation
- **arXiv:** [2611.25256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25256) [cs.IR] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Satoshi Koide, Yueqi Wang, Julian McAuley
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Generative retrieval (GR) for sequential recommendation compresses each item into a short semantic ID and trains an autoregressive decoder to generate next-item codes from a user history. While appealing for cold-start handling and storage, autoregressive GR suffers a structural scaling limit: typically, longer semantic IDs reduce quantization error in principle but degrade ranking quality and quadratic memory usage against semantic ID length. We propose Late Quantization (LQ), a non-autoregressive paradigm that confines discretization to the output (item) side of the pipeline. The encoder and scoring rule operate in continuous space, while items are stored and matched as semantic IDs produced by an end-to-end-trained residual quantizer. This architectural shift permits an additive error decomposition and a pairwise ordering theorem that jointly bound the quantization-induced ranking drop, a structural guarantee absent in prior semantic-ID recommenders. Across four public benchmarks, the resulting recommender, LQ-Rec, delivers three capabilities simultaneously: ranking quality improves monotonically with the semantic-ID length and outperforms existing baselines; a single trained model supports anytime compression, tuning ranking quality against inference cost without retraining; and LQ-Rec shows superior generalization on a memorization/generalization partition.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Non-autoregressive late quantization: encoder + scoring in continuous space; items stored/matched as semantic IDs from an end-to-end-trained residual quantizer — frees the architecture from GR's structural scaling limit.
  - Structural guarantee: additive error decomposition + pairwise ordering theorem bounding the quantization-induced ranking drop, absent in prior semantic-ID recommenders.
  - Three capabilities at once: monotonic quality improvement with ID length + anytime compression/cost-quality tradeoff without retraining + superior generalization on memorization/generalization partitions.

### 2.6 Variance-Optimal Counterfactual Policy Design for Sample-Efficient Online A/B Testing
- **arXiv:** [2611.24757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24757) [cs.LG; cs.IR] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Shashank Gupta, Subhadeep Maji
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Online A/B testing is the standard approach for evaluating changes to user-facing systems, such as a new recommendation or ranking policy, before deployment. While the difference-in-means estimator of a standard A/B test is unbiased, it often suffers from high variance. When the treatment and production policies choose the same actions with similar probabilities, the resulting observations can add noise while providing little information about the difference between the policies. Consequently, A/B tests can require large amounts of traffic and long durations to detect small treatment effects. Existing variance-reduction methods mainly improve the estimator after the data-collection scheme has been fixed, and recent overlap-aware designs only optimize a single traffic split between the two policies; thereby, they cannot direct traffic to the actions on which the policies disagree. Second, we use historical logs to jointly optimize the logging policy and a control variate to further reduce variance. Our experimental results in simulated policy A/B tests show that our designs estimate the average treatment effect more accurately than existing variance reduction methods on every evaluated setting, while generally improving statistical power and reducing how often the worse policy is selected. As a result, our designs enable practitioners to reach reliable deployment decisions with considerably less online traffic.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Variance-optimal action-level logging policy: closed-form from pairwise off-policy evaluation, jointly optimizing action sampling and the estimator; can direct traffic to the actions the policies disagree on.
  - Joint optimization with control variates: historical logs used to optimize the logging policy and control variate together; reduces variance even further.
  - Simulated A/B tests: better estimates than existing variance-reduction methods on every setting, higher statistical power, less worse-policy selection — decisions with considerably less online traffic.

### 3.3 WorldRuler: Interactive World Modeling under Configurable Rules
- **arXiv:** [2611.24869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24869) [cs.AI] — 18 Nov 2026
- **Authors:** Mingliang Zhai, Chuanhao Li, Jiaming Tan, Zhen Li, Yuwei Wu, Yunde Jia, Zian Meng, Yuanyang Yin, Kaipeng Zhang
- **Institution/Company:** Not listed in arXiv metadata
- **Abstract:** Rules govern how player actions change interactive worlds. Different rules create distinct gameplay experiences within similar scenes and action spaces. For customizable gameplay, generative world models must follow user-defined rules beyond real-world behaviors. However, realizing user-defined rules presents two challenges: Rule-State Persistence and Rule-to-Visual Realization. The former requires preserving rule definitions, dependencies, and world states across saving and loading, so that restored worlds follow the established rules. The latter requires accurately realizing rule-induced changes on designated objects and attributes, particularly when affected objects are unseen. In this paper, we propose WorldRuler, which organizes rule definitions, dependencies, and object states in an executable 3D rule graph and connects rule execution, video generation, and perceptual feedback in a closed loop. The graph supports joint saving and restoration of rules and world states, while dynamic object binding grounds rule-specified entities in visual instances and uses stored states to constrain generation from their first appearance. We further introduce object attribute state replacement and counterfactual reconstruction training, enabling accurate attribute control and compatible appearance and motion changes while limiting interference with unrelated content. We develop an automated data collection and annotation pipeline using GTA-V and Habitat to construct RuleVerse, a dataset of rule-driven interactions with detailed state and event annotations, including cases where rules take effect before affected objects become visible. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of WorldRuler in preserving rule-state consistency across saving and loading and accurately realizing rule outcomes on designated objects and attributes.
- **Key innovations:**
  - Executable 3D rule graph: organizes rule definitions, dependencies, and object states; connects rule execution, video generation, and perceptual feedback in a closed loop.
  - Rule-State Persistence: joint saving/restoration of rules and world states; dynamic object binding grounds rule entities in visuals and uses stored states to constrain generation from first appearance.
  - Accurate rule realization: object attribute state replacement + counterfactual reconstruction training, with GTA-V/Habitat-derived RuleVerse dataset (incl. rules taking effect before objects appear); experiments show rule-state consistency across save/load and accurate rule outcomes on designated objects/attributes.
