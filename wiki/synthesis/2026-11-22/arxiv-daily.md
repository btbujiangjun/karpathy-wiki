### A.1. AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control
- **arXiv**: [2611.26613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26613) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26613)) — 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Yiyang Du, Yuxuan Kuang, Qitao Zhao, Yixuan Wang, Zhanqiu Guo, Yi Xu, Xin Li, Wenbin He, Liu Ren, Chenyan Xiong
- **Institution/Company**: Carnegie Mellon University; University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign; Bosch Research North America & Bosch Center for Artificial Intelligence (BCAI)
- **Categories**: cs.RO, cs.CL, cs.CV
- **Abstract**: Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. We introduce AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which the policy agent states a local intent alongside each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode against this intent. Our action-level analysis demonstrates the promising capabilities of frontier agents on direct robot control, including closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, reliable semantic grounding across diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation without task-specific adaptation. It also identifies their current limitations, including task-relevant physical execution bottlenecks such as grasping and alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Finally, we validate our critic on a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and release a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions alongside a fine-tuned open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B, whose failure-mode accuracy rises from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling low-cost action-level analysis.

### A.2. Represented but misread: Post-training changes how language models read out what they represent
- **arXiv**: [2611.26584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26584) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26584)) — 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Jochen Laubrock
- **Institution/Company**: University of Potsdam
- **Categories**: cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Abstract**: Does post-training change what a language model represents, or how this representation is read out into an answer? We compared linear probes with the models' own answers, analyzed with signal detection theory, in six open-weight base models and their post-trained versions. For judgments of whether German texts were written by a human or an AI, probes and word n-gram classifiers separated the classes well, but the answers did not and often ordered the texts inversely. Post-training lowered the sensitivity of the answers on average ($Δd' = -0.34$ across model families; -0.29 across three prompts). It also changed the use of a stated base rate: Base models shifted their log-odds by less than the stated change in prior odds, most post-trained models by more. In two models with stage-wise checkpoints, the change occurred during supervised fine-tuning, model stitching implicated the early layers, and from mid-depth on internal states at positions after the text, not those of the text, transferred the change between checkpoints. Our corpora cannot be shared, so we add a case reproducible from public data, the truth of negated statements. Here, post-training acted in the opposite direction: Probes decoded truth at or near ceiling in every model, the answers of several base models did not use it, and post-training repaired them; by mid-depth the repair resided at the positions after the statement; below it, the representation of the statement contributed, strongly in one of four models. In a training study, adding 2,000 correctly labeled authorship judgments of news texts to otherwise identical fine-tuning data improved the answers on our newspaper and textbook corpora and inverted them on the chats, where they followed a news-trained probe of the base model. Post-training thus changed how a judgment is read out of what a model represents, and a small training signal can set its direction.

### A.7. Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher
- **arXiv**: [2611.26598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26598) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26598)) — 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Yang Meng, Zixin Ding, Robert L. Grossman, Yuxin Chen
- **Institution/Company**: not stated on arXiv
- **Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Abstract**: Acquiring every feature of an instance is often costly: a measurement can carry a monetary price, a time delay, or a privacy cost. Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) addresses this by learning a policy that decides, per instance, which features to acquire under a budget. Existing AFA methods train this policy by optimizing a self-constructed utility target rather than distilling an explicit teacher. We construct this teacher explicitly. At training time the label and the unobserved feature values are available, and together they determine the teacher in closed form: an oracle that ranks every candidate by its effect on the downstream predictor's error. Across multimodal medical diagnosis and the AFABench benchmark, OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods across the budget range, recovering 90% of the attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost. Because the teacher is an explicit algorithmic object rather than an implicit optimization target, we can also measure directly how much of its ranking is recoverable by a policy restricted to what is visible at deployment.

### B.3. Generative News Recommendation Reproduces Popularity: A Memorization-Based Analysis

- **Institution/Company**: not stated on arXiv

- **Abstract**: Text-based generative recommenders can go beyond ranking existing items by directly generating item titles or other textual representations. ### D.1. DNA: Unlocking Depth Scaling in CTR Models
- **arXiv**: [2611.25659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25659) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25659)) — 2026-11-19
- **Authors**: Caiyuan Li, Wenjie Wu, Yuheng Huang, Xiao Zhou, Silu Zhou, Ke Chen, Zinan Zhao, Xinrui Zeng, Hanmo Xu, Chaoqun Song, Hua Zong, Zongyuan Wu, Hongbin Zhou, Huimin Yi, Zhixuan Zhang, Dan Ou, Haihong Tang
- **Institution/Company**: not stated on arXiv
- **Categories**: cs.IR
- **Abstract**: Long user behavior sequences are among the strongest signals for click-through rate (CTR) prediction. Cross-attention makes these sequences practical for industrial ranking by keeping compute linear in sequence length. Yet the gains from stacking cross-attention layers taper off: on Taobao search, growing a cross-attention stack from 16 to 28 layers adds only 0.02 percentage points (pt) of AUC. We argue that the key-value side is the bottleneck. Each layer projects the same behavior sequence into keys and values, so adding depth gives the model more reads of a fixed sequence. We propose DNA (Dual-stream Nexus Attention), which lets the sequence itself evolve with depth. At each layer, the request representation (the search query, user features and scene) first reads the sequence. Each behavior token then attends to the updated request representation and uses what it reads to update itself, so the next layer reads a sequence shaped by the current request. The update runs once per request, all candidates share the resulting sequence, and both the reads and the update keep linear complexity in sequence length. In an online A/B test against the production model, it improves CTR by 0.8% and GMV by 0.5%.
- **Key innovations**:
  - We propose DNA (Dual-stream Nexus Attention), which lets the sequence itself evolve with depth

### F.2. Safe Subgame Resolving in Multiplayer Games
- **arXiv**: [2611.25981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25981) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25981)) — 2026-11-19

- **Abstract**: Poker and other games with hidden information are too large to solve in one pass. In 2p0s games, a safety theorem guarantees that subgame resolving never increases how much an adversary can gain by exploiting the resolved strategy relative to the blueprint strategy. With three or more players acting independently, no such guarantee exists yet, and heuristic resolving can increase the profile's best-response gap. We prove the first conditional safety theorem for uncorrelated multiplayer subgame resolving, a post-hoc certificate for any delivered update. The certificate flags every such update and accepts 41% (Kuhn) and 86% (Leduc) of the standard resolving updates. A resolver that keeps the blueprint whenever the certificate exceeds the tolerance delivers no harmful update. The certificate needs only one best-response pass per opponent inside the subgame, so real-time multiplayer solvers that refine a blueprint can attach it as a safety check. ### F.3. RL Can Only Reinforce What It Sees Succeed
- **arXiv**: [2611.26084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26084) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26084)) — 2026-11-19

- **Abstract**: When a familiar rule stops working, an agent has to weigh what pre-training taught it against what it now observes.
