### CTR, Recommendation, Advertising & IR

| Paper | Authors | Key Contribution |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| **Inverse Theory of Mind Modeling for Content Recommendation (IToM)** (2608.11354; *RecSys '26*) | Mengyu Chen, Feiyu Lu, Chun-Fu Chen, Lucas Vinh Tran, Jay Katukuri | **Recommender systems for generative/XR interfaces need to know *why* a user acts, not just *what* they did.** Modern recs treat observed actions as reliable preference proxies, yet interactions often reflect exploration or comparison. IToM reasons **backward** from interactions: reconstructs the decision context (what was chosen vs what alternatives were available), applies LLM-driven counterfactual reasoning to produce evidence-grounded natural-language belief statements, and synthesizes them via multi-hypothesis abductive inference into a structured user persona. On the OPeRA dataset (against ground-truth personality assessments, attitude surveys, interview-based personas) across next-action prediction / shopping-attitude alignment / Big Five inference / held-out category prediction, inferred personas **match or exceed ground-truth personas**, and multi-hypothesis reasoning is essential for personality accuracy. Includes a cross-modal VisionOS spatial-banking demo. |
| **RecSys Factory** (2608.11241) | Dongyang Ao, Kaixiang Fang, Shijie Xu | **Industrial LLM-agent platform for recommender operations — "autonomy at decision points, not over pipelines."** Frames deployment as an **autonomy–determinism–efficiency trilemma** (any two maximizable against the third). 78-day deployment across three heterogeneous Tencent recommender business lines (disjoint label semantics, A/B layer topologies, operator personas). Three deconstructions each discharge one trilemma vertex: runtime = three host-emitted event sources (Claude Code Stop hooks, corporate-IM webhooks, workflow scheduler APIs) — **zero CPU consumed during the 94% of wall-clock spent waiting** on Spark/GPU jobs; capability = a 29-file skill ecosystem (8,971 LOC) whose per-skill pitfall tables compile into a 400-entry **PitfallStore**, confining autonomy to bounded typed decision surfaces; human retained at the diagnostic-vs-execution boundary via an audit-trail card protocol (schema-validated, idempotent, replayable). **1,624 CLI-tool dispatches at a 78.6% aggregate success rate.** Onboarding-time compression observed on 2/3 lines, reported as case-study observation (no pre-platform baseline). |
| **LOFA: Learning from Online User Feedback for Shopping Agents** (2608.11604) | Haobo Zhang, Kelong Mao, Sulong Xu, Simiu Gu, Zhicheng Dou | **Shopping agents learn from real (noisy) conversational feedback, no human annotation.** Existing agents train on offline signals (user-item interactions, synthetic preferences) and waste the rich supervision in users' natural in-dialogue feedback. LOFA combines **RL over verifiable purchase outcomes** with **feedback-aware on-policy distillation** that identifies in-dialogue user directives and converts them into dense token-level supervision — jointly capturing collaborative behavioral patterns and user-specific preferences. On real-world e-commerce interaction logs it consistently improves recommendation quality, response helpfulness, and user-satisfaction alignment over strong baselines. A direct industrial-counterpart datapoint for the wiki's agentic-shopping and online-feedback threads (MemOPD, OPD from online feedback). |
| **Can Frontier LLMs Match Natively Multimodal Embeddings?** (2608.11343) | Archan Dutta, Vyanktesh Kanungo | **First direct head-to-head of native multimodal embeddings vs LLM zero-shot visual rankers.** Benchmarks **Gemini Embedding 2** (Google's March 2026 natively multimodal embedding mapping text/images/video/audio/documents into one space) against frontier-LLM ranking on hard-negative **Flickr30k** text-to-image retrieval. Finding: **GPT-4.1 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 perform on par with Gemini Embedding 2** — but once embeddings are precomputed, native multimodal embeddings are strictly better suited for low-latency serving. Relevant to the wiki's multimodal-retrieval and embedding-vs-generative-ranking lines. |
| **TRACES: A Benchmark for Epistemic Reliability in Scientific Reasoning by LLMs** (2608.11415) | Valentin Rodionov, Shamil Assylbekov | **Measures whether LLMs distinguish reliable from unreliable scientific literature — not factuality on known answers.** Probe corpus of **42 retracted, fraudulent, and pseudoscientific papers**; each probe pairs a near-verbatim preamble with a plausible study-design request, spanning five claim types (fabricated observation, pseudophysical mechanism, magical premise, legitimization bridge, cargo-cult experiment). Two scores: **IFR-a** (rejects flawed premise outright) and **IFR-i** (recognizes unreliability while engaging); **EDI** depth score measures reproduction of withheld details. Across **30 models × 10 runs**: aggregate IFR-a 0.93 ± 0.004, IFR-i 0.809 ± 0.009 — models engage with untenable premises in **95% of non-empty responses**; every model fails >71% of agentic probes, 22/30 fail >90%. Rejections concentrate on a few high-notoriety topics and disappear under matched-structure controls — read as **topic-keyed safety behavior, not robust epistemic competence**, arguing for guardrail infrastructure in scientific LLM deployment. |
| **Sci-Surf** (2608.11973) | Fang Guo, Qi Zhu, Rongcan Pei, Shuqi He, Hui Chen, Yue Zhang | **Intent-centric scientific literature discovery with feedback-driven personalization + multimodal blog-style paper digestion.** Existing academic-discovery platforms rely on static topic subscriptions / embedding similarity and short abstracts. Sci-Surf refines user intent through **LLM-based user profiling** (verbalized profiles) and generates structured summaries that synthesize textual *and* visual content from full papers. End-to-end demo; in a month-long online evaluation, **verbalized profiles gave a 10.4% average improvement in predictive alignment** with real-world user preferences. A concrete design datapoint for the wiki's intent-modeling and discovery-surface threads. |
| **LODESTAR: Reinforced Polarizer for Trustworthy Entropy Selection** (2608.11922; cross-list cs.CL→cs.IR) | Po-Jen Ko, Che-Cheng Wu, Hung-Chun Hsu, Li-Yang Chang, Chuan-Ju Wang | **A text intervention scored by the uncertainty it induces in a frozen third-party respondent.** Predicts the failure of the entropy-based RAG selection rule: a misleading passage makes a respondent *confidently wrong*, driving answer-token entropy down exactly where the signal looks most trustworthy. LODESTAR trains — once, offline, by RL, labels from gold answers + two LLM judges — a **polarizer**: a short fixed natural-language string inserted into the respondent's prompt (never its weights). On 5,008 questions under one frozen respondent: highest mean F1 of any inference-ready selector (**0.5339 vs 0.5148** for lowest-entropy), highest exact match (0.4136), highest judge score (0.6435); wins all 70 method-by-dataset F1 cells vs 14 published configurations (paired-significant), in- and out-of-domain; ablation shows the polarizer makes the respondent read the misleading passage less often (26.0% vs 30.3%). |
| **Total Recall at What Cost? Benchmarking the Serving Cost of Agentic Memory Systems** (2608.11879; cross-list cs.CL→cs.IR) | Natchanon Pollertlam, Witchayut Kornsuwannawit | **First systematic benchmark of what agentic memory costs to serve.** Compares **Mem0, Hindsight, Mastra Observational Memory** vs two reference strategies (fixed rolling window, full-transcript resubmission) across two backbones and up to 400-turn conversations, pairing every cost measurement with accuracy on **665 LoCoMo questions**. Three findings: (1) serving cost is **not predictable from conversation length/message size** — a regression tracking the references misses memory systems by 18–69%, cost driven by internal memory behavior; (2) break-even vs full transcript ranges from the first tens of turns (cheapest) to **never within 400 turns** (most expensive), highly system- and backbone-sensitive; (3) **no system wins on both axes** — accuracy spans 21–54%, and backbone choice drives cost as much as the memory system. Feeds the wiki's agent-memory cost thread (MESA's evidence-selection result pairs naturally). |

### AI, Agents & Evaluation

| Paper | Authors | Key Contribution |

| **AgonAlpha: Autonomous Alpha Discovery via Prompt Economy and Scalable Agentic Search** (2608.11250) | Weicheng Ye, Youran Sun, Xingyu Ren, Shunyao Yu, Chugang Yi, Haizhao Yang | **First alpha-mining system to search over frozen research artifacts — hypotheses, executable expressions, platform evidence, rationales, review status — rather than formulas alone.** Architecture pairs three components with a complete public evidence trail: **verified artifact search**, a **fresh-context adversarial reviewer with re-execution and veto authority** (guards against self-consistent-but-wrong candidates), and **pending-aware parallel budget allocation**. Independent deployments on **WorldQuant BRAIN** produced SPECTACULAR-grade alphas across five users and six model backends, **Fitness reaching 9.50 and Sharpe reaching 3.48**, with prompt-to-expression provenance retained for every submission. Highly relevant to the wiki's [wq101-alpha-daily](../2026-08-12/wq101-alpha-daily.md) thread — this is the arXiv-side methodology for autonomous factor discovery. |
| **FrontierFinance: A Challenging Benchmark for Measuring Frontier Intelligence of Finance Agents** (2608.11683) | Yuhao Zhang, O. Ozan Koyluoglu, Thejas Venkatesh, Richard Diehl Martinez, Vishank Bhatia, Arash Alidoust, Ashwin Paranjape | **A benchmark that captures the full investor workflow, not just data extraction.** Fully open: **220 expert-crafted queries + 11,543 source-attributed rubrics across six use cases**. Under a common harness restricted to public data: **the tool harness, not the model alone, strongly shapes quality and efficiency**; Samaya's in-house system leads at **56.0%** ahead of the strongest frontier model (Claude Fable 5, 49.2%) at ~2.2× lower cost; best open-weight (Kimi K3, 46.4%) nearly matches the best proprietary model at 4.5× lower cost. **Screening & Discovery (best 33%) and Sector/Industry/Macro (39%) remain the hardest use cases for every system.** A direct benchmark for the wiki's finance-agent thread. |
| **VAKRA: Evaluating Multi-Hop Reasoning Across APIs and Retrieval Under Tool-Use Policies** (2608.12282) | Ankita Rajaram Naik, Anupama Murthi, Benjamin Elder, Siyu Huo, Raavi Gupta, Abhinav Jain, Praveen Venkateswaran, Abdulhamid Adebayo, Danish Contractor | **Enterprise-grounded evaluation that isolates *language-mediated* failures from tool mechanics.** **8,000+ executable APIs across 62 domains** with tasks in three settings of increasing difficulty: diverse API interaction styles; multi-hop reasoning over structured APIs; multi-source reasoning under natural-language **tool-use policy constraints**. Correctness verified by re-executing predicted calls against live APIs (multiple valid paths accepted); fixed ReAct harness to isolate model capability. Even the best model scores only **70.4% on single-hop endpoint-style tasks**, drops to 50–51% on compositional APIs, degrades >50% as reasoning depth grows, and **drops to as low as 2.4% on unanswerable policy-constrained queries**. Trace analysis: failures concentrate in entity disambiguation and cross-source grounding, not tool invocation mechanics. |
| **The Sleeping Agent: What Gist-Based Context Compression Loses and Why** (2608.11775) | Nicholas E. Kyrkewood | **A diagnostic (and fix) for gist compression in long-horizon agents.** Uses **Salience-Weighted Consolidation (SWC)** — a biologically-inspired, sleep-consolidation-motivated framework scoring history by salience and applying structured gist abstraction to mid-priority tiers — as a probe across all ten LoCoMo conversations (1,935 matched text-only questions, 1,501 in the primary aggregate). Consistent task-type interaction: gist compression **substantially outperforms truncation on multi-hop reasoning and single-hop factual questions, but temporal questions remain much harder** under compression. Mechanism traced precisely: the gist prompt **preserves relational/event structure while discarding dates and times**. A one-sentence prompt modification raises temporal-expression preservation **3.05% → 62.39%** (named-entity/event preservation nearly unchanged, ×1.02/×1.11 — a precision instrument) and recovers **+0.314 judge accuracy** on temporal questions. |
| **Who Thinks Best Depends on How Long You Let Them: Budget-Dependent Rankings in LLM Evaluation** (2608.12150) | Rodrigo Guedes de Souza, Alison R. Panisson | **Model rankings are not stable across inference budgets — evaluations should be budget-conditioned.** Varying max token budget 64–4,096 (7 levels), 4 models, 3 reasoning benchmarks (56,476 inferences): (i) 3–19% of items are **non-monotone** (accuracy decreasing with more budget) even after truncation control, model-specific (cross-model overlap 6–14%); (ii) **model rankings reverse across budgets on all benchmarks** (McNemar p < 0.01); (iii) oracle analysis shows model complementarity up to **+27.8 pp**, most pronounced at constrained budgets; (iv) a budget-aware router captures only 14.1% of the oracle gap cross-domain, budget features help within-domain (+1.6–+5.7 pp) but hurt transfer (−1.2 pp). |
| **Graph-Structured Rubrics (GSR): Compiling Rubrics into Typed Evaluation Graphs for LLM Judges** (2608.12097) | Xi Chen, Jie Mu, Mo Xuan, Qun Shao | **Makes criterion *composition* explicit in rubric-based LLM evaluation.** Rubrics are usually prompt context or flat criteria — they say what to judge but leave composition implicit. GSR compiles a rubric into a **response-independent typed evaluation graph before seeing responses**: criterion nodes elicit judgments; transformation/reduction/gating operators compose them through named ports; a Readout maps the unique sink to score or preference. Malformed/type-incompatible graphs are rejected at compile time. Under GPT-OSS-120B, GSR improves exact score agreement **+0.62–6.75 pp over Prometheus-style scoring** on four pointwise datasets and achieves the numerically highest end-to-end pairwise accuracy on two preference benchmarks under native tie/abstention policies. Complements the wiki's rubric-judge thread (CalibratedRubric, Rubric Dropout). |
| **When Self-Consistency Backfires: Majority Vote Hurts the Majority of Hard Science Problems for Small LLMs** (2608.11403; *COLM 2026 WS*) | Utkarsh Bahuguna | **Pre-registered negative result on the most popular inference-time-scaling trick.** On full GPQA Diamond (198 questions), plurality majority vote **reduces per-problem accuracy on a majority of problems**: 56.6% of problems for Qwen2.5-7B, 65.7% for Llama-3-8B (from near-chance baseline). All four pre-registered confirmatory hypotheses passed. A grid oracle routing each problem to its best N ∈ {1,…,64} bounds the theoretical gain at +14 (Qwen) / +17 (Llama) accuracy points — but no verifier-free gate reaches it (plurality-agreement and token-entropy gates each move accuracy <0.002 at N=64). Mechanism: **confidence does not track correctness** on these problems (in the highest-agreement bin the plurality answer is correct only ~half the time for Qwen; for Llama that bin is less accurate than the lowest-agreement bin). Central open question flagged: reasoning-native models untested. |
| **Foresight Without Seeing (ForeWAM): Latent Futures for World Action Models** (2608.11605) | Jiakai Huang, Zhongbo Wu, Zheng Zhang, Zihan Wang, Shan You, Tao Huang | **Dynamics-conditioned direct-policy WAM exposing predictive context without decoding future video.** Explicit-future WAMs pay iterative-video-denoising inference costs; direct-policy WAMs lack an inference-time interface for predictive dynamics. **Future-KV** performs a single Video DiT prefill over the current visual latent plus stochastic future slots and reuses the layer-wise KV states throughout action denoising; **dynamics registers** are supervised by a frozen latent action teacher to capture interaction-induced transitions (object motion, contact changes, task progress). Ground-truth futures and the teacher are training-only — deployment generates **no future video**. Standard/accelerated variants reach **96.7%/96.9% LIBERO success**, standard 61.6% on LIBERO-Plus, without embodied-robot pretraining. Pairs with the daily's **RIFT** (2608.11521) in the wiki's rollout-free world-action-model line. |
| **GraphRP: Defending against Model Extraction for GNNs with Model Reprogramming** (2608.11495; *KDD 2026*) | Yan Wen, Zhenyi Wang, Heng Huang | **Proactive, structure-aware defense for graph MLaaS against model-extraction theft.** Existing defenses suffer "Euclidean bias" — transferring image-based perturbations (random noise) to graphs and degrading utility; passive watermarking can't stop theft in real time. **GraphRP** repurposes Model Reprogramming for security via a **Structure-Aware Gating Mechanism** driven by learnable topological prototypes — a dynamic "structural firewall" that preserves fidelity for benign on-manifold queries while **maximizing Fisher Information along the perturbation direction** for adversarial queries. Provides a proven **lower bound on the attacker's estimation error** increasing with the structural sensitivity of the reprogramming noise; significantly degrades both hard-label and soft-label ME attacks while preserving benign utility. |

## 🔑 Key Trends (this batch)

1. **Recommendation is moving from "what the user did" to "why the user did it" and "what the user said."** IToM infers belief-level personas via counterfactual reasoning for generative/XR interfaces; LOFA mines noisy conversational feedback into token-level supervision for shopping agents; Sci-Surf verbalizes user intent for discovery. All three treat *language of the user* and *decision context* as first-class signal — the arc from the 08-12 window's "prediction displaced by allocation" continues.
2. **Evaluation methodology keeps getting attacked from new angles — and this window it's about *budgets, confidence, and composition*.** Budget-Dependent Rankings shows model leaderboards flip with token budget; Self-Consistency-Backfires shows majority vote hurts the majority of hard problems for small models (confidence does not track correctness); TRACES shows "epistemic reliability" is really topic-keyed safety behavior; GSR makes rubric composition explicit and typed. The message across all four: standard evaluation protocols are silently confounded.
3. **LLM-agent safety/robustness is being re-engineered at the *input/context* layer, not the weights.** LODESTAR intervenes on the prompt (a learned polarizer string) to keep a frozen respondent from being confidently misled; RecSys Factory bounds agent autonomy to typed decision points with a 400-entry PitfallStore; The Sleeping Agent diagnoses exactly which context dimension gist compression destroys (temporal info) with a one-line fix; VAKRA exposes where enterprise tool-use fails (language-mediated grounding, policy adherence) — all controllable without retraining.
4. **The finance-agent stack has arrived as a first-class benchmark space.** FrontierFinance (full investor workflow, harness-dominant results, open weights near par) and AgonAlpha (verified-artifact alpha mining on WorldQuant BRAIN, Fitness 9.50/Sharpe 3.48) are the most concrete instantiation yet of the wiki's investment-domain thread — and AgonAlpha's adversarial-reviewer + verified-evidence design is directly reusable for any agentic factor/stock pipeline.
5. **Rollout-free world action models converge on KV-state reuse.** ForeWAM reuses a single Video-DiT prefill's KV through action denoising (96.7–96.9% LIBERO, no future video at deploy); the daily's RIFT constructs the future K/V cache in one backbone pass (98.8% LIBERO, −68–89% latency). The two are complementary attacks on the same problem: future conditioning without rollout cost.
