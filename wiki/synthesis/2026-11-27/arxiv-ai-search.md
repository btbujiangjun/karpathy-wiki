### 1) Mixture-of-Loops Transformer
- **arXiv:** [2611.34923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923v1) (cs.LG, cs.CL; submitted 2026-11-25; code: github.com/hustvl/MoLT)
- **Authors:** Lianghui Zhu, Shijie Wang, Xinggang Wang, Jinze Bai, Bo Wang, Xiang Bai
- **Institution/Company:** Huazhong University of Science & Technology; ByteDance Seed; University of Toronto
- **Abstract:** Looped Transformers boost expressivity by reusing shared layers across loops, yet at the cost of efficiency. Pushing their expressivity-efficiency frontier requires rethinking the core mechanism connecting adjacent loops: What should be passed as a Transformer loops? We introduce Mixture-of-Loops Transformer (MoLT), whose Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA) uses keys and values (KV) as memory for selective reuse across depths and loops. Under loop residual, MoLT-R retains the residual stream and reads local KV across loops for expressivity. Under loop KV, MoLT-KV instead restarts from embeddings and reads current-loop local KV with preceding-loop memory KV at earlier positions for efficiency. MoLT-KV eliminates same-position cross-loop dependence and, paired with loop forcing for parallel training, unlocks single-traversal Mixture-of-Loops (MoL) decoding beyond the training loop horizon. To balance efficiency and quality, we introduce U-shaped MoLT (U-MoLT), which separates memory construction from prediction readout and skips unused decoder traversals without changing predictions. In 400B-token pretraining, U-MoLT outperforms OLMo3 across 700M/1.5B dense and 3B MoE models, achieving 1.7x validation cross-entropy (CE) token efficiency at 700M while improving 3B MoE 10-domain perplexity by 0.75 and 10-task accuracy by 2.1 percentage points. In 100B-token continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base, our U-MoLT adaptation lowers CE on all 11 domains over the matched baseline with 1.4x mean token efficiency. At inference, U-MoLT with MoL-Hybrid-2 matches non-looped OLMo3 latency at 996.6 vs. 1,000.6 ms on S-size (~113M) models.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA):** reuses KV memory selectively across depths and loops instead of only passing residuals between loops.
  - **Two loop connectivity modes:** MoLT-R (loop residual + local KV reads, expressivity) and MoLT-KV (restart from embeddings + preceding-loop memory KV, efficiency; no same-position cross-loop dependence).
  - **Loop forcing + single-traversal MoL decoding:** parallel training unlocks decoding in one traversal, beyond the training loop horizon.
  - **U-shaped MoLT (U-MoLT):** separates memory construction from prediction readout; skips unused decoder traversals without changing predictions.
  - Reported: 1.7x token efficiency at 700M, +2.1 pt accuracy / −0.75 perplexity at 3B MoE in 400B-token pretraining; U-MoLT adaptation of Qwen3.5-0.8B improves CE on all 11 domains; inference latency matches non-looped OLMo3.

### 2) Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces for Hard Math Problems
- **arXiv:** [2611.34928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34928v1) (cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG; submitted 2026-11-25; accepted: NeurIPS 2026 Workshops per arXiv comment)
- **Authors:** Lukas Aichberger, Adam Golinski, Arno Blaas, Arwen Bradley, Jacob Springer, Mehrdad Farajtabar, Michael Kirchhof, Sinead Williamson
- **Institution/Company:** 1 Johannes Kepler University Linz (work done during internship at Apple Inc.); 2 Apple Inc.; 3 Carnegie Mellon University (work done during internship at Apple Inc.). Mapping per PDF superscripts: Lukas Aichberger¹; Adam Golinski, Arno Blaas, Arwen Bradley, Mehrdad Farajtabar, Michael Kirchhof, Sinead Williamson²; Jacob Springer³.
- **Abstract:** Post-training large language models (LLMs) for mathematical reasoning often relies on accepted traces whose final answers pass a verifier. For hard math problems, however, such traces can be rare under independent Monte Carlo (MC) sampling. To address this, we propose Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces (DART), a trace-mining method that starts from a rejected trace, decomposes it into reasoning steps, and ranks them by position-weighted uncertainty. It then generates deliberately distinct alternative candidates for highly ranked steps using reference-answer guidance and a meta-thought before completing the resulting traces. On greedy-unsolved DeepMath-103K problems, DART recovers 37% more problems than MC sampling at a matched number of generated traces. Its estimated generation cost per recovered problem is nearly identical, while its cost per accepted trace is 30% lower. Post-training Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on traces mined by DART achieves the highest point estimate in five of six evaluations across MATH-500 and AIME benchmarks. These results demonstrate that revisiting rejected traces with DART can expand problem coverage and provide effective post-training data.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Rejected-trace reuse:** starts from traces that fail the verifier instead of discarding them.
  - **Position-weighted uncertainty ranking** of reasoning steps to find where alternatives help most.
  - **Deliberately distinct alternative generation** with reference-answer guidance + a meta-thought before completing traces.
  - Reported: +37% problems recovered on greedy-unsolved DeepMath-103K at matched trace count; ~equal cost per recovered problem; −30% cost per accepted trace; best point estimate in 5/6 MATH-500/AIME evals after post-training Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

### 3) Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators
- **arXiv:** [2611.34934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934v1) (cs.LG, cs.AI; submitted 2026-11-25)
- **Authors:** Charlie Griffin, Oliver Makins, Asa Cooper Stickland, Alessandro Abate, Yarin Gal, Abby D'Cruz, Xander Davies
- **Institution/Company:** *Not stated on arXiv.* Case study evaluates an ex-production **OpenAI** monitor and a held-out **GPT-5.4** target monitor; the paper also surveys control-style evaluations from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and OpenAI system reports (these are not author affiliations).
- **Abstract:** AI monitors, classifiers that flag dangerous behaviour by AI agents, are often evaluated against transcripts generated by attack policies acting as proxies for dangerous behaviour in deployment. We evaluate an ex-production OpenAI monitor as a case study and find that measured false negative rate is highly sensitive to both the method used to generate attack policies and the level of optimisation applied within that method. This rate ranged from 0.5% to 20.8% across optimisation levels of a single method (reinforcement learning), and from 6.4% to 23.0% across methods at their best observed optimisation level (optimised prompting vs. transcript editing). Every attack policy was developed against proxy monitors only: all reported results show attacks transferring to the held-out GPT-5.4 target monitor. Our results suggest that monitor evasion evaluations can easily mislead: an evaluation using only basic attack methods may give false confidence that a monitor is strong. The sensitivity to attack-policy choice raises a problem: interpreting any particular result as evidence about deployment performance requires justification. The transcripts produced by attack policies are proxies for the dangerous behaviour a monitor must flag in deployment. These proxies can introduce visible artefacts, such as reasoning about a "side task" absent from the instructions shown to the monitor, that affect monitor judgements. Given the sensitivity above, the choice of proxy can change an evaluation's conclusion. Reading these results as evidence about deployment therefore requires a separate legitimacy argument for why the proxies are informative, including any justification of conservativeness. Making convincing arguments remains an open problem.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Sensitivity finding:** measured false-negative rate (FNR) of an AI safety monitor depends strongly on attack-policy method *and* optimisation level (0.5→20.8% within one method; 6.4→23.0% across methods).
  - **Transfer:** attacks developed only against proxy monitors transfer to a held-out GPT-5.4 target monitor.
  - **Proxy-legitimacy argument:** deployment conclusions require justification that attack-policy proxies are informative (and conservative); artifacts like side-task reasoning skew judgments.
  - **Methodology critique:** narrowly-optimised evaluations can falsely suggest a monitor is robust; recommends broader attack-method × optimisation-level sweeps.

### 4) DFly2: Turbocharging Long-Context Speculative Decoding by Sharing the Target's Sparsity
- **arXiv:** [2611.34776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34776v1) (cs.CL; submitted 2026-11-25; 20 pages, 8 figures; code: github.com/Tencent/AngelSpec)
- **Authors:** Hong Liu, Rui Cen, Junhan Shi, Lin Niu, Runzhi Fan, Zhaojing Zhou, Haodi Lei, Jiebin Zhang, Saiyong Yang, Junqiang Zheng, Zhenghai Xue, Guanghua Yu, Jianchen Zhu
- **Institution/Company:** Tencent Inc. (Tencent Hy)
- **Abstract:** To avoid the quadratic cost of dense attention over long contexts, models such as DeepSeek-V4 and Hy4 have turned natively sparse, and speculative decoding, which matters most where decoding is slowest, increasingly runs on them. Their drafters, however, still keep a context cache of their own, over the whole prompt or a sliding window. Beyond memory, such a cache costs prefill time, prefix-cache hits and decode concurrency. Drafters that read the target's cache avoid one of their own, but settle only which cache, not which positions, and still read everything or a recent window. Neither rule is made for the query at hand, and computing a selection per query in the drafter would cost more as the context grows. Yet a natively sparse target already picks positions for each query, over a cache it keeps anyway. Both of the drafter's choices are therefore already made, and paid for, by the target: its cache and its selection. We measure how far one selection carries: made a step early, it still covers close to nine tenths of what each later token selects on DS-V4.1-Flash. We present DFly2, a block-parallel drafter that shares the target's sparsity. Each draft layer reads the KV its paired target layer cached (KV sharing), at the positions that layer's indexer selected, a fixed number anywhere in the context (Index sharing). DFly2 thus runs no scoring pass and keeps no cache of its own. On DS-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash and Hy4 flash, whose sparse attention and architecture differ, it accepts more tokens per step than DFlash and DSpark trained on the same recipe, over general, long-context and agentic benchmarks. Its margin over the stronger baseline widens from 3.9%-10.5% on general workloads to 10.0%-31.5% on long-context and agentic ones. At high concurrency on DS-V4.1-Flash, at matched per-user decode speed, its throughput is 62% and 81% higher on two 64K sets.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **KV sharing:** each draft layer reads the KV cache of its paired target layer.
  - **Index sharing:** drafts attend at the positions the target's own indexer selected; no drafter-side scoring pass, no drafter-side cache.
  - **Selection-persistence finding:** a selection made one step early still covers ~90% of what later tokens select.
  - Reported vs DFlash/DSpark (same recipe): +3.9–10.5% token acceptance on general, +10.0–31.5% on long-context/agentic; +62%/+81% throughput at 64K high concurrency; works across natively sparse targets (DS-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash, Hy4 flash).

### 5) Repairing the Trustworthiness of Distilled Language Models
- **arXiv:** [2611.34831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34831v1) (cs.CL, cs.AI; submitted 2026-11-25)
- **Authors:** Kumar Shubham, Devendra Vyas, Vidushi Agarwal, Jayin Khanna, Prathosh AP
- **Institution/Company:** Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru (K. Shubham, J. Khanna, P. AP); two Independent Researchers (D. Vyas, V. Agarwal)
- **Abstract:** Knowledge distillation enables the deployment of large language models through smaller student models, but the compression process can introduce regressions in trustworthiness even when downstream performance is preserved. Repairing such failures is challenging because the original distillation corpus is often unavailable, while direct preference optimization may alter capabilities inherited from the teacher. We propose a targeted repair framework based on the proximal Bregman response function (PBRF) that addresses both limitations. We estimate the curvature required for PBRF using a surrogate proxy dataset, removing dependence on the original transfer corpus, and extend the framework to preference-based supervision through an odds-ratio objective. This enables the use of preferred and dispreferred response pairs while constraining changes to the distilled model. Our results show that the proposed approach improves targeted trustworthiness behaviors for both pre-training and post-training models while largely preserving downstream performance, providing a practical alternative to re-distillation or unconstrained preference optimization. Our experiment results show that across different distilled models, the trustworthy regression can be improved by up to 8% with less than 1% decrement in perplexity.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **PBRF-based targeted repair:** proximal Bregman response function to undo distillation-induced trustworthiness regressions while keeping capability.
  - **Surrogate-proxy curvature:** estimates PBRF curvature without access to the original distillation corpus.
  - **Odds-ratio preference objective:** preference-based supervision (preferred/dispreferred pairs) that constrains drift of the distilled model (vs unconstrained DPO).
  - Reported: up to +8% trustworthiness recovery with <1% perplexity decrease; works for pre- and post-training distilled models; no re-distillation needed.

### 6) Knowledge-Base Gradient Descent: Self-Structuring Learning through Question Answering
- **arXiv:** [2611.34841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34841v1) (cs.AI, cs.LG; submitted 2026-11-25)
- **Authors:** Masahiro Kato
- **Institution/Company:** The University of Tokyo
- **Abstract:** We consider the problem of constructing knowledge bases for applications based on large language models, in which the structure of a knowledge base determines how an application can retrieve and connect the information needed to answer a question. We study Self-Structuring Learning, a setting in which questions and expected answers guide the construction of a persistent knowledge base whose editable structure can be a graph schema or the organization of linked knowledge documents. We propose Knowledge-Base Gradient Descent, which diagnoses discrepancies between expected answers and responses obtained through the current knowledge base. At each round, the method proposes a structural edit, rebuilds the knowledge base, and adopts the edit when the learning loss decreases. The retained structure is evaluated on other questions about the same sources. By randomizing over candidate edits, we express the loss change of an edit as a derivative with respect to the probability of selecting it, and we evaluate joint edits as single candidates because their changes interact. Schema Descent instantiates the method for graph schemas. In experiments on large documents, each a bundle of research papers, the updated knowledge cards and graph schemas obtain higher mean Answer F1 on held-out generated questions than their initial structures, with schema gains in every document. On human questions, mean F1 rises for schemas and falls for cards: the updated structures serve the kind of question they were learned from.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Self-Structuring Learning:** questions + expected answers drive construction of a persistent, editable knowledge base (graph schema or linked-document organization).
  - **Knowledge-Base Gradient Descent:** propose structural edit → rebuild → accept if loss decreases; retained structure validated on held-out questions about the same sources.
  - **Edit-gradient trick:** randomizing over candidate edits yields a derivative w.r.t. edit-selection probability; joint edits evaluated as single candidates (interactions).
  - **Schema Descent** variant for graph schemas; reported higher mean Answer F1 on held-out generated questions; schema gains on every document; noted generalization caveat on human questions.

### 7) LazUnirec: Unifying Retrieval and Ranking in Multi-scenario Industrial Recommendation
- **arXiv:** [2611.32590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32590v1) (cs.IR; submitted 2026-11-24)
- **Authors:** Xiaodong Li, Junfeng Zhang, Shichao Nie, Ziyue Guo, Yiming Liu, Xinxin Xu, Xurong Li, Jinyuan Dang, Zhanming Ou, Jianan Yan, Tao Luo, Xiaoyi Zeng
- **Institution/Company:** Alibaba International Digital Commerce Group, Beijing, China
- **Abstract:** Modern industrial recommendation systems typically divide retrieval and ranking into two separate cascaded stages, leading to inconsistent optimization objectives, redundant encoding of user sequences, and a waste of computational resources. Previous efforts have investigated using Transformers to model both retrieval and ranking simultaneously. However, these studies still face two major limitations: (1) Since retrieval and ranking share a single Transformer backbone, the gradient conflict arises when the gradient directions of the two tasks are inconsistent. (2) When gradient conflict occurs, the model cannot determine a reliable direction for gradient updates. To this end, we propose a Unified Retrieval and Ranking approach for Multi-scenario Industrial Recommendation, termed as LazUnirec. Specifically, we construct task-shared and task-private parameters on the Transformer backbone to mitigate the gradient conflict. Wherein, for the attention components, we propose LoRA-modulated attention, enabling retrieval and ranking to share attention parameters while preserving task-private parameters. For the feed-forward networks (FFN), we present task-adaptive sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) to mitigate gradient conflict by constructing task-shared and task-private experts. Moreover, we propose an uncertainty estimation that evaluates the gradient reliability of the retrieval and ranking, thereby guiding the model toward a more reliable gradient update direction when gradient conflict occurs. In addition, knowledge distillation from ranking to retrieval further enables cross-task knowledge transfer. Extensive offline experiments and online A/B testing demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of LazUnirec. LazUnirec has been deployed in online multi-scenario industrial recommendation system and currently delivers significant gains for the platform.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Unified retrieval+ranking** on one Transformer backbone (end-to-end, multi-scenario), removing the cascaded two-stage mismatch.
  - **LoRA-modulated attention:** shared attention parameters + task-private adapters for retrieval vs ranking.
  - **Task-adaptive sparse MoE in FFN:** task-shared + task-private experts to mitigate gradient conflict.
  - **Gradient-reliability uncertainty estimation** steering updates when retrieval/ranking gradients conflict.
  - **Ranking→retrieval knowledge distillation** for cross-task transfer; deployed in production multi-scenario rec; offline + online A/B gains (author-reported).

### 8) Speculative Decoding for Semantic-ID-Based Generative Recommendations
- **arXiv:** [2611.32855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32855v1) (cs.IR; submitted 2026-11-24; 33 pages, 19 figures; method name: **SIDECAR**; accepted preprint)
- **Authors:** Harshal A. Chaudhari
- **Institution/Company:** Etsy Inc., Brooklyn, NY, USA
- **Abstract:** Generative recommender systems with Semantic IDs (SIDs) represent each item as a short sequence of L discrete tokens and use a sequence model to predict the next item's tokens from a user's history. Industry-scale deployments typically use these models to retrieve multiple candidates per request with constrained beam search. However, this incurs a fixed cost of L sequential model calls per request, and accuracy degrades as the SID generator becomes less sharp, without a guarantee on how close the output is to the exact top-K. Production systems therefore cannot detect degraded retrieval for individual requests. We propose SIDECAR, which uses approximate draft candidates and a tree-attention pass to score the entire draft in a single model call, memory permitting. This pass bounds the probability of every unexplored SID prefix and certifies the exact top-K if the draft contains them. Otherwise, a branch-and-bound fallback expands only the parts of the trie that can still change the output, reaching certification within at most L calls for any draft. Moreover, under a budget of M tokens per call and Y calls, which for Y = L matches the resources of a beam search of width M, SIDECAR announces a factor 1 + δ within which its list will be exact at every rank. Depending on model sharpness, SIDECAR returns the exact top-K using a fraction of beam search's calls or memory. Even when the budget is too small to recover the exact top-K, SIDECAR, unlike beam search, reports the announced factor, allowing the serving system to stop early once a quality target or latency limit is reached.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **SIDECAR:** speculative decoding for Semantic-ID (SID) generative recommendation; draft candidates + single tree-attention scoring pass.
  - **Exactness certificate:** bounds every unexplored SID prefix; certifies exact top-K when draft contains it; branch-and-bound fallback ≤ L calls.
  - **Announced approximation factor 1 + δ** at every rank under call/token budgets; early stop once quality/latency target met.
  - **Detectable degradation:** unlike beam search, reports an exactness factor per request in production.

### 9) From Head to Tail: LLM-Augmented Sequential Recommendation and Trade-off Optimization
- **arXiv:** [2611.31052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31052v1) (cs.IR, cs.AI; submitted 2026-11-23; accepted at GRAIL 2026 @ CIKM 2026 per arXiv comment)
- **Authors:** Adeep Hande, Shaghayegh Agah, Neeraj Sharma, Karina Halevy, Sardar Hamidian
- **Institution/Company:** 1 Applied AI Research, Comcast; 2 Department of Computer Science, Carnegie Mellon University
- **Abstract:** Recommender systems exhibit popularity bias, often under-serving long-tail and cold-start items with sparse interaction data. We propose a group-specific data augmentation approach that leverages LLMs to generate synthetic interactions, formulated as a constrained optimization problem, maximizing group-specific NDCG@20 and coverage while bounding overall NDCG@20 degradation within an acceptable threshold. Items are partitioned by interaction frequency to find the optimal augmentation strategy for each group. We further study combining the augmentation across groups simultaneously and find that while individual optimal strategies navigate the multi-group augmentation design, they are not simply additive. We further ablate on the LLM-based augmentation itself, comparing LLM scales, augmentation configuration under token constraints, finding that augmentation quality scales with contextual richness, albeit at a proportionally higher cost.   - **Group-specific LLM data augmentation** for sequential recommendation, partitioning items by interaction frequency (head vs long-tail/cold-start).
  - **Constrained multi-objective optimization:** maximize group NDCG@20 + coverage s.t. bounded overall NDCG@20 degradation; Pareto-frontier analysis; per-group optima are **not additive** across groups.
  - **Ablations:** LLM scale, augmentation config under token constraints; quality scales with contextual richness at higher cost.
  - Reported on SASRec: >100%/+60% group NDCG@20 on extreme/regular long-tail (industrial PRD), +40%/+25% (MovieLens-32M), better coverage, no significant overall degradation.

### 10) Towards Realistic Evaluation of Uncertainty Quantification for LLM-Based Recommender Systems
- **arXiv:** [2611.33036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33036v1) (cs.IR, cs.CL, cs.LG, stat.ML; submitted 2026-11-24; **published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR) 11/2026**; OpenReview GXDe6REdu3)
- **Authors:** Hao Wu, Sipeng Chen, Weikai Li, Tianyi Chen, Qiancheng Yang, Hang Zhou, Yizhou Sun, Wei Wang, Xiao Luo
- **Institution/Company:** University of Wisconsin–Madison; Northwestern University; University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA); University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (UNC)
- **Abstract:** Large language models (LLMs) provide a flexible interface for recommendation systems. However, their issued rankings require reliable instance-level uncertainty monitoring. In this scenario, comparing uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods becomes challenging because LLM rankers lack a standard item-classification head. Their scores may depend on option labels, prompts, generation procedures, and the available user and item evidence. Existing methods derive uncertainty from different sources and apply it at different stages of the recommendation process. Some methods evaluate an issued ranking, whereas others use uncertainty to change the ranking itself, making direct comparison difficult. Here we introduce a fixed-ranking benchmark which evaluates eight heterogeneous UQ signals as record-level monitors under a shared output contract. We evaluate these signals across four recommendation datasets and two LLM backbones under a shared fixed-ranking protocol. From the results, we draw three main observations. First, UQ method ordering changes across backbones even on the same reference instances. Second, ranking-aligned Top10Mass provides the strongest scalar Error@10 detection in several evaluation conditions, reflecting effective cutoff-aligned monitoring, while its advantage narrows for popularity-tail targets. Third, scalar UQ signals are weak at identifying which control has the larger clipped NDCG@10 difference, whereas source-specific signals, including Perturbation Rank Instability and simple listwise distances, provide stronger paired-control agreement, reflecting their sensitivity to the two controlled evidence sources. These findings motivate us to evaluate ranking-failure detection, robustness across recommendation conditions, and responses to controlled evidence changes as distinct properties of UQ.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Fixed-ranking UQ benchmark** isolating UQ from ranking changes; 8 heterogeneous UQ signals as record-level monitors under a shared output contract; 4 datasets × 2 LLM backbones.
  - **Method ordering is not stable across backbones** — UQ quality is model-dependent.
  - **Top10Mass** (ranking-aligned) strongest scalar Error@10 detector in several conditions; weaker on popularity-tail targets.
  - Scalar UQ weak at paired-control discrimination; **Perturbation Rank Instability** + listwise distances stronger; separates ranking-failure detection / robustness / controlled-evidence response as distinct evaluation axes.

### 11) Weight Coding: Evolving Web-Scale Recommenders through Direct Weight Edits with LLM Agents

### 12) Grounding User Preferences in Shared Multimodal Concepts for Recommendation

- **Abstract:** We study the sample complexity of learning robust coarse correlated equilibria (CCE) in finite-horizon, general-sum, distributionally robust Markov games with known rewards and a generative model of the nominal transitions. Each player evaluates a policy by its worst-case return over total-variation (TV) balls centered at the nominal transition distribution of every stage, state, and joint action; this is the standard (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set. Existing generative-model guarantees for this model scale with the number ∏_i A_i of joint actions, where A_i is the number of actions of player i. Since this number grows exponentially with the number of players, these guarantees suffer from the curse of multiagency. We design an algorithm whose sample complexity depends on the action sets only through the largest individual action-set size A* := max_i A_i. The algorithm uses Õ(S A* H^5 D^4 / ε^4) transition samples and, with probability at least 1 − δ, returns a Markov policy that is an ε-robust CCE from every initial state. Here S is the number of states, H is the horizon, and D := max_i min{H, 1/ρ_i}, where ρ_i is the TV radius of player i. Two ingredients make this possible: a plug-in estimate of the robust backup whose mean error does not depend on the number of states, and a single batch of samples per round that serves as bandit feedback for all players at once. The result shows that the dependence on ∏_i A_i can be avoided without modifying the (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set.
- **Key innovations:**
  - **Curse-of-multiagency break:** sample complexity independent of joint-action product ∏_i A_i; depends only on largest individual action set A* = max_i A_i.
  - **Robust CCE learning** in general-sum finite-horizon Markov games, (s,a)-rectangular TV-ball uncertainty; ε-robust CCE from every initial state.
  - **Bound Õ(S·A*·H^5·D^4/ε^4)**, D = max_i min{H, 1/ρ_i}; horizon/uncertainty-radius dependence made explicit.
  - **Two techniques:** plug-in robust backup estimate with state-count-independent mean error; single per-round sample batch serving as bandit feedback for all players simultaneously. (No modification of (s,a)-rectangular set.)
