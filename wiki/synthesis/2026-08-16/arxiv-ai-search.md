## 1. Ads, Experimentation, Pricing & Retail

### 1.1 Fast A/B/n Testing: Exact Multi-Policy Comparison via Tree-Coupled Feedback Sharing

- **arXiv**: [2608.12831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12831) (cs.LG / cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-13) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Yuxiao Wen
- **Institution**: Not stated.
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Online platforms increasingly compare many adaptive decision policies — ranking systems, recommendation algorithms, pricing rules, and language-model agents — while each reward-bearing interaction can be costly or risky. A direct A/B/n design gives each of J policies its own horizon-T trajectory and therefore uses JT outcomes. The paper introduces **Tree-Coupled A/B Testing (TCAB)**, an exact feedback-sharing design for arbitrary history-dependent contextual-bandit policies. At each round, a predictable tree connects current policy histories; every parent–child context–action law is maximally coupled, and one reward is shared within each component of matched tree edges. Every policy retains exactly its standalone finite-horizon trajectory law even though the policies are deliberately dependent. If D_{e,t} records a mismatch on tree edge e at round t, the number of reward queries satisfies the pathwise identity N(T)=T+ΣD_{e,t} — equal to T plus cumulative tree-edge total variation in expectation. This cost is conditionally optimal among exact edge-local designs on the selected tree, and a current-round minimum-spanning tree is myopically optimal among tree designs. For fixed J, sublinear pseudo-regret of every policy and almost-sure uniqueness of the oracle action imply E[N(T)]=T+o(T), versus JT for independent runs. Finite-sample variance bounds for pairwise policy contrasts are also obtained. Experiments on reward-model evaluation, multiple-choice language-model evaluation, and adaptive search policies demonstrate substantial improvements in the cost–precision frontier.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Exact (not approximate) feedback sharing across arbitrary history-dependent contextual-bandit policies; (2) a pathwise cost identity tying reward queries to tree-edge total variation, with a conditional-optimality and myopic-MST result; (3) sublinear (T+o(T)) total cost vs. JT for independent runs; (4) practical wins on LLM evaluation and adaptive search workloads — directly relevant to cheap multi-policy A/B testing in ads/rec systems.

### 1.2 Demand Transfer Estimation at Scale via Restricted Logit Modeling

- **arXiv**: [2608.12680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12680) (cs.LG / cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-13; accepted at IEEE ICMLA 2026 Main Conference) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Lakshya Garg, Deep Narayan Mishra, Swapnil Yadav, Haoan Wang, Sujal Alugubelli, Karthik Kumaran, Anupriya Sharma
- **Institution**: Not stated; the author group (Mishra, Kumaran, Sharma) overlaps with the Lines-and-Ladders retail pricing paper in this batch — retail/e-commerce industry affiliation likely Amazon (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Item demand forecasting is integral to store assortment optimization. Existing literature learns a customer choice model and evaluates expected demand per assortment proposal, which is inefficient for large item universes with many categories — a separate forecast per possible assortment. The alternative: forecast item demand independently, then adjust with coefficients that capture relations between item demand and the availability of similar items on the shelf. Central to this approach is estimating **Demand Transfer (DT) coefficients** — the percent of a target item's demand that is redirected to each other item in the universe if the target item is removed from the shelf. The authors introduce an approach computing DT coefficients on **large item universes (1M+ items)** using **Restricted Logit modeling**, and show that when reasonable substitution-behavior assumptions hold, the procedure accurately estimates underlying DT coefficients and improves demand forecasting, validated on historical transaction data across multiple locations and categories.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A scalable restricted-logit procedure for DT coefficients on million-scale assortments (a practical bottleneck for assortment optimization); (2) explicit substitution-behavior assumptions that characterize when the estimator is accurate; (3) measurable forecasting gains on real retail transaction data.

## 2. LLM Post-Training, Reasoning & RL

### 2.1 Scaling Automatic Research Agents via World Models (WMRL)

- **arXiv**: [2608.12564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12564) (cs.LG; submitted 2026-08-12) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Xiyuan Yang, Sheikh Sarwar, Jingru Cheng, Zhan Shi, Duanshun Li, Huiyuan Chen, Haiyang Zhang, Chenlei Guo, Jingrui He, Zhenyu Liao
- **Institution**: Not stated; Chenlei Guo and Huiyuan Chen are affiliated with Amazon, Jingrui He with UIUC, Zhan Shi with CMU (tentative, multi-org).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Automating empirical research is a long-standing AI direction, and recent AutoResearch agents bring it within reach as LLMs independently implement solutions and learn from execution outcomes; post-training (especially RL) plays a central role. The paper identifies a fundamental tension when scaling RL for these agents: the two components of every AutoResearch trajectory — agent generation and environment execution — scale in very different manners, since all generation shares compute through batching while each execution occupies an exclusive sandbox and real machine time. Environment execution dominates training cost and becomes the bottleneck as trajectories grow. They propose **World Model RL (WMRL)**, which replaces environment execution with a world model to remove this bottleneck. Because the world model can be imperfect — its rewards corrupted by bias and noise — WMRL adds two mitigations: **Online Debiasing** and **Inverse-Variance Denoising**, which offset bias and suppress noise respectively. Theoretically both mitigations strictly improve the convergence guarantee. Empirically WMRL accelerates training **3–4×** across tasks and agent scales while exceeding standard RL baselines; the post-trained **4B and 9B agents outperform much larger open-weight agents of 48B and 120B** on held-out benchmarks. WMRL also transfers to post-training embodied VLA policies.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Identifies the generation-vs-execution scaling asymmetry as the AutoResearch RL bottleneck and replaces execution with a world model; (2) two principled mitigations (Online Debiasing + Inverse-Variance Denoising) with strict convergence improvements; (3) 3–4× training speedup with better held-out performance; (4) strong small-agent results (4B/9B > 48B/120B) and transfer to VLA post-training.

### 2.2 From Refuse to Richness: Rubric Rewards for Long-Form Hallucination Reinforcement Learning

- **arXiv**: [2608.12337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12337) (cs.CL; v1 submitted 2026-06-03, in Fri Aug 14 listing) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Yudong Wang, Zhe Yang, Wenhan Ma, Rang Li, Qibin Yang, Weimin Xiong, Jiangshan Duo, Liang Zhao, Zhifang Sui
- **Institution**: Not stated; Zhifang Sui is affiliated with Peking University (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Rewards that penalize unsupported claims can improve grounding in long-form generation, but they can also teach models to answer less — the **refusal-to-richness trade-off** in long-form hallucination RL. Instead of global richness proxies (length, claim count, detail, pairwise relevance), each question is represented with a **key-point rubric** specifying the required and optional information a useful answer should cover; rubrics define coverage directly and are used both for evaluation and as reward signals. Across grounding-only, proxy-based, rubric-only, and combined rewards, the authors find a stable trade-off: strict grounding rewards improve support but suppress coverage, while unconstrained rubric rewards improve coverage but weaken grounding. A **soft combination of grounding, rubric coverage, and relevance** gives the best balance, improving in-distribution support while transferring better to out-of-distribution checklist tasks than either grounding-only or rubric-only rewards.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Names and measures the refusal-to-richness trade-off in hallucination RL; (2) rubrics as direct, decomposable coverage reward signals; (3) a soft-combined reward that beats grounding-only and rubric-only on both support and OOD transfer.

### 2.3 SSPO: Structure-Aware Similarity-Weighted Preference Optimization for Neural Combinatorial Optimization

- **arXiv**: [2608.12443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12443) (stat.ML / cs.AI / cs.LG / math.OC; submitted 2026-08-12) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Yuanyu Li, Jintao Xu, Zijiang Liu, Yongzhi Qi, Ningxuan Kang, Jianshen Zhang, Wei Qi, Chen Xie, Zuo-Jun Max Shen
- **Institution**: Not stated; Zuo-Jun Max Shen is affiliated with University of Hong Kong, Wei Qi with UC Berkeley (tentative). The trained EFL policy is **deployed in production at JD.com** (stated in abstract).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Neural combinatorial optimization (NCO) relies on parallel solution sampling for training, yet existing methods fail to fully exploit the information latent in a co-sampled solution group. Preference-optimization methods anchor on the single best solution and discard fine-grained quality and structural signal from all other peers — **gradient signal polarization**. Mean-based baselines instead weight peers uniformly, so structurally near-identical peers flood the baseline with redundant information and keep gradient variance high — **baseline redundancy**. **SSPO** (Structure-Aware Similarity-Weighted Preference Optimization) scores all B sampled solutions jointly through a **dissimilarity-weighted leave-one-out baseline**: structurally distinct peers receive higher weight, resolving both failures in a single mechanism. The baseline uses zero-parameter, problem-adaptive solution embeddings built from the encoder's existing node representations. On TSP, EFL, and JSP benchmarks SSPO shows consistent gains over best-anchor and uniform-weight baselines; a direct comparison against uniform RLOO on TSP and EFL confirms structure-aware weighting is the primary driver. The SSPO-trained EFL policy is deployed in a production facility-location system at JD.com.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A single mechanism (dissimilarity-weighted LOO baseline) resolving both gradient polarization and baseline redundancy; (2) zero-parameter structure embeddings from the encoder; (3) consistent wins over best-anchor/uniform baselines with an ablation isolating structure-aware weighting; (4) production deployment at JD.com.

### 2.4 Position: Reasoning is a Learnable Rule-Based Process

- **Authors**: Rachel Lawrence, Jacqueline Maasch
- **Institution**: Not stated; Jacqueline Maasch is affiliated with UC San Diego (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Autonomous reasoning is among the most motivating topics in AI, historically the purview of symbolic AI while recent advances emerge from deep probabilistic generative models. The authors contend that the generative-AI community has not converged on operational definitions for reasoning and often implicitly rejects the historical treatment of the topic in logic and verifiable automated reasoning. ## 3. Sequential Modeling, Architectures & Theory

### 3.1 MARCH: Scaling Recurrent Memory with Content-Routed State Anchors

- **arXiv**: [2608.12435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12435) (cs.LG; submitted 2026-08-12) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Ming Zhang, Kaisen Yang, Shu Yu, Ermo Hua, Ning Ding, Xia Hu, Bowen Zhou, Chaochao Lu, Youbang Sun
- **Institution**: Not stated; Ning Ding (Tsinghua), Xia Hu (Rice), Bowen Zhou (Shanghai Jiao Tong), Chaochao Lu (UCL) are plausible affiliations (tentative, multi-org).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Transformers owe their strong long-context retrieval to token-level memory that grows with context length, at the cost of quadratic training compute and a linearly growing KV cache. Recurrent alternatives compress history into a fixed-size state for efficient decoding but underperform on recall-intensive tasks because earlier associations get overwritten and only recent context is retained. **MARCH** (Memory-Anchor Routing across Context History) scales state-space models beyond a fixed-size dimension while staying computationally efficient on long sequences: it periodically caches cumulative recurrent-state checkpoints as **state anchors**, associates each anchor with a compact content-conditioned **anchor key**, and maintains a memory bank that grows with context length, giving a controllable trade-off between historical resolution and memory cost. Each token produces an anchor query attending all causally available state anchors, with output computed as an attention-style aggregation over historical anchors along the current state. After standard pretraining, MARCH consistently outperforms multiple linear-attention variants on commonsense reasoning, LongBench, and in-context retrieval, showing content-routed state caching substantially strengthens recurrent long-range memory while preserving the native computation path.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Content-routed state anchors turning a fixed-size recurrent state into a growing, controllable memory bank; (2) anchor-query attention over causally available state checkpoints; (3) consistent wins over linear-attention baselines on LongBench/ICL retrieval; (4) a principled historical-resolution-vs-memory-cost knob.

### 3.2 Dual-Flow Transformers: Decoupling the Primary Prefill Path from Additional Decode Computation

- **arXiv**: [2608.12385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12385) (cs.AI; v1 submitted 2026-07-31, in Fri Aug 14 listing) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Liming Liu, Mingze Wang, Tuo Zhao
- **Institution**: Not stated; Tuo Zhao and Mingze Wang are affiliated with Georgia Tech (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: As LLMs serve more requests, cumulative inference cost is increasingly important relative to one-time training cost. Prefill is parallel and typically compute-bound, while decode is sequential and often memory-bandwidth-bound; conventional width/depth scaling increases both costs together because every added layer runs in both phases. The paper asks whether additional learned computation can be allocated to **continuation prediction** while preserving the prompt-wide primary computation and a single persistent KV cache. The **Dual-Flow Transformer** has a primary flow (a complete causal LM that processes the prompt and writes the KV cache) and an auxiliary flow omitted during prompt processing and activated only from the final prompt position onward, adding continuation-prediction computation without writing persistent state or influencing the primary flow. The flows share major attention/MLP/output matrices, use separate token embeddings and lightweight coupling, and weight/cache reuse creates opportunities during grouped execution. Across matched-token comparisons, Dual-Flow achieves lower validation loss across architectures and data configurations. In MoE models, the separation makes primary and auxiliary expert fan-outs independent controls over prompt cost, continuation cost, and predictive quality; two regimes (increasing decode compute at fixed prefill expert compute; reallocating a fixed decode budget) expose a **prefill-decode quality trade-off** and the potential of phase-specific expert allocation.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Decoupling phase-specific compute (auxiliary flow only for decode/continuation); (2) shared weights + primary KV cache with grouped-execution reuse opportunities; (3) lower matched-token validation loss across architectures/data; (4) in MoE, independent control of prefill vs. decode expert budgets — directly relevant to serving-efficiency design.

### 3.3 Algebraic Decomposition Theory for Transformer Length Generalization

- **arXiv**: [2608.13433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13433) (cs.FL / cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-13) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Andy Yang, Blerta Veseli, Corentin Barloy, Michaël Cadilhac, Andreas Krebs, Charles Paperman, Howard Straubing, Michael Hahn
- **Institution**: Not stated; Michael Hahn (Saarland University) and Howard Straubing (Boston College) are plausible affiliations (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Transformers sometimes generalize to sequences longer than seen in training, but which tasks admit length generalization lacks a precise characterization — it was not even known which regular languages transformers length-generalize on. This work establishes the **first complete characterization of which regular languages transformers length-generalize on**, with a decision algorithm running in polynomial time in the size of the language's syntactic monoid. The results rely on an effective characterization of the regular languages in **C-RASP**, the formalism expressing which languages transformers length-generalize on. The characterization is challenging because classical Krohn–Rhodes decomposition theory is insufficient: its building blocks (flip-flops, simple groups) are not expressible in C-RASP, and C-RASP's building block (unbounded counting) is not expressible by finite semigroups. Length generalization on regular languages is therefore controlled by an algebraic property invisible to classical finite decomposition theory. The authors generalize classical decomposition theory from finite semigroups to the **infinite additive group on the integers**, characterizing C-RASP via iterated wreath products of the integers, and derive a provable polynomial-time decision algorithm. Experiments across a broad suite of regular languages confirm the theory captures transformers' length-generalization behavior more accurately than existing classifications.
- **Key innovations**: (1) First complete characterization of length-generalizable regular languages for transformers; (2) a polynomial-time decision algorithm (in syntactic-monoid size); (3) a substantive theory contribution — generalizing Krohn–Rhodes decomposition to the infinite additive integer group to match C-RASP's unbounded counting; (4) experimental confirmation over a broad regular-language suite.

## 4. LLM Pretraining & Data

### 4.1 Mixture of Training: Recombining Small-Scale Scaffolded Pretraining Runs into a Larger Language Model

- **arXiv**: [2608.13277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13277) (cs.CL / cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-13; accepted at the MOSS Workshop, COLM 2026) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Mohammed Sabry, Sean Augenstein, Keith Rush, Lucio Dery
- **Institution**: Not stated.
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: The paper asks whether LLM pretraining can be decomposed into smaller, independently trainable jobs that are later recomposed into a coherent larger model. **Mixture of Training (MoT)** is a scaffolded modular pretraining procedure that partitions a target Transformer into contiguous layer blocks, trains each block inside a frozen pretrained aligner scaffold, and then recomposes the trained blocks with an optional short end-to-end adaptation pass. On a 1.3B-parameter Gemma-style model trained on C4, MoT provides a small-scale proof of mechanism: independently trained depth slices can be recomposed into a usable language model, and a quality-parity schedule reaches the same reported perplexity as the monolithic baseline. The parity setting processes more aggregate tokens and has a shorter idealized layer-equivalent critical path after aligner preparation; its effective compute advantage depends on reusing the aligner across runs. MoT is presented not as a general replacement for monolithic pretraining, but as a small-scale framework for studying whether scaffolded sub-runs can act as reusable training units.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A modular pretraining decomposition (block-wise training inside a frozen aligner scaffold + recomposition); (2) quality-parity with a monolithic baseline on 1.3B/C4; (3) an explicit accounting of where the compute advantage comes from (aligner reuse across runs); (4) an honest framing of MoT as a reusable-training-units research framework rather than a pretraining replacement.

### 4.2 Measuring Task-Agnostic Training Data Influence Across Language Model Pretraining

- **arXiv**: [2608.13515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13515) (cs.CL; submitted 2026-08-13; accepted at COLM 2026) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Yuto Nishida, Hirokazu Kiyomaru, Yusuke Oda, Takashi Kodama, Chaoran Liu, Daisuke Kawahara, Yusuke Miyao, Max Müller-Eberstein, Masaru Isonuma
- **Institution**: Not stated; several authors are affiliated with Japanese national labs (NICT / AIST) and universities (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Measuring training data influence consistently across LLM pretraining is challenging: selecting downstream tasks or validation sets representative of general capabilities is hard, and relying on task performance at intermediate checkpoints complicates cross-training comparison. The paper proposes a measure of training data influence that **does not require selecting a downstream task or validation set**. An example's influence is defined by how much its gradient update reduces the squared distance to the final parameters of a given pretraining run, estimated from intermediate checkpoints **without retraining**. Applying the method to 18 configurations from the Pythia and PolyPythia suites, the authors find systematic temporal changes in influential data: early in training, literature-related data are more strongly aligned with the trajectory toward the final parameters, whereas STEM data become more strongly aligned in later stages — a qualitative crossover broadly consistent across model configurations. This provides a tractable trajectory-level view of how influential data change throughout pretraining, complementing task-specific influence analyses.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A task-agnostic, retraining-free data-influence measure defined relative to the final-parameter trajectory; (2) systematic temporal crossover (literature early, STEM late) consistent across 18 Pythia/PolyPythia configs; (3) a tractable complement to downstream-task-based influence attribution.

## 5. Agents, Memory & Synthetic Data

### 5.1 SynWeaver: Website-Prior Task and Trajectory Co-Synthesis for Web Agents

- **arXiv**: [2608.12429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12429) (cs.SE / cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-12) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Ruitao Wang, Yuwen Hao, Menglin Yang
- **Institution**: Not stated; Menglin Yang is affiliated with HKU (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Web agents often struggle to generalize to unseen websites because they lack website-specific supervision. Recent exploration-based data synthesis reduces manual annotation but fails to cover full website functionality and, without website prior knowledge, proposes hallucinated tasks that limit the diversity and efficiency of downstream trajectory synthesis. **SynWeaver** is a website-prior task–trajectory co-synthesis framework: (1) structured website exploration builds a **website map** covering functionally distinct page states and executable interactions; (2) page-level and transition-level supervision trains a **UI-aware model with website-specific priors** for more grounded task proposals; (3) **collaborative task–trajectory synthesis** jointly updates tasks and execution trajectories when inconsistent, then verifies and repairs results to produce executable, semantically aligned supervision. On WebArena and WebVoyager, SynWeaver consistently outperforms strong synthesis baselines and yields more effective supervision for both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Co-synthesis (tasks and trajectories jointly repaired/verified) rather than task-then-trajectory pipelines; (2) a website map that grounds task proposals and cuts hallucinated tasks; (3) UI-aware prior training from map-derived supervision; (4) consistent in-domain and out-of-domain gains on WebArena/WebVoyager.

### 5.2 From Observation to Intervention: Memory in Brains and Large Language Models

- **arXiv**: [2608.12377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12377) (q-bio.NC / cs.AI / cs.CL; v1 submitted 2026-07-27, in Fri Aug 14 listing; under review at Nature Machine Intelligence) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Morteza Salehjahromi, Shayan A. Zadegan, Amgad Muneer, Jia Wu
- **Institution**: Not stated.
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: A perspective article comparing brains and LLMs as fundamentally different memory systems through shared functional questions — where memory is represented, how partial cues recover broader associations, how new information is written/updated, and how memory states can be perturbed. In biology these span synapses, neuronal ensembles, hippocampal–cortical interactions, and plasticity; in LLMs, weights, activations, context windows, retrieval, and external stores. The comparison is functional and experimental rather than anatomical: human studies reveal sparse concept responses and recall-related reactivation but limited selective intervention; rodents provide more causal access to learning-related ensembles; LLMs lack lived episodic memory but permit unusually direct, repeatable manipulation of internal states. The authors argue LLMs are not ahead in memory itself but in **experimental access** — their tools may help turn broad questions about retrieval, updating, persistence, reversibility, and unintended effects into sharper biological hypotheses. The productive bridge is to transfer experimental logic, not anatomical parts.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A functional, experiment-centric comparison framework for brain vs. LLM memory; (2) the "LLMs are not ahead in memory, but in experimental access" thesis; (3) concrete transfer of experimental logic from LLM manipulation to biological hypothesis formation.

## 6. Inference Efficiency, MoE & GPU/Systems

### 6.1 Thought-Aware KV Cache Compaction for Reasoning via Adaptive Attention Matching

- **arXiv**: [2608.12331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12331) (cs.CL / cs.AI; v1 submitted 2026-06-01, in Fri Aug 14 listing) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Yang Liu, Bin Chong, Chongyang Zhang, Hao Zheng, Jiayu Liang, Xu Kefu
- **Institution**: Not stated.
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Reasoning LMs generate lengthy chain-of-thought sequences whose KV cache grows linearly and becomes a memory bottleneck during decoding. Existing compaction methods treat reasoning trajectories as flat token sequences with uniform compression, ignoring the hierarchical structure of CoT where different steps vary drastically in importance. **Thought-Aware Attention Matching (TAM)** exploits this structure via three mechanisms: (i) **thought segmentation** decomposing the trajectory into reasoning blocks, (ii) **adaptive budget allocation** assigning compression budget per segment based on importance and size, and (iii) **pivotal token protection** preserving high-attention reasoning anchors. The allocation rule is proven optimal under a convex error model, and cumulative error under sequential compaction is provably bounded. On AIME 2024 and MATH-500 with Qwen3-4B, TAM improves accuracy over uniform compaction at the same memory footprint, with periodic compaction bounding peak memory to **3.1–3.2 GB (a 65% reduction)** while maintaining competitive accuracy.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Structure-aware (segment-level) KV compaction for CoT reasoning; (2) provably optimal budget allocation + bounded cumulative error; (3) pivotal-token preservation; (4) 65% peak-memory reduction with accuracy gains over uniform compaction on AIME/MATH-500.

### 6.2 TEMPO: Makespan-Aware Expert-Parallel Load Balancing Across Memory- and Compute-Bound Regimes

- **arXiv**: [2608.13057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13057) (cs.DC / cs.AI / cs.CL / cs.GT; submitted 2026-08-13) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Jie Li, Chenxin Jia, Jinliang Shen, Cunzhuang Liu, Ruiyi Ding, Jianwen Xian, Kang He, Chengru Song
- **Institution**: Not stated; Kang He appears in the AlayaWorld (Alaya Lab / Shanda) author list in this batch (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: In expert-parallel (EP) MoE serving, every layer synchronizes at the slowest GPU. Existing dispatchers balance token counts or activated-expert counts, assuming expert time is linear in one. Measurements on two datacenter GPU generations show it is neither: below N*≈156–168 tokens, HBM weight streaming dominates (cost attaches to **activated replicas**, not tokens); above it, grouped GEMM rounds tokens to 128-tile M-tiles, so **splitting an expert adds padded compute**. A max-affine profile t=max(a+bG, c+βN) captures both regimes. Realistic decode batches hold hot experts in the linear regime and cold experts in the flat regime simultaneously; recorded batches show proxy dispatches differ by 1.4–1.6× in modeled block time (p95 up to 1.7×), and which proxy wins flips with the regime. The paper formalizes per-batch dispatch as a **fixed-charge makespan problem** — NP-hard on two fully replicated GPUs, polynomial in degenerate limits — and presents **TEMPO**, a makespan-aware dispatcher solving it in milliseconds off the critical path, integrated with SGLang out-of-process, fusing dispatch with count collection into one in-graph kernel. On an 8-GPU microbenchmark TEMPO stays within 1% of the best fixed baseline everywhere and wins by up to 15.5% where regimes mix; end-to-end on a second testbed, Qwen3-235B gains 4–6% throughput and cuts p99 latency ~15.6%, while DeepSeek-V3 (communication-dominated) shows only mechanism cost. A phase diagram — not a universal win — is the claim, predicting both outcomes before deployment.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A two-regime (replica-bound vs. tile-bound) max-affine cost model for EP MoE dispatch, empirically grounded; (2) dispatch as a fixed-charge makespan problem with a fast practical solver; (3) an honest phase-diagram framing that predicts when EP load balancing helps and when it cannot; (4) up to 15.5% win where regimes mix, 4–6% throughput / −15.6% p99 on Qwen3-235B.

### 6.3 SPADE: Speculative Decoding for Precise and Low Cost Distributed Edge Cloud Inference

- **arXiv**: [2608.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13076) (cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-13) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Divya Jyoti Bajpai, Kishan Kumar Upadhyay, Manjesh Kumar Hanawal
- **Institution**: Not stated; Manjesh Kumar Hanawal is affiliated with IIT Bombay (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: LLM deployment is constrained by high computational demands: small edge models degrade accuracy, while cloud big models cost expensive per-token computation. **SPADE** is a distributed inference framework integrating **speculative decoding across edge and cloud**: a compact draft model on the edge generates candidate tokens rapidly, and a large verifier model on the cloud validates them in parallel. Accepted tokens are retained; only rejections trigger verifier correction, substantially reducing cloud queries. The plug-and-play design shifts bulk computation to the edge, lowers inference time and cloud cost, and preserves the big model's accuracy without retraining. Across multiple NLP tasks on SpecBench and CNN/Dailymail, SPADE reduces cloud model calls by **76% with zero accuracy loss** versus the full model.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Edge-draft/cloud-verify speculative decoding with no retraining; (2) a plug-and-play design shifting compute to the edge; (3) 76% reduction in cloud calls at zero accuracy loss across SpecBench/CNN-DM.

### 6.4 A Contract-Grade Verifier for LLM-Generated GPU Kernels, and a Native Blackwell Backward for the Gated-Linear-Recurrence Family

- **arXiv**: [2608.12700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12700) (cs.LG / cs.AR / cs.DC; submitted 2026-08-13) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Rishi Shah, Rishav Shrestha
- **Institution**: Not stated (also archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.21563213).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Systems that generate GPU kernels with LLMs report high correctness rates, but those rates come from a single loose test: run the kernel on a few random inputs at one fixed shape and accept if the output is close to a reference. A kernel can pass that test and still be silently wrong — returning an ordinary number where the truth is NaN/inf, differing run to run, breaking on shape change, or accumulating in fp16 where the reference keeps an fp32 total. The paper builds a **contract-grade verifier of twelve adversarial gates**, each a property a correct kernel must satisfy, several tolerance-free (no threshold can explain a failure away). Aimed outward, it audits **2,638 machine-generated kernels** a public system's own harness already accepted: it finds **39.5% broken beyond any tolerance argument** and 62.1% carrying at least one violation; the field's standard test accepts 1,487 kernels the verifier rejects against only 14 the other way. The finding is defended four independent ways (7/7 positive control, threshold-calibration sweep, 98.5% agreement with the reference benchmark's correctness code, stratified hand-audit). Aimed inward, the verifier judges the authors' own kernel: the **first native Blackwell tcgen05 training backward for the gated-linear-recurrence (GDN) family**, including the reverse-state stage the field still runs on a fallback, established correct against a double-precision oracle, and used to train five family members.
- **Key innovations**: (1) A tolerance-free, twelve-gate contract verifier for generated GPU kernels; (2) a striking audit result (39.5% of harness-accepted kernels broken) with four independent defenses; (3) the first native Blackwell tcgen05 backward for gated-linear-recurrence models; (4) a clear takeaway for LLM-generated-kernel pipelines — correctness signals are far weaker than reported.

## 7. Games, Sports Analytics & Creative

### 7.1 H-VAEP and H-xT: Valuing Offensive On-the-Ball Actions in Handball by Estimating Probabilities

- **arXiv**: [2608.12926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12926) (cs.LG / cs.AI; submitted 2026-08-13; accepted at MLSA 2026 workshop, co-located with ECML PKDD 2026) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Julius Broermann, Oliver Müller, Michael Döring, Jochen Baumeister
- **Institution**: Not stated; the author group is affiliated with University of Paderborn (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: Traditional player evaluation in professional handball relies on box-score metrics or heuristic indices that fail to credit the multi-player build-up chain. While football analytics adopted Expected Threat (xT) and Valuing Actions by Estimating Probabilities (VAEP), these event-based action valuation frameworks had not been adapted to handball. This paper presents the first comprehensive adaptation and evaluation of xT and VAEP for handball, using five seasons of tracking-derived event data from the Handball Bundesliga. **Handball-xT (H-xT)** uses a handball-native court zoning layout, shown via simulations to be systematically more robust than standard rectangular grids. **Handball-VAEP (H-VAEP)** is optimized by tailoring its feature space and selecting context length to limit team-identity leakage, yielding exceptionally stable, discriminative, and intuitive player ratings that highlight build-up play. The complete code repository is released to help clubs deploy these models.
- **Key innovations**: (1) First adaptation of xT/VAEP action-valuation to handball with a native zoning layout; (2) evidence that the sport-native layout is more robust than rectangular grids; (3) team-identity-leakage-aware feature/context design; (4) a released codebase for production deployment — an expected-value action-valuation analogue to the football analytics the wiki already tracks.

### 7.2 StorySpark: Module-wise Evolutionary Search for Story Premise Generation

- **arXiv**: [2608.12336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12336) (cs.CL / cs.AI; v1 submitted 2026-06-03, in Fri Aug 14 listing) — **NEW**
- **Authors**: Yang Yang, Zining Zhong, Qian Cao, Jindong Li, Boyun Xu, Kaishen Yuan, Menglin Yang, Yutao Yue
- **Institution**: Not stated; Menglin Yang is affiliated with HKU (tentative).
- **Abstract (faithful summary)**: A story premise is the creative spark from which a full narrative grows, yet LLM story generation has emphasized later-stage planning, controllability, coherence, and prose expansion while premise-level ideation remains underexplored. **StorySpark** is a module-wise evolutionary search framework for story premise generation operating over interpretable narrative modules (background, persona, event, ending, twist), treating each active module as a local search space conditioned on the partial premise built so far. Per module it generates alternatives, evaluates them in context, refines them via feedback-driven mutation and recombination, preserves complementary strengths with Pareto-guided selection, and reallocates frontier capacity to balance branch coverage with promising directions. Multi-view automatic and human evaluations show StorySpark produces stronger final premises than competitive baselines with especially consistent gains in **originality**; expanded with the same story writer, its premises lead to higher-quality downstream stories while maintaining completeness, fascination, and diverse usable narrative directions.
- **Key innovations**: (1) Treating premise ideation as module-wise evolutionary search rather than single-shot generation; (2) Pareto-guided selection + frontier-capacity reallocation for diversity–quality balance; (3) consistent originality gains and better downstream stories in human/automatic evaluation.

## Cross-cutting trends

- **Experimentation economics are tightening for ads/rec.** Fast A/B/n (Tree-Coupled A/B Testing) gives exact multi-policy comparison at ~T+o(T) reward queries instead of JT, with sublinear pseudo-regret — a directly deployable cost cut for comparing ranking/rec/pricing policies. Paired with the retail papers in this batch, the theme is "cheaper, exact, and production-validated industrial decision tools" (ICMLA 2026 retail trio: DT coefficients, Lines-and-Ladders pricing taxonomies).
- **The AutoResearch/RL bottleneck is being re-architected around world models.** WMRL replaces environment execution with a (biased, noisy) world model plus Online Debiasing + Inverse-Variance Denoising, achieving 3–4× training speedup and small agents beating much larger open-weight agents — continuing the world-model thread from the 08-12/08-14 digests (Objective-Is-The-Bottleneck, ACPC, LeWorldModel) but now aimed at *training cost* rather than *planning diagnosis*.
- **Recurrent memory is reaching for content-routed, growing state.** MARCH caches content-conditioned state anchors so SSM memory grows with context while keeping the efficient recurrent path — a direct competitor framing to the token-level KV memory of transformers and to the 08-13 LinearKV "single linear state suffices" line; it argues resolution matters and routing should be content-conditioned.
- **Inference cost is being split along phase boundaries.** Dual-Flow decouples prefill computation from decode computation (shared weights, single KV cache, phase-specific MoE expert budgets); TEMPO shows EP load balancing only wins inside a regime (max-affine two-regime model, phase diagram rather than universal win); SPADE pushes draft compute to the edge while a cloud verifier validates. Together they reframe serving efficiency as "allocate the right compute to the right phase/location."
- **Correctness verification for generated code is hardening.** The Contract-Grade Verifier audits 2,638 LLM-generated GPU kernels its own harness accepted and finds 39.5% broken beyond tolerance — echoing the 08-14 Vero (verified code repositories) and the wiki's software-2.0/verifiability thread, but at the kernel/contract level where claims are tolerance-free.
- **Reasoning is being re-grounded in definitions and structure.** The "Reasoning is a Learnable Rule-Based Process" position paper argues construct validity of reasoning evals is unverifiable without operational definitions; TAM treats CoT as hierarchical (thought segments, pivotal tokens) rather than a flat token stream for KV compaction. Both point the field from "longer/better" toward *what the process is and where its structure matters*.
- **Creative and game-action value estimation mature via existing frameworks.** H-xT/H-VAEP transplant football's expected-value action valuation to handball with a sport-native zoning layout and robustness evidence; StorySpark applies module-wise evolutionary search to premise ideation with Pareto-guided selection — sports analytics and game-adjacent creative generation continue importing proven frameworks rather than inventing new ones.
