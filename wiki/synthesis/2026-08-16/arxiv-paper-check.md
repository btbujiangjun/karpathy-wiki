## 🔥 Highlights

### CTR, Recommendation, Advertising & IR

| Paper | Authors | Key Contribution |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| **HybridRAG-BN: A Retrieval-Augmented Framework with Fine-Tuned Verification for Bangla KBQA** (2608.13004) | Rathijit Aich, Nirjhar Das, Mahfuzulhoq Chowdhury | **First-place Bangla KBQA system — hybrid retrieval (BM25 + BGE-M3) paired with a LoRA-fine-tuned verifier that refines generated answers.** Answer generation uses GGUF Gemma-4-31B-Instruct; a fine-tuned verifier model then validates/refines the output, with post-processing fallback replacement and DuckDuckGo-assisted retrieval for unresolved cases. Token-level F1 **0.71654 (public) / 0.72912 (private) leaderboards** — a concrete data point for multilingual RAG robustness in low-resource settings, directly relevant to the wiki's retrieval/IR thread. |
| **Knowledge Synthesis Review Framework: Task-Level Benchmarking of LLM-Based Systems for Multi-Source Evidence Synthesis** (2608.12741) | Wafa Shafqat, Mark Patterson, Steven N. Liss | **Benchmarks LLM systems per-task across the evidence-synthesis pipeline (screening / extraction / analysis / synthesis) — and finds no system leads on all tasks.** Human-in-the-loop KSR framework; evaluated GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, NotebookLM on a 244-doc benchmark subset of a 1,893-doc AI-and-work corpus, gold standard 92.2% agreement (κ=0.80). Claude Sonnet 4 best screening accuracy (82.8%), GPT-5 best recall (91.8% at lower specificity); extraction >90% on titles/sources but degrades on author/reference fields; performance drops most in interpretive analysis and cross-source synthesis, where expert judgment remains essential. A routing-based "best system per task" blueprint — relevant to how industrial research/review pipelines should allocate LLM compute. |
| **CRAFT: LLM-Based Iterative Refinement for Temporal Reasoning over Clinical Narratives** (2608.12779) | Chengyang He, Tahreem Arif, Marko Zivkovic, Lijing Wang, Yue Ning, Ping Wang | **Tackles the under-addressed problem of reconstructing structured symptom trajectories from anchor-sparse clinical narratives — generator + constraint-based verifier in an iterative refine loop.** Clinical narratives rarely give explicit temporal anchors, and prior work focused on pairwise relation classification over timestamp-rich multi-visit records. CRAFT pairs a generator with a constraint-based verifier that iteratively produces and refines stage-wise symptom timelines through targeted feedback. Introduces **MedTempo**, a 5,347-report vaccine adverse-event benchmark (3,166 expert-validated temporal stage annotations across three COVID-19 vaccine types); consistent temporal-ordering gains across four LLM backbones, with ablations isolating generator vs verifier contributions. A strong evaluation-datapoint for temporal RAG/verification pipelines. |
| **ParliamentRAG: Authority-Aware Multi-View RAG over Italian Parliamentary Proceedings** (2608.13410) | Mirko Tritella, Riccardo Pozzi, Matteo Palmonari | **RAG that weights speakers by topical authority instead of letting most-frequent speakers dominate — a "who speaks matters" fix for politically sensitive retrieval.** Topic-dependent authority model combines interpretable components (profession, education, prior interventions); retrieval then identifies topic-relevant experts across parliamentary groups and synthesizes their perspectives with supporting quotes. Beats Google NotebookLM on 15 policy topics: group coverage **0.97 vs 0.95**, quotation faithfulness **1.00 vs 0.95**, expert preferences on source-related dimensions (NotebookLM better on prose quality). Directly addresses citation misattribution and speaker dominance — a novel fairness/authority dimension for RAG beyond pure relevance. |
| **TRACE: Jointly Predicting Courses and Grades Using a Transformer-Based Model** (2608.13409) | Paul Savala | **Joint course-set + grade prediction nearly halves grade MAE — a clean result that "predicting what students will take" improves "predicting how they'll do".** TRansformer for Academic Course-grade Estimation encodes courses per-semester to capture course concurrency (the concurrent course-load effect that sequential models miss); novel loss combines course-set prediction with grade prediction. Trained on 10 years of institutional data, **~50% lower MAE** than an identical architecture predicting grades alone, and beats LSTM and GNN baselines. An interpretable, retrainable education-analytics datapoint — conceptually parallel to multi-task rec/CTR setups where auxiliary head supervision improves the core prediction. |

### AI, Agents & Evaluation

| Paper | Authors | Key Contribution |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| **MARC v1: An Open-Source Multi-Agent Framework for Clinical AI Reasoning and Coordination** (2608.13476) | Saisha Shetty, Satvik Tripathi, Austin Lin, Colin Zhao, Theodore Kim, Don Enwerem, Jacinta Arnold, Shahriar Faghani, Tessa S Cook (Penn) | **Replaces monolithic LLM prompting with deterministic multi-agent orchestration for clinical reasoning — with a Decomposer module that auto-generates agent prompts from plain language.** Role-specialized agents for extraction, reasoning, answer generation, and evaluation; explicit context passing and traceable intermediate outputs enable stage-wise failure attribution. Entirely YAML-configurable (no code changes), API- and local-CPU-compatible, model-agnostic — explicitly designed for clinical experts without programming expertise. Open source. A clean reference architecture for regulated-domain agentic systems (cf. AstraZeneca Research Assistant 08-14). |
| **Are You Sure You're Sure? On the Impact of Instruction Tuning on Confidence and Lexical Diversity** (2608.13430) | Irina Proskurina, Mayank Kumar, Oyindolapo O. Komolafe | **Instruction tuning changes verbalized confidence while leaving accuracy roughly constant — and cross-rationale diversity consistently falls.** Three matched base↔instruction-tuned model pairs across QA benchmarks: instruction tuning alters confidence despite limited accuracy change and *decreases* likelihood-based calibration; rationale diversity shows a non-uniform effect (cross-rationale diversity down, surface-level lexical diversity mixed); effects persist after controlling for answer selection and rationale length. A calibration/overconfidence datapoint that pairs well with the wiki's RLHF-reward-model and hallucination threads. |
| **It's How You Ask: Gender-Associated Linguistic Bias in LLMs** (2608.13328) | Katherine Van Koevering, Anjalie Field | **Linguistic register (features more common in women's writing) systematically elicits shorter, less sophisticated, less formal responses — much larger and more consistent than explicit gender cues.** Hedges, tag questions, and collective reference reduce response quality across three document types and four models, persisting after controlling for prompt complexity; sign-off names encode in the same representational space as dialect yet produce no effect. Post-hoc mitigation is hard: features are culturally embedded, encoded in early transformer layers, and entangled with other features. An important fairness finding — "It's how you ask" — for anyone building evaluation or workplace LLM systems. |
| **Toward a Gricean Retreat: Probing LLMs for Knowledge Boundaries and Referent Specificity** (2608.13484) | Dananjay Srinivas, Saksham Khatwani, Maria Pacheco | **LLM activations encode knowledge-boundary and referent-specificity signals, but generation doesn't act on them — models over-generate specific referents even for unknown entities.** Gricean framing: a cooperative uncertain speaker retreats up a specificity hierarchy (trading informativeness for truthfulness); LLMs have the substrate (probes confirm both signals) but no policy to act on it, preferring specific referents even when correct generic alternatives are offered. A precise diagnosis of hallucination-as-failure-to-retreat, with a proposed "Gricean alignment" training objective — a principled mechanistic take relevant to the hallucination/RAG-faithfulness thread. |
| **LittleLearner: Language Models Under Pedagogically Controlled Knowledge Exposure** (2608.13545) | Fanfei Li, Jana Zeller, Manuel Prada-Corral, Thaddäus Wiedemer, Prasanna Mayilvahanan, Ryan Cotterell, Wieland Brendel | **A developmentally restricted pretraining sandbox: 5B LLM trained on an 88B-token curriculum limited to U.S. ≤Grade-5 material, with explicit out-of-scope knowledge boundaries.** LITTLECURRICULUM excludes concepts/facts/vocab above Grade 5, yielding LITTLELEARNER with clear capability boundaries mapped to interpretable curriculum guidelines. Post-training and ICL can better utilize existing knowledge but **do not raise out-of-scope capabilities** — strong evidence about the pretraining scope as a hard knowledge ceiling. A controlled sandbox for studying knowledge acquisition; timely given the wiki's education/pedagogy and knowledge-boundary threads. |
| **RAIL: An Automatic Classifier of the Artificial Intelligence Readiness Level** (2608.13428) | Juan Irving Vasquez, Juan Terven, Laura-Ivoone Garay-Jimenez | **Unifies three heterogeneous AI readiness frameworks into a single decidable 9-level scale, then operationalizes it as a panel-of-LLM-experts classifier.** Unified AI Readiness Level (AIRL) built on an environmental-evidence ladder + dimensional caps (specification, data existence/quality/legality, expert knowledge, algorithmic maturity) + generality-anchoring rule; decidable from natural-language description alone. RAIL uses one evidence agent + six independent dimension agents, each a narrowly-scoped LLM, delivering verdicts. A practical tooling datapoint for maturity assessment of AI investments/projects — the kind of evaluation infrastructure the "AI scientist/eval professionalization" trend (08-14) predicts. |
| **Concept Drift Detection and Adaptive Retraining of Malware Classification Models** (2608.13465) | Christofer Washington Berruz Chungata, Martin Jurecek, Katerina Potika, William B. Andreopoulos, Mark Stamp | **Drift-aware retraining (retrain only on detected drift) beats both static and periodic retraining across four model families — with OCSVM as the novel detector.** Compares OCSVM-based detection against Minibatch K-Means and MMD as a statistical baseline; four learners (MLP, Random Forest, SVM, XGBoost) × three scenarios (static, periodic, drift-aware). Analyzes the accuracy-vs-training-cost tradeoff under drift — a practical, reproducible recipe for maintaining deployed classification systems against adversarial concept drift, relevant to the wiki's security/robustness thread. |
| **MLLM-Routed Heterogeneous Ensembles for Robust Cross-Dataset Image Classification** (2608.13463) | Daniel Perkins, John Squires, Janou Milligan, Chandra Raskoti, Linda Ungerboeck | **ARMDIL — an MLLM agent routes each image to the most suitable vision backbone among a heterogeneous ensemble (ResNet / SSL / VLM).** Built on a unified label space across multiple datasets with different distributions; matches specialized training-based routers, adds adaptability via prompt-level integration of new information, and exposes natural-language reasoning traces for interpretability. A "router-in-the-loop" ensemble pattern — conceptually the vision analog of LLM-routing/mixture patterns studied in the wiki's architecture thread. |
| **TopoIntent: Compiling Security Intent into Executable, Compliance-Checked Network Topologies** (2608.13389) | Xiaokang Qu, Jianliang Ma, Zao Fan, Tianshu Chu, Tianlong Fan, Linyuan Lü | **Compiles natural-language security requirements into executable, CIS-v8.1.2-compliance-checked network topologies.** Schema contract constrains generation; dense-vector retrieval pulls reference architectures from a curated template library; staged fusion aligns intent to template and completes security details; additive schema-preserving repairs fix structural gaps; output exports to Mininet scripts with kernel-level iptables ACLs for reachability/allow-deny tests. No public benchmark existed, so the authors built one (22 templates + 44 synthetic intents across five scenarios; held-out 7 templates + 14 finance/government intents). A strong example of LLM-generated structured artifacts with executable verification. |
| **CAPRI: Contract-Aware Proof Repair for Isabelle** (2608.13459) | Jim Woodcock, Gabriel Leite, Augusto Sampaio, Ran Wei | **Proof repair that constrains the LLM with a machine-readable edit contract — preventing unauthorized changes to protected theory text.** Isabelle checks the proof; an independent checker enforces the edit contract; prompts/proposals/verdicts/hashes retained for audit. Five workflows × twelve failed proofs × three replicates = 180 runs, 138 valid repairs: proof-body-only interface 29/36 valid repairs with **zero contract violations** (vs 31/36 for the full-theory workflow); one-shot 22/36; a frozen iterative workflow 32/36. Directly relevant to the wiki's verified-software/proof-assistant thread (cf. Vero, Agentic Proving 05-xx, and CAPRI's contract discipline echoes the "artifact-confinement" pattern of AQuA 08-14). |
| **CEAA Edge SLM Virtual Agents: Think and Memory Processes** (2608.13420) | Aimilios Hadjiliasi, Louis Nisiotis | **Edge-deployed Small Language Models can carry the "Think" and "Memory" processes of embodied virtual agents — evaluated on NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX with Qwen2.5 variants.** Prototype edge-based agent gateway implementing selected Cognitive Embodied Agent Architecture (CEAA) components; simulation experiments measure routing accuracy, memory-read performance, and latency. Shows SLM-driven cognitive orchestration for Metaverse/VR agents is feasible at the edge — a latency/cost datapoint for the agent-deployment-on-constrained-hardware thread. |

## 🔑 Key Trends (this batch remainder)

1. **RAG is acquiring governance beyond relevance: authority, verification, and provenance.** ParliamentRAG weights speakers by topical authority (fixing speaker dominance and citation misattribution); HybridRAG-BN adds a fine-tuned verifier and retrieval fallback for low-resource KBQA; CRAFT couples a generator with a constraint-based verifier to reconstruct temporal symptom trajectories; the KSR framework routes each evidence-synthesis task to its best system. The theme: *retrieval pipelines are being re-engineered as multi-stage, verifiable, authority-aware systems* — consistent with the 08-14 batch's "monitor it, gate it, reformulate queries" trend.
2. **Confidence, calibration, and knowledge boundaries are being probed mechanistically.** "Are You Sure" shows instruction tuning changes verbalized confidence while degrading calibration; the Gricean Retreat paper finds LLM activations encode knowledge-boundary signals that generation fails to act on; LittleLearner provides a controlled curriculum sandbox showing post-training/ICL can't raise out-of-scope capabilities. Evidence accumulating for the wiki's hallucination/overconfidence/verifiability threads.
3. **Fairness research is moving from explicit demographic cues to implicit linguistic register.** "It's How You Ask" finds language register (hedges, tag questions, collective reference) has larger, more consistent effects than explicit gender names — with early-layer encoding making post-hoc mitigation difficult. A distinct "asking matters" fairness axis beyond content-based bias.
4. **Contract- and check-based discipline is spreading to LLM engineering.** CAPRI enforces machine-readable edit contracts on proof repair (zero contract violations in proof-body-only mode); TopoIntent compiles security intent into executable, compliance-checked topologies with additive schema-preserving repair; MARC v1 enables stage-wise failure attribution via deterministic agent orchestration. The "constrain, verify, audit" pattern seen in AQuA (08-14) and AstraZeneca (08-14) is now routine infrastructure.
