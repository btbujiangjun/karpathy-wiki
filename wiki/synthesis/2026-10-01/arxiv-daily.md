### 1.1 Generative End-to-end Ad Retrieval at Douyin (GEAR)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Shaowen Zeng, Yanhua Huang, Jiacheng Sun, Jiarui Liu, Qian Dai, Zhikai Yang, Hancheng Li, Boya Wu, Tuoyu Zhang, Yekui Chen, Xiang Sun |
| **Institution** | **ByteDance / Douyin Ads** (*confirmed by abstract: "serves hundreds of millions of daily active users on Douyin Ads"*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) |
| **Abstract** | Generative retrieval recasts recommendation as generation of discrete item tokens, but scaling it into a real recommender exposes two coupled bottlenecks: **representation collapse** (the item tokenizer degenerates under continuous distribution shift, blocking stable end-to-end adaptation) and **item collisions** (the massive candidate pool makes distinct items share identical token sequences, capping retrieval precision). The two are *inherently coupled* — enlarging the codebook to fight collisions worsens collapse. GEAR jointly optimizes tokenizer, generator and reranker end-to-end. For collapse it introduces **BasisVQ**, re-parameterizing the codebook via an **orthogonal basis** to enable *global* gradient sharing and rigid spatial rotation of the latent space — stabilizing gradient dynamics without ad-hoc heuristics — extended to prefix-aware **BasisRQ** at the same asymptotic time complexity. For collisions it adds a **context-conditioned reranking head inside the generative process**, disambiguating colliding items at minimal cost. Result is a fully differentiable, scalable paradigm, deployed on Douyin Ads with "substantial empirical improvements in extensive online A/B tests." |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The first production generative-retrieval paper on an ads surface at consumer scale** — the entire Semantic-ID line (TIGER → OneRec → FineSID 09-30 → here) lands in one artifact, and it is the *ad* side rather than the organic-feed side; (2) **the coupled-bottleneck framing is the intellectual contribution** — the collapse/collision trade-off is stated as a theorem-shaped constraint on codebook capacity, which reframes every prior "bigger codebook" result as a trade rather than a win; (3) **BasisVQ's mechanism is unusual and worth stealing**: orthogonal-basis re-parameterization for *global* gradient sharing is a stabilizer that does not need heuristics, and it transfers to other VQ bottlenecks; (4) BasisRQ's prefix-awareness at unchanged asymptotic cost addresses the prefix-stability problem pure RQ/VQ tokenizers leave open. **No specific A/B percentages appear in the abstract — the "substantial improvements" claim is unquantified here; treat the deployment scale (hundreds of millions of DAU) as the load-bearing evidence, not the magnitude.** |

### 1.3 When LLM-Inferred User Context Adds Value in Production Streaming Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Milad Sabouri, Neeraj Sharma, Sardar Hamidian, Shaghayegh Agah |
| **Institution** | (production streaming platform *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) |
| **Abstract** | Recommender context is shifting from static predefined variables to latent representations inferred from behavior; LLMs render an unstructured interaction history as a natural-language summary, yielding a **thematic user context** that can be encoded and used *in place of* an aggregate profile. Prior work characterized when such generated profiles beat aggregate embeddings only at the **domain** level. This paper evaluates on a production streaming platform ranking against the **full catalog**, over a **2×2 design space** crossing representation type (aggregate vs. LLM-generated) with contextual scope (holistic history vs. attention-fused short+long-term context). The finding is conditional, not absolute: **aggregate profiles are consistently stronger under habitual consumption — approximately four-fifths of the population — while LLM-generated profiles are stronger for exploratory users** whose subsequent interactions diverge semantically from their history. A **popularity-attractor effect** appears in LLM-generated profiles: modestly higher within-list diversity, but substantially **lower catalog coverage and reduced novelty**. Conclusion: a context-aware system should **select the profiling strategy from the inferred consumption regime** rather than apply one representation to all users. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **This is the quantitative answer to the 09-28 KuaFu and 09-30 Spotify textual-context papers** — both reported that LLM-generated text helps but is not a replacement, and neither said *for whom*. The 80/20 habitual-vs-exploratory split is the concrete operating condition; (2) **the popularity-attractor measurement is the honest cost accounting** — the method buys diversity and pays in coverage and novelty, which is exactly the axis a streaming catalog needs watched; (3) the 2×2 design (representation × context scope) is what makes the claim falsifiable rather than anecdotal; (4) it sets up §1.4 as the natural follow-on. Together the pair constitute **the strongest evidence yet that "textual user context" is a routing decision, not a representation upgrade**. |

### 1.5 RouteRec: Behavior-Guided Sparse Routing for Sequential Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Junyeong Song, Jaemin Yoo |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) — *Accepted at CIKM 2026; 12 pages, 12 figures, 7 tables* |
| **Abstract** | Sessionized interaction histories contain behavioral patterns that improve sequential recommendation, yet existing models process **all sessions through the same parameterized blocks** regardless of their behavioral differences. Mixture-of-Experts enables conditional computation but leaves open *what should guide expert allocation*. RouteRec is a sequential recommender that uses **observed session behavior as the routing criterion**, summarizing four classes of behavioral evidence from sessionized histories — **interaction tempo, item-group focus, repetition and carryover, and popularity tendency** — and uses these cues to route computation at **macro, mid, and micro scopes**. Cue-derived scores first select expert *groups*; within each selected group, the current backbone state then **refines expert selection** (a two-stage hierarchical router). Across **six public datasets and 18 dataset-metric combinations**, RouteRec ranks first in **12** and second in **3**, for the best overall **average rank 1.61 vs. 4.11** for the next-best baseline. Additional analyses suggest the behavioral cues guide expert allocation **beyond added capacity** and produce routing patterns aligned with observed behavior. Code released. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The "what routes the router" question is the paper** — the MoE-for-rec literature has largely left the routing signal unspecified or learned end-to-end; naming four *observable behavioral cues* and using them as a hierarchical gate is the contribution; (2) the **macro→mid→micro scope ladder** is a genuinely different decomposition from single-layer MoE and matches the structure of the cues themselves; (3) the **1.61 vs. 4.11 average-rank gap with an explicit capacity control** ("guidance beyond added capacity") is the kind of ablation that makes an MoE claim credible; (4) venue-anchored with code out, so the 12-of-18 rank-1 result is checkable. The academic counterpart to §1.1's industrial scaling story. |

### 1.7 Residual Trajectory Distillation for Generative Retrieval (ResTD)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Weihao Shen, Wei Chen, Fuwei Zhang, Guojun Liu, Qingsong Hua, Wei Lin, Fuzhen Zhuang |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) |
| **Abstract** | Generative retrieval represents items with discrete **Semantic IDs (SIDs)** and retrieves by autoregressive identifier generation. When SIDs are built with **residual quantization (RQ)**, standard retrieval training supervises only the selected codes and **discards the residual trajectories that produce them** — yet the same hard code can arise from different preferences over competing codewords, and the residual trajectory contains information about *subsequent* quantization decisions. Hard SID supervision therefore **collapses distinct quantization behaviors into identical targets** and leaves information available during indexing unused in retrieval training. **ResTD** treats the frozen RQ indexer as a **process teacher** and distills **residual-induced codeword preferences into SID-decoding states**. This supervision recovers distinctions hidden by hard assignments and lets earlier decoder states capture information about later quantization decisions before the corresponding SID suffix is generated — while **preserving the original retrieval index and inference procedure**. On **multilingual e-commerce retrieval** it improves consistently over strong baselines and **matched training controls**; controlled comparisons show **residual-derived targets outperform the tested codebook-only soft targets**; representation probes show future codebook preferences become more recoverable from earlier decoder states. The authors state it extends readily beyond retrieval to **generative recommendation**. Code released. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **A free-supervision argument that anyone building an RQ-based Semantic ID system should check** — the residuals are already computed and thrown away, and they are informative; this is the cheapest possible improvement to the TIGER-line training recipe; (2) **the "same hard code, different preferences" observation is the actual insight**, and it is a general statement about hard-assignment supervision collapsing a stochastic process, not just about retrieval; (3) the **index- and inference-preserving** constraint keeps the result usable in production, which is what separates it from papers that need to rebuild the index; (4) a rare paper that reports the *negative* control (codebook-only soft targets) alongside the win. Same authors as §1.8; the two read as one program. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.39319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39319) |

### 1.8 Learning Multiresolution Relevance for Hierarchical Generative Retrieval (RARS)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Weihao Shen, Wei Chen, Fuwei Zhang, Guojun Liu, Qingsong Hua, Wei Lin, Fuzhen Zhuang |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) |
| **Abstract** | With Semantic IDs, retrieval makes successive decisions over a document hierarchy: relevant documents for the same query often **share coarse prefixes and diverge at finer depths**, and the branching pattern varies across queries. Standard full-SID supervision treats these paths as separate training targets. RARS formulates **multiresolution relevance** as consistent conditional distributions induced by a **single document-level relevance measure** across the SID hierarchy, and uses the resulting refinement-level distributions to supervise a **shared query representation**. It aggregates document relevance over prefixes and trains a **prefix-conditioned predictor to allocate relevance among sibling branches**; all relevance-bearing children participate in local competition, and each local loss is **weighted by the relevance mass reaching its parent**. The predictor is **discarded after training**, preserving standard autoregressive retrieval at inference. On **three multilingual ESCI locales** it improves consistently over matched full-SID training under autoregressive decoding, and outperforms **grouped soft-target, decoder soft-target, and sampled-tree supervision** under a common retrieval rule. Gains persist across alternative identifier structures and relevance definitions. Code released. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The claim is about the training objective's information content, not the model** — "full-SID supervision throws away the query-dependent branching structure" is a structural critique of the dominant SID training recipe; (2) **relevance-mass-weighted local losses** is a specific, non-obvious way to route supervision down a hierarchy and is the reusable idea; (3) **the train-then-discard predictor is the honesty move** — no inference cost, so the comparison against full-SID training is clean and the result is deployable; (4) it beats three named soft-target alternatives under a common retrieval rule, which is the comparison the SID literature has been missing. Read with §1.7: two independent critiques of hard-SID supervision from the same group in one day. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.39312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39312) |

### 1.9 AdaM-Rec: Adaptive Modality Routing for Multimodal Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Honghao Fu, Jiacheng Chen, Manxi Lin, Junjun Zheng, Xiangheng Kong, Yiwei Wang, Xin Yu, Miao Xu, Yuning Jiang, Yujun Cai |
| **Institution** | (academic/industrial *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) |
| **Abstract** | Multimodal recommenders improve downstream performance by incorporating visual and textual information, but most rely on **static modality fusion**, assuming the relative importance of textual and visual signals remains stable across recommendation scenarios. That assumption breaks: some queries require **fine-grained visual cues**, others are better served by **textual or functional semantics**, and indiscriminate fusion brings in uninformative cues and impairs quality. AdaM-Rec is an **LLM-based framework for adaptive modality routing**, enabling dynamic calibration of reliance on textual vs. multimodal evidence for user-specific queries. Built on **structured natural-language representations of items and user preferences**, it estimates modality reliability using **proxy recall tasks**: it generates pseudo-queries that match the granularity of the actual query while pointing to the user's positively interacted items as **verifiable proxy targets**, evaluates which modality yields better recall in analogous scenarios, and **optimizes the routing strategy in an agentic manner**. It then performs routed recall, enriches results with collaborative items, and ranks candidates by relevance to both query and user preference. Reported to deliver strong performance against state-of-the-art baselines. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **"Static fusion is the bug"** — the framing relocates multimodal rec's central assumption from architecture to routing policy, and it generalizes beyond multimodality to any multi-signal fusion system; (2) **the proxy-recall probe is a clever evaluation trick** — with no per-query modality ground truth, manufacture pseudo-queries at matched granularity with known-positive targets, turning an unmeasurable routing problem into a measurable one; (3) the **agentic optimization of the routing strategy** (rather than learning it end-to-end) keeps the mechanism inspectable; (4) the natural-language item representation makes this a direct sibling of §1.3/§1.4's textual-profile line — same premise (text is a usable interface to user/item structure), opposite conclusion (route across modalities rather than across profiles). |

### 1.10 SimTrace: Grounded Multimodal User Trajectories Generation for Online User Modeling

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yunan Lu, Shuang Xie, Meghna Allamudi, Mingyu Zhao, Han Li, Lingyun Wang, Zhou Yu |
| **Institution** | (academic/industrial *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.AI) |
| **Abstract** | **Virtual clients** offer a cost-effective approach to A/B testing, recommender development and interface evaluation, but building them requires **large-scale, semantically faithful, fine-grained** online user trajectories. ### 2.1 Efficiency of Generalized Proportional First-Price Auctions Under Auto-bidding

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yang Cai, Vineet Gupta, Yanchen Jiang, Christopher Liaw, Aranyak Mehta, Grigoris Velegkas, Di Wang |
| **Institution** | **Google** (*tentative*; the author set matches Google's ads-economics org — the abstract names "online advertising platforms" and ROS constraints) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.GT) |
| **Abstract** | **Auto-bidding** is now widely adopted in online advertising platforms, allowing advertisers to specify high-level campaign objectives — such as maximizing total value subject to a **return-on-spend (ROS) constraint** — rather than manual per-query bids. A central question in algorithmic mechanism design is characterizing the worst-case efficiency loss, or **Price of Anarchy (PoA)**, across auction formats in this **prior-free** setting. Result 1: for two bidders, standard proportional first-price (`r = 1`) achieves a **tight PoA ≤ 1.5**, complemented by a matching lower bound showing **no anonymous, monotone mechanism can do better**. Result 2: for general `n ≥ 2`, setting `r = 2n` achieves **PoA ≤ 2 − 1/(4n+1) = 2 − Ω(1/n)** across all undominated bid profiles, **breaking the deterministic barrier of 2 for every finite n** and asymptotically matching the known `2 − Θ(1/n)` lower bound. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **Two long-standing open questions closed with matching upper and lower bounds** — the tight 1.5 for two bidders is a clean, small, quotable result and the kind of thing that ends a line of papers; (2) **the mechanism is trivially deployable**: "win probability ∝ bid^r, pay bid if you win" is one parameter, and `r` can be surfaced as a tuning knob without changing the auction format; (3) the **asymptotic-optimality of `r = 2n`** is the nontrivial part — the exponent is a function of bidder count, so the rule is not a constant; (4) this is the strongest possible answer to yesterday's "no ad-auction papers" observation, and it comes from the ads-economics community rather than the classical game-theory community, which is itself a signal about where the field's center of gravity now sits. |

### 3.2 Trust the Critic More (Actor-Critic with Action Chunking, AC2)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Kaiyue Wen, Luke Bailey, Arvind Mahankali, Tengyu Ma |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.LG) |
| **Abstract** | Standard LM RL algorithms credit **every token of a long rollout with the same advantage** determined by the terminal reward. Actor-critic methods can provide finer-grained credit assignment, but **learned critics are generally considered too inaccurate to trust** when training LLMs with RL — and in recent works, even when a critic is present it is used **only for baseline estimation**, so every trajectory must be rolled out to its terminal reward. **AC2** removes that need: it assigns credit to **action chunks** — short continuations of prefixes of past trajectories — with a learned critic scoring the **state reached at the end of each chunk**, allowing the policy to update **without observing a terminal reward**. Three design choices make critic-based credit assignment reliable: (1) **local readiness** — use critic-based updates on a problem only when the critic is sufficiently accurate **on that particular problem**; (2) when available, provide the critic with a **reference solution from a previous successful rollout**; (3) assign credit over **action chunks of 10k tokens** rather than individual tokens, giving the critic a more meaningful portion of the trajectory to evaluate. Conceptually, the authors demonstrate one can **remove the need to roll out every trajectory to completion**, opening a large previously unexplored design space for LLM RL. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The compute claim is the contribution** — 2.5× fewer decoding FLOPs than GRPO at *exceeding* peak score, with the decomposition into steps × tokens-per-step as a real mechanistic accounting rather than an end-point comparison; (2) **removing the "roll every trajectory to completion" requirement is the structural unlock**, and the authors correctly identify it as a design space rather than a trick; (3) **"local readiness" is the answer to the trust problem** — gate the critic per-problem instead of globally, converting an unusable-accuracy objection into a usable-when-accurate mechanism; (4) the **10k-token chunk** is a deliberately coarse credit interval, which is counterintuitive and well-motivated: critics evaluate distributions, not tokens. ### 3.4 Smaller Models, Better Rejects: Preference Distillation Scaling

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Rui Cai, Wenhui Zhu, Xiwen Chen, Jincheng Cao, Han Yu, Shayan Mohajer Hamidi, Zelin He, Qiyao Ma, Daiwei Chen, Xuanzhao Dong, Yuanda Xu, Jelena Markovic-Voronov, Kayhan Behdin, Zhengze Zhou, Ran He, Alborz Geramifard, Rohit Jain, Zhe Zhao |

### 3.5 Uncertainty-Normalized Margins for Direct Preference Optimization (UNM-DPO)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Sadegh Khorasani, Petrus Mikkola, Matthias Grossglauser |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.LG) — *36 pages* |

## 5. Memory, Retrieval & Context Management

> Six papers on the *retrieval* side of agent context, and three of them are unfriendly results. The headline: **the standard IR recipe matches LLM-mediated skill retrieval** (§5.3), **plain BM25 beats the best graph memory** on one benchmark (§5.5), and **direct-decision models compress the ordinal scale you give them** (§5.6). The constructive responses are correspondingly concrete — attention-derived importance as a cheap compactor (§5.1) and executable memory programs that evolve (§5.2). This section is the strongest single-thread narrative in the window: **memory systems are harder to compare than to build, and the honest baselines keep winning.**

### 5.1 Persistent Context Graphs for Efficient Memory Compaction in LLM Agents (ReCAP)

| Field | Detail |

| **Authors** | Jingbo Yang, Kwei-Herng Lai, Xiaowen Wang, Zhaoxuan Tan, Pei Zhou, Mengting Wan, Yaar Harari, Evgeniy Gabrilovich, Shiyu Chang |

## 6. Security, Privacy & Oversight

> The densest section of the window, and the one with the sharpest internal logic. Two papers attack the **KV-cache / inference substrate** (§6.4, §6.5 — and §6.5 is a first systematic study with source-audit evidence); two attack **agent harnesses as security boundaries** (§6.1, §6.2); one evaluates **multi-agent debate as a security property** (§6.3); one audits **real-world AI labeling under EU law** (§6.6). The through-line: **every layer the field added for capability became an unexamined trust boundary.** Notably, two of the six (§6.1, §6.5) report honest negative results about their own proposed defenses.

### 6.1 Approval Laundering: Systematizing Approval–Execution Binding Failures in AI Coding-Agent Harnesses

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yang Wang |
| **Institution** | (independent *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.CR; cross-listed cs.AI, cs.SE) |
| **Abstract** | Modern AI coding-agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Cursor) rest their security boundary on a **largely unexamined assumption: that the action A a human approves is the same action A′ the harness executes**, where A is fixed by a stated policy for what a scope grant or session-scoped approval authorizes. | Field | Detail |

| **Authors** | Yuwan Liu, Jiaming Zhang, Yue Huang, Sisi Duan |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.AI) |
| **Abstract** | **Multi-agent debate (MAD)** can improve LLM reasoning by letting multiple agents **exchange and critique answers**. ## 7. Serving, Inference Efficiency & Attention Alternatives

> The economic side of inference. Two papers directly target the cost/latency trade-off via lifecycle-aware KV management for ReAct agents (§7.1, KVTether) and a sparse-first engine with cross-request state management (§7.2, SparseEngine). A third proposes weight-space compensation for compressed KV instead of dropping more tokens (§7.3, PatchKV). The final two are architectural: MoH-inspired sparse activation to scale heads without scaling KV access (§7.4, NAMOH) and a switching linear attention derived from online EM (§7.5, SwiLA). Together with §2.7's latency-aware pricing, these form the infra-economics backbone of the window.

### 7.1 Capture the lifecycle: KV Cache management in ReAct Agents with KVTether

| Field | Detail |

| **Authors** | Kaihua Fu, Yukun Zhou, Chaokun Chang, Yinghao Yu, Luping Wang, Guodong Yang, Jiuchen Shi, Quan Chen, Wei Wang |
| **Institution** | (industrial/academic *tentative*) |

### 7.3 PatchKV: Weight-Space Compensation of KV Cache

## 8. Time-Series & Sequential Modeling

> This wiki's original domain, and the window's most internally-consistent methodological message. **Two of these six papers are unfavourable to time-series foundation models**, and they are unfavourable in the precise, well-controlled way this literature needs: §8.1 finds released point forecasts barely beat chance-corrected references for *ranking* sparse events, with lightweight probes on frozen features beating the forecasts; §8.2 attributes a systematic part of industrial train–deploy gap to the **statistical priors embedded in the loss function** rather than to model capacity. The constructive entries (§8.3 MoE unification, §8.4 digital-twin topology) sit precisely in the gaps those two papers identify.

### 8.1 When, Not How Much: Evaluating Time-Series Foundation Models on Sparse Events

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Daniel Schoess, Florian von Wangenheim |
| **Institution** | (industry *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.LG) |
| **Abstract** | Pretrained **time-series foundation models (TSFMs)** are evaluated as **forecasters of future values**, yet for **sparse series many decisions depend only on which future periods contain activity**. Standard benchmarks do not assess this. On **five sparse datasets**, the authors **rank positions within forecast windows that contain both events and zeros**. The **released point forecasts of 12 TSFMs improve chance-corrected average precision over training-free references by at most 0.031**, and in **chance-corrected AUC the median TSFM falls below them on every dataset**. With **event supervision, linear probes of six frozen backbones improve on their backbone's point forecast in 29 of 30 backbone–dataset pairs**. **Averaging the predicted quantiles instead of taking their median** improves the ranking of most TSFMs that forecast the median, and on two datasets **the strongest such outputs rival the probes**. The probes' advantage over raw-context learners depends on the dataset, and under the same probe **pretrained features outperform randomly initialized ones for five of six backbones**. Conclusion: for sparse-event ranking, **released point forecasts add little over simple references**, whereas **lightweight event heads on frozen TSFMs rank events better** — and the best of them **exceed gradient-boosted trees trained on the raw context on three of five datasets**. More broadly, assessing pretrained forecasters beyond value forecasting requires reporting **their outputs, supervised probes of their representations, and raw-context and randomized controls side by side, since each supports a different conclusion**. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The task reframing is the contribution** — "when" (which periods have activity) is what sparse-series decisions actually need, and no standard benchmark measures it; (2) **the results are properly calibrated**: chance-corrected metrics (vs. event-prevalence baselines) and the median-TSFM-below-baseline finding are exactly what prevents the usual "foundation models beat baselines" artifact; (3) **the three-way comparison (released forecasts / probes on frozen features / raw-context and randomized controls) is a template for the whole field**, and the closing sentence is the paper's real thesis — different controls support different conclusions, so reporting only the flattering one is how this literature goes wrong; (4) **quantile-averaging beating median-forecasting for ranking** is a free, actionable finding for anyone deploying a distributional TSFM. For this wiki's rec line, note the analogue: many industrial decisions are "will this fire", not "how much". |

### 8.2 Beyond Model Ranking: Regime Diagnosis for Distributional-Statistical Misspecification in Industrial Time-Series Forecasting

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Pengyu Nie, Chenglang Xu, Yaoshi Chen, Chaogan Ren, Wei Hu, Chao Yang, Jiangong Zhang |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.LG) — *31 pages, 12 figures* |
| **Abstract** | Time-series forecasting models achieve strong benchmark performance but exhibit **severe systematic bias in industrial deployments**. This **train–deploy gap** is conventionally attributed to **temporal-structural errors or distribution shifts**. The paper characterizes a **complementary source these explanations overlook: canonical losses embed fixed statistical priors, while industrial demand mixes benign and pathological regimes — zero-inflation, skewness, high variability — in which these priors are systematically violated.** The induced bias **persists even under perfect temporal modeling**, remains in a **distributional-shape component that normalization cannot remove**, and creates an **aggregation trade-off invisible to aggregate metrics**. The authors present an evaluation toolkit centered on the **Regime-wise Relative Bias Vector (RBV)**: a **metric-agnostic, regime-decomposed diagnostic** auditing how pooled training allocates systematic mismatch across pathological subpopulations. A **controlled attribution analysis** decomposes RBV into a **model-independent intrinsic floor set by each loss's estimand** and an **excess component attributable to training**, tracing observed bias to the **loss rather than the model**. A large-scale study — **13 loss objectives, 3 seeds, 60,000+ series** spanning **RetailShiftBench and M5**, with **random-split controls** — shows regime-aware diagnosis **separates optimization-type from bias-type failure**, and that **regime-aware training resolves the pooling-induced bias that capacity scaling cannot**, for mean-type losses. A formal observation — **risk under evaluation-distribution contamination is affine in the pathology mixture weight** — grounds the findings. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The diagnosis relocates the problem from the model to the objective** — MAE/MSE imply distributional priors that industrial zero-inflated, skewed series violate, so some deployed bias is *baked in at training time* and no amount of backbone scaling removes it; (2) **the RBV decomposition (intrinsic floor from the loss's estimand vs. excess from training) is the reusable instrument**, and separating them is what allows attributing bias to the *objective* rather than the architecture; (3) **scale (13 objectives, 3 seeds, 60k+ series, random-split controls) makes the negative claim about capacity credible** — "regime-aware training resolves pooling-induced bias that capacity scaling cannot" is a strong claim that requires exactly this kind of controlled evidence; (4) **the affine-in-mixture-weight result** explains why aggregate metrics cannot see the problem: aggregate error is a mixture-weighted average that hides which regime is being systematically mispredicted. Strong methodological paper and the right companion to §8.1. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.40117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40117) |

| **Abstract** | Autoregressive models are trained to predict a system's behavior one step at a time, and recursive generation allows the learned dynamics to unfold over long horizons. ## 9. World Models, Embodied Agents & Physical Grounding

> Six papers on learning dynamics and acting in the world. The most valuable is also the most negative: §9.1 shows conventional world models **cannot complete rollouts** on cellular automata — a CNN gets 96.3% of cells right and finishes only 18.9% of rollouts — and then fixes it with three information-flow changes rather than architecture changes. §9.2's embodied frontier-model evaluation is equally blunt about the gap between task decisions and reliable physical control (0/5 on a locomotion course). §9.6 predicts a **critical communication degree** for multi-agent search and reports only mixed empirical agreement — a rare combination of theory plus honest disconfirmation.

### 9.1 Why Do Conventional World Models Fail to Learn Cellular Automata?

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Shaoyang Guo, Ziming Liu |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.AI) — *35 pages, 18 figures*; code and reproduction materials released |
| **Abstract** | Although conventional world models — autoregressive or diffusion models based on transformers or CNNs — may learn **surface statistics** of world dynamics, can they learn the **exact** dynamics from observed history? Using **cellular automata** as a simple testbed, the answer is **no in many cases**. Conventional architectures **predict most pixels correctly yet rarely complete a rollout**: a **CNN predicts 96.3% of cells but completes 18.9% of rollouts**; a **joint diffusion model completes none**. The authors trace the gap to three failure modes: failure to exactly capture **spatial locality, temporal locality, or temporal stability**. Simple changes repair each: (1) for **spatial locality**, **2D rotary positions** lift a transformer from **39.1% → 100%** on **Game of Life**; (2) for **temporal locality**, giving each token **its cell's previous-frame neighbourhood** lifts the same transformer from **25.8% → 99.9% on unseen rules**; (3) for **temporal stability**, **causal freezing** lifts the same diffusion weights from **42.2% → 99.9%**. **None of the three changes touches the architectural backbone; each only modifies the information flow within it.** The paper also compares **joint vs. ordered sampling** on billiards and, exploratorily, on a simulated Burgers equation. |
| **Key Innovations** | (1) **The pixel-accuracy-vs-rollout-completion gap is the finding that should change evaluation practice** — 96.3% cell accuracy coexisting with 18.9% rollout completion means per-step metrics systematically hide world models that cannot actually roll out, and a diffusion model scoring 0% completes nothing; (2) **each failure mode has a one-line fix that leaves the backbone untouched** — 2D RoPE for spatial locality, previous-frame neighbourhood tokens for temporal locality, causal freezing for temporal stability — which is a much more actionable diagnosis than "world models are hard"; (3) **generalization to unseen rules (25.8% → 99.9%)** shows the fix buys *dynamics* rather than memorizing a specific update table, which is the whole point of the testbed; (4) **cellular automata as the testbed is a methodological gift** — a system with known exact ground-truth dynamics and a scarcity of plausible-looking statistics, so it cannot be faked. Should be read as a diagnostic checklist anyone evaluating a world model should run. |

## 11. Scaling Laws, Continual Adaptation & the Governance Layer

> The window's "meta" section. Two papers take the mechanisms of this wiki's own subject apart: §11.1 shows **power-law learning curves are not properties of a model** but of how you schedule learning rate and batch size, with exact conditions under which a schedule *destroys* the power law; §11.2 gives an **asymptotically exact dynamical theory of LoRA in continual learning** and finds transfer saturates at the task's intrinsic dimensionality while forgetting keeps growing with rank. §11.3 is a landmark RAG-for-recommendation result (GENRE). The closing pair (§11.6, §11.5) is the strongest governance evidence in the window — a **37,561-reader randomized field experiment at The Washington Post** and a data-centre-grounded analysis of AI concentration and EU human rights.

### 11.1 From Spectra to Joint Schedules in LLM Pre-training: 3+3(+2) Scaling-Law Regimes

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yichen Wang, Fanghui Liu, Yudong Chen |
| **Institution** | (academic *tentative*) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.LG) |
| **Abstract** | **Power-law learning curves are often treated as fixed properties of a model and its data**, although **learning-rate and batch-size schedules can change the observed loss**. ## Cross-Cutting Patterns — What This Window Adds

**1. The pattern of the window is information-flow repairs, not architecture changes.** §9.1 fixes world models by changing what information reaches which token (2D RoPE, previous-frame neighbourhood, causal freezing) and explicitly notes **none of the three touches the backbone**. §9.4 supervises visual feature *change* instead of visual frames. §5.1 reuses attention weights already computed. §3.1 supervises the student's own states rather than the teacher's. §10.4 wins 10.5× by *not* solving the whole instance. If one methodological lesson dominates 2026-10-01, it is that the remaining failures in this literature are wiring problems, and the papers that find them name a specific primitive and change one line.

**2. Six negative results, and they are the most valuable papers here.** bm25 beats graph memory on one benchmark and loses on another, correctly attributed (§5.5); the standard IR recipe matches an LLM retrieval loop at half the cost (§5.3); goal-representation corruption barely matters while the state pathway matters twice as much (§10.6); a 2016 unitary recurrence matches GRUs (§10.5); a linear recurrence matches GRU *and* its world-model teacher (§R5); DLFP works on one model and not on three (§R5); graph attention looks active even when disabled (§R6). A window where this many papers damage their own field's defaults is a window where measurement has caught up.

**3. Every performance layer added for capability turned out to be an unexamined security boundary.** §6.5 finds shipped vLLM connectors erase KV provenance, silently dropping cross-adapter accuracy 0.94 → 0.64 and reasoning to *zero*. §6.4 finds sparse attention leaks through GPU page access patterns — 90.9% attribute inference, 87.3% response reconstruction. §6.1 finds approved action ≠ executed action in all six laundering classes. §6.2 finds benign models conceal credentials *because the rule banned plaintext, not character codes*. §6.3 finds debate amplifies unauthorized workspace reads while looking robust on answers. This is the strongest recurring theme in the window and it deserves its own tracked claim.

**4.
