## 1. CTR Prediction & Ranking Architectures

### 1.1 HELIX: Purified and Unified — Rethinking Feature Interaction and Sequence Modeling for Large-Scale Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yuntao Zheng, Miao Zhang, Yadong Ding, Yanchuan Tang, Lixiyu Chen, Hao Wang, Quan Li, Shiying Cai, Yue Lin, Jiayu Li, Yu Feng, Wentao Yang, Rongkun Xing, Jiekai Wang, Mingge Zhang, Feiling Gong, Xiang Gao, Jinyu Dong, Yajing Zhang, Pengfei Ren, Yinzhou Wang |
| **Institution** | **ByteDance / TikTok** *(inferred — arXiv lists no author block; the abstract states "Deployed in TikTok's e-commerce recommendation system")* |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) — technical report, 17 pages |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Industrial ranking models scale along two axes: **feature interaction** over heterogeneous user/item/context/cross features, and **sequence modeling** over long multi-type behavior histories. Scaling either in isolation is insufficient — each has a limited scaling ceiling and a suboptimal scaling-law slope. The authors conjecture a better slope requires **jointly scaling both axes**, and present **HELIX**: an architecture that **interleaves sequence retrieval and feature interaction while enforcing one-way information flow from reusable sequence states to candidate-conditioned mix-tokens**. This preserves cross-depth communication between the axes while keeping user-side sequence computation amortizable, enabling **flexible, asymmetric scaling** of sequence modeling vs. feature interaction. Offline CTR AUC, CVR AUC and other ranking metrics improve; online A/B gives **~6% lift in e-commerce video GMV per user**. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The thesis is the contribution, not the block.** Stating that both scaling axes individually saturate with poor slopes — and that the fix is co-scaling — reframes a decade of "which tower wins" argument as a joint-optimization problem. 2. The **one-way flow constraint** (sequence states → mix-tokens, never back) is the mechanism that makes co-scaling *affordable*: it keeps user-side computation amortizable across candidates, which is exactly the serving constraint that stops most unified-backbone designs from shipping. 3. **Asymmetric scaling** (sequence depth vs. interaction width decoupled) is the practical escape hatch from the parameter/latency lockstep that unified recommenders usually suffer. 4. **~6% GMV/user is a large, clean industrial number** — this is the strongest single online figure in this report and is reported without a confidence interval, so treat it as directional. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.37183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37183) |

### 1.2 ReST: From Language to Behavior — Scaling Sequence Transformers for Industrial Recommendation Ranking with Rec-Native Designs

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Jie Chen, Xiangqian Yu, Yanchao Lian, Tan Lu, Run Yang, Zhengchun Shang, Xing Wang, Cheng Chen, Ke Hu, Qiang Li, Tianjiu Yin, Xiaobing Liu |
| **Institution** | **ByteDance** |
| **Published** | 1 Sep 2026 (cs.IR, cs.AI, cs.LG) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Transplanting LLM scaling into production behavior-sequence ranking fails for two structural reasons. **Signal quality**: behavior sequences are noisy, temporally irregular, sparsely supervised. **Computation asymmetry**: each request scores *many candidates against one shared user history* under tight latency. ReST is a **recommendation-native Transformer scaling framework**. For signal quality: dual-gated attention, rotary positional + temporal embedding, stabilized residual normalization, training-only auxiliary objectives. For compute asymmetry: factorize ranking into a **heavy reusable encoder + lightweight cross decoder** with projection-free KV attention and token-specific parameterization, coupling **user-level shared-prefix training with shared-prefix serving** for *compute-once, decode-many-times* ranking. ReST scales more consistently along sequence length, depth and width where LLM-style blocks saturate. A one-week online A/B on a production advertising platform: **+1.31% online AUC, +11.93% on a core revenue metric, within a 50 ms P99 budget**. Fully deployed. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The two-failure diagnosis is the useful artifact.** Naming *computation asymmetry* (one history, N candidates) as distinct from LLM's one-sequence-one-output framing is why prior transplants underperform — and it dictates the encoder/decoder factorization. 2. **"Compute-once, decode-many-times"** is the correct serving-side primitive for ranking, and coupling it to training (shared-prefix) rather than bolting it on at inference is the non-obvious part. 3. **The 50 ms P99 constraint is stated up front**, which is unusual and makes the claims falsifiable — most CTR papers hide the serving budget. 4. **+11.93% revenue on +1.31% AUC** is a large revenue-per-AUC ratio, implying the gain is partly a *calibration/targeting* effect rather than pure ranking quality — worth interrogating before copying. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.01240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01240) |

### 1.3 DeRes: Decoupling Residual Stability and Adaptivity for Scalable CTR Prediction

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Wenzhuo Cheng, Shipeng Nie, Qixin Guo, Xuefeng Sun, Jianguo Lou, Zhengwei Zheng |
| **Institution** | **Google Research** (+ likely Google co-authors; work done during a Google Research internship per author block) |
| **Published** | 6 Jun 2026 (cs.IR) *(carryover)* |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Transformer-based CTR models hit a bottleneck at the **residual connection**: under Pre-Norm, early user-interest signals are diluted layer-by-layer; the identity skip cannot forget stale interests; each layer sees only its immediate predecessor, losing long-range cross-layer dependencies. Attention-based residual variants (**AttnRes**) fix parts of this in LMs but **drop the protective identity skip** and have not been tried in recommendation. Drawing on **Dual Path Networks** and the **HORNN** view of residuals, **DeRes** routes each layer through two parallel paths: an **Identity residual path** (preserves first-order feature reuse and gradient flow) and a **Block Attention Residual path** (attends over compressed outputs of *all* earlier blocks for high-order recall), with a **vector-wise gate** deciding per-hidden-dimension weights. **Pointwise AttnRes** replaces cross-layer Softmax with **SiLU** so multiple past blocks activate simultaneously and irrelevant ones get negative (forgetting) weights — better matched to CTR's parallel multi-interest structure. On 331M interactions from a major social platform, Criteo (45M) and Avazu (40M), DeRes beats twelve baselines (incl. OneTrans, TokenMixer-Large, UniMixer, mHC, AttnRes) by up to **+0.32% AUC at <5% extra FLOPs**, and fits a **steeper compute–AUC scaling law (γ=0.118 vs. 0.071 for OneTrans)** — an 8-layer DeRes matches a 16-layer OneTrans (~2× compute saving at equal AUC). |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The cleanest attack yet on Pre-Norm signal dilution as the CTR scaling bottleneck.** Reframing the residual block — not the attention operator — as the scaling constraint is a genuinely different diagnosis from the "unify everything" literature. 2. **Vector-wise gating, not scalar** — per-hidden-dimension path weights is the small detail that makes dual-path work here; it matches the observation that CTR interest structure is *per-feature*, not global. 3. **SiLU-instead-of-Softmax in cross-layer attention** is a clever mechanistic substitution: it converts a normalized weighted average (which can only convex-combine past blocks) into one that can *negatively weight* stale interests — directly addressing the "identity skip cannot forget" problem. 4. **Reporting the scaling-law exponent γ (0.118 vs 0.071) rather than just a single AUC point is the most valuable number in this section.** A 1.66× slope gap predicts future returns honestly; single operating points do not. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2606.07980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07980) |

### 1.4 FAT: From Scaling to Structured Expressivity — Rethinking Transformers for CTR Prediction

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Bencheng Yan, Yuejie Lei, Zhiyuan Zeng, Zheye Deng, Di Wang, Kaiyi Lin, Pengjie Wang, Chuan Yu, Jian Xu, Bo Zheng |
| **Institution** | **Alibaba Group / Taobao** (Taobao sponsored search) |
| **Published** | 15 Nov 2025 (v2 31 May 2026) (cs.IR, cs.LG) — **KDD 2026**, Jeju Island, DOI 10.1145/3770855.3818420 |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Despite massive investment in scale, deep CTR models show rapid diminishing returns — a stark contrast to LLMs' *predictable scaling laws*. Root cause identified as **fundamental structural misalignment**: standard Transformers assume **sequential compositionality**, whereas CTR data demands **combinatorial reasoning over heterogeneous fields**. **Field-Aware Transformer (FAT)** reconstructs the standard Transformer block with **field-centric parameters**, achieving *structured expressivity* and shifting model complexity dependence from total vocabulary size *n* (n ≫ F) to the number of fields **F**. To decouple capacity from field cardinality, FAT uses a **Basis-Composed Hypernetwork** to synthesize field-specific parameters from shared bases. Grounded in a **formal scaling law based on Rademacher complexity**. Empirically: up to **+4.38% AUC** over prior SOTA, **+2.33% CTR** and **+0.66% RPM** in live production. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The complexity-dependence swap is the whole paper.** Moving from O(n) vocabulary complexity to O(F) field complexity — with F ≪ n — is the first *explanatory* account of why CTR resists scaling, rather than another architecture patch. 2. **The mismatch framing (sequential-compositionality vs. combinatorial-fields) is falsifiable and testable**, and it explains why naive Transformer transplants disappoint while field-aware hybrids (DCN, xDeepFM lineage) never died. 3. **Rademacher-complexity grounding** is rare in applied CTR work and makes the scaling law an actual theorem rather than a fitted curve. 4. **+4.38% AUC is very large** — the largest reported AUC delta in this report — and it ships live on Taobao sponsored search. Caveat: FAT is a **2025 paper revised for KDD 2026**, so it predates HELIX/ReST and those papers do not cite it as resolved; whether the *structured expressivity* thesis and the *joint co-scaling* thesis compose is an open question. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2511.12081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12081) |

### 1.5 UniCon: A Unified Context-Centric Modeling Paradigm for CTR Prediction

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Jiajun Cui, Zhengqi Xu, Fan Zhang, Zhangteng, Gu Tang, Honghong Zhu, Mengxi Wu, Yulin Liang, Xingxing Wang |
| **Institution** | **Meituan** (search advertising) |
| **Published** | 3 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) — 10 pages |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Unified modeling is a major direction in industrial CTR, but existing approaches unify sequential and non-sequential signals **at the token level** and share a backbone. This division **originates from legacy feature-engineering practice and is misaligned with the underlying decision process**. The claim: user behavior is inherently a sequence of **homogeneous context units**; at the input-organization level, historical behavior and the current request differ **only in whether their outcomes are observed or pending prediction**. Treating them as heterogeneous signals obscures structural dependencies inside the user's decision context, limiting scaling efficiency and prediction quality — especially in **context-rich surfaces** (e-commerce shelves, waterfall feeds). **UniCon** makes the **request context the basic modeling unit**, organizing history and targets as homogeneous context units. **Intra-context attention** captures local item coupling (**Locality**); **inter-context attention** models dynamic evolution of decision states across contexts (**Dynamics**). Context-unit-level sequence compression cuts deployment overhead. On Meituan search ads: **+0.0139 offline AUC** over a strong production baseline; 7-day A/B (20% traffic) gives **+3.09% RPM, +2.07% CTR, +2.95% revenue**. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **This is the sharpest anti-legacy critique in the report.** "Sequential vs. non-sequential is a feature-engineering artifact, not a property of the decision problem" reframes the token-level unification that OneTrans/HELIX/ReST all build on — they unify the two signal types; UniCon argues they shouldn't be typed separately at all. 2. **The Locality/Dynamics decomposition** is a clean inductive-bias statement that maps onto the actual structure of feed/shelf decisions. 3. **Naming the surfaces where it matters most** (context-rich: shelves, waterfalls) is the honest scoping — this is a *context* paper, not a general CTR paper. 4. **The online numbers are unusually complete** (RPM + CTR + revenue, with traffic split and duration disclosed) — the highest-provenance online evidence in this report alongside HELIX. |

### 1.6 RECAP: Beyond Interaction Capacity — Estimator Scaling with Recursive Models for CTR Prediction

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Shivang Chopra, Fotis Iliopoulos, Zsolt Kira, Gaurav Menghani |
| **Institution** | **Google Research** + **Georgia Institute of Technology** (work done during internship at Google Research) |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.LG, cs.AI) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | CTR prediction models interactions among sparse categorical features. Explicit **cross networks** are the central paradigm, and recent progress has come from **increasing the interaction capacity of a single predictor** (deeper cross networks, more expressive cross operators). This paper asks whether that remains the most effective lever — and finds its benefits hit **diminishing returns even as capacity keeps growing**. That motivates a complementary scaling direction the authors call **estimator scaling**: spend additional resources incorporating **multiple related estimators** rather than enlarging one predictor. Theory shows the gains are governed by the **amount of non-shared predictive variation across estimators**. Naive exploitation is expensive (independently trained ensembles grow deployment cost), motivating a **parameter-efficient realization**. **RECAP** (RECursive Averaged Predictor) operationalizes estimator scaling at three levels: **distillation across independently trained models**, **exponential moving averaging over training trajectories**, and **aggregation over inference-time routes within a weight-shared recursive backbone**. New SOTA on standard benchmarks with a favorable performance–parameter Pareto frontier. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **This is the theoretical counterweight to §1.1–1.5.** HELIX/ReST say "scale both axes together", FAT says "fix the structural mismatch", DeRes says "the residual block is the bottleneck" — RECAP says *all of these are scaling a single predictor, and that axis is saturating*. The "estimator scaling vs. interaction capacity" dichotomy is the most interesting open framing in industrial CTR right now. 2. **The governing quantity — non-shared predictive variation across estimators — is measurable**, which makes this falsifiable rather than a slogan. 3. **Three-level recursion (cross-model distillation → EMA over trajectory → inference-route aggregation inside a weight-shared backbone)** is an unusually cheap way to buy ensemble diversity without ensemble deployment cost. 4. **Caveat**: the abstract does not disclose online A/B results — this is offline-benchmark SOTA only, and the parameter-count advantage over an equally-large single predictor is the load-bearing claim, not the AUC. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.37905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37905) |

## 2. Generative Recommendation & Semantic IDs

### 2.1 GEAR: Generative End-to-end Ad Retrieval at Douyin

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Shaowen Zeng, Yanhua Huang, Jiacheng Sun, Jiarui Liu, Qian Dai, Zhikai Yang, Hancheng Li, Boya Wu, Tuoyu Zhang, Yekui Chen, Xiang Sun |
| **Institution** | **ByteDance / Douyin Ads** *(confirmed by abstract)* |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.IR) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Generative retrieval recasts recommendation as generating discrete item tokens. Scaling it into a real recommender exposes two critical, **inherently coupled** bottlenecks: **representation collapse** (the item tokenizer degenerates under continuous distribution shift, blocking stable end-to-end adaptation) and **item collisions** (the huge candidate pool makes distinct items share identical token sequences, capping retrieval precision). Expanding codebook capacity to fix collisions **inevitably worsens collapse**. **GEAR** is an end-to-end framework jointly optimizing **tokenizer, generator and reranker**. For collapse: **BasisVQ** re-parameterizes the codebook via an **orthogonal basis** to enable *global gradient sharing* and **rigid spatial rotation** of the latent space — stabilizing gradient dynamics **without ad-hoc heuristics** — extended to prefix-aware **BasisRQ** at the same asymptotic complexity. For collisions: a **context-conditioned reranking head inside the generative process** disambiguating colliding items at minimal cost. Fully differentiable, scalable; serves **hundreds of millions of DAU on Douyin Ads**. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The first production generative-retrieval result on an ads surface at consumer scale** — the whole TIGER → OneRec → FineSID arc lands in one live artifact, and on the *ad* side rather than the organic-feed side. 2. **Stating collapse↔collision as a coupled trade-off** (bigger codebook ⇒ worse collapse) converts every prior "increase codebook size" result into a trade rather than a win. This is the theoretical spine the Semantic-ID literature was missing. 3. **BasisVQ's orthogonal-basis reparameterization for *global* gradient sharing** is a heuristic-free stabilizer that likely transfers to other VQ bottlenecks (audio, video, motion tokens). 4. **Caveat**: the abstract says "substantial empirical improvements" with **no percentages**. The deployment scale is the evidence; the magnitude is unquantified. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.39327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39327) |

### 2.2 FineSID: Scalable and Efficient Semantic Identifier Learning for Generative Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Song-Li Wu, Weinan Gan, Zhaocheng Du, Xianquan Wang, Jingyi Wang |
| **Institution** | **Tsinghua University**, **Huawei Noah's Ark Lab**, **University of Science and Technology of China** |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.AI) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | A critical prerequisite of generative recommendation is **semantic identifiers (SIDs)** that are both scalable to large item sets and efficiently learnable. Existing methods fundamentally rely on **Top-1 hard assignment** during vector quantization. Heuristics — clustering-based initialization, forced post-hoc collision resolution — can inflate codebook coverage but **disrupt end-to-end semantic alignment** and leave the underlying optimization bottleneck unaddressed: **sparse gradient propagation**. Under Top-1, gradients concentrate on a narrow subset of frequently selected codewords, leaving the majority under-trained and causing severe SID collisions. **FineSID** moves beyond Top-1 by enabling **fine-grained gradient propagation across the entire codebook** — distributing learning signals to *all* codewords in a soft, differentiable manner, promoting globally balanced codebook optimization while **strictly preserving semantic consistency**. Robust to initialization, improves both codebook utilization and recommendation accuracy on multiple public benchmarks. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **Correctly diagnoses the root cause rather than the symptom.** "Sparse gradient propagation from Top-1 assignment" is the mechanism behind SID collisions; every prior fix (better init, collision merging) treats the symptom and breaks semantic alignment. 2. **Initialization-agnostic** is the practical selling point — the line has accumulated a thicket of init heuristics, and removing that dependency makes the recipe portable. 3. **Read alongside GEAR (§2.1)**: FineSID attacks collisions from the *quantizer's learning dynamics*; GEAR attacks them from *reparameterization + reranking*. These compose in principle. FineSID's *open* problem is collapse, which GEAR addresses and FineSID does not. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.36670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36670) |

### 2.4 KuaFu: Compressing Long User Behavior into Understanding at Billion Scale

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Jiahao Hui, Lin Zhu, Yishen Hu, Jingdong Shu, Zetai Jiang, Xining Ran, Ben Tan, Yeshou Cai, Gong Chen, Haijie Gu, Jie Jiang |
| **Institution** | **Tencent** (advertising & recommendation platform) |
| **Published** | 25 Sep 2026 (cs.IR, cs.CL, cs.LG) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Conversational agents, generative recommenders and personalized ads all rest on one capability: **understanding each user from raw behavior**. Industrial practice is **task-specific** — extract a relevant subsequence per task, train a dedicated model — hitting two production bottlenecks: (1) even after filtering a single-task sequence is extremely long (content-interest summarization reads several hundred items/user, tens of thousands of tokens serialized as prompt text); (2) **profiles refresh routinely — a billion users weekly, ~100K QPM aggregate**, which under fixed GPU budget sets a hard throughput floor. Compression is mandatory, but **truncation or coarse compression silently distorts the profile**, introducing four hallucination types — **fabrication, omission, date misattribution, broken logic** — which, with no way to evaluate the compressed representation itself, surface only as diffuse downstream degradation. **KuaFu** is a unified behavior-compression layer whose minimal unit is **one behavior item**: a **two-axis projector** compresses each item into **2–4 tokens of width 128–256** (~10× token axis, ~20× width; per-item cache **10 KB → 0.5 KB**), with fidelity-oriented **four-stage training** and **layered intermediate evaluation**. Across four production profiling tasks it matches or exceeds uncompressed single-task models on all five headline metrics, raises per-GPU throughput **37%–350%**, saves **190 GPUs**. On public benchmarks it beats prior compressors at matched compression ratio (up to **+17.7 EM** on out-of-domain MRQA); on RecBench a **4B model surpasses its 8B counterpart by 1.90 points**. Running on Tencent's platform for **ten months**, lifting overall **GMV by 1.37%**. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The per-item compression unit is the right abstraction** and it's not obvious. Prior work compresses whole sequences; KuaFu's 10 KB → 0.5 KB *per item* is what makes billion-user weekly refresh affordable, and it's why the compression is auditable rather than a black box. 2. **Naming four specific hallucination modes** (fabrication / omission / date misattribution / broken logic) and building **layered intermediate evaluation** addresses the paper's own best point: compressed representations have been unevaluable, so compression errors were invisible until they showed up as metric noise. 3. **The 4B-beats-8B result on RecBench** is the cleanest single number here — compression that improves quality, not just throughput. 4. **+1.37% GMV over ten months** is a modest but *long-horizon* production figure, and the ten-month duration is itself evidence the system is stable rather than a short A/B spike. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.31045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31045) |

## 3. Sequential Modeling & Long Sequences

### 3.1 SequenceO1: End-to-End Ultra-Long (100K) Sequence Modeling with Low-Rank Caching

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Lin Guan, Jia-Qi Yang, Zhishan Zhao, Jiaqi Huang, Hangyu Wang, Longbin Li, Beichuan Zhang, Haonan Jiang, Jinan Ni, Xiangyu Fan, Xiaowen Li, Ziyao Ren, Yuhang Qi, Xiaolong Zhu, Xuanyuan Luo, Qiwei Chen, Yi Cheng, Lele Yu |
| **Institution** | **ByteDance / Douyin** |
| **Published** | 8 Sep 2026 (cs.IR, cs.AI) — **RecSys '26 Industry Track, long oral**; production deployment on Douyin |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Long-term behavior modeling is central to sequential recommendation and billion-scale industrial ranking, yet production models face strict **latency, memory, communication and training-throughput** constraints. At the 100K scale the problem is no longer attention complexity: **raw sequence features must be stored, transferred, and repeatedly processed** during training *and* online serving. Prior approaches (history truncation, multi-stage behavior retrieval, compressed lifelong histories, train-short/infer-long extrapolation) either weaken end-to-end optimization or retain length-dependent cost. **SequenceO1** is an end-to-end framework for **100K-interaction histories**, deployed at full Douyin traffic, following a **compress-then-reason** design. **Sketch Attention (SA)** uses learnable prototypes + prototype-wise normalization to compress raw history into a **fixed-size, target-agnostic user representation**. **Target-conditioned Stacked Target-to-History Cross Attention (STCA)** then models complementary time scales: a **recent 10K suffix** for short-term interest plus the compact sketch for long-term preference. Practicality comes from **low-rank user-representation caching**, **multi-request user-level batching**, **pipeline lift**, and a **fused FlashSA kernel** — amortizing feature storage, communication and computation across targets, training instances and consecutive requests. ### 8.1 The Evolution of Attention in LLMs: Mechanisms, Trade-offs, and Emerging Trends

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Zhentao Tan, Jingyi Shen, Yanbo Li, Yao Liu, Yue Wu, Jieping Ye |
| **Institution** | **Alibaba Token Hub, Alibaba Group** (+ co-authors; author block lists institutional affiliations not fully enumerated) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.CL) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Self-attention gives LLMs fine-grained query-dependent access to context, but dense token interactions incur **quadratic prefill cost** and a **KV cache growing with context length**. Research spans **explicit-memory compression, sparse access, recurrent state construction, structured state dynamics, and heterogeneous mechanism composition**. This survey analyzes these developments as **model-internal contextual memory**, introducing a **five-dimensional lens — Memory Representation, Memory Update, Access, Readout, Integration** — describing what is represented, how it changes, what is query-eligible, how it is read, and how readouts form outputs. ### 8.2 NAMOH: Scaling Parameter and Context in Attention — Native Sparse Attention from Mixture-of-Head

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Zizhuo Fu, Runsheng Wang, Meng Li |
| **Institution** | **Peking University** |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.CL) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Scaling attention parameters improves LM quality, but retaining **full token histories** makes additional heads **costly at long contexts** — and since attention *retrieves and combines* contextual information, **parameter scaling should also support longer contexts**. The paper asks whether **attention parameter scaling can directly enable efficient and effective context scaling**, and introduces **NAMOH**, an **architecture-native sparse attention** mechanism activating **K of H heads per token**. Each head retains **only its assigned tokens** and performs causal attention **within that subsequence**. Head selection thus **jointly determines active parameters and available context** without scanning full history. Under balanced assignments, increasing H at fixed K **shortens head histories** and reduces per-token KV access **without increasing total KV storage**. They further support **head-relative RoPE** to shorten positional spans within routed subsequences, mitigating position-induced attention noise. NAMOH can **outperform fully-activated models with the same total parameters**, and enables more efficient long-context inference than smaller dense models with matched active parameters. Compatible with **GQA** and existing sparse attention mechanisms. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **The reframe is the contribution: head selection as *jointly* routing parameters and context.** Existing sparse attention routes *tokens*; NAMOH routes *heads*, so a head's available context is a consequence of its routing. This makes parameter scaling and context scaling the same knob — hence "outperforms the fully-activated model with the same total parameters." 2. **Growing H at fixed K gives longer-context capability at *constant* total KV storage** — the practical claim that matters, and it inverts the usual accuracy/cost trade. 3. **Head-relative RoPE for routed subsequences** is the correct positional fix and dovetails with T-RoPE's finding (§3.3) that standard RoPE is *translation-invariant* and therefore cannot express time — a routed subsequence needs its own local position origin. 4. **Compatibility with GQA and existing sparse mechanisms** is what makes it adoptable rather than a parallel universe. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.38832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38832) |

### 8.3 SwiLA: Switching Linear Attention

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Hyun Dong Lee, Xavier Gonzalez, Nicolas Zucchet, E. Kelly Buchanan, Emily B. Fox, Scott W. Linderman |
| **Institution** | **Stanford University** (work done at Stanford; one author now at Unconventional AI) |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL) — **COLM 2026** |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Designing expressive sequence layers with efficient inference is a central challenge. **Softmax attention** performs excellently through rich nonlinear token interactions but requires a **KV cache growing linearly with sequence length**. **Linear attention** enables recurrent computation with a **constant memory footprint**, yet its **reduced expressivity** often yields inferior modeling. **Switching Linear Attention (SwiLA)** bridges the gap, enhancing representational capacity while retaining the **fixed-size recurrent state** of linear attention. The recurrence is derived from the **test-time regression** framework, casting the state update rule as **online expectation-maximization in a mixture-of-linear-regressions** model. At test time, each **output dimension dynamically selects among multiple linear attention components based on the input**. Across **associative recall, in-context language learning, and language modeling** benchmarks, SwiLA shows strong performance, **narrows the gap to softmax attention, and surpasses it in several settings**. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **Deriving the recurrence from test-time regression is the elegant move.** Framing linear attention as online EM in a MoLR model means "more components" is not an arbitrary capacity knob — it's the standard, well-understood solution to a mixture-of-regressions inference problem. Theory and architecture are the same object. 2. **Per-output-dimension input-dependent switching** is the expressivity fix, and it is *free* with respect to the constant-memory guarantee — unlike adding depth (which breaks constant state) or widening. 3. **Surpassing softmax attention on some benchmarks while keeping a fixed-size state** is the headline; if it holds at scale it changes the serving cost model, since constant memory means no KV-cache growth at all. 4. **The recurring theoretical pattern in this report is notable** — SwiLA (test-time regression), NAMOH (§8.2, routing), T-RoPE (§3.3, invariance analysis), RWML (§6.4, embedding-space prediction). The current wave is characterized by *deriving the architecture from a solved inference problem* rather than by search. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.39034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39034) |

### 8.4 CoEM: Empowering Long-Context Reasoning with Commit-on-Evidence Memory

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Jingguang Li, Yebo Wu, Zuyi Guo, Kailang Ma, Xianjie Dai, Han Zheng, Benwang Chen, Li Li, Can Rong, Heye Huang |
| **Institution** | **KAIST**, **University of Macau**, **MIT** |
| **Published** | 29 Sep 2026 (cs.AI, cs.CL) — 38 pages; code at github.com/benmagnifico/CoEM |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Long-context reasoning is essential for complex long-horizon tasks, yet LLM performance **degrades as context length increases**. Recent approaches process input **chunk by chunk while maintaining a bounded textual memory** in context — but **premature information compression can discard critical details** essential for later reasoning. **Commit-on-Evidence Memory (CoEM)** learns **when** to convert source evidence into compact memory facts. Under a fixed context-memory budget, CoEM **preserves potentially useful source excerpts verbatim in a *pending* set**, letting subsequent context clarify their relevance **before irreversible compression**. As new context arrives, a **learned policy** revisits each pending excerpt and decides whether to **promote** it to committed memory, **retain** it, or **discard** it. A **frozen verifier** ensures proposed facts are accepted only if supported by retained excerpts and current context. The policy is trained with **RL combining fine-grained step-level evidence rewards with final answer rewards**. Experiments: on **6,400-document** long-context input, CoEM **outperforms the strongest memory baseline by 10.4–11.4 F1 points** on Qwen3.5-9B. |
| **Key Innovations** *(analyst read)* | 1. **Two-stage memory (pending → committed) solves the irreversibility problem.** This is the key insight: the failure mode of bounded textual memory is not that compression is lossy, it's that compression is **premature and irreversible**. Deferring the write decision until relevance is known costs only a pending-set budget. 2. **The learned policy + frozen verifier split is well-designed**: the *policy* learns *when to write* (a judgment problem), the *verifier* enforces *what is supported* (a correctness constraint). Asking one model to do both is the usual mistake. 3. **Step-level evidence rewards + answer rewards** is the same dense-plus-sparse combination as LongRLVR (§5.7) — a converging pattern across two independent groups. 4. **+10.4–11.4 F1 over the strongest baseline is large**, and the 6,400-document setting is long enough that the mechanism is actually stressed. **Caveat**: single model family (Qwen3.5), single context length regime. |
| **Link** | [arXiv:2609.36935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36935) |

### 8.5 V-Skip: Look Less, Reason More — Block-wise Attention Skipping for Efficient Multimodal LLMs

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Jie Ma, Zhike Qiu, Jiayi Ji, Xiaoshuai Sun, Rongrong Ji |
| **Institution** | **Xiamen University** |
| **Published** | 7 Jun 2026 (cs.CV) *(carryover)* |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | MLLMs face an inference bottleneck from the **quadratic cost of self-attention over long visual token sequences**. Visual tokens **rapidly establish spatial structure and intra-modal relationships in early layers**, making visual-to-visual self-attention in **deeper layers computationally redundant**. ### 8.6 RoPE at the End of Its Rope? Theory, Diagnosis, and Mitigation of Long-Context Failures

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Authors** | Yuyang Wu, Yufeng Du, Hao Peng |
| **Institution** | **Independent Researcher** (Yuyang Wu) and **University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign** (Yufeng Du, Hao Peng) *(from the paper's own author block in the arXiv HTML)* |
| **Published** | 30 Sep 2026 (cs.LG, cs.CL) |
| **Abstract** *(condensed)* | Long-context failures of RoPE-based models trace to an **intrinsic tradeoff** between stable token preferences and distinguishing nearby positions. The authors extend prior theory by **allowing unequal query–key scales across RoPE frequencies**, which matches empirical observations better than symmetric assumptions. ## 9. Cross-Cutting Synthesis

### 9.1 Four independent answers to "fixed-rate compute is the real bottleneck"

The strongest single pattern in this report, stated independently by four groups in four different modalities:

| Paper | Modality | Diagnosis | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DP-Rec** (§3.2) | sequential recommendation | Uniform per-item compute regardless of information content | **Contrastive-entropy-surprise segmentation** into patches; compute per patch, not per item |
| **RouteRec** (§3.4) | sequential recommendation | All sessions through identical parameterized blocks | **Behavior-derived routing** (tempo, item-group focus, repetition, popularity) at macro/mid/micro scopes |
| **Looped Actor** (§5.4) | RL policies | Fixed-runtime policies waste compute on easy states | **Weight-tied depth recurrence with state-adaptive loop count** |
| **V-Skip** (§8.5) | multimodal LLMs | Deep-layer visual self-attention is redundant | **Block-wise attention skipping**, task-adaptive via few-shot calibration |
| *(related)* **T-RoPE** (§3.3) | sequential recommendation | LLM positional machinery ignores elapsed time | **Timestamp-based rotation breaking time-translation invariance** |

**Why this matters.** Each paper reports gains within its own benchmark, so none of them establishes the cross-domain claim. But if the *diagnosis* is right — that per-instance/per-item compute allocation, not capacity, is the binding constraint — then the four mechanisms are four instances of one algorithmic primitive, and any system that allocates compute uniformly is leaving performance on the table. **Confidence: moderate-high on the diagnosis, low on any specific mechanism's generality.** (single-source per modality)

### 9.2 The critic-free consensus is contested, and the reconciliation axis is reward latency

- **Critic-free**: Autobidding GRPO (§4.4) removes the critic because alternating actor-critic training is *unstable and noise-fragile*; OneBid's CROP (§4.1) uses a learned critic *only* for offline group-relative scoring.
- **Critic-valuable**: RFPO (§5.3) argues a pretrained critic provides dense per-prefix outcome signals, that critic-free *long-CoT* instability is an **optimization artifact** fixable by small low-variance updates, and that binarized RFPO **matches supervised PPO with zero labels**.
- **Reconciliation** *(analyst synthesis, not claimed by either side)*: the right axis is **reward latency**. When a verifier terminates the episode (RLVR on code/math), the critic adds cost without adding information. When outcomes arrive *after* long generation (long-horizon agents, long-context reasoning), the critic is the only source of intermediate signal — and it can even reward *unfinished* rollouts, changing the cost model. Both camps are right on their own regime.

**Actionable prediction**: agent/recsys RLVR will increasingly keep a critic for long-horizon tasks while dropping it for verifiable-short tasks. *(tentative)*

### 9.3 "LLM machinery transfers to recommendation" is now under active attack

Three independent pieces of evidence against blanket transfer, from three different directions:

1. **Kuaishou (§2.3)**: natural-language CoT **does not always improve** recommendation performance — stated as a preliminary negative result, with structured templates + RL needed to make reasoning pay off.
2. **T-RoPE (§3.3)**: proved that RoPE **on timestamps is still time-translation invariant** — i.e. the most obviously applicable LLM mechanism is *provably* the wrong inductive bias for time-aware recommendation.
3. **ReST (§1.2)**: recommendation has structural properties LLM modeling does not — **computation asymmetry** (one history, N candidates) and worse **signal quality** — which is why naive transplants need purpose-built fixes.

**Counterpoint from §9.1**: the *meta* pattern transfers well. Uniform-compute allocation is a shared problem, and mechanisms like adaptive routing/patching likely transfer. **So: architectural primitives transfer; alignment and position assumptions do not.** *(high confidence — three independent sources)*

### 9.4 Industrial papers are getting better at disclosing, with a consistent bias toward headline metrics

- **Best provenance** (§1.5 UniCon): traffic percentage, A/B duration, three complementary metrics (RPM + CTR + revenue).
- **Best reporting discipline** (§4.5 Pinch of SFT): reports the regression, CIs, and a leakage audit.
- **Best scale evidence** (§2.1 GEAR): "hundreds of millions of DAU," but **no effect size**.
- **Recurring omission**: 6 of 8 advertising papers (§4.1, §4.2, §4.3, §4.4, §4.6, and §4.7 in part) disclose *no online A/B percentage* in the abstract, despite claiming deployment. OneBid says "fully deployed"; LangBP says "business gains"; ADAPT and Autobidding GRPO are benchmark-only.
- **Also under-reported**: no paper in §4 discloses its *compute cost* or serving budget, except ReST (§1.2, 50 ms P99) and SequenceO1 (§3.1, implicitly). For industrial ranking this is the number that determines deployability.

**Guidance**: treat "fully deployed / substantial improvements" without numbers as *existence* evidence, not *magnitude* evidence.

### 9.5 Two self-improvement lines are pulling in opposite directions

- **Optimistic** (§6.1 PhantomEnvironments, §6.2 Turbo Harness): free synthetic environments transfer to real benchmarks; per-instance harness adaptation beats global harnesses; world models can be trained without labels or judges (§6.4 RWML).
- **Skeptical** (§7.4 GameBoyWorlds): **world modelling and autonomous skill discovery fail** in embodied games; curiosity-based guide-writing achieves only partial success; sub-50% baseline on frontier models; the bottleneck is **multimodal grounding**.

**Reading**: the optimistic results are all in **text**; the negative results are all **perceptual**. Self-improvement machinery currently works where the observation space is symbolic and fails where it is visual. **Open and load-bearing question.** *(tentative — 2 sources, opposite modalities)*

### 9.6 What to read first, and why

If you read only five, read these — maximum coverage of the report's claims:

1. **§1.1 HELIX** (2609.37183) — the strongest industrial scaling thesis + a real GMV number.
2. **§1.4 FAT** (2511.12081) — the only *theoretical* account of why CTR resists scaling (+4.38% AUC live).
3. **§2.1 GEAR** (2609.39327) — the collapse↔collision trade-off that reorganizes the entire Semantic-ID line.
4. **§5.1 CATCH** (2609.39533) — CoT monitors are themselves hackable; changes how you should evaluate RLVR safety.
5. **§4.5 Pinch of SFT, A Dash of RL** (2609.22194) — the best methodology in applied agent/ads RL, including a counterintuitive leakage result.

### 9.7 Gaps in this sweep

- **No RLHF-era scaling-law or data-mixture papers** surfaced; this report's LLM coverage is post-training/agents/attention-heavy, so §5's absence of pretraining-data and tokenizer work is a *sampling* gap, not a field-level gap.
- **No papers on LLM serving infrastructure** (vLLM/SGLang kernel work) except through the attention-efficiency lens (§8).
- **3 mechanism-design papers** (§4.6 LAMA, §4.7 Inference Auctions, §4.8 ROI-constrained auctions) — this line is thinly represented relative to §1–§2 and likely under-searched by keyword rather than genuinely quiet.
- **No retrieval-augmented-generation papers** despite KuaFu (§2.4) and CoEM (§8.4) both being memory-shaped; RAG as a category was not queried.
- **Single-author entry** (§7.1) and a single Workshop paper (§5.2) — flagged as lower-review-threshold venues, weighted accordingly in §9.
