# arXiv AI Search 2026-10-21 — Wednesday's fresh block, siblings-first

> **Wednesday run, same-day collision.** This report ran 10:10–10:30 AM, about an hour behind the two sibling reports published this morning: [`arxiv-daily` 2026-10-21](arxiv-daily.md) (10:19) and [`arxiv-paper-check` 2026-10-21](arxiv-paper-check.md) (10:25). This report therefore features only the **unclaimed remainder** (17 full + 6 condensed + 2 one-liners), and cross-references the sibling claims in §4.4 rather than re-running them.
>
> The visible announcement block at run time is the **2026-10-19 / 2026-10-20 submission wave** (the 10-21 block had not posted; matching the sibling reports' observation). Channel: arXiv **Export API**, paced 15–16 s apart — 5 category queries × 200 most-recent (`cs.IR`, `cs.CL`, `cs.AI`, `cs.LG`, `cs.MA`) + 5 topical sweeps (LLM×recommendation, recommendation/ads, sequential behaviour, retrieval-agent, games/MAS terms), then per-paper affiliation fetches from `arxiv.org/html`. **946 unique papers parsed; 523 new-to-wiki in the 10-19/10-20 band (157 + 366); 26 claimed by same-day siblings; 497 left, of which 19 are cs.IR.**
>
> **Four threads run through the unclaimed remainder.**
>
> **① The unclaimed recommendation lane is no longer about new rankers — it is about *measurement* and *retrieval efficiency*.** The academic rec/CTR-architecture frontier was claimed by the siblings (Instagram two-term debiasing, EASformer, ECHO, SPAR, SID topology/unlearning, AGR, FLMNet, Decision4Rec — §4.4). What is left is a coherent evaluation cluster: [`2610.27850`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27850) (DPFR, TORS 2026) shows existing fairness/relevance measures are **not reliable proxies for Pareto-optimal joint evaluation** — 4 recommenders × 3 fairness-aware re-rankers × 6 datasets disagree with the frontier; [`2610.28054`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28054) (AutoSim, SIGIR-AP 2026) shows the user-simulator half of evaluation is *fixable by weight-tuning but not by prompting* — no prompt separates real from simulated sessions better than AUC 0.85, fine-tuning drives it to **0.45**, and a prompted simulator **over-estimates retrieval effectiveness by +0.136 nDCG** and returns a relevant passage on 57% of turns where real users find none; [`2610.26260`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26260) (NevIR) separates *pairwise preference* from *positional utility* — a pairwise-trained ranker improves strict preference (52.3% → 56.6%) while **halving** preferred-passage MRR (0.576 → 0.306); [`2610.27829`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829) (GARRET) attacks the ground-truth granularity problem for visual-document RAG, training region-level retrievers from (page, Q, A) triplets without query-to-region annotations. On the efficiency side, [`2610.27273`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27273) (FLINT, Berkeley × UIUC) pools ColBERT-style late interaction down to a **fixed vector budget on both sides** — 1.5–2.8× faster end-to-end, **5.3–9.3× smaller** than ColBERTv2, at parity or better on BEIR, BrowseComp+, CRUMB and TREC Tip-of-the-Tongue 2025. **Explicit CTR-prediction papers left unclaimed: 0.** FLMNet [`2610.26143`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26143) (10-19) did end the eight-consecutive-window CTR drought recorded by the [10-20 run](../2026-10-20/arxiv-ai-search.md) — but it belongs to [`arxiv-daily`](arxiv-daily.md), so `[[ctr-scaling-landscape]]` still gains no entrant from this report.
>
> **② Test-time compute becomes a denominated currency: joules, tokens, API calls.** Yesterday's ATEST energy paper and today's unclaimed trio form a cluster fast. [`2610.28576`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28576) (Token-Energy Law, NVIDIA × Texas Tech × TACC) prices decode energy per output token in two coordinates — **active bytes** (shared dense weights vs private per-request KV histories) and **realized decode time** — in one affine form per model/hardware config, held-out mean error **≤3.73%** on Hopper and Blackwell BF16. [`2610.28409`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28409) (Apple × Oxford) trains Qwen3.5 agents to *spend* budgets they are told about, not just to succeed: 11 reward formulations over tokens/API calls/steps on AppWorld and BabyAI, with a **sigmoid overspend penalty** most often winning within-budget success and a **fixed negative penalty** winning natural compliance, zero-shot transferring to unseen budgets and a new benchmark. [`2610.28294`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28294) (ARR, Oklahoma × Stanford × NUST) stops paying for revision when revision hits a fixed point: replay decoding verifies unchanged tokens in parallel to detect the fixed point, then reallocates the freed compute to an independent resample — MATH500 **84.6% at 41% of majority voting's serial passes**, up to **2.4×** fewer critical-path passes across 7 models × 7 benchmarks. The same budget instinct shows up in decoding: BitMTP [`2610.28500`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28500) (PolyU, NeurIPS 2026) fuses multi-token prediction into ultra-low-bit backbones (**2.2×** on BitNet b1.58-2B), and AMTP [`2610.28619`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28619) (Georgia Tech) learns the speculation horizon online per context (**up to 4.1×**, +10% throughput in 8/10 settings).
>
> **③ The consolidation math keeps tightening: merging and distillation are one objective, and the write-back must be gated.** [`2610.26525`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26525) (ZJU × Meituan LongCat × NJU × SJTU) proves that within a shared basin, multi-teacher on-policy distillation is **to second order exactly the Fisher-merging objective** — so the closed-form Fisher merge is the optimal stage-1 consolidation, and its RGBM procedure (task-vector-subspace merge, then a residual spectrum deciding which domains may train an isolated branch and write back with calibrated shrinkage) beats fully-trained multi-teacher OPD with **zero extra joint-training steps**, while *every* ungated write-back — including continued joint distillation from the merge — falls below it. [`2610.28229`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28229) (TPO, Tongji, NeurIPS 2026) does the analogous structural work for preference optimization: DPO's i.i.d. assumption fails *hierarchically* (group / prompt / preference levels), and a tri-level Wasserstein ambiguity set yields a closed-form preference-margin penalty + prompt-level adversarial embedding perturbation + worst-case group reweighting, with finite-sample guarantees. The step-credit thread from the [10-20 block](../2026-10-20/arxiv-ai-search.md) continues under sibling custody (Self-Credit, R2S — §4.4).
>
> **④ The MAS lane turns self-critical: a pre-registered negative result, the first convergence theory, and a 500-million-agent scale-up — in the same day.** [`2610.27655`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27655) (UPenn) re-tests the capability-saturation rule for multi-agent vs single-agent systems where the agents actually work — conversing with a user under policy on τ²-bench's dual-control domains — with ~7,950 simulated conversations, one completion-token ledger per episode, and a public pre-registration changelog: **the MAS is worse in 12 of 13 valid cells and tied in the thirteenth** (weighted mean −28 points, 95% CI [−44, −12]); on the two cells where the rule predicts a benefit it loses by 13 and 22 points, and without a budget ceiling it at best draws level at **5.5× the tokens**. Its verdict: *for agents that serve people, the single-agent system is the default; a multi-agent design must show compute-matched evidence from its own setting.* [`2610.28286`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28286) (KAUST) supplies the constructive counterpart — collaborative problem-solving as block optimization with cross-part corrections and a coordinator that merges proposals — proving **linear convergence to an error floor**, and finding on GPT-OSS-120B robot routing that merging the *best checked combination* of proposals (not only non-interfering ones) cuts team search time by ~30%. At the scale-up end, [`2610.27746`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27746) (HKU × NUS × Jiangxi S&T Normal) runs **500 million survey-grounded household personas** with a ledger-tracked economy and a 12M-agent paired-simulation panel; [`2610.28554`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28554) (Meta AI × Oxford) fixes auxiliary world-centric camera views as a consistency proxy for multi-agent video world models; [`2610.28397`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28397) (MAS-Guard) unifies guardrails across instrumentable and opaque agents (**attack success −71.5–86.3%** on 6 benchmarks).

## 4. Per-Paper Detail

### 4.1 Lane A — Recommendation, Retrieval, Reranking, CTR

> Lane yield in the unclaimed remainder: **4 evaluation papers, 1 efficiency paper, 1 RAG-abstention benchmark, 1 condensed retrieval-agent paper; 0 advertising; 0 CTR.** The entire industrial ranking block of this window (Instagram, EASformer, ECHO, SPAR, SID topology, NAU, AGR, FLMNet, Rerank-System-1, Decision4Rec) and two rec-lane papers from the [paper-check](arxiv-paper-check.md) run (Next Best Action, MOSS) were claimed by the siblings first — see §4.4.

#### A1. FLINT: Fixed-budget Late Interaction
- **Authors**: Himank Handa, Negar Arabzadeh, Matei Zaharia
- **Institution**: University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (Handa); UC Berkeley (Arabzadeh, Zaharia) — stated
- **arXiv**: [2610.27273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27273) · cs.IR, cs.CL · submitted 2026-10-19
- **Abstract in one line**: multi-vector models like ColBERT beat single-vector biencoders at matched scale by matching tokens, but per-token storage and comparison kill scalability. FLINT applies **attention-based pooling symmetrically to queries and documents**, representing both with a fixed number of vectors — unlike prior work that pools only documents while keeping token-level queries — so both index size and the number of comparisons are bounded.
- **Key results**: BEIR parity with the closest baselines; wins on longer, harder query sets (BrowseComp+, CRUMB, TREC Tip-of-the-Tongue 2025); **1.5–2.8× faster end-to-end** than ConstBERT on long-query corpora; **5.3–9.3× smaller index** than ColBERTv2; effect holds on two backbones (BERT and ModernBERT).
- **Why it matters**: late interaction is the quality/efficiency hinge of modern retrieval, and FLINT shows the token-level budget is a *design parameter*, not a fixed cost. Pairs with the wiki's retrieval-efficiency reading of the sibling-claimed ECHO (100K-sequence cascade) — both buy efficiency by *selecting* representation slots rather than compressing blindly. (Abstract + HTML; single-source.)

#### A2. GARRET: Generative Feedback for Region Retrieval in Visual Documents
- **Authors**: Weijian Jian, Siyang Chen, Yuguang Yang, Yajun Zhang, Chunyu Xie, Dawei Leng, Yuhui Yin
- **Institution**: 360 AI Research; SAIS, UCAS; CASIA; SEIE, BUAA (stated)
- **arXiv**: [2610.27829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829) · cs.CV, cs.IR · submitted 2026-10-20
- **Abstract in one line**: multimodal RAG over visually rich documents needs evidence finer than whole pages, but region-level training usually needs costly query-to-region annotations or noisy model labels. GARRET trains from **(page, question, answer) triplets only**: a shared context-preserving retriever produces page- and region-level multi-vectors; their scores form a **soft routing distribution** over candidate region crops; an auxiliary VLM attends to each crop, aggregates contexts weighted by that routing, and predicts the answer under next-token prediction — so gradients through the routing assign query-conditioned credit to indexed regions. The auxiliary model is removed at inference.
- **Key results**: new **ViDoRe-V3-Region** benchmark (200,682 fixed candidate regions, 8 domains); macro nDCG@10 **0.4126**, +2.94 over the strongest visual baseline; page retrieval comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing **47.8% fewer vectors** per page.
- **Why it matters**: it converts the region-supervision bottleneck into a self-supervised routing credit-assignment problem — the same "credit through a differentiable router" move as the LLM-lane credit papers, applied to multimodal retrieval ground truth. (Abstract + HTML.)

#### A3. Robustness Analysis of Pareto-based Joint Evaluation of Fairness and Relevance in Recommender Systems (DPFR)
- **Authors**: Theresia Veronika Rampisela, Maria Maistro, Tuukka Ruotsalo, Christina Lioma
- **Institution**: University of Copenhagen × LUT University (stated); accepted to ACM TORS, extension of arXiv:2502.11921
- **arXiv**: [2610.27850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27850) · cs.IR · submitted 2026-10-20
- **Abstract in one line**: evaluating fairness and relevance separately yields two disagreeing model rankings; "relevance-aware" fairness measures unbalance the two. **DPFR** scores a recommender by its **distance to the Pareto frontier** of existing relevance and fairness measures computed from interaction data — modular (any existing measures), with the frontier's trade-offs as the reference.

#### B3. Adaptive Replay Refinement: Allocating Test-Time Compute Between Revision and Resampling (ARR)

#### B4. A Fisher-Geometric View of Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (→ RGBM)
- **Authors**: Liu Kaiyuan, Zhuang Ziyuan, Ma Haoxuan, Gu Yannian, Song Mingfei, Weng Rongxiang, Ye Jieping
- **Institution**: Zhejiang University × Meituan LongCat × Nanjing University × Shanghai Jiao Tong University (stated)

## 5. Trend Notes

1. **The rec lane has split cleanly into "industrial architecture" (siblings) and "academic measurement" (this report).** Every new ranking/CTR/generative-retrieval architecture in this window is production-attached and was claimed by `arxiv-daily`; what remains unclaimed is an evaluation cluster — DPFR (joint fairness×relevance proxies disagree), AutoSim (simulator fidelity is weight-level, not prompt-level), NevIR (pairwise preference ≠ positional utility), DeclineCraft (abstention degrades under iterative RAG), GARRET (ground-truth granularity). The academic rec-eval gap the [10-20 report](../2026-10-20/arxiv-ai-search.md) flagged is filling fast, and from a measurement-first direction.
2. **Test-time compute now has three currencies, priced in three different units.** Tokens (ARR's serial passes), joules (Token-Energy Law's affine active-bytes × realized-time model), and resource-constrained turns (budget-aware rewards' tokens/API-calls/steps). Read with ATEST (10-20), this is a forming *inference-economics* cluster: the same controller problem — reallocate compute where its marginal value is highest — attacked from the systems, reward-design and algorithm sides in the same week.
3. **Consolidation is becoming algebra.** The Fisher-geometric OPD paper's second-order identity (merging ≡ multi-teacher OPD) plus TPO's tri-level ambiguity decomposition both replace heuristics with structure that has knobs and guarantees — the same tightening the step-credit papers applied to advantage estimation on 10-20. The recurring warning: **gating matters more than the merge itself** (ungated write-backs strictly lose; success-only budget training strictly loses).
4. **MAS is in a self-disciplining phase.** One day produced a pre-registered, token-ledger-accounted negative result against the field's scaling heuristic (τ²-bench dual control), the first convergence theory checked against its own runs (KAUST), a 500M-persona population instrument, a structural fix for multi-agent world consistency, and charter/control-plane guardrails. Contrast this with the pure scale-up narrative: coordination mechanisms, not agent counts, are where the contested claims now sit.
5. **A small but notable honest-negative cluster**: Geometry of Memory (embedding proximity doesn't predict memory failures better than difficulty), Does-MAS-Pay (multi-agent doesn't help in 12/13 user-facing cells), Probing-Latent-Reasoning (a failed probe ≠ an absent value). Three independent "the signal you hoped for isn't there / isn't measurable that way" papers in one window — a healthy sign for the wiki's evaluation-oriented reading of the field.
